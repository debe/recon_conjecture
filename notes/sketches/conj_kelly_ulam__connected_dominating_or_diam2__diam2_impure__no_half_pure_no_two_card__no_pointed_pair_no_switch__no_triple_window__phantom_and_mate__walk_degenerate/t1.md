## 1. Setup and the exact meaning of "tied"

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3, A = A_G. H hypomorphic to G through σ; K := H.comap σ, so K − v ≅ G − v for
every v ("aligned hypomorph"); A′ = A_K; Δ := A_K − A_G (symmetric, entries 0, ±1, zero
diagonal; sign as in the checked file, `r u x = AK u x - AG u x`).

- w_k = A^k 1, c_k(v) = (A^k)_vv, m_b(v) = #{y ∼ v : deg y = b}, t(v) = triangles through v
  (c₃ = 2t), 𝒲 = span{w_k}, 𝒟 = span of degree-class indicators, 𝒰 = 𝒲 + 𝒟.
- A **vertex invariant** g assigns a number g(G, v) to every (graph, vertex), invariantly under
  isomorphism. g is **tied** if g(K, v) = g(G, v) for every G, every aligned hypomorph K of G
  and every v. 𝒯 := tied invariants; ℳ := {g ∈ 𝒯 : Ag ∈ 𝒯} ("rooted Kelly" RK(g) of t3).
  For g ∈ ℳ: Δg = 0, so every row of Δ is orthogonal to g.
- Known: deg ∈ 𝒯 (checked `deg_T0`); m_b ∈ 𝒯, i.e. 𝒟 ⊆ ℳ (checked `nbr_bal`; t3's T1);
  w_k, c_k ∈ 𝒯, i.e. 𝒲 ⊆ ℳ (Theorem H, checked inside `walk_degree_corank_one`).
- **Removal-similar**: x ≈ y iff G − x ≅ G − y. R(G) := the partition into ≈-classes ("card
  types"). Π(G) := the permutations of V preserving every class = ∏ Sym(R_i).
  Aut(G) ≤ Π(G). Twins: x, y with the transposition (x y) ∈ Aut(G).

**Lemma 1.1 (coset freedom; paper).** If σ is a hypomorphism G → H and π ∈ Π(G), then σ ∘ π is a
hypomorphism. In particular for H = G: every π ∈ Π(G) is a hypomorphism of G to itself, and
K = G.comap π is an aligned hypomorph of G, isomorphic to G, with K = G iff π ∈ Aut(G).
Argument: G − x ≅ G − πx ≅ H − σπx. ∎

**Lemma 1.2 (what "tied" means; paper).** g is tied iff g(G, v) depends only on the deck of G
and the isomorphism type of the card G − v.
Argument. (⇐) K has the deck of G and K − v ≅ G − v. (⇒) If G, G′ have the same deck and
G − v ≅ G′ − v′, choose a hypomorphism σ : G → G′; by Lemma 1.1 (compose with a transposition
inside the card-type class) we may take σv = v′; then g(G, v) = g(G′.comap σ, v) = g(G′, v′). ∎

So 𝒯 restricted to one graph is ℝ^{V/R}: **every tied function is constant on removal-similar
vertices**, and Theorem H / T1 are the statements "the walk, closed-walk and neighbour-degree
profiles of v are functions of the card G − v and the deck" (Hagos's referee's remark in this
language; t2 Lemma RS). The stable colour of v is not such a function (§2). t3 §1 ("Caveat")
already says this in one sentence; §2 makes it explicit and locates the first failing depth.

## 2. One explicit family: a chiral 3-necklace minus one bead

L₀, L₁, L₂ of L, the vertex t of L_i identified with the vertex s of L_{i+1} (indices mod 3);
call the three identified vertices u₀, u₁, u₂ (u_i = s of L_i). θ := the index shift, an

below does not use any source.) By Lemma 1.1, σ := (u₀ u₁) is a hypomorphism of G_L to itself
and K := G_L.comap σ is an aligned hypomorph. A tied g must satisfy g(u₀) = g(u₁) in G_L.

## 3. Why: where exactly A = Aᵀ is used, and why it stops at paths

### 3.1 The chirality term (paper)

In G = H − u₂ of §2, for a rooted tree (T, r): hom(T, G; u₀) counts maps into H with r ↦ u₀
avoiding u₂; hom(T, G; u₁) is, after applying θ⁻¹, the number with r ↦ u₀ avoiding u₁. By
inclusion–exclusion over the set S of vertices of T sent to the avoided vertex,

    hom(T, G; u₀) − hom(T, G; u₁) = Σ_{∅≠S} (−1)^{|S|} [ h_T(r ↦ u₀, S ↦ u₂) − h_T(r ↦ u₀, S ↦ u₁) ],

and h_T(r ↦ u₀, s ↦ u₂) = h_T(r ↦ u₁, s ↦ u₀) by θ. The first-order terms are therefore the
**antisymmetric parts of the two-point functions** h_T^{(r,s)}(x, y) := hom(T; r ↦ x, s ↦ y) at
(x, y) = (u₀, u₁).

- T a path with ends r, s: h(x, y) = (A^k)_{xy}, symmetric **because A = Aᵀ and the path has an
  automorphism exchanging r and s**. Inner s: (A^i)_{xy}·w_{k−i}(y), antisymmetric part
  (A^i)_{u₀u₁}(w_{k−i}(u₀) − w_{k−i}(u₁)) = 0 in H. Higher orders likewise. This is Lemma RS of
  t2 seen from the construction, and it is the content of (II) and (III) in Theorem H.
- T branching: exchanging the roles of r and s re-roots T, and (T, r, s) ≇ (T, s, r) in
  general. Member 2 with T = the 5-vertex tree of §2.2, s = the far leaf: the only walk of
  length 3 from u₀ to u₁ is u₀ y₀ z₀ u₁; read from u₀ the branch vertex sits on y₀ (degree 3),
  read from u₁ it sits on z₀ (degree 2). Only S = {far leaf} contributes (d(u₀, u₁) = 3), so the
  difference is exactly −(2 − 3) = +1 = 23 − 22. In general, for a link whose s–t distance is 3
  and this T: difference = Σ over the s–t paths s y z t of length 3 of (deg_H y − deg_H z).

So the tie of a rooted pattern needs a re-rooting symmetry of the pattern **and** symmetry of
A. Paths have the first; undirected graphs have the second; tournaments have neither (for a
digraph the reversal of a directed path is not a walk, which is where Theorem H already stops
at k = 1, t2 §8). A Hadamard product has no anti-automorphism playing the role of transposition.

### 3.2 The ceiling of all "σ itself is an isomorphism" arguments (paper)

**Proposition CT.** Let 𝒞 be a class for which one can argue "every aligned hypomorph K of G
equals G" (Δ = 0). Then 𝒞 ⊆ TW := {G : removal-similar vertices are twins}. For G ∈ TW,
Aut(G) = Π(G) = ∏ Sym(R_i) with every R_i a twin class; a twin-free member of TW is asymmetric
with pairwise non-isomorphic cards.
Argument: if x ≈ y are not twins, K := G.comap (x y) is an aligned hypomorph (Lemma 1.1) with
K ≠ G. If all ≈-pairs are twins, Sym(R_i) ≤ Aut(G) for each i, and Aut(G) ≤ Π(G) always. ∎

Consequences.
1. DWC₀, DWC₁ (checked), controllable graphs, and the class the angle aimed at (few or
   singleton stable cells, argued through Δ) are inside TW or their argument is wrong. The
   stable-cell version is wrong: §2.1 has n − 1 cells and is outside TW.
2. For a reconstructible G the aligned hypomorphs are exactly G.comap π, π ∈ Π(G). So on
   reconstructible graphs, and in particular for n ≤ 13, **"g is tied on G" is the statement
   "g is constant on removal-similar vertices"** and ℳ is {g ∈ ℝ^{V/R} : Ag ∈ ℝ^{V/R}}. A
   vertexwise lemma can be refuted there; RC does not protect it.
3. Any non-twin removal-similar pair x ≈ y gives e_x − e_y and 1_{N(x)∖N[y]} − 1_{N(y)∖N[x]} in
   𝒰^⊥ (rows of the Δ of the transposition), hence corank 𝒰 ≥ 2 (t2 Prop. PS with 𝒰). So the
   clause "walk vectors and degree classes miss two dimensions" of this step holds for every
   graph with one pair of isomorphic cards that are not twins. The step's residual is not
   "exotic linear algebra"; it contains by default everything with a non-trivial removal
   symmetry, and no Δ = 0 class can cut into that part.

## 5. What is tied: the algebra, the module, and the identity that carries Hadamard products

### 5.1 Theorem S (symmetrisation principle; paper)

For a graph F put hit_F(G, v) := hom(F, G) − hom(F, G − v), the number of homomorphisms whose
image contains v. For G, H hypomorphic through σ and **every** v:

    hit_F(G, v) − hit_F(H, σv) = hom(F, G) − hom(F, H).

Argument: hom(F, G − v) = hom(F, H − σv). ∎ So **hit_F is tied iff hom(F, ·) is recognizable**,
and Σ_v hit_F(G, v) = Σ_φ |im φ| gives Kelly's lemma in hom form: Σ_j (n − j)·δhom_j(F) = 0,
where hom_j counts homomorphisms with image of size j; hence δhom(F) = δhom_n(F) (the
vertex-surjective, "spanning" layer; t3 §2.1).

By inclusion–exclusion over the fibre of v (an independent set S of F),

    hit_F(G, v) = Σ_{∅≠S indep.} (−1)^{|S|+1} hom(F/S, G; s ↦ v),        (F/S: S merged to s)

so a tied hit count is a **signed sum over all rootings** of F (and of its quotients), never a
single rooted count. For a path this sum has one unknown (the two end-rootings coincide); that
is equation (b) of Theorem H. For a tree with two leaf orbits it has two unknown functions and
one equation (t3's "master system"); §2 shows the two are separately untied.

Recognizable hom counts (so tied hit counts) available today: every F all of whose components
have fewer than n vertices (Kelly; checked `kelly_induced_count`), and every **bouquet**
(paths and cycles glued at one vertex, any size): hom = Σ_v Π_i w_{a_i}(v) Π_j c_{k_j}(v)
(t3's T3, from Theorem H).

Hand check (member 2, F = spider S(1,1,2), all terms listed, no triangles so codegree terms
vanish): hit_F(v) = deg²w₂ + 2·A(deg ∘ w₂) + deg·A(deg²) + A²(deg²) − w₃ − 2·deg·w₂ − deg³ + deg².
All terms except 2·A(deg ∘ w₂) + A²(deg²) are in the tied algebra, and
2·22 + 19 = 63 = 2·23 + 17 at s₃ and s₆. In a general graph the tied combination is
2·A(deg ∘ w₂) + A²(deg²) − 2·Σ_{y∼v} deg(y)·codeg(v, y).

### 5.2 The three-variable resolvent identity (β) (paper; uses A = Aᵀ once)

Notation of t2 §3: N(t) = (I − tA)⁻¹, N₀ for the card G − v, C_v = N_vv, w_x(t) = (N(t)1)_x,
ω_v := w_v / C_v. Entry (x, ·) of (α) summed over the second index gives, for x ≠ v,
(N₀1)_x = w_x − N_xv·ω_v, and the right side vanishes at x = v. Hence, for the spider series
Sp_G(t₁, t₂, t₃) := Σ_x Π_i w_x(t_i),

    Sp_{G−v} = Sp_G − Σ_i ω_v(t_i)·U_i + Σ_i ω_v(t_j)ω_v(t_k)·V_i − ω_v(t₁)ω_v(t₂)ω_v(t₃)·Y,     (β)

    U_i := [N(t_i)(w(t_j) ∘ w(t_k))]_v,   V_i := Σ_x w_x(t_i) N_xv(t_j) N_xv(t_k),   Y := Σ_x Π_i N_xv(t_i),

({i, j, k} = {1, 2, 3}; U_i uses N_xv = N_vx). Sp_{G−v} is a card invariant, Sp_G is
recognizable, ω_v is tied (Theorem H). So **Ξ_v := Σ ω U − Σ ωω V + ωωω Y is tied.**
The coefficients of U_i are the wanted vectors A^m(w_a ∘ w_b)(v). (β) is the generating
function of Theorem S for spiders. It is the two-variable identity the angle asked for, and
it carries one combination, not U alone: U (rooted trees), V (a cycle through v with a tail)
and Y (theta graphs through v) enter together, and by §2 U alone is not tied. With m legs
the same expansion gives 2^m − 1 families of unknowns and one tied combination.

### 5.3 Theorem M (the tied algebra and the tied module; paper)

1. 𝒯 is closed under pointwise operations. It contains the Schur algebra generated by
   {m_b}, {w_k}, {c_k} and {hit_F : F a bouquet or a graph with all components < n vertices}.
   The last family is new and is not obviously in the algebra of the first three
   (e.g. 2A(deg ∘ w₂) + A²(deg²) − 2Σ deg·codeg).
2. ℳ ⊇ 𝒲 + 𝒟 = 𝒰; A𝒲 ⊆ 𝒲; A𝒟 ⊆ 𝒯 but A𝒟 ⊄ ℳ.
3. Not in ℳ (§2): deg ∘ w₂, w₂ ∘ w₂, 1_{deg=b} ∘ w₂, m_b, c₃ = 2t. Hence ℳ is not Schur-closed,
   not a module over functions of the degree, and not A-invariant.
4. Global shadow (uses A = Aᵀ): gᵀAg′ is recognizable for g ∈ 𝒯, g′ ∈ ℳ (t3 §2.4, "what symmetry
   gives for free"). Nothing is known for g, g′ ∈ 𝒯 ∖ ℳ beyond the Kelly range.
5. Conditional case (t3's T4): if the degree partition of G is equitable, all rooted tree
   counts are functions of the degree in G and in K with the same parameters, so the tree tie
   holds trivially and adds nothing to 𝒟.

I have no argument for any element of ℳ outside 𝒰. By §3.2 item 2 the true ℳ of a
reconstructible graph is {g ∈ ℝ^{V/R} : Ag ∈ ℝ^{V/R}}, which for a graph with pairwise
non-isomorphic cards is everything; the gap between 𝒰 and that is the conjecture for
card-distinct graphs, not a missing trick.

## 6. Sound replacement targets (coset-invariant; all open; no class proposed from them)

A statement can only be true if it is invariant under σ ↦ σπ, π ∈ Π(G).

- **GT (global tree tie).** hom(T, G) = hom(T, H) for hypomorphic G, H and every tree T.
  Equivalent to: G, H are not distinguished by colour refinement, and to fractional
  isomorphism (Dell–Grohe–Rattan 2018, Theorem 1 (i)–(iii); statement read in the arXiv
  text; Tinhofer for (iii) as cited there). RC ⇒ GT. By Theorem S, GT for |T| = n says:
  **the number of spanning trees of G isomorphic to T is recognizable, for every tree T.**
  Known cases: stars; paths (Tutte; Theorem H); spiders and all bouquets (T3). First open
  case: a caterpillar with two legs on n vertices, in vector form (w_a ∘ w_b)ᵀA^m(w_c ∘ w_d)
  with a + b + c + d + m + 1 ≥ n. I found no source for or against (search limited to the
  repository notes and two web queries; UNVERIFIED negative; `hom_counts_and_logic.md`
  open gap 2 lists the same question).
  If GT held, the intrinsic class "every equitable colouring of G is injective" (discrete
  stable partition) would be reconstructible, because such a graph is identified by colour
  refinement (standard; Arvind–Köbler–Rattan–Verbitsky 2015 characterise all such graphs;
  UNVERIFIED, from memory). Lean body, **not proposed** (no argument):
  ```lean
  Recon.ClassReconstructible (fun n G => ∀ c : Fin n → ℕ, (∀ x y : Fin n, c x = c y → ∀ j : ℕ, {z : Fin n | G.Adj x z ∧ c z = j}.ncard = {z : Fin n | G.Adj y z ∧ c z = j}.ncard) → Function.Injective c)
  ```
  Tournament filter: GT's analogue fails already for paths (Stockmeyer's 5-vertex pair has 61
  against 63 walks of length 4, t3 §2.5), so any argument must use A = Aᵀ beyond Theorem H's
  two uses, and by §3.1 it cannot be a vertexwise argument.
- **GT⁺ (joint tie).** The multiset {(type of G − v, stable colour of v)} is recognizable.
  Equivalent to: some hypomorphism in the coset σΠ(G) satisfies the vertexwise tree tie.
  This is the corrected form of the angle's goal. Then, and only then, the Δ of that
  representative is orthogonal to the stable cells.
- **EC (edge census by card type).** #{edges xy : G − x ≅ C, G − y ≅ C′} is recognizable for all
  C, C′; equivalently 1_Cᵀ Δ 1_{C′} = 0, i.e. Δ vanishes as a bilinear form on 𝒯 × 𝒯 (by
  Lemma 1.2 the indicators of card types span 𝒯 on G). Known on 𝒯 × 𝒰 only. Since the
  adjacency of x, y is read off e(G − x − y), deg x, deg y, EC is a statement about the census
  of pairs by (type of G − x, type of G − y, type of G − x − y); that is the territory of the
  pair censuses of rounds 9–13, and I add nothing to it here.
- The corrected RK: "the multiset {(type of G − v, (Ag)(v))} is recognizable" for tied g. For
  g = t this is the right form of RK(paw).

## 7. The class child the module does support: corank two

𝒩 := 𝒰^⊥ ∩ ℤ^V: integer vectors f with Σ_x f(x) w_k(x) = 0 for all k and Σ_{deg x = j} f(x) = 0 for
all j. Rows of Δ lie in 𝒩 (checked: `hr1`, `hr2` in the DWC₁ proof).

A **balanced bi-switch pair** of G: p, q : V → {−1, 0, 1}, both non-zero, with disjoint
supports P, Q, both in 𝒩, such that for x ∈ P, y ∈ Q: x ∼ y iff p(x)q(y) = 1. (So G is complete
between P⁺ and Q⁺ and between P⁻ and Q⁻, empty between P⁺ and Q⁻ and between P⁻ and Q⁺;
|P⁺ ∩ D_j| = |P⁻ ∩ D_j| in every degree class D_j, and p, q are walk-balanced.)

**Theorem DWC₂ (paper, all n ≥ 3).** If any three vectors of 𝒩 are linearly dependent and G
has no balanced bi-switch pair, then every aligned hypomorph of G equals G; G is
reconstructible.

Argument (integers only). Let r := Δ, rows r_u ∈ 𝒩, r symmetric, r_uu = 0, entries in {0, ±1}.
Suppose r_xy =: ε ≠ 0. For any u pick (a, b, c) ≠ 0 with a·r_u + b·r_x + c·r_y = 0. Coordinate x:
a·r_ux + c·r_yx = 0; coordinate y: a·r_uy + b·r_xy = 0. If a = 0 then c = b = 0; so a ≠ 0,
c = −aε·r_ux, b = −aε·r_uy, and cancelling a:

    r_uz = ε·(r_uy·r_xz + r_ux·r_yz) = p(u)q(z) + q(u)p(z),      p := r_y,  q := ε·r_x.

p(x) = ε ≠ 0, q(y) = 1; 0 = r_uu = 2p(u)q(u) gives disjoint supports; p, q ∈ 𝒩 as rows of Δ. For
x′ ∈ P, y′ ∈ Q: r_{x′y′} = p(x′)q(y′) = ±1, and r = A_K − A_G says r = 1 ⇒ non-edge of G,
r = −1 ⇒ edge of G. So (p, −q) is a balanced bi-switch pair. Contradiction; r = 0. ∎

This is S6 of `notes/sketches/conj_kelly_ulam__connected_dominating_or_diam2/t3.md` with 𝒲
replaced by 𝒰 and the real spectral decomposition replaced by a three-row dependence (no
eigenvalues, no rank over ℚ). General corank c: the same rows give rank Δ ≤ c and nothing
more.

**Theorem DWC₂′ (crossing the ceiling by one step; paper).** Call a balanced bi-switch pair
*trivial* if P = {x₁, x₂} and Q = (N(x₁) △ N(x₂)) ∖ {x₁, x₂}, or the same with p, q exchanged.
If any three vectors of 𝒩 are dependent and every balanced bi-switch pair of G is trivial,
then G is reconstructible.
Argument: as above, Δ ≠ 0 gives a pair with Δ = ±(pqᵀ + qpᵀ) (restricted to P × Q and Q × P);
if it is trivial with P = {x₁, x₂}, K differs from G exactly on the pairs {x_i, z}, z ∈ Q, where
it exchanges the roles of x₁ and x₂; so K = G.comap (x₁ x₂) ≅ G. ∎
By §3.2 item 3 every graph with a non-twin removal-similar pair x ≈ y has the bi-switch pair
(e_x − e_y, 1_{N(x)∖N[y]} − 1_{N(y)∖N[x]}), which is trivial in the sense above; so DWC₂ ⊆ TW, while
DWC₂′ is not confined to TW: it admits one such pair when the corank is exactly 2. It is the
smallest step past Proposition CT that the linear method allows. I exhibit no member of
DWC₂′ ∖ DWC₂ and none is needed for the argument.

Lean body for DWC₂ (style of the frozen statement; **not elaborated in this session**):
```lean
Recon.ClassReconstructible (fun n G => ∀ w : ℕ → Fin n → ℤ, (∀ x : Fin n, w 0 x = 1) → (∀ k : ℕ, ∀ x : Fin n, w (k + 1) x = ∑ y : Fin n, Set.indicator (G.neighborSet x) (w k) y) → (∀ f g h : Fin n → ℤ, (∀ k : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, f x * w k x = 0) → (∀ j : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, Set.indicator {x : Fin n | (G.neighborSet x).ncard = j} f x = 0) → (∀ k : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, g x * w k x = 0) → (∀ j : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, Set.indicator {x : Fin n | (G.neighborSet x).ncard = j} g x = 0) → (∀ k : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, h x * w k x = 0) → (∀ j : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, Set.indicator {x : Fin n | (G.neighborSet x).ncard = j} h x = 0) → ∃ a b c : ℤ, (a ≠ 0 ∨ b ≠ 0 ∨ c ≠ 0) ∧ ∀ x : Fin n, a * f x + b * g x + c * h x = 0) ∧ ¬ (∃ p q : Fin n → ℤ, (∃ x : Fin n, p x ≠ 0) ∧ (∃ x : Fin n, q x ≠ 0) ∧ (∀ x : Fin n, p x = 0 ∨ q x = 0) ∧ (∀ x : Fin n, p x * p x ≤ 1 ∧ q x * q x ≤ 1) ∧ (∀ k : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, p x * w k x = 0) ∧ (∀ j : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, Set.indicator {x : Fin n | (G.neighborSet x).ncard = j} p x = 0) ∧ (∀ k : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, q x * w k x = 0) ∧ (∀ j : ℕ, ∑ x : Fin n, Set.indicator {x : Fin n | (G.neighborSet x).ncard = j} q x = 0) ∧ ∀ x y : Fin n, (p x * q y = 1 → G.Adj x y) ∧ (p x * q y = -1 → ¬ G.Adj x y)))
```
DWC₂′ replaces `¬ (∃ p q, …)` by `∀ p q, … → (trivial p q ∨ trivial q p)` with
`trivial p q := ∃ x₁ x₂, x₁ ≠ x₂ ∧ (∀ z, p z ≠ 0 ↔ z = x₁ ∨ z = x₂) ∧ ∀ z, z ≠ x₁ → z ≠ x₂ → (q z ≠ 0 ↔ ¬ (G.Adj x₁ z ↔ G.Adj x₂ z))`.
The residual after DWC₂′: corank 𝒰 ≥ 3, or a non-trivial balanced bi-switch pair.

## 10. Where this could break

1. **§2 is hand arithmetic.** Each number was computed twice; Theorem H and T1 hold at the
   pair in both members (w₃, w₄, c₄, neighbour degrees), which is a check of the tables. A red
   team should recompute member 1 from the edge list (8 vertices, 9 edges).
2. **Reading of the goal.** I read "hom(T, K; v) = hom(T, G; v)" vertexwise for K = H.comap σ and
   an arbitrary hypomorphism σ, as in the task. For a cleverly chosen σ in its coset the
   statement is GT⁺ and is open (§6).
3. **Proposition CT is trivial**, and that is its weakness as a guide: it says the Δ = 0 method
   is confined to TW, not that the residual avoids TW. No member of the residual is known.
4. **DWC₂ may be disjoint from the residual**, like DWC₁ (t2 §7.3). Its value is the same:
   it converts "corank ≥ 2" into "corank ≥ 3 or an explicit switch pair".
5. **The Lean bodies of §6 and §7 were not elaborated.** The quantifier `∀ w` over walk
   sequences follows the frozen statement; `Set.indicator` on a set-builder needs the same
   coercions as there.
6. **GT and EC**: "no source found" is a literature negative after a shallow search;
   UNVERIFIED. The equivalence "GT at |T| = n ⇔ spanning trees by type" uses only
   hom = Σ over quotients of injective counts.
