## 1. Recast (part (a))

### 1.1 Decorated signed base

Setting and notation of t2 §5.1: τ ∈ Aut(G), τ² = 1, fixed points F, pairs P_i = {u_i, u′_i} (i ∈ I),
matching graph M = M_τ on I, signature σ, twists G^Q, partial swaps π_S, δ(S) the cut of S in M.
**H1:** 𝒰(G) = Fix(τ).

The **decorated base** D̄ is the quotient on F ⊔ I with block types: F–F adjacency; F–i ∈ {0, 1}
(f sees both of P_i or neither); i–j ∈ {0, m, 2} (empty, matching, complete); ι_i ∈ {0, 1}
(u_i ~ u′_i). Its type-m edges are M. Then G = Cov(D̄, σ), and Theorem PM (r15 paper) says every
aligned hypomorph is K = Cov(D̄, σ·(−1)^Q) = G^Q: **same D̄, two signatures**. τ is an automorphism
of both. The orbit partition is equitable in both with the same quotient matrix q(O, O′).

For a τ-invariant U ⊆ V write G[U] for the induced graph; it is τ-symmetric with matching graph
M[U] (the pairs inside U), and K[U] = G[U]^{Q ∩ E(M[U])}.

### 1.2 Orbit-preserving maps are partial swaps

**Lemma OP (paper, no H1).** Let U be τ-invariant, R ⊆ E(M[U]), and φ : G[U]^R → G[U] an
isomorphism mapping every τ-orbit in U onto itself. Then φ = π_S|_U for S = {i : φ(u_i) = u′_i}, and
R = δ_{M[U]}(S).

Argument. φ is a bijection preserving each pair, so it is π_S. Twisting commutes with relabelling
inside pairs: π_S(G[U]^R) = (π_S G[U])^R = G[U]^{R + δ(S)} (t2 Theorem PM.2). This equals G[U] iff
R + δ(S) = ∅. ∎

**Definition.** A τ-invariant U is **orbit-rigid** if for every R ⊆ E(M[U]) every isomorphism
G[U]^R → G[U] maps every τ-orbit onto itself. By Lemma OP, for orbit-rigid U:
G[U]^R ≅ G[U] ⟺ R is a cut of M[U].

Two sufficient conditions, both intrinsic to G[U]:
- **H1 for (G[U], τ|_U).** Walk vectors of G[U]^R and G[U] coincide pointwise (the difference of the
  adjacency matrices kills Fix(τ), and A preserves Fix(τ); induction on k). If 𝒰(G[U]) = Fix(τ|_U),
  two different orbits differ in some w_k (degree is w_1), so an isomorphism, which preserves walk
  counts, preserves orbits.
- **SC for G[U]:** the coarsest equitable partition of G[U] is the orbit partition. Stable colours
  are computed from the quotient matrix of any equitable partition, and twisting does not change
  q; so the stable colouring of G[U]^R equals that of G[U] pointwise.

**Lemma R (paper).** Under H1, V itself is orbit-rigid. Hence: every isomorphism G^Q → G is some
π_S; G^Q ≅ G ⟺ Q is a cut; Aut(G) = {π_S : S a union of components of M} ≅ ℤ₂^{c(M)}.

So under H1 **SCL is the statement "Q is a cut"**, and there is nothing to gain from "up to an
automorphism of the cover": the decorated base is asymmetric (an automorphism of the quotient
matrix preserves quotient walk counts, hence fixes every orbit), and the cover has no isomorphism
that ignores fibres. TF-isomorphisms (Lauri–Mizzi–Scapellato, LIB §3.3) can occur only between
*deleted* graphs.

### 1.3 The signed-graph statement

> **SCL, signed form.** D̄ a decorated base whose quotient walk vectors and degree classes span
> ℝ^{F ⊔ I}. σ, σ′ = σ·(−1)^Q signatures on M. If Cov(D̄, σ′) − v ≅ Cov(D̄, σ) − v for every vertex v
> (same v on both sides), then σ′ and σ are switching equivalent.

By Zaslavsky's circle-sign lemma (arXiv:1303.3347, Lemma 4.1, read by the librarian, LIB §3.2)
the conclusion is: every circle of M has the same sign in σ and σ′, i.e. every cycle is Q-even.
Parity of |Z ∩ Q| is linear on the cycle space, so **it suffices to treat a generating set of
cycles** (used in §4).

Calibration (LIB §5): with M = K_n, no decoration and no H1, the fibre-preserving form is the open
two-graph Conjecture 2 of Duncan–Hoffman–Solazzo, arXiv:0810.3189. Every argument below uses H1
(through Lemma R / asymmetry of D̄) or a rigidity hypothesis, and says where.

## 2. What one card carries

### 2.1 The card as a one-point extension

Fix i ∈ I, u = u_i, a = u′_i. Put X_i := G − P_i (τ-symmetric, matching graph M − i) and
A_i := N_G(a) ∖ {u} ⊆ V(X_i). Then

    G − u = X_i +_a A_i   (a new vertex a joined to A_i),     N_G(u) ∖ {a} = τA_i.

For K: X_i^{Q_i} with Q_i := Q ∖ star(i), and A_i^Q := A_i Δ P_{Q(i)}, P_{Q(i)} := ⋃{P_j : ij ∈ Q}.
G is recovered from (X_i, A_i, τA_i, ι_i); the card keeps (X_i, A_i) and **forgets the mate τA_i**.
SCL at this card asks whether the isomorphism type of (X; A) fixes that of (X; {A, τA}).

### 2.2 Fibre-preserving card maps kill [Q]

**Lemma FB (paper; sharpens LIB Lemma E).** Assume H1. Let f : K − u_i → G − u_i be an isomorphism
with f(a) = a that maps τ-orbits of X_i onto τ-orbits. Then Q is a cut.

Argument. f induces a bijection ᾱ of (F ⊔ I) ∖ {i}. It preserves block types (numbers of edges
between orbits; twisting does not change them) and, since f(N_K(a)) = N_G(a) and
|N_K(a) ∩ O| = |N_G(a) ∩ O|, also the block types at i. So ᾱ ∪ {i ↦ i} is an automorphism of the
quotient matrix of D̄, hence the identity (§1.2). Now f|_{X_i} is orbit-wise the identity, so by
Lemma OP f|_{X_i} = π_S with Q_i = δ_{M−i}(S), i ∉ S. Finally f(A_i Δ P_{Q(i)}) = A_i reads
Q(i) = S ∩ M(i). Together Q = δ_M(S). ∎

So for [Q] ≠ 0 **every pair-card map breaks the orbit partition**. For a fixed-point card
g : K − f → G − f the same argument gives less: g may cover a non-trivial automorphism of D̄ − f,
because nothing ties g to N(f).

### 2.3 Where a card map may move orbits: the reach of δ

Let v be any vertex, C = G − v, f_v : K − v → G − v a card map, N = N_G(v), N′ = f_v(N_K(v)),
δ_v = 1_{N′} − 1_N (the vector of (R_c), t2 §1). Write W_y(t) = Σ_k w_k(y) t^k for the walk series
in G.

**Lemma W (paper).** For every y ≠ v,

    W_{f_v⁻¹(y)}(t) − W_y(t) = t · W_v(t) · Σ_k t^k (A_C^k δ_v)_y.

Hence, under H1: **f_v⁻¹(y) lies in the τ-orbit of y iff y ∉ reach(δ_v)** :=
{y : (A_C^k δ_v)_y ≠ 0 for some k}. In particular supp δ_v ⊆ reach(δ_v), and f_v preserves every
orbit iff δ_v = 0.

Argument. Let K′ be the image of K under f_v ∪ {v ↦ v}; K′ and G agree off v, and v has
neighbourhood N′ in K′. Split the walks from y in K′ at their first visit to v:
W^{K′}_y = W^C_y + t·⟨1_{N′}, (1 − tA_C)⁻¹ e_y⟩·W^{K′}_v, and the same for G with N. Here
W^{K′}_v = W^K_v = W^G_v (K ≅ K′ fixing v; walk vectors of K and G agree, §1.2). Subtract.
The left side is W^{K′}_y − W^G_y with W^{K′}_y = W^K_{f⁻¹y} = W^G_{f⁻¹y}. W_v(t) has constant term
1, so the right side vanishes iff the sum does; under H1 equal walk series means equal orbit. ∎

Reading. The pin "f_i(a) = a" of LIB Lemma E is the linear condition ⟨δ_i, A_C^k e_a⟩ = 0 for
all k. Lemma W is the exact form of "a card map is orbit-preserving away from the hole". It is a
count identity and by itself gives no contradiction: (R_c) only says Σ_y (A_C^k δ)_y = 0.

### 2.4 The single-card criterion is a double coset

Assume a card map f at i with f(a) = a. Put Λ := Aut(X_i), Λ_A its stabiliser of the set A_i, and
τ′ := f τ f⁻¹ ∈ Λ (f restricted to X_i^{Q_i} → X_i; τ is an automorphism of both). So X_i carries
two involutions: τ with quotient (D̄ − i, σ) and τ′ with quotient (D̄ − i, σ^Q).

**Proposition DC (paper).** There is an isomorphism K → G mapping P_i onto P_i (under H1: any
isomorphism) **iff τ′ ∈ Λ_A τ Λ_A**. There is a τ-equivariant isomorphism X_i^{Q_i} → X_i **iff τ′ is
conjugate to τ in Λ**.

Argument. An isomorphism ψ with ψ(P_i) = P_i may be composed with τ so that ψ(a) = a, ψ(u) = u.
Then g := ψ|_{X} = λf with λ ∈ Λ, and ψ is an isomorphism iff g(A^Q) = A and g(τA^Q) = τA. The
first says λ ∈ Λ_A. Since fτA^Q = τ′fA^Q = τ′A, the second says λτ′A = τA, i.e. τλτ′ ∈ Λ_A, i.e.
τ′ ∈ λ⁻¹τΛ_A. Conversely τ′ = λ₁τλ₂ with λ₁, λ₂ ∈ Λ_A gives λ = λ₁⁻¹. The second claim: λf is
equivariant iff λτ′λ⁻¹ = τ. ∎

Remarks.
- This is the form in which LIB's lever L4 ("conjugacy classes of fibre-switching involutions",
  Mizzi arXiv:2603.27559, abstract only per LIB) enters: not conjugacy, but a double coset of the
  stabiliser of the partner's neighbourhood.
- Kocay-type arithmetic (LIB §1.1, Theorem 3.1): ⟨τ, τ′⟩ is dihedral, ρ := τ′τ. If ρ has **odd
  order** r, then τ′ = κτκ⁻¹ with κ = ρ^{(r+1)/2}, so the pair-deleted graphs are equivariantly
  isomorphic. This is conjugacy, not the double coset: it would suffice to find a conjugator in
  Λ_A·C_Λ(τ), and I have no argument for that.
- The second card map that Kocay's §5 asks for is free here: τ f τ : K − a → G − a.

### 2.5 Theorem ONE (single pinned pair with rigid deletion)

**Theorem ONE (paper).** Assume H1 and, for some i:
1. (pin) for all R ⊆ E(M − i) and all b ∈ V ∖ P_i: (G − P_i)^R ≇ G − {u_i, b};
2. V ∖ P_i is orbit-rigid (for instance H1 holds for (G − P_i, τ)).

Then every aligned hypomorph of G is π_S G; G is reconstructible.

Argument. K = G^Q by PM. If f(a) = b ≠ a then f restricts to K − P_i = X_i^{Q_i} ≅ G − {u_i, b},
against 1. So f(a) = a and f|_{X_i} : X_i^{Q_i} → X_i; by 2 it is orbit-wise the identity; the last
three lines of Lemma FB give Q = δ(S). ∎

**Lemma FP (paper).** Assume H1 and some f ∈ F with V ∖ {f} orbit-rigid. Then Q is a cut.
(K − f = (G − f)^Q ≅ G − f, aligned; Lemma OP with M[V ∖ f] = M.)

Both hypotheses concern G only. Neither follows from H1 (§2.6).

### 2.6 The one explicit family: a pinned card map that is blind (hand verification)

Claim refuted: *"if the decorated base is asymmetric and a card map at u_i fixes the partner
u′_i, then that card decides [Q]"* (the hope behind lever L1 without the equivariance clause of
Lemma E). **I do not claim H1 for this family** (it would need a rank computation I cannot do by
hand for all m); it has the consequence of H1 that Lemma FB uses, an asymmetric base.

Construction, m ≥ 4. Y_m: vertices c_k, x_k, y_k, p_k, q_k, r_k (k ∈ ℤ_m); edges c_kx_k, x_ky_k,
y_kc_{k+1} (a cycle of length 3m), x_kp_k, y_kq_k, q_kr_k. Base M := Y_m + i with i ~ c_0, c_1, c_2;
σ = + on Y_m, σ(ic_0) = +, σ(ic_1) = σ(ic_2) = −. No fixed points, no complete blocks, no inner
edges. G := Cov(M, σ): vertices z⁺, z⁻ (z ∈ Y_m), u = i⁺, a = i⁻; edges z^εw^ε for zw ∈ E(Y_m);
u ~ c_0⁺, c_1⁻, c_2⁻; a ~ c_0⁻, c_1⁺, c_2⁺. n = 12m + 2 ≥ 50. Q := {ic_0, ic_2}, K := G^Q:
u ~ c_0⁻, c_1⁻, c_2⁺; a ~ c_0⁺, c_1⁺, c_2⁻.

1. *Aut(Y_m) = {s_j : k ↦ k + j} ≅ ℤ_m.* The 3m-cycle is the only cycle. On it, x-vertices are
   those adjacent to a leaf, y-vertices the other vertices of degree 3, c-vertices have degree 2.
   The cyclic type word (c, x, y) is not a rotation of its reverse (c, y, x), so only rotations by
   multiples of 3 survive; pendants follow.
2. *The base is asymmetric.* i is the only vertex of degree 3 that is not adjacent to a leaf and
   has no neighbour adjacent to a leaf (x_k ~ p_k; y_k ~ x_k; c_k ~ x_k). So an automorphism fixes
   i, restricts to some s_j preserving {c_0, c_1, c_2}, and j = 0 because m ≥ 4.
3. *Q is not a cut.* M − i = Y_m is connected and Q-free, so a cut contains none or all of the
   edges at i.
4. *The card map.* X := G − {u, a} = K − {u, a} = Y⁺ ⊔ Y⁻. Define f(a) = a, f(z⁺) = (s_1z)⁺,
   f(z⁻) = (s_{−2}z)⁻. It is an automorphism of X, and f(N_K(a)) = f{c_0⁺, c_1⁺, c_2⁻} =
   {c_1⁺, c_2⁺, c_0⁻} = N_G(a). So **f : K − u → G − u is an isomorphism fixing a**; by τ also
   K − a ≅ G − a. f is not fibre-preserving: f(c_0⁺) = c_1⁺, f(c_0⁻) = c_{−2}⁻.
5. *K ≇ G.* M has girth 5 with exactly two 5-cycles, Z₁ = i c_0x_0y_0c_1 and Z₂ = i c_1x_1y_1c_2
   (the other cycles have lengths 8, 3m − 4, 3m − 1, 3m). A closed 5-walk in a cover projects to
   a 5-cycle of M, and a 5-cycle lifts to two 5-cycles if positive, to none if negative. In G:
   sign Z₁ = −, sign Z₂ = +, so the 5-cycles are (u, c_1⁻, x_1⁻, y_1⁻, c_2⁻) and its τ-image. In K:
   sign Z₁ = +, sign Z₂ = −; the 5-cycles are (u, c_0⁻, x_0⁻, y_0⁻, c_1⁻) and its τ-image.
   Let φ : K → G be an isomorphism. On a 5-cycle the x-vertex is the one adjacent to a leaf, the
   y-vertex its cycle-neighbour having an off-cycle neighbour of degree 2, and the lift of i is
   determined from these. So φ{u, a} = {u, a}; composing with τ, φ(u) = u, φ(a) = a, and
   φ(c_0⁻) = c_1⁻. Then φ maps Y⁻ → Y⁻ by s_1, and the third neighbour of u gives φ(c_2⁺) = c_0⁺,
   so φ = s_{−2} on Y⁺. But then φ(N_K(a)) = {c_{−2}⁺, c_{−1}⁺, c_3⁻} ≠ {c_0⁻, c_1⁺, c_2⁺} = N_G(a),
   since c_3 ≠ c_0. Contradiction.
6. *In the language of §2.4:* Λ = ℤ_m ≀ ℤ₂, Λ_A = 1 (m ≥ 4), τ′ = fτf⁻¹ identifies the sheets by s_3
   instead of the identity, τ′ ≠ τ, so τ′ ∉ Λ_Aτ Λ_A. The pair-deleted graph is disconnected,
   **M − i is balanced**, and the two sheets move independently.
7. *K is not an aligned hypomorph.* c_0⁺ lies on a 5-cycle of K and on none of G, so K − c_0⁺ has
   one 5-cycle and G − c_0⁺ has two. (Also PM⁺ at c_0.) No claim is made about the
   reconstructibility of G.

Conclusion: a pinned card map need not be τ-equivariant on N(a), even over an asymmetric base.
The failure is a failure of orbit-rigidity of X_i. One card can be blind; the question is
whether all can.

## 3. L2: the common super- and subgraph

Γ⁺ := G ∪ G^Q (Q-blocks filled to K_{2,2}), Γ⁻ := G ∩ G^Q (Q-blocks emptied).

**Proposition L2 (paper).**
1. Γ± are τ-symmetric with matching graph M ∖ Q. {π_S : δ_M(S) ⊆ Q} ≤ Aut(Γ±), a group ≅ ℤ₂^{c(M∖Q)};
   π_S exchanges the two matchings of the block jk ∈ Q iff jk ∈ δ(S).
2. Under H1, G^Q ≅ G iff some π_S with δ_M(S) ⊆ Q exchanges the matchings of **every** Q-block,
   iff the graph M/(M ∖ Q) (contract each component of M ∖ Q; keep Q as edges) is loopless and
   bipartite.
3. "Both matchings of each Q-block lie in one edge orbit" is necessary and not sufficient:
   three components of M ∖ Q pairwise joined by Q-edges give a triangle in M/(M ∖ Q); each block
   is exchanged by one π_S, no π_S exchanges all three.
4. A card map f_v : K − v → G − v is an automorphism of Γ⁺ − v iff it maps the edges of E(G) ∖ E(K)
   off v (the G-matchings of the Q-blocks) onto the edges of E(K) ∖ E(G) off v.

Argument. 1: t2 PM.2 applied to Γ±. 2: Lemma R, and Q = δ(S) with δ(S) ⊆ Q means S is a union of
components of M ∖ Q 2-colouring the contracted graph. 3 and 4: direct. ∎

**Why Kocay's Lemma 1.5 does not transfer.** In Kocay 1984 (LIB §1.1, read there) G and H differ
at one vertex, the common supergraph adds one edge, and the card map extends because the
endpoints of the missing edge are the vertices of degree k − 1: *the difference is recognisable
inside the card*. Here item 4 would need the Q-blocks to be recognisable in the card, and (R)
says the opposite: Δ kills every degree class and every walk vector, so no walk-type invariant
marks them. The honest transfer of Kocay's setting is the one-vertex form of §2.3: G and K′
agree off v, Γ_v := G ∪ K′ joins v to N ∪ N′, and an extension lemma would need N Δ N′ = supp δ_v
to be recognisable in the *other* cards. By Lemma W it is contained in the set of vertices whose
orbit f_v moves; that is all I can say. **L2 does not close.** What it leaves is item 2: a
bipartiteness statement that is equivalent to SCL, not easier.

## 4. The τ-fixed sub-deck: a parity lever that uses the common involution

### 4.1 Fixed-point Kelly lemma

**Lemma FPK (paper).** Let K be an aligned hypomorph of G and τ an involution in
Aut(G) ∩ Aut(K) (under H1: K = G^Q and τ itself). For every graph X on fewer than n vertices

    #{W ⊆ V : τW = W, G[W] ≅ X}  ≡  #{W ⊆ V : τW = W, K[W] ≅ X}   (mod 2).

Argument. Kelly's lemma (induced form; checked as
`conj_kelly_ulam__disconnected__kelly_induced_count`) gives equal numbers of all W. τ acts on
{W : G[W] ≅ X} with orbits of size 1 and 2; likewise for K. ∎

The same holds for any common 2-subgroup of automorphisms (here {π_S : δ(S) = ∅}; it has the same
invariant sets). For |W| = n − 2 the τ-invariant complements are the pairs P_i and the 2-subsets
of F.

What is different from the dead ends. This is not the real average over Aut(G) of Prop. AV
(dead end 3): it is a congruence between counts of *isomorphism types of sub-covers*, which is
not a linear function of A and does see cycle signs. It is not a tie along σ of a vertex
function (dead end 1). It is not a Δ = 0 statement (dead end 2): the conclusion is K[U] ≅ G[U].

### 4.2 Descent

**Definition.** A proper τ-invariant U is **twist-unique** if for every τ-invariant U′ ≠ U with
|U′| = |U| and every R ⊆ E(M[U′]): G[U′]^R ≇ G[U]. (A G-only condition. It holds, for instance,
when some twist-independent invariant — degree sequence, total walk series, stable-colour
multiset — separates G[U] from every other G[U′].)

**Lemma FPK-U (paper).** If U is twist-unique then K[U] ≅ G[U]. If U is also orbit-rigid then
Q ∩ E(M[U]) is a cut of M[U].

Argument. With X = G[U]: the left count of FPK is 1; the right count is [K[U] ≅ G[U]], because
K[W] = G[W]^{Q∩E(M[W])}. Then Lemma OP. ∎

**Theorem FPD (paper).** Assume H1. If the cycle space of M_τ is generated by cycles each
contained in M[U] for some proper τ-invariant, twist-unique, orbit-rigid U, then every aligned
hypomorph of G is π_S G; G is reconstructible.

Argument. Each generator is Q-even by Lemma FPK-U; Q-parity is linear on the cycle space. ∎

**Corollary (pair level).** Assume H1 and that V ∖ P_i is twist-unique and orbit-rigid for every
i ∈ I. Then Q is a cut. (A shortest Q-odd cycle Z is chordless. If Z misses some i, it lies in
M − i: contradiction. Otherwise M = Z is a cycle and PM⁺, r15 paper, applies.)

Position. FPD never looks at signs or lengths of cycles, so it is incomparable with PM⁺ and
covers PM⁺-open configurations (a vertex on two equal-length cycles of opposite sign) whenever
the deleted graphs stay rigid. It uses card *isomorphisms*, through the (n − k)-deck, and it
uses that τ is an automorphism of **both** graphs, which is what PM delivers.

### 4.3 What FPD does not give

H1 does not imply orbit-rigidity or twist-uniqueness of any proper U. In §2.6, V ∖ P_i fails
orbit-rigidity. In the CFI graph (§5.4) the pair-deleted graphs of G and K are *equal*, and FPD
is silent there.

## 5. Part (b): Stockmeyer, Kocay, CFI

### 5.1 Why the known twists work, and what a graph lacks

**Stockmeyer** (facts as read by the librarian and r14-t1: Egrot–Hirsch arXiv:2008.01327 §6–7;
Census II arXiv:2110.06158; LIB §2.1). The card map at k, extended by k ↦ k, carries one tournament
to the other except on the single arc between k and its antipode k̄. In the notation of §2.1: the
two tournaments are (T − k; O) and (T − k; O Δ {k̄}), a **one-element change of the neighbourhood of
the deleted vertex**.

Two graph features block this, both [derived, paper]:
- *The card knows the degree of its missing vertex.* deg v = e(G) − e(G − v) with e(G) from the
  deck. So Σ_y δ_v(y) = 0, and by (R_c) δ_v is orthogonal to all card walk vectors and card degree
  classes. A tournament card always has (n−1)(n−2)/2 arcs and knows nothing of the score of its
  missing vertex. Under H1 this becomes PM.1: Δ_{x,τx} = 0.
- *The pairing is not an automorphism.* A tournament has no involutory automorphism at all (it
  would reverse the arc inside a swapped pair). So there is no Lemma FPK in Stockmeyer's world,
  and the place where FPK fails is the place where his twist sits. For graphs PM makes τ a common
  automorphism of G and K.

**Kocay's 3-graphs** (Cooper–Okur arXiv:2312.16152 Defs. 10–15, read by the librarian; Kocay's
proof not read, **why the decks agree is UNVERIFIED**, LIB §2.2). The two hypergraphs differ by a
matching flip between four residue classes *inside the link of the apex*. [derived] In a graph the
link of a vertex is a set; a matching flip must sit between two pairs, and then each of its four
endpoints has a partner whose neighbourhood is the mirror image of the lost one (§2.1). The apex
of a 3-graph has no such witness: it is alone with its link.

**What a graph analogue would need** (profile; §6 makes it exact): an asymmetric decorated base
in which every single deletion creates a symmetry — the "self-similar relabelling at every
vertex" of LIB §2 — and that symmetry must land in the wrong double coset of §2.4 at every pair
vertex. [heuristic, not a result] The obvious source of "asymmetric, but symmetric after any
deletion" is a vertex-transitive structure with regular elementary abelian 2-group minus a point
(the translation by x is an involution of the structure minus {0, x}); this is again the 2-adic
world of both known examples. I did not find a signature on such a base that passes PM⁺ and the
cards, and did not find a reason why none exists.

### 5.2 The CFI gadget is a negative 4-cycle; necklaces are blind for Theorem H

Construction recalled from Cai–Fürer–Immerman, Combinatorica 12 (1992) 389–410 (**not re-read:
UNVERIFIED as a citation**; everything below is argued from the definition as stated here). For a
vertex v of degree 3 with edges e₁, e₂, e₃: pairs {a_j, b_j} (j = 1, 2, 3) and middle vertices m_S for
even S ⊆ {1, 2, 3}; m_S ~ a_j if j ∈ S, m_S ~ b_j if j ∉ S. An edge e = vw joins a_e^v ~ a_e^w and
b_e^v ~ b_e^w; the twist at e joins a_e^v ~ b_e^w and b_e^v ~ a_e^w.

[derived, paper] For a cycle Z of the base graph through v along e₁, e₂, the map τ_Z (swap a_j ↔ b_j
for j = 1, 2 at every vertex of Z; m_S ↦ m_{S Δ {1,2}}) is an automorphism of the CFI graph and of
every twist. Its pairs at v are P₁ = {a₁, b₁}, P₂ = {a₂, b₂}, R = {m_∅, m_{12}}, R′ = {m_{13}, m_{23}}; a₃, b₃
are fixed. All four blocks P₁R, RP₂, P₂R′, R′P₁ are matchings, and the lift
a₁ – m_{12} – a₂ – m_{23} – b₁ returns on the other sheet: **the 4-cycle P₁RP₂R′ of M_{τ_Z} is negative.**
The CFI twist at an edge of Z is G^Q for Q = one link of the resulting necklace. So, relative to
τ_Z, a CFI pair *is* a signed-cover pair (this refines LIB §4, which compared twist groups), and
it lands exactly on the configuration PM⁺ leaves open: every vertex lies on two equal-length
cycles of opposite sign.

**Necklace N_ℓ (ℓ ≥ 3), abstractly.** M: vertices A_v, R_v, R′_v (v ∈ ℤ_ℓ), edges A_vR_v, R_vA_{v+1},
A_vR′_v, R′_vA_{v+1}; any signature σ making every 4-cycle A_vR_vA_{v+1}R′_v negative; any τ-symmetric
decoration (complete blocks, inner edges, attachments to F; M_τ is exactly this M) with
**ι_{R_v} = ι_{R′_v} for every v**; Q := {A_0R_0, A_0R′_0} (the winding twist; not a cut). In a CFI graph
all ι are 0.

**Proposition N1 (paper).** For every j ∈ I and every k: (B_σ^k)_{jj} = (B_{σ^Q}^k)_{jj}. So the
closed-walk tie (R_cl), and with (R) all of Theorem H as used so far, is blind on necklaces.

Argument. Fix j and a gadget v₁ not containing j (ℓ ≥ 3). A *transit* of a closed walk is a
stretch A_{v₁} → R_{v₁} → (loops at R_{v₁}) → A_{v₁+1}, the same through R′_{v₁}, or a reverse. Exchanging
R_{v₁} ↔ R′_{v₁} in the first transit, keeping the number of loops, is an involution on closed walks
from j with at least one transit (earlier steps are untouched, and a transit cannot begin at a
middle vertex). The loop weights agree because ι_{R} = ι_{R′}; the two routes differ by the sign of
the 4-cycle, −1, for σ and for σ^Q. So those walks cancel in both matrices. A closed walk
without a transit at v₁ has winding number 0, so it lifts to a closed walk in the necklace
unrolled at v₁ (a chain), where the lift of Q is a cut; it crosses Q evenly and has the same
sign in σ and σ^Q. ∎

The hypothesis on ι is needed: if ι_{R_v} ≠ ι_{R′_v} the two routes through a gadget have transfer
series s·t²/(1 + ι_R t) and −s·t²/(1 + ι_{R′} t), which no longer cancel, and the closed-walk tie does
see the winding sign. So inner edges on the middle pairs are a decoration that Theorem H can
read; complete blocks and attachments to F are not.

So no extension of PM⁺ by walk or closed-walk data can settle SCL: **the CFI gadget is the
general reason Theorem H stops.** In the cover the gadget is transparent: the four middle
vertices subdivide the four possible edges between the fibres of A_v and A_{v+1}; R_v marks one
perfect matching ("red"), R′_v the complementary one ("blue"). Red is a signed cover of C_ℓ, blue
the cover with the edgewise opposite signature, and **the twist exchanges which colour is
unicyclic-doubled** (for ℓ odd: red 2 cycles and blue 1 cycle in G, the reverse in K). Without
the decoration telling red from blue, G^Q ≅ G.

### 5.3 The cards see a necklace twist whenever the marking survives a deletion

Call the vertices over the A_v *fibre vertices* and those over the R_v *R-vertices*.

**Proposition N2 (paper).** Let G = Cov(N_ℓ with decoration, σ), K = G^Q, with no complete block
between an A-pair and an R-pair. Then for no vertex x is there a card map g : K − x → G − x that
maps fibre vertices to fibre vertices and R-vertices to R-vertices.

Argument. Let Red(·) be the bipartite graph of all edges between fibre vertices and R-vertices.
It is the subdivided cover of C_ℓ under the red signature, a disjoint union of c cycles in G and
c′ cycles in K with {c, c′} = {1, 2} (the winding sign flips). A type-respecting g maps Red(K) − x
onto Red(G) − x. Deleting a fibre or R-vertex destroys exactly one cycle on both sides; deleting
any other vertex destroys none. The numbers of cycles differ. ∎

So the cards carry **the circle sign of any sub-signature that is recognisable inside the card**.
A necklace-type counterexample needs card maps that mix red, blue and fibre vertices at every x,
while H1 demands that the decoration separates them in G. A marking by local structure (anything
readable in a ball that one deletion cannot empty) survives. Only a marking that is global — an
asymmetry destroyed by each single deletion — could fail, which is the profile of §5.1 again.

### 5.4 Genuine CFI pairs: pair-deleted cards equal, single cards separated by the handshake lemma

[derived, paper, within the flip automorphisms; I did not exclude other automorphisms of a CFI
graph over a symmetric base.] Let X be the CFI graph of a connected cubic base, X̃ its twist at
e = vw, P = {a_e^v, b_e^v}.
- X − P = X̃ − P **literally**: the twist only rewires edges at P. The τ-fixed 2-deck at P-pairs
  is blind; FPD says nothing there (Q ∖ star(P) = ∅).
- X − a_e^v against X̃ − a_e^v: both are (X − P) plus b := b_e^v joined to M_b ∪ {b_e^w} resp.
  M_b ∪ {a_e^w}, where M_b = {m_S^v : e ∉ S}. An isomorphism fixing b is a λ ∈ Aut(X − P) with
  λ(M_b) = M_b and λ(a_e^w) = b_e^w. The flip automorphisms of X − P are given by edge sets Z with
  even degree at every vertex other than v (at v the gadget has lost a pair and accepts any
  Z_v ⊆ E(v) ∖ e). λ(a_e^w) = b_e^w needs e ∈ Z; λ(M_b) = M_b needs |Z_v ∖ e| even. Then v is the only
  vertex of odd degree in Z: impossible.

So for CFI the single card sees the twist through **the partner's section**: b and its middle
neighbours record on which side of the gadget parity the lost vertex sat, and parity is
conserved (one deleted vertex opens one end; a twist needs two ends to leave). In the terms of
§2.4, Λ_A is too small. This is the second invariant of §0, and it is not a count.

### 5.5 Outcome of (b)

I did not construct a family in which the cards cannot see the twist. What I have:
1. a family where **one** pinned card is blind (§2.6), by independent sheets over a balanced
   M − i;
2. a gadget for which **all walk data** are blind (§5.2), with every card sighted as long as the
   marking is card-recognisable (§5.3);
3. no way to combine them: 1 needs M − i balanced with a symmetric sheet, which a negative
   4-cycle away from i prevents; 2 needs negative 4-cycles everywhere.

I report **no evidence that SCL is false**.

## 6. The open kernel, exactly

Assume H1 and K = G^Q with [Q] ≠ 0, K an aligned hypomorph. Then all of the following hold
(each is the contrapositive of a paper statement above):

1. (Lemma FB) at every pair vertex, **no** card map is orbit-preserving; by Lemma W each has
   δ ≠ 0 and moves exactly the orbits in reach(δ).
2. (Lemma FB, Prop. DC, Theorem ONE) for every i and every card map f at u_i: either f(u′_i) ≠ u′_i,
   or the transported involution τ′ = fτf⁻¹ lies outside Λ_Aτ Λ_A; in the second case X_i = G − P_i is
   not orbit-rigid.
3. (Lemma FP) for every f ∈ F: G − f is not orbit-rigid.
4. (Lemma FPK-U) every proper τ-invariant U containing the fibres of a Q-odd cycle is not
   twist-unique or not orbit-rigid.
5. (PM⁺, r15 paper) every vertex of a shortest Q-odd cycle lies on another of the same length
   and opposite sign.

**Kernel question.** Does H1 for G force, for at least one orbit O off some Q-odd cycle (or one
pair with a pinned card map), orbit-rigidity of G − O? Equivalently: can a decorated base have
walk vectors and degree classes spanning everything, while the removal of *each* orbit creates a
non-main structure (a non-trivial cell of the coarsest equitable partition of the deleted cover)
realised by an actual fibre-breaking isomorphism? Linear algebra alone does not forbid it: an
eigenvector z of the quotient matrix of D̄ − O with z ⊥ 1 extends by zero to a non-main eigenvector
of D̄ only if it is orthogonal to the row of O, and H1 just says it is not.

I could not prove SCL, and could not build the obstruction. The exact point where card maps
fail to beat counts: **a card map is free to break fibres wherever the deleted graph has a
symmetry that the whole graph lacks, and H1 controls only the whole graph.**

## 9. Where this could break

1. **Lemma R / orbit-rigidity from H1** relies on "equal walk series ⇒ equal orbit", i.e. on 𝒰
   being spanned by the w_k and degree indicators (degree = w_1). If H1 were stated with a larger
   𝒰, the separation argument must be redone.
2. **Lemma FB** treats pair cards only. For fixed-point cards the base automorphism need not be
   trivial; I did not claim it.
3. **Prop. DC** assumes a pinned card map; without the pin the group is replaced by a groupoid on
   {a, b} and I did not work it out.
4. **FPK** needs τ ∈ Aut(K). That is PM (r15 paper, unchecked). Without H1 it is an extra
   hypothesis.
5. **Twist-uniqueness** quantifies over all twists of all other τ-invariant sets; I gave no
   example inside the residual where it holds together with orbit-rigidity. The classes may be
   empty there.
6. **§2.6** is a counterexample to a lemma about asymmetric bases, not to a lemma under H1. Under
   H1 proper a pinned blind card is not exhibited.
7. **§5.2–5.4** start from the CFI definition as I recalled it; the citation is UNVERIFIED. N1
   and N2 are stated for the abstract necklace and do not depend on the citation. §5.4 is argued
   only inside the flip group.
8. **§5.1** leans on the librarian's and r14-t1's reading of Stockmeyer; Kocay's deck argument is
   UNVERIFIED and I used only the shape of his pair.
