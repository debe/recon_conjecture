## 1. Setting and conventions

G on V = Fin n, n ≥ 3. H1: τ ∈ Aut(G), τ² = 1, 𝒰(G) = Fix(τ). F, pairs P_j = {u_j, u′_j}
(j ∈ I), matching graph M_τ, signature σ, G^Q, π_S as in t2 §5.1. K = G^Q is an aligned
hypomorph: for every x there is an isomorphism f_x : K − x → G − x (a *card map*).

- **Names.** ν(v) := the τ-orbit of v. Under H1 the τ-orbits are the stable colour classes of G
  (t2 §5.1), and ν(v) = ν(w) iff W_G(v) = W_G(w), where W_G(v) := Σ_k t^k w_k(v) is the walk
  generating function. (If W agrees at v and w then so do all w_k and the degree, hence all of
  𝒰 = Fix(τ), which contains the indicator of the orbit of v.)
- **Sheets.** ε_j := e_{u_j} − e_{u′_j}. A^s := A(I + T)/2 and A^a := A(I − T)/2 with T the
  permutation matrix of τ. A^s(p, q) depends only on (ν p, ν q); A^a(τp, q) = A^a(p, τq) =
  −A^a(p, q); A^a(u_j^a, u_k^b) = ½·ab·B_{jk} for sheets a, b ∈ {±1}, with B the signed matrix of
  t2 §5.4 (B_{jk} = σ(jk) on M_τ, B_{jj} = −[u_j ~ u′_j]); A^a vanishes on rows and columns in F.
  For K: A^s_K = A^s_G and B_K = B with the signs on Q reversed.
- **Card data.** For a vertex x: C := G − x, N := N_G(x), R_C := (I − tA_C)^{−1} (a matrix of
  power series), N′ := f_x(N_K(x)), δ_x := 1_{N′} − 1_N. Checked helper (R_c), used as a black
  box exactly as in t2 §1: δ_x ⊥ 𝒰(C), and δ_x = 0 gives K ≅ G.
- **Q-girth.** ℓ := least length of a Q-odd cycle of M_τ (Q not a cut). A closed walk of M_τ
  (loops allowed at vertices with an inner edge) that meets Q an odd number of times has
  length ≥ ℓ, with equality only for one traversal of a Q-odd ℓ-cycle (t2 §5.4).
- **Normalising Q at i.** For S ⊆ I ∖ {i}, π_S K = G^{Q △ δ(S)} and π_S fixes u_i, so
  π_S K − u_i ≅ K − u_i ≅ G − u_i. Take a breadth-first spanning tree T_i of the component of
  i in M_τ and the unique S with (Q △ δS) ∩ E(T_i) = ∅. So **for statements about the single
  card at u_i we may assume Q ∩ E(T_i) = ∅**; in particular Q has no edge at i and
  N_K(u_i) = N_G(u_i). Label the pairs so that the tree edges are positive. d(j) := the
  distance from i in M_τ.
  - *Far-Q fact.* Every jk ∈ Q has d(j) + d(k) + 1 ≥ ℓ (the two tree paths and jk form a closed
    walk meeting Q once). Since |d(j) − d(k)| ≤ 1, both ends have d ≥ ⌈ℓ/2⌉ − 1.

## 2. Lemma E, re-verified (plan step 1)

**Lemma E (scout, `card_maps_beyond_counts.md` §3.1).** G, K on V with a common involutive
automorphism τ; x ≠ τx; (x ~_K τx) ⟺ (x ~_G τx); f : K − x → G − x an isomorphism with
f(τx) = τx and f(τy) = τ f(y) for every y ∈ N_K(τx) ∖ {x}. Then f ∪ {x ↦ x} is an isomorphism
K → G.

Check. Put A := N_K(τx) ∖ {x}. (i) τ ∈ Aut(K) gives N_K(x) ∖ {τx} = τA; τ ∈ Aut(G) gives
N_G(x) ∖ {τx} = τ(N_G(τx) ∖ {x}). (ii) f is an isomorphism of the cards fixing τx, so
f(A) = N_G(τx) ∖ {x}. (iii) For y ∈ A, τy ≠ x (because τx ∉ N_K(τx)), so f(τy) is defined, and
the hypothesis gives f(τA) = τ f(A) = N_G(x) ∖ {τx}. So f maps N_K(x) ∖ {τx} onto
N_G(x) ∖ {τx}; being a bijection of V ∖ {x}, it maps the complement onto the complement.
(iv) The pair {x, τx} has the same state in both graphs and f(τx) = τx. ∎

**The statement is correct as written; no repair is needed.** Remarks:
- It needs x ∉ F (otherwise "f(τx) = τx" is void). For x ∈ F the analogue is trivial: N_K(x)
  and N_G(x) are the same union of whole τ-orbits, so f extends iff f(N_K(x)) = N_G(x).
- **E-set.** Step (iii) uses only the set identity f(τA) = τ f(A).
- **E-name (the form used for SCL).** With K = G^Q, x = u_i: if ν(f(v)) = ν(v) for all
  v ∈ N_K(u_i) ∪ N_K(u′_i) ∪ {u′_i}, then f(u′_i) = u′_i, f maps each pair meeting A to itself,
  hence commutes with τ there, and Lemma E applies. PM.1 supplies the pair clause.
- **E′ (intertwining form).** Let f : K − x → G − x′ be an isomorphism, α ∈ Aut(K), β ∈ Aut(G)
  with αx ≠ x, f(αx) = βx′, f(αy) = β f(y) for all y ∈ N_K(x) ∖ {α^{−1}x}, and
  (x ~_K α^{−1}x) ⟺ (x′ ~_G f(α^{−1}x)). Then f ∪ {x ↦ x′} is an isomorphism. (Same proof:
  y ~_K x ⟺ αy ~_K αx ⟺ f(αy) ~_G βx′ ⟺ βf(y) ~_G βx′ ⟺ f(y) ~_G x′, and a cardinality count for
  the converse.) This is the form closest to Kocay 1984, Lemma 1.5 (read by the scout): there
  "recognised by degree" plays the role of "intertwines α and β".

## 3. Rigidity: under H1, G^Q ≅ G only for cuts

### 3.1 The stable colouring is shared

**Fact EQ.** If a partition π of V is equitable for two graphs K and G with the same quotient
matrix, then for every r the r-round colour-refinement colour of v in K equals the one in G
(canonical colours), and it is constant on cells. (Induction on r: the round-(r+1) colour of v
is determined by its round-r colour and, for each cell, the number of neighbours of v in that
cell.) Elementary; no source needed.

The τ-orbit partition is equitable for G and for every G^Q with the same quotient matrix
(blocks are empty, complete or a perfect matching, and flipping a matching keeps the counts).
So **every rooted-tree count hom((T, r), (·, v)) is the same function of ν(v) for G and for
G^Q.** Write t_T(j) for its value on the orbit j. The functions t_T are closed under pointwise
product (glue trees at the root), contain w_k (paths) and deg^e (stars), and under H1 they
span all functions of the name.

This is the only sense in which tree counts are "tied" below. It is special to the pair
(G, G^Q); it is not the false general tie of the first dead end.

### 3.2 Prop ISO

**Prop ISO (paper).** Assume H1. If φ : G^Q → G is an isomorphism, then φ = π_S for some
S ⊆ I, and Q = δ(S). Hence **G^Q ≅ G ⟺ Q is a cut**, and Aut(G) = {π_S : S a union of
components of M_τ}.

Argument. w_k^{G^Q} = w_k^G as vectors (A_K − A_G kills Fix(τ) ∋ 1, t2 §5.2), and degrees
agree. An isomorphism transports walk counts and degrees: W_G(φv) = W_{G^Q}(v) = W_G(v). By
§1, ν(φv) = ν(v). So φ maps each orbit to itself: φ = π_S. Then G = π_S G^Q, so
G^Q = π_S G = G^{δ(S)} (PM.2), and Q = δ(S) because G^Q determines Q. ∎

Consequences.
- SCL in the form "Q is a cut" and in the form "G^Q ≅ G" are the same statement.
- A signed-graph caution for angle 2 of the brief: under H1, switching *isomorphism* by a
  non-trivial automorphism of M_τ never helps; the decoration pins every vertex of M_τ.
- (Scout §3.1, re-derived.) If a card map f_x preserves names everywhere on V ∖ {x}, then
  f_x = π_S on V ∖ {x}, and Q = δ(S). So **for [Q] ≠ 0 every card map at every x has a
  non-empty set of moved vertices V_x := {v ≠ x : ν(f_x v) ≠ ν(v)}.**

## 4. What a single card map is forced to do

### 4.1 Lemma N (name-shift formula)

**Lemma N (paper).** For any graph G, vertex x, C = G − x, N = N_G(x):

    W_G(v) = W_C(v) + t · W_G(x) · (R_C 1_N)(v)      for v ≠ x.

(First passage: a walk from v either stays in C, or walks in C to some y ∈ N, steps to x, and
continues freely.) Consequently, for K = G^Q, a card map f = f_x and δ = δ_x:

    W_G(v) − W_G(f v) = t · W_G(x) · (R_C δ)(f v)      for every v ≠ x.

Argument. Apply the first identity to K and transport by f: W_{K−x}(v) = W_C(fv) and
(R_{K−x} 1_{N_K(x)})(v) = (R_C 1_{N′})(fv). Use W_K = W_G (as vectors) and subtract the
identity for G at fv. ∎

Since W_G(x) is an invertible power series, **under H1**

    V_x = f_x^{−1}(U_x),   U_x := supp(R_C δ_x) = {w : (A_C^k δ_x)(w) ≠ 0 for some k}.

U_x is the walk-hull of the swap: the union of the supports of the vectors A_C^k δ_x, k ≥ 0.
It contains supp δ_x = N △ N′. So:
- f_x preserves names exactly off the walk-hull of δ_x; V_x = ∅ iff δ_x = 0.
- The same set governs closed walks: R_G(v, v) = R_C(v, v) + t² R_G(x,x) (R_C 1_N)(v)², so with
  (R_cl), R_G(v,v) − R_G(fv,fv) = t² R_G(x,x) · (R_C δ)(fv) · (R_C(1_N + 1_{N′}))(fv), and the last
  factor is non-zero (G connected; non-negative coefficients). So f_x preserves W and the
  closed-walk series at the same vertices.
- The first coefficient is the familiar degree shift: deg(v) − deg(f v) = δ_x(f v) ∈ {0, ±1}.

Lemma N does not use H1 or τ; it is the exact form of "walk data tie the card map" for any
aligned hypomorph. It also shows why plan step 2 cannot be carried by walk and degree data:
those data force f_x to preserve names off U_x and say nothing on U_x.

### 4.2 Lemma T (the moved set is a transversal of lifted Q-odd cycles)

For a cycle Z of M_τ let Z̃ := ⋃_{j ∈ Z} P_j.

**Lemma T (paper).** Assume H1, K = G^Q, x any vertex, f a card map at x. For every Q-odd
cycle Z, V_x ∩ Z̃ ≠ ∅.

Argument. Suppose ν(fv) = ν(v) for all v ∈ Z̃ ∖ {x}. For j ∈ Z with x ∉ P_j, f permutes P_j;
let S := {j : f swaps P_j}. If x = u_i with i ∈ Z, then f(u′_i) = u′_i; put i ∉ S. For an edge
jk of Z with x ∉ P_j ∪ P_k, f is an isomorphism from the K-block to the G-block, the K-block is
the G-block flipped iff jk ∈ Q, and π_S flips it iff |S ∩ {j, k}| = 1. For an edge ij at the
hole, compare the unique neighbour of u′_i in P_j: f swaps P_j iff ij ∈ Q. So
jk ∈ Q ⟺ |S ∩ {j,k}| = 1 on every edge of Z, and |Q ∩ E(Z)| = |δ(S) ∩ E(Z)| is even. ∎

With Lemma N: **for every x and every Q-odd cycle Z, the walk-hull U_x meets f_x(Z̃).** The
swap at any vertex must be felt, through walks of the card, on every Q-odd cycle of M_τ,
however far it is from x.

### 4.3 Relation to the levers

- L1 (recognise the neighbourhood, then extend): E-name plus Lemma N say that recognition of
  N(u_i) ∪ N(u′_i) by names is *equivalent* to δ_x = 0. Names are W-values of G, and
  W_G = W_C + tW_G(x)·R_C 1_N is not a card invariant: it needs N. This is the precise
  obstruction to a Kocay-style degree recognition.
- L3 (orbit chains): the composite f_y f_x^{−1} is a corank-2 partial automorphism of G that
  preserves names off U_x ∪ f_x(V_y). Not developed further here.
- L4: by Prop ISO the "second escape route" (a card map covering a non-trivial automorphism of
  M_τ) does not exist globally under H1; it can exist only card by card.

## 5. The residual under H1: no card inherits H1

**Lemma SC (sign coherence under an N-preserving involution; paper).** Let x be a vertex and θ
an involution of V ∖ {x} with θ(N_G(x)) = N_G(x). If every vector orthogonal to 𝒰(G − x) is
θ-antisymmetric, then δ_x = 0 for every card map, for every aligned hypomorph.

Argument. δ := δ_x ⊥ 𝒰(C) by (R_c), so δ(θw) = −δ(w). If w ∈ N then θw ∈ N and both values lie
in {0, −1}; if w ∉ N both lie in {0, 1}. Either way both vanish. ∎

Natural θ under H1: θ_x := τ on the τ-pairs whose two vertices are both adjacent or both
non-adjacent to x, and the identity elsewhere. For x ∈ F this is τ itself. The hypothesis then
reads "𝒰(G − x) contains every function that is τ-symmetric away from the pairs matched to
x": the card inherits H1 as far as it can.

**This is not a new class.** The checked class WR (`…__walk_rigid_apex`) asks exactly that the
only sign-patterned vector orthogonal to 𝒰(G − x) be 0, and Lemma SC proves that. So:

> Inside this step (¬WR), under H1, **no card inherits H1**: for every x there is a vector
> orthogonal to 𝒰(G − x) that is not θ_x-antisymmetric. H1 holds for G and fails hereditarily
> at every vertex. Also |I| ≥ 2 (¬DWC₁: the corank of 𝒰 is |I|).

This is what "walk-degenerate" means under H1, and it is why §§6–7 use data that are not
linear in walks.

## 6. What counting still gives: Kelly on unicyclic test graphs

### 6.1 Lemma X (even-subgraph expansion)

**Lemma X (paper).** Let G have an involutive automorphism τ (H1 not needed), H any graph.
Then

    hom(H, G) = Σ_{E′ ⊆ E(H), E′ even} Σ_{ψ : V(H) → V(G)} Π_{e ∉ E′} A^s(ψe) · Π_{e ∈ E′} A^a(ψe),

where "even" means every vertex of H has even degree in E′.

Argument. Expand Π_e (A^s + A^a)(ψe) over subsets E′ (`Finset.prod_add`). For E′ with a vertex
v₀ of odd E′-degree, ψ ↦ (ψ with ψ(v₀) replaced by τψ(v₀)) is an involution that multiplies
the term by (−1)^{deg_{E′} v₀} = −1; its fixed points have ψ(v₀) ∈ F, where A^a vanishes
(`Finset.sum_involution`). ∎

The same argument with a root that is not summed over shows that a tree contributes only
E′ = ∅ (a non-empty forest has a non-root leaf): hom((T, r), (G, p)) = Σ_ψ Π_e A^s(ψe) = t_T(ν p).

For K = G^Q the factor Π_{E′} A^a changes by (−1)^{|ψ(E′) ∩ Q|}. The image of an even E′ is,
mod 2, an element of the cycle space of M_τ; it is Q-odd only if |E′| ≥ ℓ, with equality iff E′
is an ℓ-cycle of H mapped bijectively onto a Q-odd ℓ-cycle of M_τ (a vertex of the image cycle
has total preimage degree 2, so one preimage vertex).

### 6.2 Theorem K (multi-point PM⁺)

Let H be unicyclic: a cycle v_1 … v_ℓ of length ℓ = the Q-girth, with a rooted tree (T_m, v_m)
attached at each v_m. Its even subgraphs are ∅ and the cycle. By Lemma X (2^ℓ sheet choices,
each contributing 2^{−ℓ}σ(Z), the sheet signs cancelling in pairs):

    hom(H, G^Q) − hom(H, G) = −2 · Σ_{(j_1,…,j_ℓ)} σ_G(Z) · Π_{m=1}^{ℓ} t_{T_m}(j_m),          (K)

the sum over all traversals (2ℓ per cycle) of all Q-odd ℓ-cycles Z of M_τ.

Hand check of the constant on C₆ with τ the antipodal map: three pairs, M_τ a triangle with
negative sign, Q one edge, G^Q = 2C₃, H = C₃ with trivial trees: hom(C₃, 2C₃) − hom(C₃, C₆) =
12 − 0, and −2 · 6 · (−1) = 12. (H1 fails for C₆; (K) does not need it.)

**Theorem K (paper).** Let K = G^Q be hypomorphic to G, τ ∈ Aut(G) an involution, ℓ the
Q-girth. For all rooted trees T_1, …, T_ℓ with ℓ + Σ_m (|V(T_m)| − 1) ≤ n − 1:

    Λ(t_{T_1}, …, t_{T_ℓ}) := Σ_{traversals} σ(Z) Π_m t_{T_m}(j_m) = 0.

Argument. Kelly's lemma: hom(H, ·) is determined by the deck when |V(H)| < n (hom counts are
integer combinations of induced-subgraph counts on ≤ |V(H)| vertices; the induced form is the
checked step `conj_kelly_ulam__disconnected__kelly_induced_count`; source Kelly 1957 as in
CLAUDE.md §3 and Bondy 1991, not re-opened this session). Then (K). ∎

Remarks.
- PM⁺ is the case of one non-trivial tree, a path, with no size bound (it comes from (R_cl)
  instead of Kelly). Theorem K weights **all** ℓ cycle vertices at once and pays with the bound
  n − 1.
- The gadgets need not be trees: any rooted graphs all of whose cycles are shorter than ℓ work
  (their even subgraphs have Q-even images), with t replaced by the rooted hom count.
- The counts in (K) are counts in the uncoloured graph; nothing is assumed about coloured
  decks.

### 6.3 Class PM⁺⁺ (budgeted)

Let 𝒫_k := span{t_T : T has ≤ k edges}, I_cyc := the vertices of M_τ on cycles, and

- κ := the least k such that for every j ∈ I_cyc some g ∈ 𝒫_k equals the indicator of j on
  I_cyc. Under H1, κ ≤ max(r, D) − 1 with r = dim 𝒲 and D the number of distinct degrees
  (indicators are linear in w_0, …, w_{r−1} and in powers deg^e, e < D).
- ℓ_max := the least L such that the cycles of M_τ of length ≤ L generate its cycle space
  (≤ 2·diam + 1 per component, by fundamental cycles of a breadth-first tree). A non-cut Q has
  Q-girth ≤ ℓ_max.

**Theorem PM⁺⁺ (paper).** H1 and ℓ_max · (κ + 1) ≤ n − 1 imply that G is reconstructible.

Argument. If Q is not a cut, take a Q-odd ℓ-cycle Z₀ = (j⁰_1, …, j⁰_ℓ), ℓ ≤ ℓ_max, and g_m ∈ 𝒫_κ
equal to the indicator of j⁰_m on I_cyc. By multilinearity and Theorem K, Λ(g_1, …, g_ℓ) = 0.
But only the one traversal (j⁰_1, …, j⁰_ℓ) survives, so Λ = σ(Z₀) ≠ 0. ∎

How thin is it? With only the linear bound κ ≤ max(r, D) − 1 and r + D − 1 ≥ n − |I|, the
condition can hold only for ℓ_max = 3 and balanced r ≈ D. With products of trees κ is usually
far smaller, but H1 gives no bound on it. **The obstruction is the size bound of Kelly's lemma
and nothing else**: if (K) were available for all tree sizes, SCL would follow (form NK of §9).
Counts of spanning unicyclic subgraphs with a given cycle length are known to be
reconstructible (Tutte 1979, Kocay 1981; from memory, **UNVERIFIED**, not used), but not with
the trees prescribed at prescribed cycle vertices.

### 6.4 Why walk data alone are blind (the one explicit family)

*Claim refuted:* "two signatures on the same graph with equal diagonals of all powers of B are
switching equivalent" (the hope behind using only (R_cl)).

Take M = K₄,₄ and a 4 × 4 Hadamard matrix H as the signature between the two sides:
B = [[0, H], [Hᵀ, 0]]. Then B² = 4·I, so (B^k)_{ii} = 4^{k/2} for even k and 0 for odd k,
whatever H is. Let H be normalised (first row and column +1) with rows 2, 3 equal to
(+,+,−,−), (+,−,+,−), and H″ the same with rows 2 and 3 exchanged. If H″ = D₁HD₂ with diagonal
sign matrices, the first row gives D₂ = ±I and the first column D₁ = ±I, so H″ = H; but
H″ ≠ H. So the two signatures are not switching equivalent. Q, the set of edges where H and
H″ differ, consists of the four entries in rows 2, 3 and columns 2, 3; a Q-odd 4-cycle is, for
example, (row 2, column 2, row 1, column 1), which contains exactly one Q-edge. All
closed-walk data agree at every vertex. Moreover B^{2m} = 4^m·I and B^{2m+1} = 4^m·B for both
signatures, so B_{H″}^k = ± B_H^k entrywise and every two-point walk series agrees up to sheet.
PM⁺ and every rooted walk tie are blind here.

I do **not** claim that a τ-symmetric decoration realising H1 around this signed graph exists
with hypomorphic covers; that would be a counterexample to the conjecture. The family only
shows that the proof of SCL must use more than (R), (R_cl) and two-point walk series. Theorem
K does see it: a 4-cycle with gadgets naming row 2, column 2, row 1, column 1 gives Λ ≠ 0,
whenever those four gadgets fit in n − 5 vertices.

## 7. Card-local ties with no size bound: the Q-blind radius

### 7.1 Theorem QB

Fix i ∈ I and normalise Q at i (§1). Let

    h := min over jk ∈ Q in the component of i of min(d(j), d(k))   (∞ if there is none);

by the far-Q fact h ≥ ⌈ℓ/2⌉ − 1. Let c_r denote r rounds of colour refinement on a card
(canonical colours).

**Theorem QB (paper).** For every r ≤ h and every p ≠ u_i: c_r^{K − u_i}(p) = c_r^{G − u_i}(p).
Hence a card map f : K − u_i → G − u_i maps every c_r-class of G − u_i (r ≤ h) onto itself. For
x ∈ F the same holds for all r, with no normalisation.

Argument. Induction on r. Let ρ_r be the partition of V ∖ {u_i} into {u_j}, {u′_j} for
1 ≤ d(j) ≤ r, {u′_i}, the pairs P_j with d(j) > r, and the singletons of F. Claim: both
colourings c_r are the same function, and for every p and every cell 𝔠 of ρ_r the number of
neighbours of p in 𝔠 is the same in K − u_i and in G − u_i. The two cards differ only inside
the blocks P_j × P_k with jk ∈ Q, where the neighbour of p ∈ P_j in P_k is replaced by its
τ-partner. If d(k) > r, P_k is one cell and the count is 1 either way. For r ≤ h − 1 every end
of every Q-edge has d ≥ h > r, so this is the only case. (c_r is constant on ρ_r-cells by the
same induction, using that a pair at distance > r + 1 has no matching block to a split pair
or to P_i.) For x ∈ F the τ-orbit partition is equitable on both cards with the same quotient
(Fact EQ). ∎

**Corollary QB-pure.** If Q is not a cut, then for every i and every Q-odd cycle Z there is
j ∈ Z such that P_j ∖ {u_i} is not a union of c_h-classes of G − u_i: some vertex of P_j shares
its h-round card colour with a vertex of another name. (Otherwise f preserves names on Z̃ and
Lemma T applies; the normalised K has the same Q-odd cycles.) The same at x ∈ F with the
stable colouring of G − x.

**Class FP (paper).** H1, and there is x ∈ F such that every pair P_j with j ∈ I_cyc is a
stable colour class of G − x. Then G is reconstructible; a single card K − x ≅ G − x already
forces Q to be a cut. The same holds with x = u_i when the component of i in M_τ is a tree
(h = ∞). FP is not visibly inside WR: it asks for colour-refinement classes of the card, WR
for walk vectors and degree classes, and (R_c) ties only the latter. I have not shown that FP
has members in this step.

So Kocay's "the hole's neighbourhood is recognisable in the card" has an exact analogue here,
with a radius: **within distance h of the hole, the card's colour refinement is common to K
and G, and a card map must respect it.** Beyond h the colours of the two cards are different
functions and the card map is free to use the difference.

### 7.2 Edge counts between Q-blind classes: DS and CR-odd

With Q normalised at i, A_K − A_G = −Σ_{jk ∈ Q} σ(jk)(ε_j ε_kᵀ + ε_k ε_jᵀ) on V ∖ {u_i}. For
c_r-classes c, c′ (r ≤ h) the number of card edges between c and c′ is a card invariant and the
classes are the same vertex sets in both cards. With ξ_c(j) := [u_j ∈ c] − [u′_j ∈ c]:

    Σ_{jk ∈ Q} σ(jk) · (ξ_c(j) ξ_{c′}(k) + ξ_c(k) ξ_{c′}(j)) = 0.                        (CR)

ξ_c(j) ≠ 0 needs c_r to split P_j, hence d(j) ≤ r. So (CR) is non-trivial only for r = h and
Q-edges with d(j) = d(k) = h; then ℓ = 2h + 1 is odd and these Q-edges are exactly the *far
edges* (opposite to i) of the Q-odd ℓ-cycles through i.

- **DS (ℓ = 3, exact).** h = 1 and c_1 is the card degree: u_j has card degree deg u_j − 1 and
  u′_j has deg u_j, for j ∈ N_M(i). (CR) says that the signed measure
  μ_i := Σ_{jk ∈ Q, j,k ∈ N_M(i)} σ(jk) · δ_{{deg u_j, deg u_k}} on unordered pairs of integers is
  killed by the mixed difference (1 − S)⊗(1 − S), S the shift; a finitely supported measure
  with that property is 0. So **for every i and every pair of degrees {d, d′}, the signed
  number of Q-odd triangles {i, j, k} with {deg u_j, deg u_k} = {d, d′} is zero.** PM⁺ is the
  sum over all {d, d′}. In plain words: the card G − u_i counts its edges between degree
  classes, and a Q-odd triangle at i moves one edge between classes.
- **CR-odd (ℓ = 2h + 1).** The far edges of the Q-odd ℓ-cycles through i, signed, cancel in
  every pair of h-round card colour classes.

### 7.3 Tree counts in the card: CR1

No normalisation. Let ℓ ≥ 4 and H := a path v_0 … v_ℓ with a_m pendant leaves at v_m for
2 ≤ m ≤ ℓ − 2. The main path is the only path of length ≥ ℓ in H. Expand
hom(H, K − u_i) − hom(H, G − u_i) as in Lemma X; now the flip is unavailable at vertices
mapped to u′_i, so E′ may have odd-degree vertices, all mapped to u′_i (and no edge has both
ends at u′_i). Such an E′ is a forest, an edge-disjoint union of paths between vertices mapped
to u′_i; each maps to a closed walk at i; a Q-odd one has length ≥ ℓ, so it is the main path,
mapped once around a Q-odd ℓ-cycle through i. A pendant edge cannot be added to E′: its leaf
and v_m would both map to u′_i or v_m would need a matching block to P_i, and a shortest Q-odd
cycle has no chord at i. The pendant leaves therefore contribute
Σ_{q ≠ u_i} A^s(p, q) = deg(p) − [p ~ u_i] =: d_i(ν p), the card degree, which is a function of
the name at positions 2, …, ℓ − 2. Since the cards are isomorphic the difference is 0:

    Σ_{Z ∋ i, two directions} σ(Z) · Π_{m=2}^{ℓ−2} g_m(d_i(j_m)) = 0   for all functions g_m.      (CR1)

**The signed number of Q-odd ℓ-cycles through i with prescribed card degrees at the positions
2, …, ℓ − 2 is zero**, with no bound on the size of the test graph. I expect the same with
rooted trees of depth ≤ min(m, ℓ − m) − 1 at position m (weights: τ-symmetrised card tree
counts); I have checked only the depth-1 case above. **Flag: the deeper case is not written
out.**

An even-ℓ analogue of (CR) counts 2-paths of the card whose ends lie in prescribed h-round
classes and whose middle vertex lies in an unsplit pair at distance h + 1; it gives the same
kind of cancellation for the two neighbours of the antipode. **Flag: stated, not written out.**

## 8. The first open case, explicitly (plan step 3)

Setting: Q not a cut, ℓ the Q-girth, and (what PM⁺ leaves) every vertex of every Q-odd ℓ-cycle
lies on Q-odd ℓ-cycles whose signs sum to zero. **Minimal configuration at i:** exactly two
Q-odd ℓ-cycles pass through i, Z = (i, j_1, …, j_{ℓ−1}) and Z′ = (i, j′_1, …, j′_{ℓ−1}), with
σ(Z) = −σ(Z′).

What the lifts look like. In G − u_i the positive cycle is an ℓ-cycle through u′_i plus a path
on ℓ − 1 vertices; the negative one is a path on 2ℓ − 1 vertices centred at u′_i. In K − u_i
the roles of Z and Z′ are exchanged. A card map fixing u′_i and exchanging the lifts of Z and
Z′ is locally consistent; it moves names. By Prop ISO it does not extend.

What is forced.

1. **Position-wise card degrees (ℓ ≥ 4).** By (CR1) with indicator functions,
   (d_i(j_2), …, d_i(j_{ℓ−2})) equals (d_i(j′_2), …, d_i(j′_{ℓ−2})) or its reversal. Otherwise
   the two terms cannot cancel.
2. **Far edges (ℓ = 2h + 1).** Normalise at i. The far edges e = j_h j_{h+1} and e′ = j′_h j′_{h+1}
   are the only Q-edges at level (h, h).
   - *Case e ≠ e′.* Each far edge lies on one Q-odd ℓ-cycle through i, so the tree paths to its
     ends are the arcs of that cycle (another geodesic would close a third Q-odd ℓ-cycle
     through i), and σ(e) = σ(Z) = −σ(Z′) = −σ(e′). Write a, a′ for the h-round card colours of
     the two sheets over j_h, and b, b′, α, α′, β, β′ likewise for j_{h+1}, j′_h, j′_{h+1}. If the
     colours split the sheets (a ≠ a′, and so on), (CR) says that the multiset of colour pairs
     of the lifted far edges is the same in G and in K: {a,b}, {a′,b′}, {α,β′}, {α′,β} versus
     {a,b′}, {a′,b}, {α,β}, {α′,β′}. Since {a,b} is not {a,b′} or {a′,b}, it equals {α,β} or
     {α′,β′}. So **the far edge of Z′ has, sheet by sheet, the same h-round card colours as the
     far edge of Z.** For ℓ = 3 this is DS: {deg u_{j_1}, deg u_{j_2}} = {deg u_{j′_1}, deg u_{j′_2}}.
     If the colours do not split one of the four pairs, (CR) carries no information on the
     edge at that pair.
   - *Case e = e′.* Then Z and Z′ differ in an arc, and (CR) has a single term: the h-round
     colours cannot split both far pairs. (Two geodesics of opposite sign reach one end of e,
     which is what makes its two sheets look alike from u′_i at depth h.) This case is empty
     for ℓ = 3.
3. **Two orbit indicators within budget.** Z ≠ Z′, so some j lies on Z and not on Z′, at
   position m. If the indicators of i and of j on I_cyc lie in 𝒫_κ and ℓ + 2κ ≤ n − 1, Theorem
   K with those two gadgets gives σ(Z)·(number of traversals of Z with i, j at the two marked
   positions) = 0, which is false.

**Prop FO (paper).** In the minimal configuration, G is reconstructible unless, seen from the
card G − u_i, Z′ is a *Q-blind twin* of Z (items 1 and 2 hold) **and** item 3 is out of budget.
The same holds at every vertex of Z and of Z′, so a surviving configuration needs the twin
relation simultaneously from all holes on the two cycles.

What I cannot do. In the twin case the two cards agree on every invariant of §7, because those
invariants are common functions on K − u_i and G − u_i by construction. To separate the cards
one must use colours of depth > h, which are different functions on the two cards, and then
the card map is constrained only through Lemma N. I have no argument that a twin of the lift
of Z, to all depths, contradicts H1. That is the missing lemma.

## 9. The one missing lemma

**ML (one lifted cycle).** Assume H1 and let K = G^Q be an aligned hypomorph with Q not a cut.
Then there are a vertex x, a card map f : K − x → G − x and a Q-odd cycle Z of M_τ such that
ν(f v) = ν(v) for every v ∈ Z̃ ∖ {x}.

- ML ⟹ SCL by Lemma T (the conclusion is contradictory, so Q is a cut); SCL ⟹ ML vacuously.
- By Lemma N, the conclusion of ML reads: **supp(R_C δ_x) ∩ f(Z̃) = ∅**, the walk-hull of one
  swap misses one lifted Q-odd cycle. This is weaker in appearance than δ_x = 0.
- By Theorem QB it is enough to find a Q-blind canonical colouring of the cards at x whose
  classes are the pairs along one Q-odd cycle. Proven instances: FP (x ∈ F or a tree component,
  stable colouring), and the bounded-depth statements of §7.
- **Counting form (NK, named Kelly).** Under H1, for every ℓ and every cyclic sequence of names,
  the number of closed ℓ-walks of an aligned hypomorph through those names in that order equals
  the number in G. NK ⟺ SCL (one direction by (K) with indicator weights, the other trivially).
  Theorem K is NK under the size bound.

Where a proof of ML would have to work: M_τ connected, F empty or every fixed-point card
merging names, every Q-odd cycle within the Q-blind radius of no hole, and twins as in §8. The
Hadamard signatures of §6.4 are the smallest pattern of this kind that I know on the
signed-graph side.

## 10. Where the symmetry of the adjacency is used; tournaments, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

1. **Block trichotomy** (t2 §5.1, used everywhere): between two τ-pairs a τ-invariant set of
   *unordered* edges is empty, complete or one of two perfect matchings, because τ × τ has two
   orbits of size 2 on the four possible edges. For arcs there are four orbits; a tournament
   block has 4 τ-invariant states, the twist group per block is not ℤ₂, and (M_τ, σ) is not a
   signed graph.
2. **The pair state** (Lemma E step (iv); E′ last clause; PM.1): an unordered pair {x, τx} is
   τ-invariant, so "edge or not" is a τ-invariant state, equal in K and G. For arcs the state
   of {x, τx} is reversed by τ, so no tournament has an involutory automorphism at all and H1
   is empty there. The scout's reading of Stockmeyer (Census II, via r14-t1; not re-read by me)
   is that the card map extended by k ↦ k is an isomorphism except that it reverses the arc
   between k and its antipode: exactly the failure of clause (iv), with everything else in
   Lemma E intact. In a graph clause (iv) is even automatic once f maps N_K(x) ∖ τx onto
   N_G(x) ∖ τx, because deg_K x = deg_G x. So a graph analogue cannot put its twist inside a
   pair; it must put it in the failure of f to commute with τ on N(τx), i.e. in the blocks,
   where Lemma T, Theorem K and Theorem QB act.
3. **(R), (R_c), (R_cl)**: inherited, checked, graph-specific (t2 §8).

Not used: Lemma N (first passage works for digraphs with in-neighbourhoods), Prop ISO, Fact EQ.
Lemma X uses that B is symmetric only to give a cycle one sign for both directions.

- 3-uniform hypergraphs: no block trichotomy (Kocay's pair flips a matching of four classes in
  the link of an apex, scout §2.2); Lemma X has no analogue, since a triple edge carries a
  product of three sheet signs and the parity cancellation changes.
- Infinite graphs: hom counts and W are infinite; Kelly's lemma fails; the Bowler et al. trees
  have no twist (scout §2.3).

## 12. Where this could break

- **Lemma N**: the transport W_{K−x}(v) = W_C(f v) and W_K = W_G as vectors (PM.1). I am
  confident.
- **Lemma T**: the bookkeeping at the hole (u′_i has lost its partner). Checked by hand above.
- **Lemma X / (K)**: inner edges (loops of B) and F are handled by "A^a vanishes on F" and "a
  closed walk using a loop has a shorter non-loop part". The constant −2 was checked on C₆.
  Kelly for hom counts with |V(H)| ≤ n − 1 is standard but only the induced form is checked.
- **PM⁺⁺** may be thin; H1 gives no bound on κ beyond max(r, D) − 1.
- **Theorem QB**: K is replaced by π_S K at the card u_i; this changes the alignment only at
  other vertices and is harmless for one card. The induction needs r ≤ h − 1 at each step.
- **(CR1)**: the exclusion of pendant edges from E′ uses "no chord at i"; written out. The
  deeper-tree and even-ℓ variants are **not** written out.
- **Prop FO** treats only the minimal configuration (two cycles at i). With more cycles through
  i the ties of §7 are linear relations among several signed terms and conclude less.
- **§5** shows that the natural linear class is already checked; if a refuter finds H1 graphs in
  ¬WR whose cards do inherit H1, Lemma SC or my reading of the WR statement is wrong.
- **Prior.** Neutral. Nothing here is evidence for or against SCL beyond n ≤ 13.
