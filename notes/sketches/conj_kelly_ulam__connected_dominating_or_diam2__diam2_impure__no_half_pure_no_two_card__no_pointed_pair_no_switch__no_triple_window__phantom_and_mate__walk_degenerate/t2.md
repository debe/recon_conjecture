## 2. Four layers of the complement

ℝ^{π_st} ⊆ ℝ^{π_orb} = Fix(Γ) ⊆ ℝ^{π_tw}. Taking complements:

    𝒰^⊥  ⊇  (ℝ^{π_st})^⊥  ⊇  Fix(Γ)^⊥  ⊇  𝒯 := span{e_u − e_v : u ≈ v}.

| symmetric layer Fix(Γ)^⊥ ⊖ 𝒯 | e_u − e_{γu}, γ not a product of twin swaps | symmetry proper | real methods blind (§4.2); involution with forest/sign condition handled (§5) |

## 3. The twin layer: sign coherence

### 3.1 Sign Lemma

**Lemma S.** Let x ∈ V and B ⊆ V ∖ {x} with x adjacent to all of B or to none of B. If
Σ_{y∈B} Δ_{xy} = 0, then Δ_{xy} = 0 for every y ∈ B. More generally, if g : V → ℝ has g ≥ 0 on N(x),
g ≤ 0 on V ∖ N[x] and Σ_y Δ_{xy} g(y) = 0, then Δ_{xy} = 0 wherever g(y) ≠ 0.
Argument: by (S) all terms have the same weak sign. ∎

With (R): **row x of Δ vanishes at y whenever some g ∈ 𝒰(G) weakly separates N(x) from its
complement and g(y) ≠ 0.** By Tucker's complementarity theorem (standard LP duality; not needed
for any class below) no such g exists for y exactly when there is a real z ∈ 𝒰^⊥ with z(x) = 0,
z ≤ 0 on N(x), z ≥ 0 off N[x] and z(y) ≠ 0. So the Sign Lemma is the complete real test for one
row; integrality is what it cannot see.

### 3.2 Theorem CT (coloured threshold) and its corollaries TC, MOD

An **alternating 4-cycle** of G is (a, b, a′, b′) with ab, a′b′ ∈ E, ba′, b′a ∉ E, b ≠ a′, b′ ≠ a
(then the four are distinct). For a colouring c it is **c-coloured** if c(a) = c(a′), c(b) = c(b′).

**Class CT.** There is a colouring c of V such that (i) every colour-class indicator lies in
𝒰(G) (dual form: every integer f orthogonal to all w_k and all degree-class indicators sums to
zero on every colour class), and (ii) G has no c-coloured alternating 4-cycle.

**Theorem CT.** For G in CT every aligned hypomorph equals G; so G is reconstructible.

Paper argument. Let R = E(G), B = E(K). By (R) and (i), for every vertex x and colour class C,
|N_R(x) ∩ C| = |N_B(x) ∩ C|; so among the pairs xy with y ∈ C, as many lie in B ∖ R as in R ∖ B
(**balance**). Suppose R ≠ B and take a pair of R △ B with endpoints in classes C, C′ (possibly
equal). S := the vertices of C with an (R △ B)-neighbour in C′; r(u) := |N_R(u) ∩ C′|. Choose
u ∈ S with r(u) maximal. Balance at u towards C′ gives w ∈ C′ with uw ∈ B ∖ R; balance at w
towards C gives w′ ∈ C with ww′ ∈ R ∖ B; so w′ ∈ S and r(w′) ≤ r(u). If every R-neighbour of u in
C′ other than w′ were an R-neighbour of w′, then y ↦ y (y ≠ w′), w′ ↦ u would inject
N_R(u) ∩ C′ into (N_R(w′) ∩ C′) ∖ {w} (w is an R-neighbour of w′ and not of u; if w′ ∈ N_R(u) ∩ C′
then C = C′ and u ∈ N_R(w′) ∩ C′), giving r(u) < r(w′). So there is a ∈ N_R(u) ∩ C′ with a ≠ w′,
aw′ ∉ R, and (u, a, w′, w) is a c-coloured alternating 4-cycle. ∎

This is the coloured form of `thr_unique` of r14-t1 §3.4 (uncoloured version reported there as
compiling in Scratch; the classical fact behind it is the uniqueness of threshold and
difference-graph realisations, attribution Hammer–Simeone / Chvátal–Hammer from memory,
UNVERIFIED; the argument above is self-contained).

Corollaries (paper; each is CT for a particular colouring):

- **TC ("controllable modulo twins").** If every twin-class indicator lies in 𝒰(G),
  equivalently dim 𝒰(G) = number of twin classes, then G is reconstructible. (c = twin class.
  A coloured alternating 4-cycle would have twins a ≈ a′ and b adjacent to a, not to a′,
  b ∉ {a, a′}.) Direct three-line form: by (R) each row of Δ sums to zero on each twin class T;
  x is homogeneous to T ∖ {x}; Lemma S. **TC contains the controllable graphs and the checked
  DWC₀ and has no corank bound**: a graph with t twin classes has dim 𝒰^⊥ ≥ n − t.
  Non-vacuity, by hand: stars K_{1,m}, m ≥ 2 (two twin classes, 𝒰 = span{1, deg}).
- **MOD.** A partition of V into modules M with 1_M ∈ 𝒰(G) and every G[M] threshold (no
  alternating 4-cycle). (Between distinct modules adjacency is homogeneous.)
- **DT** (c = deg; (i) holds for every graph): no alternating 4-cycle (a, b, a′, b′) with
  deg a = deg a′, deg b = deg b′. This case is **not new**: Δ is a degree-balanced switch, so DT
  lies inside the checked BS₄ class (`…__aligned_switch_kelly4`), negated in the frozen statement.
- For an **equitable** colouring (for instance the orbits of any subgroup of Γ) condition (ii)
  forces every block to be complete or empty, i.e. CT reduces to TC. So CT adds to TC only
  through non-equitable colourings with classes in 𝒰.

**Lemma MB (module boundary).** If M is a module of G and 1_M ∈ 𝒰(G), in particular if M is a
module that is a union of degree classes, then K and G agree on all pairs between M and V ∖ M,
and deg_{K[M]} = deg_{G[M]} on M. (Lemma S for x ∉ M; (R) for x ∈ M.) So M is a module of K with the
same outside, and all of Δ sits inside M and inside V ∖ M.

**Relation to the ceiling of sibling t1 (§3.2 there).** t1 observes that any class whose
argument ends in "every aligned hypomorph equals G" lies inside {removal-similar ⇒ twins}
(for π ∈ Π the graph πG is an aligned hypomorph). CT, TC, MOD and TC₁ below are of that kind and
lie under that ceiling; they are the part of it that (R) and Lemma S reach. WRT (§3.5) and
PM, PM⁺ (§5) end in "K = πG for an explicit π ∉ Aut(G)" and cross it, as does t1's DWC₂′
(corank ≤ 2 with one transposition switch); PM has no corank bound but needs the involution to
explain all of 𝒰^⊥. Naming: t1 calls the ceiling class "TW"; my twin-controllable class is
therefore called **TC** here.

### 3.3 Theorem TC₁ (twin-reduced corank one)

𝒩̃ := 𝒰^⊥ ∩ ℝ^{π_tw}, the twin-constant part of the complement; dim 𝒩̃ = (number of twin classes) −
dim 𝒰. P := the class-averaging projection onto ℝ^{π_tw}; since 𝒰 ⊆ ℝ^{π_tw}, P maps 𝒰^⊥ into 𝒩̃.

**Class TC₁.** dim 𝒩̃ ≤ 1, and if 𝒩̃ = ℝb with b ≠ 0 then b(x₀) ≠ 0 for some vertex x₀ without a twin.

**Theorem TC₁.** For G in TC₁ every aligned hypomorph equals G. TC₁ ⊇ TC and TC₁ ⊇ DWC₁.

Paper argument. Write z_x for row x of Δ. P z_x = λ_x b. For twin classes S, T put
Δ̃(S, T) := Σ_{x∈S, y∈T} Δ_{xy} = Λ(S)·m(T) with m(T) := |T| b(T), Λ(S) := Σ_{x∈S} λ_x. Symmetry of Δ̃ and
m({x₀}) = b(x₀) ≠ 0 give Λ = c·m for a constant c, so Δ̃ = c·m mᵀ. The diagonal entry at {x₀} is
Δ_{x₀x₀} = 0 = c·b(x₀)², so c = 0 and Δ̃ = 0. Every block S × T is sign-coherent (two twin classes
are completely joined or not at all; a twin class is a clique or independent), so Δ = 0 by
Lemma S. Inclusions: a DWC₁ graph with a twin pair u, v has 𝒰^⊥ = ℝ(e_u − e_v) and 𝒩̃ = 0; one
without twins satisfies the clause trivially. ∎

### 3.4 The visibility clause is needed (the one explicit example, by hand)

G = C₄ on {1, 2, 3, 4} with edges 12, 23, 34, 41. Twin classes {1, 3}, {2, 4}; 𝒰 = ℝ1 (regular);
𝒩̃ = ℝb, b = (1, −1, 1, −1); no vertex is twin-free. K := edges 13, 34, 42, 21, again a 4-cycle.
K − v ≅ P₃ ≅ G − v for every v, so K is an aligned hypomorph, and K ≠ G: Δ gains 13, 24 and loses
23, 41. Row 1 of Δ is (0, 0, 1, −1), orthogonal to 1; P z₁ = ½ b; Δ̃ = ½·m mᵀ with m = (2, −2).
So with dim 𝒩̃ = 1 and no twin-free support the conclusion "K = G" fails (here K ≅ G, as it must
for a regular graph). The general exceptional shape, from the same computation: the classes
with b ≠ 0 are all independent (c > 0) or all cliques (c < 0), classes of equal sign of b are
mutually non-adjacent and classes of opposite sign completely adjacent (or the complement).
I did not pursue whether hypomorphy excludes K ≇ G there.

### 3.5 Card level: Theorem WRT

**Class WRT (walk-rigid apex modulo twins).** Some vertex x such that every δ : V ∖ x → {0, ±1}
with the three clauses of the checked WR class (δ ≤ 0 on N(x), ≥ 0 off; balanced on every
card-degree class; orthogonal to every walk vector of G − x) sums to zero on every twin class
of the card G − x. (WR asks δ = 0.)

**Theorem WRT.** Every graph in WRT is reconstructible.
Paper argument. By (R_c) δ_x satisfies the clauses, so |N′ ∩ T| = |N ∩ T| for every twin class T
of C = G − x. Choose for each T a bijection g_T of T with g_T(N ∩ T) = N′ ∩ T; g := ⋃ g_T is an
automorphism of C (it permutes each twin class) with g(N) = N′. So C + (x joined to N′) ≅ G,
and the left side is isomorphic to H. ∎

Inclusions (paper). WR ⊆ WRT. "Some card is controllable modulo twins" (every twin-class
indicator of G − x lies in 𝒰(G − x)) ⊆ WRT; this contains Hong's class. **Wang–Wen–Guo's class
⊆ WRT**: if 𝒲(C) has corank 1 and C has an automorphism γ ≠ 1, then Fix(γ)^⊥ ⊆ 𝒲(C)^⊥ has dimension
1, so γ is one transposition (u v), hence u, v are twins of C and 𝒲(C)^⊥ = ℝ(e_u − e_v)
(arXiv:2408.02488, abstract read in round 14; their proof not read; the inclusion is my
three-line argument).

WRT is the symmetric repair that r14-t3 §3.2 asked for ("WR fails whenever an automorphism of
the card moves N(x)"), restricted to the automorphisms that linear data can certify. The full
"up to Aut(G − x)" form (every admissible δ equals 1_{gN} − 1_N for some g ∈ Aut(G − x)) has the
same argument and is also G-only; it is not proposed because nothing structural certifies it
beyond twins (§4.2 explains why).

## 4. Route (b): the action of Aut(G), and what counting can and cannot do

### 4.1 The action

𝒦 := the aligned hypomorphs of G (graphs K on V with K − v ≅ G − v for all v). Π := the
permutations preserving removal-similarity classes; Γ ≤ Π.
- Π acts on 𝒦 (r14-t1 Remark 1.1), the isomorphs of G in 𝒦 are the orbit Π·G of size |Π|/|Γ|, and
  G is reconstructible iff 𝒦 = Π·G.
- Γ acts on 𝒦 ∖ Π·G, and Δ(γK) = P_γ Δ(K) P_γᵀ. Burnside gives nothing: no known quantity ties |𝒦|.
  (r14-t1 Prop. 2.3: the gluing data form a pointed set, not a group.)

**Lemma AVG (no cancellation).** Δ̄ := |Γ|⁻¹ Σ_γ P_γ Δ P_γᵀ is Γ-invariant, annihilates 𝒰(G), has
the sign pattern (S) with real entries in [−1, 1], and **Δ̄ = 0 iff K = G**.
Argument: (S) is a property of the pair (x, y) relative to G, which Γ preserves; a sum of terms
of one sign vanishes only termwise. Explicitly Δ̄_{xy} = (density of K on the Γ-orbit of {x, y})
− [xy ∈ E(G)]. ∎

So K ↦ Δ̄(K) sends the non-trivial Γ-orbits on 𝒦 to non-zero points of the orbital (centraliser)
algebra of Γ, in the cone cut out by (S) and by "annihilates 𝒰". This is an injectivity
statement of Nash-Williams type: one integer that cannot vanish. Class it suggests: **ORB**,
"that cone is {0}". ORB is intrinsic (it speaks of the orbitals of Aut(G) only).

### 4.2 Proposition AV: the averaged test sees only twins

**Proposition AV (paper).** If some pair of Γ-orbits (O, O′), possibly O = O′, contains both an
edge and a non-edge of G (between distinct vertices), then the cone of §4.1 is non-zero. Hence
ORB ⊆ {graphs whose Aut-orbits are twin classes}, and on those graphs ORB is a real form of
the twin-reduced test of §3.3 (invariant matrices are constant on twin-class blocks).
Argument. Let R ⊆ E(G) and R′ ∩ E(G) = ∅ be symmetric Γ-invariant relations between O and O′
(orbitals, symmetrised). Each is biregular: a, b neighbours per vertex of O, and a|O|/|O′|,
b|O|/|O′| per vertex of O′. M := −A_R/a + A_{R′}/b is symmetric, zero on the diagonal, has the sign
pattern, and M·1_{O″} = 0 for every orbit O″ (−1 + 1 on O; −|O|/|O′| + |O|/|O′| on O′; 0 elsewhere).
So M annihilates Fix(Γ) ⊇ 𝒰. If no orbit pair is mixed, every orbit is a clique or an
independent set and is completely joined or not to every other, so its vertices are twins. ∎

Reading. A non-twin symmetry always carries a *fractional* walk-neutral switch, whether or not
a hypomorph exists (and integral ones too: P_π A P_πᵀ − A for π ∈ ∏ Sym(orbits), which are
isomorphs). Therefore:
1. no real-relaxed or averaged criterion reaches beyond the twin layer;
2. beyond twins the structural question is necessarily "which integral switches are
   isomorphs", i.e. a description of the integral points, which §5 does for one involution;
3. this agrees with `groups_and_harmonic.md` E-section: the kernel of the marginal map is
   non-zero, and a vertex argument must use integrality or positivity. Lemma S is the
   positivity lever; Proposition AV is its exact range.

### 4.3 Why the Nash-Williams lever fails at n − 1

For graphs G, K on V and X ⊆ E(G) let |G → K|_X be the number of permutations π of V with
π(E(G)) ∩ E(K) = π(X), and inj(Y, K) := #{π : π(Y) ⊆ E(K)} for Y ⊆ E(G). Inclusion–exclusion:

    |G → K|_X = Σ_{X ⊆ Y ⊆ E(G)} (−1)^{|Y ∖ X|} inj(Y, K).

- **Edge deck.** inj(Y, K) = inj(Y, G) for every Y ≠ E(G) (edge Kelly lemma), so exactly one
  term survives: |G → K|_X − |G → G|_X = (−1)^{|E ∖ X|}(iso(G, K) − aut G). For K ≇ G the right side
  is ± aut(G) ≠ 0 **for every X**, and one then picks X where the left side vanishes for a
  counting reason (X = ∅ and m > ½·C(n,2): Lovász; parity over all X and 2^{m−1} > n!: Müller).
  (Nash-Williams 1978 as in `groups_and_harmonic.md` S2, S6, there verified through
  Maynard–Siemons and Thatte; primary not read.)
- **Vertex deck.** inj(Y, K) = inj(Y, G) when the spanning subgraph (V, Y) has an isolated
  vertex (Kelly; checked `kelly_induced_count` is the engine) and, by Tutte 1979 / Kocay 1981
  (UNVERIFIED primaries; `algebraic_methods.md`), when (V, Y) is disconnected. What survives:

      |G → K|_X − |G → G|_X = Σ_{Y ⊇ X, (V,Y) connected} (−1)^{|Y ∖ X|} (inj(Y, K) − inj(Y, G)).

  The unknowns form the **filter of connected spanning subgraphs above X**, a whole Boolean
  interval when X is connected. The sum is not forced to be non-zero for any X except X = E(G)
  (where it is the statement K ≇ G). That is the precise failure: not the counting step but
  the collapse to a single term.
- **What the residual's structure adds.** Theorem H and T3 of r14-t3 tie hom(F, ·) for every F
  glued from paths, closed walks and stars at one vertex, of any size; subtracting the proper
  quotients (Kelly range) ties inj(Y, ·) for Y a spanning path, Hamiltonian cycle, spanning
  spider or kite. So those Y leave the filter. Every connected spanning Y with two branch
  vertices stays. The balanced swap at every vertex (¬WR) is a per-card object; it puts no
  relation on inj(Y, ·) for such Y that I can find. **The lever is not restored.** What is
  restored is the weaker one-sign lever of §4.1, with the range of §4.2.

## 5. The symmetric layer for one involution: signed double covers

### 5.1 Setting

**Hypothesis H1.** τ ∈ Aut(G), τ² = 1, and every vector orthogonal to 𝒰(G) is τ-antisymmetric:
f(τx) = −f(x). Equivalently 𝒰(G) = Fix(τ). (Then 𝒰 ⊆ ℝ^{π_st} ⊆ Fix(Γ) ⊆ Fix(τ) = 𝒰: the orbits
of τ, the Γ-orbits and the stable cells coincide, and SC of §6 holds. Conversely, if the stable
partition has all cells of size ≤ 2 and 𝒰 = ℝ^{π_st}, then the product of the cell
transpositions is such a τ: an equitable partition with cells of size ≤ 2 has empty, complete
or perfect-matching blocks.)

F := the fixed points of τ; pairs P_i = {u_i, u′_i}, u′_i = τu_i, i ∈ I. Between two pairs P_i, P_j the
adjacency is τ-invariant, so it is empty, complete, or one of the two perfect matchings.
**Matching graph M_τ** on I: ij ∈ M_τ iff the adjacency between P_i and P_j is a perfect matching
(iff u_i is adjacent to exactly one of u_j, u′_j). Signature σ(ij) = +1 if u_iu_j ∈ E, −1 if
u_iu′_j ∈ E (depends on the labelling inside pairs; switching a pair changes the signs at it).
For Q ⊆ E(M_τ) let **G^Q** be G with the adjacency between P_i and P_j complemented for ij ∈ Q
(each such matching replaced by the other one). For S ⊆ I let π_S := ∏_{i∈S} (u_i u′_i).

### 5.2 Theorem PM

**Theorem PM (paper).** Assume H1.
1. Every aligned hypomorph of G is G^Q for some Q ⊆ E(M_τ). In particular K = G on all pairs
   meeting F, inside every P_i, and between pairs not joined in M_τ.
2. π_S G = G^{δ(S)}, δ(S) the cut of S in M_τ. So G^Q ≅ G whenever Q is a cut.
3. If M_τ is a forest, G is reconstructible.

Argument. 1: by (R) and H1 every row of Δ is τ-antisymmetric: Δ_{x,τy} = −Δ_{x,y}. For y ∈ F this
gives Δ_{xy} = 0; for y = x it gives Δ_{x,τx} = 0. For x ∈ P_i, y ∈ P_j, i ≠ j, using symmetry of Δ twice,
Δ_{τx,τy} = Δ_{x,y} and Δ_{x,τy} = Δ_{τx,y} = −Δ_{x,y}: the block is a·(1, −1; −1, 1) with a ∈ {0, ±1}. If a ≠ 0,
(S) says the +a positions are non-edges or edges of G according to the sign and the −a
positions the opposite; so the block of G is a perfect matching, ij ∈ M_τ, and K has the other
matching. **This is the step that uses integrality**: a real antisymmetric row could move
weight between complete and empty blocks; a 0/±1 one cannot.
2: for i ∈ S, j ∉ S the block P_i × P_j of π_S G is the block of G with the two rows swapped: a
matching is complemented, an empty or complete block is unchanged. For i, j both in S or both
outside, and for pairs meeting F, τ-invariance gives no change.
3: in a forest every edge set is a cut (2-colour each tree by the parity of Q along the path
from a root). So K = G^Q = π_S G ≅ G. ∎

Hand check of the definitions on P₄ = a–b–c–d, τ = (a d)(b c): 𝒰 = span{1, deg} = Fix(τ); the
block between {a, d} and {b, c} is the matching {ab, dc}, so M_τ is one edge; G^Q for that edge
has edges ac, db, bc, the path a–c–b–d, which is π_S G for S = {the pair {a, d}}. Consistent.

### 5.3 The obstruction is a cohomology class

Q ↦ [Q] ∈ ℤ₂^{E(M_τ)} / cuts = H¹(M_τ; ℤ₂) (dimension |E| − |I| + components). [Q] = 0 iff Q meets
every cycle of M_τ evenly iff G^Q = π_S G. In signed-graph language G restricted to the pairs is
the double cover of the signed graph (M_τ, σ) (plus τ-symmetric decoration: complete blocks,
inner edges u_iu′_i, attachments to F), G^Q is the cover of (M_τ, σ·(−1)^Q), and [Q] is the change
of the balance of cycles. A cycle of length ℓ lifts to two ℓ-cycles if positive and to one
2ℓ-cycle if negative. This is the Cai–Fürer–Immerman twist in its smallest form, the "sign"
that r14-t1 §7 extracted from Ivanov's shared-card examples, and the ℤ/2-cocycle that r14-t1
§4.3 expected for orbits of size ≤ 2. Under H1 the walk-level linear data are blind to [Q]:
every G^Q has the automorphism τ, the same quotient, 𝒰(G^Q) = Fix(τ), and Δ·𝒰 = 0.

### 5.4 Theorem PM⁺: the closed-walk tie kills isolated cycles

On Anti(τ) = span{ε_i := e_{u_i} − e_{u′_i}} the operator A acts by the signed matrix
B_{ij} = σ(ij) for ij ∈ M_τ, B_{ii} = −[u_i ~ u′_i], 0 otherwise; for G^Q the signs on Q are reversed.
Since A_K = A_G on Fix(τ) and both commute with τ,

    c_k(u_i) = ¼·s_iᵀA^k s_i + ½·(B^k)_{ii},   s_i := e_{u_i} + e_{u′_i},

with the first term common to G and K. So **(R_cl) gives (B_K^k)_{ii} = (B_G^k)_{ii} for all i, k**:
the two signatures have the same signed closed-walk counts at every vertex of M_τ.

Let Q be not a cut and ℓ the least length of a cycle of M_τ meeting Q oddly ("Q-odd"). A
closed walk whose sign changes traverses Q an odd number of times; the edges it uses an odd
number of times form an even subgraph containing a Q-odd cycle; so it has length ≥ ℓ, with
equality only for the two traversals of a Q-odd ℓ-cycle (no loops, no backtracking). Hence

    (B_K^ℓ)_{ii} − (B_G^ℓ)_{ii} = −4·Σ_{Z ∋ i, |Z| = ℓ, Q-odd} σ_G(Z),

and (R_cl) forces: **every vertex of a shortest Q-odd cycle lies on another Q-odd cycle of the
same length and opposite sign.**

**Class PM⁺.** H1, and no vertex of M_τ lies on two cycles of M_τ of equal length and opposite
sign. (Contains: M_τ a forest; a pseudoforest; any M_τ with balanced signature; cacti whose
cycles at each cut vertex have distinct lengths.)

**Theorem PM⁺ (paper).** Every graph in PM⁺ is reconstructible. (No Q-odd cycle can exist, so
Q is a cut; Theorem PM.2.) ∎

Position. By the AKRV characterisation (Theorem 9, conditions A, B, G, H; read on ar5iv) the
forest case of PM consists of amenable graphs (cells of size ≤ 2, matchings between cells,
anisotropic components trees, no heterogeneous cell). A unicyclic component violates their
condition D ("no uniform anisotropic cycle"), so such a G is **not** amenable: it has a
fractionally isomorphic non-isomorph G^Q. PM⁺ says the deck still separates them. So the deck
is strictly stronger than colour refinement here, through quadratic (closed-walk) data.

### 5.5 What stays open in this layer (the signed-cover problem)

Under H1: can two signatures σ, σ′ on the same M_τ, not switching-equivalent, with equal
diagonals of all powers of B (and the same decoration), give hypomorphic covers? Only Theorem H
has been used; the cards themselves have not. For x = u_i the card G − u_i has lost τ, and a
Q-odd cycle through i lifts to a path on 2ℓ − 1 vertices in one cover and to a cycle plus a
path in the other, so the card sees the sign *if* the card isomorphism respects the pair
structure near i. I have no argument that it must. This is a finite-dimensional, purely
combinatorial question about signed graphs and is the natural next thinker target of this
angle. For larger groups the reduction of §5.2.1 fails at the first step: for an orbit of size
3 a degree-1 block is a perfect matching of two triples, six of them, three Γ-invariant, and
0/1 rows with zero orbit sums no longer force invariance.

## 6. Route (a): fractional isomorphism, compactness, amenability

### 6.1 What the ties give

- **Walk ties.** X := P_𝒲 (orthogonal projection onto the main space). Then X = Xᵀ = X², X1 = 1,
  A_G X = X A_G = A_K X = X A_K. So A_G X = X A_K with X "doubly quasi-stochastic": row and column
  sums 1, **entries of both signs**. X is aligned to the identity in the sense that it fixes
  every walk vector of both graphs. This is the path-homomorphism relaxation (equal walk
  counts ⇔ a real solution of the fractional-isomorphism equations without non-negativity;
  Dell–Grohe–Rattan, arXiv:1802.08876, statement from memory, UNVERIFIED). Degree classes add
  Δ P_𝒰 = 0 but 𝒰 need not be A-invariant.
- **When is it doubly stochastic?** A symmetric idempotent with non-negative entries and row
  sums 1 is the averaging matrix X_π of a partition π (standard: positivity of an entry is an
  equivalence relation and the matrix is constant on blocks). So the ties give a doubly
  stochastic X exactly when the tied space is a partition space. With (R) alone:
  **SC: 𝒰(G) = ℝ^{π_st(G)}**, i.e. dim 𝒰 = number of stable colour classes ("walks and degrees
  already reach colour refinement"). Under SC, Δ·1_C = 0 for every stable cell C, so π_st is
  equitable for K with the parameters of G: X_{π_st} A_G = A_G X_{π_st} = A_K X_{π_st} = X_{π_st} A_K. K and G are
  fractionally isomorphic **with the same partition at the same vertices** (Ramana–
  Scheinerman–Ullman, Discrete Math. 132 (1994) 247–265: fractional isomorphism ⇔ common
  coarsest equitable partition; cited from memory and through AKRV's introduction, read).
  r14-t3 T4 is the special case π_st = π_deg.

### 6.2 Compactness is not the condition; amenability is

- Tinhofer (Discrete Appl. Math. 30 (1991) 253–264; catalogue record and AKRV's account read,
  paper not read): G is **compact** if every doubly stochastic matrix commuting with A_G is a
  convex combination of automorphisms. For compact G the polytope of fractional isomorphisms
  to H has only integral vertices if G ≅ H and **no** integral vertex otherwise (AKRV §1, §6,
  read). So compactness turns "is there an integral point" into LP; it does **not** say that a
  fractional isomorphism implies an isomorphism. AKRV §8 (read): cycles are compact
  (Tinhofer 1986) and C_n is fractionally isomorphic to C_{n₁} + C_{n₂}. Our X_{π_st} is a particular,
  non-integral point; compactness of G says nothing about it.
- **Amenable** (AKRV 2015/2017, Comput. Complexity, arXiv:1502.01255; read on ar5iv): colour
  refinement separates G from every non-isomorphic graph, i.e. fractionally isomorphic to G
  implies isomorphic. Theorem 9: G is amenable iff conditions A, B, G, H hold (cells induce
  empty, complete, mK₂, its complement or C₅; cell pairs induce empty, complete, disjoint
  stars or the complement; anisotropic components of the cell graph are trees, monotone in
  cell size from a minimum cell; at most one heterogeneous cell per component, of minimum
  size). Theorem 13: amenable ⇒ compact (not conversely).

**Theorem FA (paper, modulo AKRV Theorem 9 and RSU).** If SC holds and G is amenable, then G is
reconstructible. By Theorem 9 the hypothesis is intrinsic. ∎ (§6.1 and the definition.)

TC (all blocks homogeneous) and the forest case of PM are the fragments of FA with
self-contained arguments. CT with a non-equitable colouring, TC₁, WRT and PM⁺ are not inside FA.

### 6.3 What must still be proved if only walk ties are available

SC is a hypothesis on G. It cannot be removed by a vertexwise tie: **"the stable colouring of K
is that of G at the same vertices" is false in general.** If u, v are removal-similar and lie
in different stable cells (pseudosimilar vertices; r14-t3 §1 caveat), then (u v) is a
hypomorphism of G to itself and the aligned hypomorph (u v)G has the two colours exchanged.
Sibling t1 of this round (§2, an explicit pair verified there by hand; not re-verified by me)
records the same failure for A(w₂ ∘ w₂), for RK(paw) and for CC₁. Under SC the phenomenon cannot
occur, because walk and degree data are tied and separate the stable cells.

The sound statement is global (invariant under the coset freedom σ ↦ σπ, π ∈ Π):

**GT.** Hypomorphic graphs are fractionally isomorphic; equivalently hom(T, ·) is recognizable
for every tree T of every size (Dvořák 2010; Dell–Grohe–Rattan 2018, via
`hom_counts_and_logic.md`); t1 §6 states it in the same form.

- GT ⇒ **every amenable graph is reconstructible** (definition of amenable; no alignment is
  needed). The conjecture ⇒ GT trivially. So: conjecture ⇒ GT ⇒ "amenable graphs are
  reconstructible". GT is a consequence of the conjecture for which I found no source and no
  argument (UNVERIFIED negative; r14-t3 §2.1 records the same gap).
- For compact but not amenable G (cycles; the unicyclic PM⁺ graphs) even GT is not enough;
  one needs quadratic data, as in §5.4.
- With walk ties only, what is available is exactly SC-type hypotheses: an intrinsic
  condition saying that the tied module 𝒰 already contains the indicators one wants (twin
  classes in TC, colour classes in CT, τ-orbits in PM, stable cells in FA).

### 10.2 Reach

  prime with walk rank 2 (`options_combinatorial.md` §0, small-n evidence recorded there, not
  re-run). On that face CT = DT (already inside BS₄), H1 and SC are impossible for n > 4, and
  WRT = WR unless a card has twins. **So these classes may be disjoint from the hard part.**
- What they do change: the residual's "corank ≥ 2" clause is satisfied for free by any graph
  with two twin pairs or one non-twin symmetry. After CT/TC₁/WRT the clause means twin-reduced
  corank ≥ 2 and every card swap is unbalanced on a twin class; after PM it excludes the
  smallest symmetric explanation.
- Novelty: I found no source for TC, CT, WRT, PM, PM⁺ or Proposition AV and did not search
  beyond the papers listed; novelty UNVERIFIED and not claimed. WRT ⊇ Wang–Wen–Guo is my
  reading of their abstract.

### 10.3 Recommendation

- **Reducer:** children `walk_coloured_threshold` (CT) and `walk_rigid_apex_mod_twins` (WRT),
  remainder with both negated. If one slot more is wanted: `involution_forest` (PM). TC₁ can
  replace DWC₁-style reasoning later; it is not worth a slot now.
- **Thinkers:** §5.5 (signed covers: do the cards force [Q] = 0 under H1?) and its relaxation
  to "orbits of size ≤ 2 without H1" via r14-t1 BAL.3, which gives the same block law for the
  holonomy-orbit partition of a given hypomorph.
- **Do not** spend effort on compactness, on Burnside-type counts over 𝒦, or on a vertex
  Nash-Williams identity: §4.2, §4.3 and §6.2 say why.
