## 1. Notation and the exact form of "outside W3"

G on V, n ≥ 3, deg = degree in G. For distinct u, v: S = V ∖ {u, v}, F = G[S], α = [u ~ v],
δ = deg v − α, N_u = N(u) ∖ v, N_v = N(v) ∖ u (subsets of S). For a pin w ∈ S: A = Aut(F),
A_w its stabiliser. As in r12-t2 §4, for the class of (u, v, w):
- 𝒜 = {(x, y, z) : (G − x; y, z) ≈ (G − u; v, w)};
- 𝒞 = {(x, y, z) : (G − x; y, z) ≈ (G − y′; x′, z′) for some (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜};
- **port class** 𝒫 = {(x, y, z) : (G − x − y; z) ≈ (F; w)}; the window is deg x ∈ {δ, δ + 1};
- ℛ = {t ∈ 𝒫 : window, t ∉ 𝒞}. (W): |ℛ| < |𝒜|.
- (K): for every (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒜 and g ∈ Aut(G − x − y)_z: g fixes N(x) ∖ y, or g preserves deg.

W3 = some class with (W) and (K). **Clause (i)** at (u, v): deg u ∉ {δ, δ + 1}.
G ∉ W3 means: at every class, **F1** (|ℛ| ≥ |𝒜|) or **F2** ((W) holds and (K) fails).

Facts used below (paper, one line each).
- (i) implies deg u ≠ deg v. A graph has a pair with (i) iff it is not regular (r12-t3 §5.5).
- If (i) fails at (u, v), every member of 𝒜 is in the window, so (W) forces 𝒜 ⊆ 𝒞: some
  (x′, y′, z′) has (x′, y′, z′) and (y′, x′, z′) both in 𝒜 (a "symmetric" class).
- Edge count: for (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒫 with deg x = δ + β, deg y = deg u − β + [x ~ y].

## 2. The ambient classes exclude the classical constructions

Paper. Let G be connected with connected complement.
- *Diameter-2 twin* (diam G ≤ 2 and diam Ḡ ≤ 2): G has no vertex of degree 1 (its neighbour
  would be adjacent to every vertex, so Ḡ would be disconnected) and none of degree n − 2.
- *Dominating-edge twin*: every pendant vertex is attached to an end of the dominating edge, so
  at most two vertices carry pendant vertices.

So trees, unicyclic graphs, coronas and every "attach a rooted graph at a pseudosimilar vertex"
construction with pendant parts are outside the diameter-2 class, and coronas of graphs with
more than two vertices are outside both. The classical constructions enter the residual's
ambient class only after a **transport**: X[A] or C₅ with a vertex replaced by A
(diameter 2 in G and Ḡ whenever X has it, e.g. X = C₅, Petersen, Paley; paper: two vertices of
one fibre have a common neighbour in an adjacent fibre, and the complement is X̄[Ā]), or
P₄[A], bull[A] for the dominating-edge twin. §4.3 and §4.4 treat exactly these.

## 3. General lemmas (paper, all graphs)

### 3.1 Lemma X: absorption

Let (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒫 through φ : (G − x − y; z) → (F; w). Put
𝔘_w = {ψ(N(x′) ∖ y′) : (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜, ψ a witness (G − x′; y′, z′) ≅ (G − u; v, w)}
(the attachment sets, inside F, of the **pointed mates** of u; N_u ∈ 𝔘_w).

**Lemma X.** (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒞 ⟺ φ(N(y) ∖ x) ∈ A_w · 𝔘_w. In particular, if
φ(N(x) ∖ y) = N_v, then (y, x, z) ∈ 𝒜 and (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒞.
Argument. (⇒) Let χ : G − x → G − y′ with y ↦ x′, z ↦ z′ and (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜 through ψ. Then
ψ ∘ χ restricted to V ∖ {x, y} is an isomorphism (G − x − y; z) → (F; w), so it is a ∘ φ with
a ∈ A_w, and aφ(N(y) ∖ x) = ψ(N(x′) ∖ y′). (⇐) χ := ψ⁻¹ ∘ a ∘ φ, extended by y ↦ x′. The last
sentence: φ ∪ {x ↦ v} is then an isomorphism G − y → G − u, and (x′, y′, z′) := (y, x, z). ∎

Reading. **Membership in 𝒞 looks only at where the second vertex is attached, and it accepts
every pointed mate of u, not only u.** So a vertex pseudosimilar to u (same card, no
automorphism) enlarges 𝔘_w and with it 𝒞: vertex pseudosimilarity is absorbed by the census.
F1 needs port copies whose second vertex sits at a set that is *not* the attachment set of a
pointed mate of u: a coincidence of two-deleted subgraphs that no card isomorphism explains.
Tested (claim X of `t3s_claims.py`).

### 3.2 Lemma K₂: the kill clause is a statement about two subgroups

For (x, y, z) put P = Aut(G − x − y)_z, P₁ = {g ∈ P : g fixes N(x) ∖ y},
P₂ = {g ∈ P : g preserves deg}. Both are subgroups, and g ∈ P₂ iff g preserves the function
1_{N(x)} + 1_{N(y)} on V ∖ {x, y} (g preserves degrees in G − x − y).

**Lemma K₂.** (K) at (x, y, z) ⟺ P = P₁ or P = P₂.
Argument: a group is not the union of two proper subgroups. ∎
So **F2 at a member** means: one pinned automorphism moves N(x) ∖ y *and* one (possibly another)
moves 1_{N(x)} + 1_{N(y)}.

### 3.3 Lemma N: normal form at a unique-copy pair with (i)

Let (u, v) satisfy (i) and let G − x − y ≅ F only for {x, y} = {u, v}. Put
K_v = Stab_A(N_u) (the restrictions of Aut(G − v)_u) and K_u = Stab_A(N_v).

**Lemma N.** For every pin w: 𝒜 = {(u, v, z) : z ∈ K_u w},
𝒞 ∩ 𝒫 = {(v, u, z) : z ∈ K_v K_u w}, ℛ = {(v, u, z) : z ∈ A w ∖ K_v K_u w}. Hence
- (W) ⟺ |A w| − |K_v K_u w| < |K_u w|;
- (K) ⟺ for every k ∈ K_u: A_{kw} ≤ K_v or A_{kw} ≤ D, D the degree-preserving subgroup of A.
Argument. A member of 𝒜 restricts to a port isomorphism, so its first two entries are u, v
in some order; (v, u, ·) ∈ 𝒜 would give deg u = deg v against (i). The third entry runs over
the orbit of Aut(G − u)_v. The triples (u, v, z) of 𝒫 are outside the window by (i); (v, u, z)
is in 𝒞 iff z ∈ Aut(G − v)_u · (K_u w). (K) by Lemma K₂. ∎
Tested: claim N of `t3s_claims.py`, NO WITNESS.

Consequences (paper).
- **A pin with trivial stabiliser reproduces the checked gluing clause.** If A_w = 1 the three
  orbits are regular and (W) reads |A| − |K_v K_u| < |K_u|. The complement of K_v K_u in A is a
  union of left cosets of K_u, so (W) ⟺ A = K_v K_u, and (K) is vacuous. So at a unique-copy
  pair with (i), everything W3 adds to `unique_copy_gluing` lives at pins with a non-trivial
  stabiliser: smaller orbits make the census easier, and Lemma K₂ is the price.
- The same computation with a tuple of pins that is a base of A (r12-t2 §6.2) gives the same
  equivalence: at such a pair the base-pin variant coincides with the gluing clause.
- **Corollary R (rigid cards).** If the doubly pointed cards of G are pairwise non-isomorphic,
  then G ∈ W3 iff some pair (u, v) with (i) has no copy G − x − y ≅ F, {x, y} ≠ {u, v}, with
  deg x in the window, and Aut(F) has a fixed point and preserves degrees. For regular G there
  is no pair with (i): this is r12-t2 §8.

## 4. The families

### 4.1 Regular graphs with pairwise non-isomorphic doubly pointed cards

Outside W3 (r12-t2 §8; Corollary R). Failure mode: F1 in its degenerate form, 𝒜 = {(u, v, w)} ⊆ ℛ.
In `pure_degree_class`, hence in neither residual. The Frucht graph is the computed example.
This stays the only all-n "outside W3" statement I know.

### 4.2 Coronas

H∘K₁: every vertex h of H gets a pendant vertex h′.

**Lemma C (paper).** If δ(H) ≥ 3, then H∘K₁ ∈ W3 at (h′, v, w) for every h, every vertex
v ≠ h of H and every third vertex w. No hypothesis on Aut(H) or on pseudosimilarity in H.
Argument. In G minus at most two vertices a vertex of H has degree ≥ 2 and a pendant vertex
has degree ≤ 1, so the two kinds are told apart; a remaining pendant t′ is adjacent to t or
isolated. α = 0, deg h′ = 1, deg v ≥ 4, so (i) holds and the window lies in [4, ∞).
- *𝒜.* If (G − x; y, z) ≈ (G − h′; v, w) then G − x has no isolated vertex, so x = t′ is
  pendant, y is a vertex of H other than t, and N(x) ∖ y = {t}.
- *(K).* In G − t′ − y, t is the only vertex of H without a neighbour of degree ≤ 1. Every
  automorphism fixes t = N(x) ∖ y: first disjunct, no group needed.
- *(W).* Let (x₁, y₁, z₁) ∈ 𝒫 with deg x₁ in the window. Then x₁ ∈ H. G − h′ − v has exactly
  one isolated vertex, so y₁ = s′ is pendant with s ≠ x₁. The port isomorphism φ maps s (the
  vertex of H without pendant) to h, so φ ∪ {s′ ↦ h′} is an isomorphism G − x₁ → G − v with
  y₁ ↦ h′ = u: by Lemma X (second vertex attached at N_u), (x₁, y₁, z₁) ∈ 𝒞. So ℛ = ∅. ∎
Tested: `t3s_corona.py`, 13 graphs H (4 ≤ |H| ≤ 8, among them K₄, K₅ − e, W₄, K₃,₃, prism,
Q₃), all 4348 predicted triples are W3 triples.

Reading. Pseudosimilar vertices of H (Kimble–Schwenk–Stockmeyer give k mutually pseudosimilar
vertices for every k, as reported by Kocay 1984 p. 183; their graphs UNVERIFIED) do not matter:
the pendant vertex is a marker, the port isomorphism extends, the twist is trivial.

### 4.3 Lexicographic products X[A], X prime

X prime (no module other than ∅, singletons, V(X)), |X| ≥ 4; A any graph on m ≥ 3 vertices;
G = X[A] on pairs (p, a); deg (p, a) = m·deg_X p + deg_A a. Λ = Aut(X).

**Lemma M (paper; the modular decomposition theorem, Gallai 1967, UNVERIFIED as a citation;
the case needed is argued here).** If all B_p are non-empty, the maximal proper modules of
X[B_p : p ∈ X] are the fibres. So every isomorphism between two such graphs is a pair
(π ∈ Λ, fibre isomorphisms B_p → B′_{πp}).
Argument. For a proper module M, Q = {p : B_p ∩ M ≠ ∅} is a module of X (a vertex outside M in
a fibre outside Q is uniform to M). If Q = V(X), a vertex t ∉ M in fibre s sees M in a fibre
adjacent to s and in one non-adjacent to s (X is connected and co-connected): contradiction. ∎
Since m ≥ 3, G − x and G − x − y are again of this form over the same X.

For a ∈ A let ρ(a) = {c : A − c ≅ A − a} (removal class) and call c **untwisted** if every
automorphism of A − c fixes N_A(c); **untwisted at a pin e′** if this holds for the
automorphisms fixing e′.

**Lemma J (fibre calculus; paper).** Let u = (p, a), v = (p′, b), w = (s, e) be distinct.
1. (J1) p = p′ = s: (W) and (K) hold at (u, v, w) in G iff they hold at (a, b, e) in A.
2. (J2) p = p′ ≠ s: W3 at (u, v, w) iff the pair analogue W2 of r12-t2 §5 holds at (a, b) in A.
3. (J3) p ≠ p′ and (i) holds at (u, v): **(W) holds with ℛ = ∅.** (K) holds iff
   - s = p: every c with (A − c; e′) ≅ (A − a; e) for some e′ is untwisted at the pin e′;
   - s ≠ p: every c ∈ ρ(a) is untwisted.
Argument. (J1), (J2): G − u − v has one fibre of size m − 2, so every port copy deletes two
vertices of one fibre q ∈ Λp; 𝒜, 𝒞, ℛ are Λ-orbit × (the same set for A), the window shifts by
m·deg_X p on both sides, and an automorphism of G − x − y fixing z acts on fibre q by an
element of Aut(A − c − c′) (fixing e′ in case J1, arbitrary in case J2) and elsewhere by maps
that fix N(x) and degrees.
(J3), census. G − u − v has exactly two fibres of size m − 1. A port copy (x, y, z), x = (q, c),
y = (q′, c′), comes with π{q, q′} = {p, p′}.
- *x plays u* (πq = p): A − c ≅ A − a, so deg_A c = e(A) − e(A − c) = deg_A a and deg x = deg u,
  outside the window by (i).
- *x plays v* (πq = p′, πq′ = p): φ_q : A − c ≅ A − b, φ_{q′} : A − c′ ≅ A − a. Put x′ := y,
  y′ := (q, b). The map ψ that is φ on all fibres except q and the identity of A from fibre q
  to fibre p′ is an isomorphism G − x′ → G − u with y′ ↦ v, so (x′, y′, ψ⁻¹w) ∈ 𝒜; and the map
  that is φ_q on fibre q and the identity elsewhere is an isomorphism G − x → G − y′ with
  y ↦ x′, z ↦ ψ⁻¹w. So (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒞. **No hypothesis on c′: it may be pseudosimilar to a.**
(J3), kill. For (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒜, x = (q, c), an automorphism g of G − x − y fixing z cannot swap
the two deficient fibres (deg x = deg u ≠ deg v = deg y), so it fixes q. Outside fibre q it
preserves adjacency to x. On fibre q it is some g_q ∈ Aut(A − c), and both disjuncts of (K)
reduce to "g_q fixes N_A(c)" (g_q preserves degrees in A − c, so it preserves deg_A iff it
fixes N_A(c)). Every g_q occurs (extend by the identity), with g_q e′ = e′ iff z = (q, e′). ∎

**Corollary J4 (paper).** Let G = X[A] be non-regular (X or A non-regular). G ∈ W3 if one of:
- (a) A ∈ W3 (fibre-internal class, J1);
- (b) X is non-regular and A is regular (take p, p′ of different degree in X and b = a: (i)
  holds since |deg u − deg v| ≥ m ≥ 3; in a k-regular A, N_A(c) is the set of vertices of
  degree k − 1 of A − c, so every vertex is untwisted);
- (c) A is non-regular and some pinned removal class of A is untwisted, for example when A
  has an isolated or a dominating vertex a (then N_A(c) ∈ {∅, A − c} for every c with
  deg c = deg a). For regular X choose b with deg_A b ≠ deg_A a and p, p′ adjacent or not so
  that (i) holds (a prime X has both kinds of pairs); for non-regular X take b = a as in (b).

Tested: `t3s_fibre.py`, 12 products (X ∈ {P₄, C₅, bull}, A ∈ {P₃, K₁+K₂, K₃, 3K₁, paw, P₄, claw,
K₁+P₃, C₄}, n = 12, 15, 16): J1, J2, J3 predicted against the computed W3 triples for all 23090
ordered triples with a prediction, **0 disagreements**; the census never failed at a
cross-fibre triple with (i). Both truth values of the predictions occur (in P₃ an end vertex
is twisted, and untwisted when the pin is the centre).

What is left of this family. X[A] ∉ W3 needs: A ∉ W3 and A ∉ W2 (so |A| ≥ 9 by the exhaustive
computation of r12-t2, a computed input); A non-regular, or X regular; and **every** pinned
removal class of A (for a with a partner b giving (i)) contains a vertex twisted at its pin.
I know no such A. The symmetric cross-fibre classes without (i) were not analysed (they can
only add to W3).

Not covered: X-joins with fibres of different sizes or edge numbers (then "x plays u" can fall
into the window: two fibres A₁ ≇ A₂ with a common card and different edge numbers), and
quotients that are not prime (apply the lemma to the prime quotient; the fibres are then not
uniform). For a quotient on at most 3 vertices the product is disconnected or co-disconnected.

Literature. Brignall–Georgiou–Waters (J. Comb. 3 (2012) 123–134, arXiv:1112.1509; read by the
librarian, `options_combinatorial.md` O1; not re-read by me) reconstruct large families of
decomposable graphs from the skeleton; their open case is a single module of size 2 with
pseudosimilar structure. Lemma J is not their argument (it is a statement about the checked
class W3, not about reconstruction), and I did not compare the classes.

### 4.4 C₅ with one vertex replaced by a graph

G_A: the cycle p₁ … p₅ with p₁ replaced by A (p₂, p₅ adjacent to all of A). Diameter 2 in G_A
and its complement (C₅ is self-complementary).

**Lemma J5 (paper).** If |A| = m ≥ 4 and A has no isolated vertex, then G_A ∈ W3 at
(p₃, p₂, w) for every w.
Argument. deg p₃ = 2, deg p₂ = m + 1, α = 1: (i) holds, window {m, m + 1}.
F = G − p₃ − p₂ is A joined to p₅, plus the pendant vertex p₄ at p₅; Aut(F) = Aut(A) fixes p₄, p₅.
- *(K).* G − x ≅ G − p₃ forces x ∈ {p₃, p₄} (one pendant vertex whose neighbour has degree
  m + 1) and y the matching neighbour; N(x) ∖ y is the pendant vertex of the port, fixed by
  every automorphism.
- *(W).* Window and edge count leave: (p₂, p₃, ·) and its mirror image (p₅, p₄, ·), in 𝒞 by
  Lemma X; x ∈ A dominating in A, with no admissible y; x ∈ A of degree m − 2 in A with
  y ∈ {p₃, p₄} (the neighbour of the pendant vertex is then not adjacent to everything, unlike
  in F) or y ∈ A (no pendant vertex at all). So ℛ = ∅. ∎
Tested: `t3s_c5sub.py`, 17 graphs A (all seven on 4 vertices without isolated vertex, five on
5, the three former residual members `Ffk}W`, `Ff]mw`, `Gl]o^[`, Petersen, Frucht; n ≤ 16).

Reading. A frame vertex (p₃) whose neighbourhood is a marker of the port gives a class at
which nothing inside A matters. **A residual member has no frame**: every vertex must sit
inside the part of the graph that carries the coincidences.

### 4.5 Vertex-transitive minus a vertex (the family of Theorem S, r12-t3 §6)

X connected, vertex-transitive, not complete, Γ = Aut(X), G = X − r. Take an induced path
r – v – u and T = {r, u, v}. In G: deg u = d, deg v = d − 1, α = 1, so (i) holds.
**Port tightness at (T, w)**: every isomorphism (X − T′; z) → (X − T; w) with r ∈ T′, |T′| = 3,
is the restriction of an element of Γ.

**Proposition V (paper, conditional on port tightness at (T, w)).** G ∈ W3 at (u, v, w), where
w is any vertex outside T with [w ~ r] ≠ [w ~ u], or any vertex outside T if there is none.
Argument. *(W).* A port copy with x in the window is γ(T, w) with x ∈ N(r); the path γT has
centre x or r. In each of the four cases the doubly pointed card of (x, y, z) is the γ-image
of (X − {r, v}; u, w) or of (X − {u, v}; r, w). The first is the swap of (u, v, w). The second
is the swap of the member (γ₁r, γ₁v, γ₁w) of 𝒜, for any γ₁ ∈ Γ with γ₁u = r (transitivity).
So ℛ = ∅. *(K).* The pinned stabiliser of a member port is a conjugate of Γ_{T,w}, which fixes
the centre v; an element swapping r and u would give [w ~ r] = [w ~ u]. So it fixes r and the
deleted vertex: both disjuncts hold. If no w separates r and u outside T, a swap maps
N(x) ∖ T′ to N(r) ∖ T′ = N(x) ∖ T′. ∎
The same calculus predicts every class: (W) ⟺ (i) or the class is symmetric under Γ_w; (K) at
an (i)-class ⟺ every element of Γ_{T,w} fixes N_X(u) ∖ T or N_X(r) ∖ T.

Tested (`t3s_vtminus.py`, 16 graphs, n ≤ 13): the prediction agrees at **every** class for
Q₃, Petersen, K₃□K₃, C₁₀(1,4), C₁₂(1,5), C₁₃(1,5), Paley 13, C₁₃(1,2,4), icosahedron, K₄□K₃,
Heawood; it disagrees at some classes for C₅□K₂, C₉(1,2), C₁₁(1,2), C₁₁(1,3), C₁₂(1,3)
(8–12 census disagreements each): there tightness fails. All 16 graphs are in W3.

Status of the family: **not decided on paper.** I have no argument for tightness in a family
(sparse circulants refute it), and none for W3 without it. Where tightness fails there are
port isomorphisms outside Γ; Lemma X says they hurt only if they are also outside the card
groupoid. For paths (C_n − r, maximally non-tight) a direct paper check gives W3 at
(p₂, p₁, w), w not the midpoint of p₃ … p_m: the extra copy {p₁, p_m} of the port is absorbed
by the reversal of the card G − p₁ (tested for 5 ≤ m ≤ 11, `t3s_paths.py`: exactly these pins).

Relation to Theorem S: G = X − r is its one-added-vertex case, and by Kocay (1984; abstract and
pp. 181–183 read) every graph with k mutually pseudosimilar vertices comes from a group acting
on cosets, in general with more than one added vertex. For G = Y − Q, |Q| ≥ 2, under the
analogous tightness, the port class of (u, v, w) is governed by the graph 𝒦 on Q ∪ {u, v} with
a — b iff Q ∪ {u, v} ∖ {a, b} lies in the Γ-orbit of Q: types of port copies are the
non-isolated vertices, 𝒜 ∪ 𝒞 is the closed neighbourhood of v, and a foreign type is a vertex
a ∈ Q with a neighbour in 𝒦 and a ≁ v. For |Q| = 1, 𝒦 is a triangle (Proposition V). For larger
Q foreign types exist only for the boundedly many pairs {u, v} that complete a translate of
most of Q, so a pair with (i) away from them has none. **Paper remark, conditional, untested.**

### 4.6 Other products

Cartesian, strong and tensor products of a vertex-transitive graph with a small non-regular
graph are prime for the modular decomposition, so §4.3 does not apply. What applies is the
case Q = ∅ of the calculus: if every port isomorphism at (u, v, w) with (i) is induced by an
automorphism of G, then ℛ = ∅ (Lemma X) and the pinned stabiliser fixes u and v (their degrees
differ), so W3 holds. **Conditional on tightness; not decided on paper.** Computed
(r12-t2 §7): 122 products and their vertex- and edge-deleted subgraphs, all non-regular ones
in W3.

### 4.7 The general pseudosimilarity constructions

Godsil–Kocay (JCTB 32 (1982) 146–155; content through Kocay 1984 p. 181: "all graphs with a
pair of pseudo-similar vertices could be constructed in this way") and Kocay 1984 (abstract:
"in all graphs with pseudo-similar vertices, the vertices are pseudo-similar due to the action
of a group on the cosets of some subgroup") are characterisations, not families: they
describe **all** graphs with pseudosimilar vertices. Harary–Palmer 1966 (where pseudosimilar
vertices were noticed, per Kocay p. 182), Kimble–Schwenk–Stockmeyer (JGT 5 (1981) 171–181) and
Krishnamoorthy–Parthasarathy (k a power of two, per Kocay p. 183): constructions not read,
UNVERIFIED; I can decide them only through the transports of §2, where Lemmas C, J3, J5
apply to arbitrary H or A. What the three lemmas have in common is Lemma X: the census at a
class whose first vertex is a frame vertex does not see vertex pseudosimilarity.

## 5. Structure statements: what survives, and the gap

All statements are about a single graph (no deck). "Refuted" = a graph violating it exists.

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| RIGID: every non-regular graph has a class with (W) whose member ports have trivial pinned stabiliser | **refuted, paper, all n = \|X\|·m**: in X[A] with Aut(A) ≠ 1, every pinned port has a fibre containing none of x, y, z, and a non-trivial automorphism of that fibre extends by the identity. Computed: fails for 24 of 148 and 112 of 1038 non-regular graphs on 6 and 7 vertices |
| TRIV: … a class with (W) at which every pinned twist fixes N(x) | **refuted, computed only**: `EYWO`, `EZEG`, `EhdW`, `ElfO` (n = 6), `FhEJ?`, `FxVD?`, `F@U}o`, `F\VMo` (n = 7). So the second disjunct of (K) is needed already there; no all-n family |
| ST-B: every non-regular graph is in W3 | not refuted: n ≤ 8 (r12-t2), Lemmas C, J, J5, Proposition V. Would imply the root conjecture |
| ST-A: every non-regular graph has a class with (i) and (W) | not refuted: all 7, 31, 148, 1038 non-regular graphs on 4–7 vertices (`t3s_stats.py`) |
| "outside W3 ⟹ vertex-transitive minus a vertex" | void (no member known); and Proposition V says that tight members of that family are *inside* W3, so this cannot be the shape of the residual |

**What ST-A would give (paper).** If G has a class with (W), then by Lemma W and Lemma A₃ of
r12-t2 every hypomorph H of G is isomorphic to a twist G_g: G with one vertex x re-attached at
g⁻¹(N(x) ∖ y), g a pinned automorphism of a member port. So H and G share the card G − x and
differ only in the attachment of x, by an explicit finite list. If G has several census
classes, H is such a twist at each of them. The residual becomes: *no pinned twist that moves
N(x) and moves 1_{N(x)} + 1_{N(y)} is hypomorphic to G* (clause K-iso of r12-t2 §3).

**The gap between ST-A and ST-B** is the class R_tw of graphs that have census classes and
fail (K) at each of them. By Lemma K₂ a member needs, at every census class, a member port
(x, y, z) and pinned automorphisms g₁, g₂ with g₁ moving N(x) ∖ y and g₂ moving
1_{N(x)} + 1_{N(y)}. At a unique-copy pair with (i), by Lemma N, in group terms:
for every pin w, |A w| − |K_v K_u w| ≥ |K_u w|, or A_{kw} ⊄ K_v and A_{kw} ⊄ D for some k ∈ K_u.
The true criterion at such a pair is the double coset statement of the round-9 sketch t4
(L5–L7; `novelty_open_core_lemmas.md` row 5b, not re-derived here): every g ∈ A with G_g
hypomorphic to G lies in K_v K_u. W3, the gluing class and every k-pin variant are sufficient
conditions for it that never look at hypomorphy of G_g; the gap is exactly the set of
g ∈ A ∖ K_v K_u that no subgroup test (pins, N(x), degrees) excludes.

**Below ST-A** is the class R_cen of graphs with no census class at all: at every pair with
(i) and every pin, at least |𝒜| port copies whose second vertex is attached at a set that is
not the attachment set of a pointed mate of u (Lemma X). I know no non-regular example of any
size, and none of the families produces one on paper.

**Necessary shape of a residual member** (paper, from §2–§4): non-regular; 2 ≤ δ, Δ ≤ n − 3
(diameter-2 twin); no frame vertex in the sense of Lemmas C, J5; if decomposable with uniform
fibres over a prime quotient, then fibres outside W3 ∪ W2 with every pinned removal class
twisted; if vertex-transitive minus a vertex, then not port-tight; at every pair with (i)
either an unabsorbed port copy or two "independent" bad pinned automorphisms.

## 6. Where F2 comes from: split near-twins (paper remark, computed members)

t, t′ are near-twins split by u if N(t) Δ N(t′) ⊆ {u, t, t′}. If u splits two disjoint pairs
that v does not split, then for every pin w one of the two transpositions is an automorphism
of G − u − v fixing w, moving N(u), and moving degrees (deg t = deg t′ ± 1): F2 at every
class (u, v, w) with (W), unless another member of 𝒜 is treated differently. A vertex with a
twin splits nothing, which is why X[A] is harmless across fibres.
A set U of k ≥ 2 vertices with 2^k vertices realising all traces on U and uniform elsewhere
makes every u ∈ U split 2^{k−1} disjoint pairs. Computed (`t3s_neartwin.py`): in the
point–subset graph on 3 + 8 vertices every class with (W) and (K) has its first vertex among
the 8 subset vertices, none among the 3 points; half graphs (n ≤ 12), half graphs with one
side or both sides doubled, the BIT graphs on 9–12 vertices: between 9% and 78% of the census
classes fail (K), and all
these graphs are in W3 through other classes. For a member of R_tw every vertex that occurs
first in a census class would have to split two pairs; I could not build such a graph (the
vertices that realise the traces split nothing).

## 7. Is there a child to state?

No class child: no family escapes. Two reduction shapes are available and neither is
recommended on its own, because both children would stay open and no known graph separates
them:
- split by ST-A: "this step's predicate ∧ (some class has (W))" and "this step's predicate ∧
  (no class has (W))". The first conjunct is the W3 body without its last conjunct (K); the
  implication is one `by_cases`. Worth doing only together with an argument for one side.
- a K-iso child (r12-t2 §3): its hypothesis mentions hypomorphy of twists and is not a
  property of G alone.

## 8. Tournaments, digraphs, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

| Piece | Tournaments / digraphs | Graph-specific ingredient |
|---|---|---|
| Lemma X, Lemma K₂ (first subgroup), Lemma M | hold verbatim | none |
| Lemma N | needs (i) to exclude (v, u, ·) from 𝒜 and (u, v, ·) from the window | T0 (deg x = e(G) − e(G − x)) |
| Lemma C, J3, J5, Proposition V | the census step "x plays u ⟹ deg x = deg u, outside the window" fails: a tournament card does not give the score of the deleted vertex | T0, clause (i) |
| ST-A, ST-B | no analogue: without the window a census class must be symmetric | T0 |

So for tournaments the fibre calculus keeps only "x plays v ⟹ in 𝒞", and the copies "x plays
u" stay in ℛ: no census at a cross-fibre class, nothing is claimed. This is consistent with the
repo's Stockmeyer check (`options_combinatorial.md` O1: at n = 5, C₂ has the module {0, 2, 3},
B₂ is prime, and they are hypomorphic): modules do not help tournaments, and the place where
they help graphs is exactly the window. For a vertex-transitive tournament X with Γ regular of
odd order, X − r has, under the analogous tightness, no symmetric class (a symmetric class needs
an element of Γ with a fixed point that moves T), so the windowless analogue of Proposition V is
void there. A proof of
ST-A that did not use T0 would apply to Stockmeyer's pairs (J. Graph Theory 1 (1977) 19–25,
UNVERIFIED) and is therefore suspect; r12-t3 §8 computed that their anchoring depth is 2.
3-uniform hypergraphs: Lemma A₃ already fails (r12-t2 §9). Infinite graphs: the census compares
finite cardinalities; Lemma M holds, Lemma J3's count does not.

## 9. Lean

Nothing here is proposed for a builder. Notes for a reducer who wants to use it.
- **No new class is needed for the families**: Lemmas C, J, J5 and Proposition V say that the

- Lexicographic products and modules are not in Mathlib (grep in the pinned tree:

  high cost, no gain.
- The ST-A split (§7) needs only what the checked W3 file has: `Set.ncard`, the two set-builder

- Lemma N as a statement would go through automorphisms as `G.induce s ≃g G.induce s`, orbits

  (`Mathlib/GroupTheory/GroupAction/Quotient.lean`) exists but there is no `MulAction` of
  `≃g` on vertices packaged for induced subgraphs. Not on any route.
- Lemma K₂ is not needed in Lean (the checked body quantifies over single automorphisms).

## 10. Where this could break

1. **Nothing here closes the step**, and "no family escapes" is a statement about the families I
   examined, not about the residual.
2. **Proposition V and §4.6 are conditional on tightness**, which fails in small sparse cases.
   The orbit calculus of §4.5 for |Q| ≥ 2 is a remark and was not tested.
3. **Lemma J is for uniform fibres over a prime quotient**, m ≥ 3. Cross-fibre classes without
   (i) are not analysed. The statement "A ∈ W3 for |A| ≤ 8" is r12-t2's computation.
4. **Lemma J5 and Lemma C have side conditions** (no isolated vertex and m ≥ 4; δ(H) ≥ 3) that I
   did not try to remove.
5. **The scripts use canonical forms from r12-t2's library** (`canon_marked`, self-tested there).
   My kill clause is the two-disjunct clause of the Lean body; r12-t2's Python mode "K" has a
   third disjunct and is not used. I did not evaluate the Lean body itself.
6. **ST-A and ST-B are untested beyond n = 7 (resp. n = 8 and families).** I expect ST-B to fail
   for large n and have no opinion backed by an argument on ST-A.
7. **Citations.** Gallai's theorem and the papers of Harary–Palmer, Kimble–Schwenk–Stockmeyer,
   Krishnamoorthy–Parthasarathy, Godsil–Kocay were not read. Lemma M is argued in the text;
   nothing else depends on them.
8. **Attribution.** I found no source for Lemmas X, K₂, N, C, J, J5 or Proposition V and did not
   search beyond the repository notes and two web queries. They are elementary.
9. **Only W3 was decided for the families.** T⁺ ⊆ W2 (r12-t2 §5); membership of the families in
   T, T⁺ or US was not examined, because W3 already puts them outside this residual.
10. The lease beat at the start reported a lost race; `desk.py leases` showed the lease held by
   the shared holder `round13-struct` (a sibling of this sweep), so I continued.
