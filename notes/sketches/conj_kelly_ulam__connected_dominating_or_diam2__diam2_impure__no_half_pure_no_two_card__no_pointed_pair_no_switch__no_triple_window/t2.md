## 2. The failure of W3, restated

Round-12 `t2.md` §8: a non-regular graph is outside W3 iff at every doubly pointed class
(u, v, w) either **(F1)** |ℛ| ≥ |𝒜|, or **(F2)** some member (x, y, z) of 𝒜 has an automorphism
of G − x − y fixing z that moves N(x) and moves G-degrees. Two remarks that drive this sketch.

1. *Both clauses are statements about the two-deleted type F = G − u − v only.* 𝒜, 𝒞, ℛ are
   sets of triples whose port is (F; w), and their sizes are functions of the numbers of
   ordered pairs of each pointed type (G − x, y) with G − x − y ≅ F: a pointed card of type τ
   contains a fixed number o(τ′) of third points giving the doubly pointed type τ′.
2. *The window is one-sided for a reason.* For (x, y, z) with the port of (u, v, w) and
   deg x ∈ {δ, δ + 1}, the edge count gives (deg x, deg y, [x ~ y]) ∈ {(δ, deg u, 0),
   (δ + 1, deg u, 1), (δ, deg u + 1, 1), (δ + 1, deg u − 1, 0)}. Only the first two shapes can be
   the swap of a member of 𝒜_H, but the bit [x ~ y] is not transported by Φ₃, so ℛ cannot be cut
   down to them. (The pointed analogue of Lemma G fixes the *number* of adjacent triples per
   port and degree, not which types carry them.) I found no sharper census by degrees alone.

## 3. Slices, phantoms, and the class PC⁺

### 3.1 Definitions and Theorem PC

For a graph K on V and a graph F on n − 2 vertices:
- P_F(K) := ordered pairs (x, y), x ≠ y, with K − x − y ≅ F;
- **slice_F(K)** := the function τ ↦ #{(x, y) ∈ P_F(K) : (K − x, y) has pointed type τ}.
  (Its total is |P_F(K)|. It is the part of the pointed deck lying over F.)
- K is an **F-phantom of G** if K ≇ G, keys(K) = keys(G) and slice_F(K) = slice_F(G).
  A **weak F-phantom** asks only for the degree multiset instead of the keys.
- **PC⁺**: some u ≠ v such that G has no (G − u − v)-phantom. **PC**: no weak phantom.

**Theorem PC.** PC ⊆ PC⁺, and every graph in PC⁺ is reconstructible.
Paper argument. Let H be hypomorphic to G. keys(H) = keys(G) by T0 and W1 (through σ).
slice_F(H) = slice_F(G) for every F by L1⁺, and |P_F(H)| = |P_F(G)| by the same bijection
(x, y) ↦ (σx, f_x y) restricted to pairs (checked `restrict`). So H has the keys and the
slice of G at F = G − u − v; G has no phantom there; hence H ≅ G. ∎
`PC_reconstructible` and `PCplus_reconstructible` compile in Scratch (§9).

The class is **semantic**: its hypothesis quantifies over all graphs K on n vertices. It is a
property of G alone and decidable, but it is a ceiling, not a structural criterion. Its use is
in §3.2 and §6, not in its argument.

## 4. The dichotomy: twisted or re-paired

Let K be a weak F-phantom of G. Call K **twisted at F** if some (a, b) ∈ P_F(K) and
(x, y) ∈ P_F(G) have (K − a, b) ≈ (G − x, y) and (K − b, a) ≈ (G − y, x); otherwise **re-paired**.
(The swap is a fixed-point-free involution on P_F; it pairs pointed types. G and K have the same
types with the same multiplicities. Twisted: the two pairings share an edge. Re-paired: they
share none.)

### 4.1 Twisted phantoms (paper)

By Lemma A, K ≅ G_g for the twist of x by some g ∈ Aut(G − x − y). So **the twisted phantoms at F
are exactly the twists G_g, (x, y) ∈ P_F(G), g ∈ Aut(G − x − y), that are not isomorphic to G and
have the degree multiset (keys) and the F-slice of G.** For such g:
- g moves N(x) ∖ y and does not preserve N(y) ∖ x (otherwise G_g ≅ G); more generally
  g ∉ Stab(N(x))∘Stab(N(y)) (TL.4; this is the factorization clause of the checked GLUE class);
- level counts: #{t : ε_t = +1, deg t = j} = #{t : ε_t = −1, deg t = j + 1} for all j
  (degree multiset; round-11 t3 §3.1);
- Δ₀(g) = 0, and Δ₁(g) = 0 if K has the keys (§5).

### 4.2 Re-paired phantoms (paper)

**Lemma RP.** Let K be a re-paired weak phantom at F. Then
1. F has at least two copies in G as an unordered pair (with one copy {u, v}, K has one pair
   {a, b} with the pointed types of (u, v), (v, u), which is an anchor);
2. for every pointed class 𝒜 over F the pair window census fails, |ℛ_w(𝒜)| ≥ |𝒜|: the swap maps
   𝒜_K injectively into ℛ_K (Lemma W, step 2: the swap of a member of 𝒜_K has its first degree in
   the window), and the sizes are those of G;
3. hence (Lemma O of round-12 t2) the triple window census fails at every doubly pointed class
   over F, for every third point, and the same for every k-pointed refinement.
So a re-paired phantom is a single graph that realises F1 at **all** classes over F at once.

**Example RP (computed, `t2s_example_rp.py`).** G = `H@~~ibT`, K = `HO~}ibt` (n = 9, degrees
3 4 4 5 5 5 5 6 7, |Aut G| = |Aut K| = 2, same (deg, w₂) keys, cospectral, 3 common cards). One
two-deleted type F (|Aut F| = 4) has two copies in G, {2, 5} (adjacent, degrees 4, 7) and {5, 7}
(non-adjacent, degrees 7, 3), with four pointed types t1 = (2, 5), t2 = (5, 2), t3 = (5, 7),
t4 = (7, 5). G pairs t1–t2 and t3–t4; K pairs t1–t3 and t2–t4. No pair of K is anchored; no
pointed class over F passes the pair or the triple window census in G, as Lemma RP says. The two
exchanged types live on the same card: 2 and 7 are **pseudosimilar in G − 5** (removal-similar,
and no automorphism of G − 5 maps one to the other), and K is not a twist of 5 by an automorphism
of G − 5. The same K is a twisted phantom of G at a second type (one copy, |Aut| = 72, all
Δ_j = 0). G is in W3 at (0, 1, 2) and in PC⁺ (24 of its 26 types have no phantom).
Re-checked with brute-force marked isomorphisms (networkx only, no canonical forms): K ≇ G, same
keys, same slice over F, no anchored pair.
I have no argument that re-pairing always involves a pseudosimilar pair in a card: two copies
{x₁, y₁}, {x₂, y₂} with four different card types and compatible degrees could be re-paired without
one (paper remark; no example).

### 4.3 Degree-adapted pairs: where F1 cannot happen (paper)

Call u **degree-isolated** if no other vertex has degree deg u − 1, deg u or deg u + 1.

**Lemma DI.** Let u be degree-isolated, v ≠ u, C := G − u, and assume every x with
C − x ≅ C − v lies in the orbit Aut(C)·v (v has no pseudosimilar mate in the card of u). Then no
weak phantom at F = G − u − v is re-paired: every graph with the degree multiset and the F-slice
of G is G_g for some y′ ∈ Aut(C)·v and g ∈ Aut(G − u − y′).
Argument. Take 𝒜 = the pointed class of (u, v). A member (x′, y′) has deg x′ = deg u, so x′ = u and
y′ ∈ Aut(C)·v. A pair (x, y) ∈ P_F(G) with deg x ∈ {δ, δ + 1} has deg y ∈ {deg u − 1, deg u,
deg u + 1} by the edge count (§2, remark 2), so y = u and C − x ≅ C − v, so x ∈ Aut(C)·v, so
(x, u) is the swap of the member (u, x) of 𝒜: ℛ_w(𝒜) = ∅ < |𝒜|. Apply Lemma RP.2. ∎

This is the window census of W2 in the one situation where it is automatic. It is the precise
form of item (iii) of the angle: the degree choice removes F1, and what is left is the twist
group Aut(G − u − y′). Read with one more step (paper): if u is degree-isolated then u is
the unique vertex of its degree in every card of every reconstruction, all card isomorphisms
respect it, and "H hypomorphic to G" becomes "(G − u, N(u)) and (G − u, N′) are hypomorphic as
vertex-2-coloured graphs". I did not pursue this (coloured reconstruction is not in the tree).

Residual members need not have a degree-isolated vertex; the lemma says where not to look for
re-pairing. Graphs whose degrees lie in a short interval (the nearly regular ones) are the
ones where the window protects nothing.

## 5. The transfer law for twists (candidate (ii))

### 5.1 Identities (paper; single-graph, no deck)

Fix u ≠ v, g ∈ Aut(F), A := A_F. Define the **transfer coefficients**
Δ_j(g) := εᵀ A^j m = (number of walks u → v of length j + 2 with interior in S, in G_g) − (same in G).
Δ₀ = |g⁻¹N(u) ∩ N(v)| − |N(u) ∩ N(v)| is the change of the codegree of (u, v).

**Theorem TL.**
1. Σ_S deg(t) ε_t = Δ₀ − k, and Σ deg² (G_g) − Σ deg² (G) = 2Δ₀.
2. W₃(G_g) − W₃(G) = 2Δ₁ + 2(deg u + deg v)Δ₀, where W₃ = Σ_x deg(x) w₂(x) is the number of
   walks of length 3. Also w₂ at u and at v both change by Δ₀.
3. φ(G_g) = φ(G) (characteristic polynomials) ⟺ Δ_j = 0 for all j ≥ 0 ⟺ Δ_j = 0 for
   0 ≤ j ≤ n − 3 ⟺ W_k(G_g) = W_k(G) for all k (total walk counts). Triangular form: if Δ_i = 0 for
   i < j then W_k agree for k < j + 2 and W_{j+2}(G_g) − W_{j+2}(G) = 2Δ_j.
4. If g preserves N(v), or g = g₁∘g₂ (apply g₂ first) with g₁ preserving N(u) and g₂ preserving N(v),
   all Δ_j vanish (and G_g ≅ G). Write Stab(N(u))∘Stab(N(v)) for the set of such products.
   If g preserves G-degrees on S then Δ₀ = k.

Arguments. Write deg t = deg_F t + n(t) + m(t) on S; deg_F and A commute with g.
1: Σ deg_F ε = 0 (reindex by g), Σ n ε = |N ∩ g⁻¹N| − |N| = −k, Σ m ε = Δ₀; then
Σ_S ((deg + ε)² − deg²) = 2(Δ₀ − k) + 2k.
2: split the edges of G_g into those inside F, at v, at u. Inside F:
Σ_t ε_t (A deg)(t) + ½ εᵀAε, and εᵀA deg_F = 0, n′ᵀAn′ = nᵀAn give ½ εᵀAε = −εᵀAn, so this is
εᵀAm = Δ₁. At v:
deg v · Δ₀. At u: deg u · (Σ deg ε + k) = deg u · Δ₀.
3: Schur complement on the block S. With R := (λ − A)⁻¹,
φ(G) = φ(F) · [(λ − nᵀRn)(λ − mᵀRm) − (α + nᵀRm)²], and n′ᵀRn′ = nᵀRn. So φ(G_g) = φ(G) iff
(α + n′ᵀRm)² = (α + nᵀRm)². Both nᵀRm and n′ᵀRm are O(1/λ) with non-negative coefficients, so the
sign −1 would force α = 0 and all coefficients zero; hence n′ᵀRm = nᵀRm, i.e. all Δ_j = 0;
Cayley–Hamilton on F bounds j. For total walks: 1ᵀ(λ − A_G)⁻¹1 = 1ᵀR1 + pᵀM⁻¹p with
p = (1 + nᵀR1, 1 + mᵀR1) unchanged by g and M the 2 × 2 Schur block; it is a function
(c₁ + c₂τ)/(c₃ − τ²) of τ = α + n′ᵀRm with c₂c₃ ~ 2λ² at infinity, hence injective in τ there.
4: Pm = m gives n′ᵀA^jm = nᵀA^jPᵀm = nᵀA^jm; the twist depends only on the coset; Σ deg ε = 0 under
degree preservation (round-11 Lemma B).

### 5.2 The kill ladder, extended; class W3cd

### 5.3 The spectral form (paper, on top of literature)

Tutte (1979; UNVERIFIED here, see §12) : hypomorphic graphs have the same characteristic
polynomial. With TL.3:

**Corollary TL.** Let H be hypomorphic to G and anchored at (u, v) with twist g. Then
ε = 1_{g⁻¹N(u)} − 1_{N(u)} is orthogonal to the A_F-cyclic subspace Z(m) = span{A_F^j 1_{N(v)}}, and
symmetrically 1_{gN(v)} − 1_{N(v)} ⟂ Z(1_{N(u)}). In particular, if (A_F, 1_{N(v)}) or (A_F, 1_{N(u)}) is
a controllable pair (the cyclic subspace is everything; Godsil 2012), then H ≅ G.

So a class "window census at (u, v, w) and, at every member (x, y, z) of 𝒜, the pair
(A_{G−x−y}, 1_{N(y)}) is controllable" is reconstructible *given Tutte's theorem*. Almost all
graphs are controllable with respect to the all-ones vector (O'Rourke–Touri 2016; for
neighbourhood indicators I have no source). **Not proposed as a child**: Tutte's theorem is not in
the tree (a third-party Lean development claims it, §12; not audited). The elementary shadows are
K-cd (j = 0, T0) and K-w3 (j = 1, W1); j = 2 would follow from Σ w₂² (W1) by the triangular law
(paper, not worked out).

This answers item (ii) of the angle in one direction only: F2 at an anchor forces a *spectral
orthogonality* on the twist, not a two-walk regularity of G. I found no argument that "F2
everywhere" makes G two-walk-linear.

### 5.4 The two-walk-linear face: class AS (paper)

Let G be two-walk (a, b)-linear (w₂ = a·deg + b; round-11 t1). For u ≠ v say **PIN(u, v)** holds
if the only f : V → ℤ with f(u) = f(v) = 0 and (A_G f)(t) = (a − deg u) f(t) for all t ≠ u is f = 0.

**Theorem AS.** If G is two-walk (a, b)-linear, some class (u, v, w) passes the window census of W3
(or of W2), and PIN(x, y) holds for every (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒜, then G is reconstructible.
Paper argument. Let H be hypomorphic to G. H is two-walk (a, b)-linear (T0, W1). By Lemma W, H is
anchored at some (x, y; z) ∈ 𝒜 with f : (G − x, y) → (H − a, b), and deg_H a = deg x. The checked
`rel` with (K, w, e) = (H, a, f) gives z′(t) = [x ~ t] − [a ~ f t] (z′(x) = 0) with
(A z′)(t) = (a − deg x) z′(t) for t ≠ x. The second anchor card gives [a ~ b] = [x ~ y] (Lemma A.4),
i.e. z′(y) = 0. PIN gives z′ = 0, and the checked `iso_of_nbhd` (`…pure_degree_class`) gives G ≅ H. ∎

Remarks.
- The second card is used for one linear equation, z′(y) = 0; no automorphism of G − x − y appears
  in the hypothesis. On the nonsingular part of this face (a − deg x ∉ spec A_G) the solution
  space before pinning is at most a line (Theorem U.1), spanned by the unique switch z₀, and PIN
  says z₀(y) ≠ 0: **choose y inside the switch set of x**. So there F2 is void and only the census
  can fail. This is the reverse of Lemma DI.
- AS does not need the non-extending card isomorphism of US, so it reaches into Zinchenko's
  class E (every card isomorphism extends), which US leaves open (round-11 t1 §7.1), whenever a
  census class with a pinned pair exists. I know no member of either residual in AS; the
  two-walk-linear residual members known in round 11 are in US.
- Computed (`t2s_check_as.py`, exhaustive n ≤ 7): AS contains 5/5, 11/12, 27/28, 42/44 of the
  non-regular two-walk-linear graphs on 4..7 vertices (US: 1, 2, 4, 5). Census form (every H with
  the keys of G passing the census in H is isomorphic to G): no witness, 188 classes. The single
  graph lemma (a two-walk-linear twist satisfies the pinned equation; PIN forces it trivial):
  no witness over 42916 twists, 4560 of them non-trivial. Non-vacuity is weak: no
  non-isomorphic H with the keys of G passes the census even without PIN for n ≤ 7.

## 6. The structured remainder

**Theorem R (paper).** Let G be outside PC⁺ (in particular: in this step's residual and outside
the proposed child). Then for **every** ordered pair (u, v), with F = G − u − v, there is a graph
K_{uv} on V, not isomorphic to G, with keys(K_{uv}) = keys(G) and slice_F(K_{uv}) = slice_F(G), and
one of the following holds.

- **(tw)** K_{uv} ≅ G_g for some (x, y) ∈ P_F(G) and g ∈ Aut(G − x − y) with: g ∉ Stab(N(x))∘Stab(N(y))
  (TL.4; in particular g moves N(x) ∖ y and does not preserve N(y) ∖ x); the level counts hold;
  Δ₀(g) = Δ₁(g) = 0; and G_g has the pointed types of G over F, with the same multiplicities.
- **(rp)** K_{uv} is re-paired: F has at least two copies in G, and the window census fails at
  every pointed class over F at every pointing level (Lemma RP).

Moreover (rp) is excluded when F has one copy, and at the pairs of Lemma DI; and (tw) is
excluded at a pair of a two-walk-linear G where PIN holds on the pointed class (Theorem AS).
For a non-reconstructible G with reconstruction H ≇ G the same graph K_{uv} = H serves every pair,
and then (Tutte, UNVERIFIED) all Δ_j(g) vanish in (tw).

Argument. Definition of PC⁺, the dichotomy of §4, Lemma A, TL.1–2 (keys fix Σ deg² and
Σ deg·w₂), Lemma RP, Lemma DI, Theorem AS. ∎

How this is "strictly more structured" than ¬W3:

| | ¬W3 at a class (u, v, w) | ¬PC⁺ at the pair (u, v) |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | a counting inequality #ℛ ≥ #𝒜 for that class | one graph K with all pointed cards of G over F, pairing them with no pair in common with G; the inequality at **every** class over F |
| F2 | some automorphism in the stabiliser of z moves N(x) and moves degrees | a twist G_g ≇ G that keeps the degree multiset, the two-walk keys, the codegree, Δ₁ and the whole slice |
| quantifier | every class; the two clauses may alternate from class to class | every pair; one witness graph per two-deleted type |
| n ≤ 8 | no non-regular graph known outside W3 | no graph outside PC⁺; no phantom at any pair |

What the theorem does **not** give: any relation between K_{uv} for different pairs. That is
exactly the gap between "every slice is ambiguous" and "the deck is ambiguous", and it is where
the next argument has to work (two slices sharing a vertex, i.e. three cards; Kelly counts of
(n − 3)-subgraphs; the full characteristic polynomial).

A remark the strategist should keep in mind: for the *endgame* a hypothesis ¬C in a residual is
never usable, because a non-reconstructible G is outside every reconstructible class anyway.
Residual predicates are bookkeeping of what the methods so far cannot see. What Theorem R adds
to the endgame is its last sentence: the shape of a reconstruction H relative to each F.

## 10. Where this could break

1. **Nothing here closes the step**, and PC⁺ does not bring it closer in the sense of §6's last
   remark. Its value is: one cheap child that subsumes the pair-census line, and Theorem R.
2. **PC⁺ is a semantic class.** A reviewer may object that "determined by partial deck data" is
   reconstructibility with a smaller deck, and that is what it is. If the strategist wants
   structural hypotheses only, the children are W3cd and AS, and Theorem R is then a statement
   about ¬PC⁺ that no step records.
3. **Proposition SUB is a paper argument read off the checked proofs**, not a checked inclusion.
   It was tested per type for W3 and its variants only. For US the argument uses the slice
   through one card; for AS and W3cd there is no checked proof to read.
4. **"Every graph on n ≤ 8 vertices is in PC⁺ at every pair" is suspicious until the gate has
   seen the class.** Against a logic error: the tracer compiles; the phantom counts are not
   vacuous (6468 weak phantoms at n = 8, removed by the keys; phantoms with the keys from n = 9
   on, found by a second routine that enumerates re-attachments). Not done: evaluating the Lean
   body itself on graphs. The Python predicate compares multisets of (deg, w₂) and slices as
   counters of canonical forms; a fidelity reviewer should compare it with the three hypotheses
   of the body.
5. **TL.3 uses rational functions of λ and an argument at infinity.** The identity was tested
   exactly (integer power sums) for n ≤ 7 and by total walks up to length 2n for n ≤ 6, but the
   paper argument should be re-read by a red team (sign case α = 0).
6. **Corollary TL rests on Tutte 1979**, which I did not read (§12). Nothing proposed for Lean
   depends on it.
7. **Theorem AS**: the bit z′(y) = 0 uses T0 at the anchor (Lemma A.4), so it needs the full
   anchor, not one card. PIN quantifies over all members of 𝒜, as (K) does in W3.
8. **Lemma DI and the 2-coloured remark** are paper only; the remark is not an argument for
   anything in the tree.
9. **The chain PC_k** (§3.3) is a paper remark; C₃ ⊆ PC₃ was not checked against round-12 t1's
   argument line by line.
10. **Membership lists.** No new member of either residual is claimed. Every "in the residual"
    statement is inherited from rounds 11–12.
11. **Attribution.** I found no source for slices and phantoms as defined here, for the transfer
    coefficients of a two-card twist, or for Theorem AS; I searched only for Tutte's theorem and
    for controllability. The Schur-complement step is textbook linear algebra. Related known
    notions (not used): degree-associated reconstruction numbers (Ramachandran; Barrus–West),
    which I did not look up — UNVERIFIED and not relied on.
