## 2. What "outside W3" says, and why the census kind stops

Notation of round-12 `t2.md`: for u ≠ v, S = V ∖ {u, v}, F = G − u − v (the *port*),
α = [u ~ v], δ = deg v − α. Γ = Aut(F); A_u, A_v ≤ Γ the stabilisers of N(u) ∩ S, N(v) ∩ S;
DP ≤ Γ the automorphisms of F that preserve G-degrees. 𝒜, 𝒞, ℛ as in t2 §4.

### 2.1 Proposition N (W3 at a window-unique pair; paper)

Call (u, v) **window-unique** if (v, u) is the only ordered pair (x, y) with G − x − y ≅ F and
deg x ∈ {δ, δ + 1}. (Then deg u ∉ {δ, δ + 1}: otherwise (u, v) itself would qualify.)

**Proposition N.** Let (u, v) be window-unique and w ∈ S. Then
𝒜₃ = {(u, v, z) : z ∈ A_v·w}, ℛ₃ = {(v, u, z) : z ∈ Γ·w ∖ A_u A_v·w}, and W3 holds at (u, v, w) iff

    |Γ·w ∖ A_u A_v·w| < |A_v·w|   and   Γ_{a w} ⊆ A_u ∪ DP for every a ∈ A_v.

Argument. If (x, y) ≈ (u, v) as pointed cards, then G − x − y ≅ F, deg x = deg u and
deg y = δ + [x ~ y], so (y, x) is a window copy and (x, y) = (u, v). The third entries are the
orbit of w under Aut(G − u)_v, which restricts to A_v. A triple (x, y, z) with pointed port
(F; w) and deg x in the window has (x, y) = (v, u) and z ∈ Γ·w; it lies in 𝒞₃ iff
(G − v; u, z) ≈ (G − v; u, z′) for some z′ ∈ A_v·w, i.e. iff z ∈ A_u A_v·w (Aut(G − v)_u
restricts to A_u). Clause (K) is the last condition. ∎

Consequences (paper).
1. Γ = 1 ⇒ W3 at (u, v, w) for every w. So **in a graph outside W3 every window-unique pair has
   a port with a non-trivial automorphism.**
2. w fixed by Γ: W3 ⟺ Γ ⊆ A_u ∪ DP ⟺ Γ = A_u or Γ = DP (a group is not the union of two proper
   subgroups).
3. Γ_w = 1: the kill clause is vacuous and the census reads |Γ| − |A_u A_v| < |A_v|. Both sides
   are unions of left cosets of A_v, so this is Γ = A_u A_v: the factorisation clause of the
   checked GLUE class. A free pin buys nothing over GLUE.
4. **W3 does not contain its pair form W2.** If Γ = {1, g} with g fixed-point-free on S,
   g ∈ DP, g ∉ A_u ∪ A_v, then the pair census holds with ℛ₂ = ∅ and (K) holds through K-dp
   (W2 at (u, v)), but for every w: 𝒜₃ = {(u, v, w)}, ℛ₃ = {(v, u, g w)}, and the triple census
   fails. This is local to the pair; I have no graph whose only usable pair is of this kind
   (round 12 computed W2 ⊆ W3 for n ≤ 8). W2 has the paper argument of W3 without the pin and is
   a cheap child.

### 2.2 Pins never improve the census

Round-12 Lemma O (t2) and Proposition M (t3): a census at k + 1 points implies the census at k
points. So after W3 the census side can only be changed in kind (2.3), not sharpened by points.

### 2.3 The two ceilings of every two-card class

- **Census ceiling.** Let Θ be the set of pointed-card types τ(x, y) of G, m(t) the number of
  ordered pairs of type t (the same in H, L1⁺), and M_G the multigraph on Θ with one edge
  {τ(x, y), τ(y, x)} per unordered pair. Two types are *compatible* if they have the same port
  and the window relation holds (t2 §4, first remark). H has **no** 2-anchor iff M_H and M_G
  have no edge in common. M_H has the degrees m and lives inside the compatibility graph. So a
  census can force an anchor iff the compatibility graph minus the edges of M_G carries no
  multigraph with degrees m. The checkable sufficient form is Hall's: a set 𝒯 ⊆ Θ with
  m(N_Q(𝒯)) < m(𝒯), where N_Q(t) = compatible types that are not M_G-neighbours of t. Lemma W is
  the case |𝒯| = 1 (paper: the swap is injective from the pairs with type in 𝒯 into those with
  type in N_Q(𝒯) when no anchor exists).
- **Kill ceiling.** After an anchor, H ≅ G_g for a port automorphism g. The weakest correct
  clause is K-iso of t2 §3: "G_g hypomorphic to G ⇒ G_g ≅ G". That is the conjecture restricted
  to twists.

So the exact content of the two-card method is: *reconstructible ⟸ (every hypomorph is
2-anchored) ∧ (every hypomorphic twist is trivial)*, and both halves are implied by the
conjecture. A residual left by sufficient conditions for the two halves is therefore not a
structure; it is the set of graphs where the chosen sufficient conditions fail. Concretely,
outside W3 **every** triple (u, v, w) has |ℛ₃| ≥ |𝒜₃| (rogue window copies of the pointed port,
not related to the class by a card isomorphism) or a pointed-stabiliser automorphism that moves
N(x) ∖ {y} and moves degrees. For a graph with a window-unique pair this is Proposition N; for a
graph without one it says that every two-deleted subgraph repeats inside its degree window.
I know no argument that turns either into a group, a scheme or a completion X ⊇ G (round-12
Theorem S gives the reason on the asymmetric face: the swaps into different vertices need
different sets R).

### 2.4 Remark: a rigid unique core (menu item 9; paper, generic)

Let K ⊆ V, |K| = k ≥ 3, R = V ∖ K, such that G[R] has a unique copy among the (n − k)-sets,
every automorphism of G[R] preserves N(z) ∩ R for each z ∈ K, and the traces N(z) ∩ R are
pairwise distinct. Then G is reconstructible: by Kelly's count H has one copy R_H; every
extended card isomorphism F_x (x ∈ K) maps R onto R_H and they agree on traces; the multiset
argument of round-12 Claim U identifies F_x(z) = σ z; pairs inside K are seen in a third card.
For k = 2 the pair uv needs T0, and the class is inside GLUE; for k = 3 it is inside C₃.
Nothing graph-specific for k ≥ 3, so it cannot be decisive. Not proposed.

## 3. Theorem Sw: the difference set of a hypomorphism (paper, all n ≥ 3)

### 3.1 Setting

G, H on V, |V| = n ≥ 3; σ a hypomorphism with card isomorphisms f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx.
H′ := the pull-back of H along σ (w ~′ y ⟺ σw ~_H σy); θ_x := σ⁻¹ ∘ f_x is an isomorphism
G − x → H′ − x, a permutation of V ∖ x. deg, ~ refer to G; deg′, ~′ to H′.

    D = D_σ := { {w, y} : [w ~′ y] ≠ [w ~ y] },      H ≅ H′ = G △ D.

- **A0 (checked T0).** deg′ x = deg x for every x.
- **A4.** D = ∅ ⟺ σ is an isomorphism G → H.

### 3.2 Switchable pairs

**Definition.** A pair {w, y} is **switchable** in G if there are y₁, w₁ ∉ {w, y} with

    deg_{G−w}(y₁) = deg y − 1 + [w ~ y],   deg_{G−y}(w₁) = deg w − 1 + [w ~ y],
    G − w − y₁ ≅ G − y − w₁.

Sw(G) is the set of switchable pairs. (deg_{G−w}(y₁) = deg y₁ − [w ~ y₁].) Reading: G − w − y₁
and G − y − w₁ are two views of the port that the pair {w, y} *would* have if its adjacency
were flipped and nothing else changed at w and y: by the bit law that port has
e(G − w − y) + 1 − 2[w ~ y] edges, so it is never isomorphic to G − w − y.

**Lemma Sw (two cards).** If {w, y} ∈ D_σ then {w, y} ∈ Sw(G), with witnesses y₁ = θ_w⁻¹(y),
w₁ = θ_y⁻¹(w).
Argument. θ_w maps y₁ to y, so it restricts to an isomorphism G − w − y₁ → H′ − w − y, and
deg_{G−w}(y₁) = deg_{H′−w}(y) = deg′ y − [w ~′ y] = deg y − (1 − [w ~ y]) by A0. If y₁ = y this
reads deg y − [w ~ y] = deg y − 1 + [w ~ y], impossible; so y₁ ≠ y, and y₁ ≠ w by domain. The same
for w₁ with θ_y. Both restrictions land in H′ − w − y, so G − w − y₁ ≅ G − y − w₁. ∎
Only two cards and the two degrees are used: the statement holds for any graph H′ on V with
H′ − w ≅ G − w, H′ − y ≅ G − y, deg′ w = deg w, deg′ y = deg y and a flipped pair wy.

**Lemma Sw₃ (third cards; optional sharpening).** If {w, y} ∈ D_σ, then with the same y₁, for
every z ∉ {w, y} there are w_z ≠ y_z in V ∖ z and z′ ∈ V ∖ {w, y₁} with [w_z ~ y_z] ≠ [w ~ y],
deg_{G−z}(w_z) ∈ {deg w − 1, deg w}, deg_{G−z}(y_z) ∈ {deg y − 1, deg y},
deg_{G−w}(z′) ∈ {deg z − 1, deg z} and G − z − w_z − y_z ≅ G − w − y₁ − z′.
Argument. w_z = θ_z⁻¹ w, y_z = θ_z⁻¹ y, z′ = θ_w⁻¹ z; both sides are views of H′ − w − y − z. ∎
Sw₃(G) ⊆ Sw(G) is the set of pairs with such witnesses.

### 3.3 Balance and Kelly counts

**Lemma B (degree balance).** For every v and k:
#{y : {v, y} ∈ D, v ~ y, deg y = k} = #{y : {v, y} ∈ D, v ≁ y, deg y = k}.
Argument. θ_v preserves card degrees, and deg′ = deg, so for every j the number of y ≠ v with
deg y − [v ~ y] = j equals the number with deg y − [v ~′ y] = j. The level-count lemma (checked
`level_bal`, applied to J = n − deg) gives: for every k, v has as many neighbours of degree k
in G as in H′. Subtract the common neighbours. ∎ (This is the row "Δ·1_{D_k} = 0" of the table
in `…connected_dominating_or_diam2/t3.md` §1; nothing new.)

**Lemma Ke (Kelly counts).** For every k < n and every graph F on k vertices, G △ D and G have
the same number of k-sets inducing F. (Checked `kelly_induced_count` for G, H; transport along
σ.) For k = 3 and n ≥ 4, together with the card isomorphisms: the number of triangles through
each vertex, and of independent triples through each vertex, agree (K0 of `debe_codegree.md`).

**Theorem Sw.** For every hypomorphism σ : G → H, the set D = D_σ satisfies D ⊆ Sw(G) ∩ Sw₃(G),
Lemma B and Lemma Ke, and G △ D ≅ H. If D = ∅ then σ is an isomorphism.

## 4. The classes and the dichotomy

Call a set D of pairs **degree-balanced** (in G) if it satisfies the equation of Lemma B.

- **NS**: no nonempty degree-balanced D ⊆ Sw(G).
- **BS₀**: every degree-balanced D ⊆ Sw(G) has G △ D ≅ G.
- **BS_m** (m fixed, 3 or 4): every degree-balanced D ⊆ Sw(G) such that G △ D and G have the
  same number of induced copies of every graph on k vertices, for all k ≤ m with k < n, has
  G △ D ≅ G.
- **BS_m•** (rooted variant): the same with the counts taken per vertex, i.e. for every v the
  number of k-sets through v inducing F agrees (card-tied: the count in G minus the count in
  G − v). In the scripts only the rooted counts of K₃ and 3K₁ are used ("keys", "BSK").
- **X_m(G)** := ¬ BS_m: G has an **m-mate**, a degree-balanced D ⊆ Sw(G) with the Kelly counts up
  to m and G △ D ≇ G. X(G) := ¬ NS.

NS ⊆ BS₀ ⊆ BS₃ ⊆ BS₄ ⊆ … and BS_m ⊆ BS_m• (`NS_le_NSiso`, `NSiso_le_BSK` in the Scratch file are
the pattern). Variant with the same argument: Sw₃ in place of Sw.

**Theorem BS (paper).** Every graph in BS_m (in particular in NS, BS₀) is reconstructible. If
G ∈ NS, every hypomorphism from G to any H is an isomorphism.
Argument. Let σ : G → H be a hypomorphism, D = D_σ. By Theorem Sw, D ⊆ Sw(G), D is
degree-balanced and G △ D has the Kelly counts of G for every k < n, in particular for k ≤ m.
So G ≅ G △ D ≅ H. In NS, D = ∅ and σ is an isomorphism (A4). ∎

**Dichotomy (all n ≥ 3).** For every graph G and every fixed m: G ∈ BS_m, or G has an m-mate.
If G is not reconstructible, G has an m-mate for every m ≤ n − 1, and one of them is hypomorphic
to G; conversely the rung m = n − 1 is the conjecture for G (the Kelly counts at k = n − 1 are
the deck). So the rungs are honest classes only for fixed small m, and the structure X_m is a
necessary condition for a counterexample that is **checkable on G alone**.

What an m-mate is, concretely: an edge-2-coloured graph D on V (red = edges of G, blue =
non-edges of G) in which every vertex has, towards each degree class of G, as many red as blue
neighbours — so D is a union of closed trails alternating red and blue, with at least 4
vertices —, every pair of D carries a two-card port coincidence with a degree shift (3.2), and
the switched graph is a different graph with the same small-subgraph statistics.

## 5. The ceiling of the aligned route: Proposition TW and pseudosimilarity

**TW**: any two vertices of G with isomorphic cards are twins (N(a) ∖ b = N(b) ∖ a).

**Proposition TW (paper).**
1. If G − a ≅ G − b and a, b are not twins, then σ = (a b) is a hypomorphism G → G that is not
   an automorphism, and D_σ = {{a, x}, {b, x} : x ∈ (N(a) △ N(b)) ∖ {a, b}} is a nonempty
   degree-balanced subset of Sw(G). Hence **NS ⊆ TW**. (Here G △ D_σ ≅ G, so this D is not a mate.)
2. Let G be reconstructible. Then every hypomorphism from G is an isomorphism iff G has TW.
Argument. 1: (a b)(G − a) = (a b)G − b and G − a ≅ G − b, so the cards match; Theorem Sw applies
with H = G. 2 (⇐): for a hypomorphism σ : G → H take an isomorphism φ : G → H; π = φ⁻¹σ
preserves card types, hence permutes classes of mutual twins, hence is an automorphism; σ = φπ.
(⇒) is 1. ∎

Reading.
- The aligned route proves "σ itself is an isomorphism", so NS can never contain a graph with
  two removal-similar non-twin vertices. Such vertices are similar (an automorphism maps a to
  b) or **pseudosimilar** (Harary–Palmer 1966; KSS 1981). This is where the menu's
  pseudosimilarity literature enters, and it enters as the exact boundary, not as a tool.
- Above NS the rungs BS_m tolerate isomorphic switches, so they can contain non-TW graphs:
  computed, BS₀ has 148, 970 graphs at n = 6, 7 against TW = 96, 688.
- Candidate lemma **killed by search**: "pairwise non-isomorphic cards ⇒ NS" holds for n ≤ 7
  (8 and 152 graphs) and fails at n = 8 for 16 of 3660 graphs (`GhoWEc` is one;
  `t1s_claim_cd8.py`). In the four n = 7 graphs of TW ∖ NS every balanced set is a single
  alternating 4-cycle whose switch shares **no** aligned card with G (`t1s_probe_exceptions.py`);
  Sw₃ removes three of the four, the triangle count removes all four.
- For a pseudosimilar pair the Godsil–Kocay description (G = X minus part of an orbit of an
  automorphism θ of X; UNVERIFIED) is the natural structure. I did not find a way to use it:
  the hypomorph H need not embed in any graph related to X.

## 6. What a member of the residual must look like (paper unless marked)

Let G be in this step's class (connected, co-connected, diameter 2 in G and Gᶜ for this twin,
outside PURE, GLUE, LHP, NARROW, T⁺, T, US, W3). Then:

1. G is not regular (regular ⇒ PURE; round-12 t2 §8).
2. **Bollobás' property fails at every pair** (¬GLUE): G − u − v has a second copy, or some
   automorphism of G − u − v is not in A_v·A_u.
3. **Every window-unique pair has a port with symmetry** (Proposition N.1), and more precisely
   for every w the subgroup condition of Proposition N fails: |Γ·w ∖ A_u A_v·w| ≥ |A_v·w|, or
   some g ∈ Γ fixing a point of A_v·w lies outside A_u ∪ DP. In particular Γ ≠ A_u and, when
   some vertex of S is fixed by Γ, Γ ≠ DP.
4. Every pair (u, v) with deg u ∉ {δ, δ + 1} is window-unique (then item 3 applies) or its port
   has a second window copy (x, y) ≠ (v, u); pairs with deg u ∉ {δ, δ + 1} exist in every
   non-regular graph (round-12 t3 §5.5: clause (i) fails everywhere only for regular graphs).
5. If G is also outside BS_m: G has an m-mate (§4). If G is not reconstructible: for every
   hypomorphism σ the set D_σ is such a mate, for every m ≤ n − 1.
6. If G is outside NS: G has two non-twin vertices with isomorphic cards, or (rare for n ≤ 8:
   4 and 34 graphs) a "phantom" degree-balanced set of switchable pairs with TW intact.

Items 2–4 are the census-kind description and are not a structure (§2.3). Item 5 is the
structure I propose to put into the tree. No graph satisfying 1–4 is known, so nothing can be
said from examples about how 5 interacts with them; the mechanisms are independent (a census
of pointed types against a labelled switch), and on the graphs computed they fail on opposite
faces (§7).

## 8. What breaks for tournaments, 3-uniform hypergraphs, infinite graphs

| Step | Tournaments | Graph-specific ingredient |
|---|---|---|
| pull-back, θ_x, A4 | hold verbatim | none |
| A0 (deg′ = deg) | fails as an argument | T0: deg x = e(G) − e(G − x); every tournament card has C(n − 1, 2) arcs |
| Lemma Sw (y₁ ≠ y) | **fails**: a reversed arc wy does not change any count seen in the cards of w and y, so y₁ = y is possible and every pair is "switchable" | the bit law: a flipped pair changes the degree of y inside the card of w |
| Lemma B | fails with A0 | T0 |
| Lemma Ke | holds (Kelly's lemma is generic) | none |
| Proposition TW | holds verbatim | none |

So on tournaments the class NS is empty or void (Sw is everything, and any two scores can be
"balanced"), and the argument says nothing about Stockmeyer's pairs (J. Graph Theory 1 (1977);
UNVERIFIED). 3-uniform hypergraphs: the pair {w, y} carries a codegree, not a bit; "D = ∅" (all
codegrees agree) does not make σ an isomorphism. Infinite graphs: card degrees and edge counts
are infinite cardinals and Lemma Sw's inequality y₁ ≠ y is lost.

## 10. Where this could break

1. **Nothing here closes the step,** and I cannot show that BS_m meets the residual: no member
   of the residual is known. What is argued is that BS_m is reconstructible by a mechanism
   independent of every carved class, and what is computed is that it takes the graphs on which
   the twist clauses were the difficulty.
2. **The rungs BS_m have an isomorphism in the conclusion.** They are properties of G alone,
   but they are not "structural" classes; NS is. With m = n − 1 the rung is the conjecture. The
   honest reading of the dichotomy is "a counterexample has a labelled mate with these
   properties", not "the residual has a classification".
3. **A class that contains every graph on ≤ 7 vertices** (BS₃, BS₄) is suspicious until the gate
   has seen it. Against a logic error: the labelled tests of §7 range over all graphs H on the
   vertex set and are not vacuous (308 aligned pairs with H ≠ G at n = 6); the classes are *not*
   everything at n = 8 (132 graphs outside BS₃, 4 outside BS₄). For n ≤ m + 1 the rung BS_m is
   the set of reconstructible graphs by definition (the filter at k = n − 1 is the deck), so
   "BS₄ contains every graph on ≤ 5 vertices" carries no information; the rows n = 6, 7, 8 do,
   and even there the unlabelled 4-vertex counts alone determine most graphs (baseline in §7).
4. **The strongly regular face is out of reach of the rungs up to m = 3** (§7, paper remark),
   and BS₄ is undecided there. Bounded Kelly filters can only separate mates whose small
   subgraph statistics differ; graphs with a non-isomorphic mate that agrees on all m-vertex
   counts (m fixed) exist for the unrestricted problem, and nothing here excludes them inside
   Sw. On such graphs the reduction contributes nothing; it relies on US and W3.
5. **Proposition N assumes window-uniqueness.** For pairs with window copies I give only the
   census statement, no group form. Consequence N.4 is local to a pair; no graph is known whose
   membership it decides.
6. **The census ceiling of §2.3 is stated, not worked out** (the symmetric version needs a
   Tutte-type condition; Hall's condition is the directed relaxation). It is used only to say
   what a census can at best do.
7. **Open questions I could not settle** (each would sharpen X): (a) can D_σ be a single
   alternating 4-cycle for a non-isomorphic hypomorph (a 2-switch that is isomorphism-preserving
   in every G − w with w outside it)? (b) is the second colour-refinement round card-tied, i.e.
   is D balanced towards the classes of "multiset of neighbour degrees"? (c) is there an
   all-n reason why rigid decks have small Sw?
8. **Transcriptions.** The six former members are taken from round-12 t2 §7; W3-membership
   statements are round 12's. Sw₃ as coded lets the two unknown H-bits at z vary independently;
   that is weaker than Lemma Sw₃ and still necessary.
9. **Attribution.** Aligned pairs and Lemma B are in earlier sketches of this repository; the
   neighbour-degree statement is classical. For Lemma Sw, Theorem Sw, Proposition TW and
   Proposition N I found no source; they are elementary and I did not search beyond the menu.
