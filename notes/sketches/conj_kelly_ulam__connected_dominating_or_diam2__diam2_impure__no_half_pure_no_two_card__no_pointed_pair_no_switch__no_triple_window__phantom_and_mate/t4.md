## 1. Notation

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3. A **partial isomorphism** of G is an isomorphism between two induced
subgraphs. Its **level** is the number of deleted vertices: level 1 = card isomorphism
G − u → G − v, level 2 = isomorphism G − u − v → G − x − y. It is **explained** if it is the
restriction of an automorphism of G.

For u ≠ v: S = V ∖ {u, v}, F = G[S], N_u = N(u) ∩ S, N_v = N(v) ∩ S, α = [u ~ v],
A_u = Stab_{Aut(F)}(N_u), A_v = Stab_{Aut(F)}(N_v). For g ∈ Aut(F), G_g is G with u re-attached to
g⁻¹N_u (r13-t2 §1). Inputs used as given: T0 (`deg_T0`, checked), Lemma A (an anchored K with
e(K) = e(G) is some G_g; inside the checked `endgame`), Theorem R and Lemma RP (r13-t2, paper),
Theorem S (r12-t3, paper).

u, v are **removal-similar** if G − u ≅ G − v, **similar** if an automorphism maps u to v,
**pseudosimilar** if removal-similar and not similar (Harary–Palmer 1966; definition as in Kocay
1984a, Definition 2.1, read).

## 3. The residual in this language: Lemma P

Theorem R (r13-t2): outside PC⁺ every ordered pair (u, v) has a phantom K_{uv}, which is
**twisted** (K ≅ G_g for a copy (x, y) of F = G − u − v and g ∈ Aut(G − x − y) ∖ A_x A_y) or
**re-paired** (F has at least two copies).

Read without automorphisms this is the shadow
**(H\*)**: for every pair {u, v}, G − u − v has a second copy or Aut(G − u − v) ≠ A_u A_v.
(H\*) does not see whether the second copy is explained. The next lemma does.

**Lemma P (paper).** Let K be a re-paired weak phantom of G at F = G − u − v, and suppose all
copies of F in G are in one Aut(G)-orbit of unordered pairs (q copies). Then deg u = deg v, the
pointed cards (G − u, v) and (G − v, u) are not isomorphic, q is even, and every pair {a, b} of K
over F has [a ~ b] = [u ~ v].

Argument. The pointed types over F in G are t₁ = type(G − u, v) and t₂ = type(G − v, u), because
an automorphism carries pointed cards to pointed cards. K has the same types with the same
multiplicities. Take a pair {a, b} of K over F. If t₁ = t₂, or if the two orientations of {a, b}
have different types, K is anchored at (u, v): not re-paired. So t₁ ≠ t₂ and every pair of K is
of kind t₁t₁ or t₂t₂. G has q ordered pairs of each type, so K has q/2 pairs of each kind: q is
even and both kinds occur. For a t₁t₁ pair: K − a ≅ K − b gives deg_K a = deg_K b, and
= e(K) − e(G − u) = deg u (the degree multiset gives e(K) = e(G)); the pointed vertex gives
deg_K b − [a ~ b] = deg v − α. So deg u − deg v = [a ~ b] − α. A t₂t₂ pair {a′, b′} gives
deg v − deg u = [a′ ~ b′] − α. Adding: [a ~ b] + [a′ ~ b′] = 2α, so both bits equal α and
deg u = deg v. ∎

**Corollary P.** Let G be outside PC⁺ and deg u ≠ deg v. Then G has an **unexplained level-2
partial isomorphism at the type of (u, v)**: either a copy {x, y} of G − u − v outside the
Aut(G)-orbit of {u, v} ("pseudosimilar pairs"), or a copy (x, y) and an automorphism g of
G − x − y with g ∉ A_x A_y (not a product of an automorphism that extends over x and one that
extends over y; in particular not the restriction of an automorphism of G fixing x and y).

This is the precise sense in which the residual is "pervasively pseudosimilar": at level 2, at
every pair of unequal degrees. At pairs of equal degree Lemma P leaves a third kind (all copies
explained, q even, no automorphism of G exchanges u and v through pointed cards); I have no
example of it and no argument against it.

Graph-specific input: T0 only (the two degree equations). For tournaments the two equations
become "scores differ by one", and the conclusion changes; see §8.

## 4. Completion theorems

### 4.1 Theorem C: one card isomorphism (Godsil–Kocay form, with the exact size)

Let p : G − u → G − v be an isomorphism (u = v allowed: a card automorphism). As a partial
permutation of V it has one open orbit (Kocay 1984a Theorem 3.1, read): x₀ = v, x_{i+1} = p(x_i),
ending at x_k = u (k = 0 iff u = v). Put C = V ∖ {x₀, …, x_k} (the closed part; p permutes C and
is an automorphism of G[C]), ν = N(v) ∩ C, and a(δ) = [x₀ ~ x_δ] for 1 ≤ δ ≤ k. Since p is an
isomorphism off u: [x_i ~ x_j] = a(j − i) for 0 ≤ i < j ≤ k, and N(x_j) ∩ C = p^j ν.

**Theorem C (paper).** For an integer M ≥ k + 1 the following are equivalent.
- (a) There are a graph X ⊇ G (G induced) with |V(X) ∖ V| = M − k − 1 and θ ∈ Aut(X) extending p
  such that {x₀, …, x_k} ∪ (V(X) ∖ V) is one θ-orbit.
- (b) p^M ν = ν, and a(δ) = a(M − δ) whenever 1 ≤ δ ≤ k and 1 ≤ M − δ ≤ k.

Every pair (X′, θ′) with G induced in X′ and θ′ ⊇ p contains such an X (the θ′-orbit of u). So
the **completion number** of p is c(p) = M_min − k − 1, M_min the least M ≥ k + 1 with (b).
c(p) = 0 iff p ∪ {u ↦ v} ∈ Aut(G). With m = |⟨p⟩-orbit of ν|: M_min ≤ m⌈(2k + 1)/m⌉, so
c(p) ≤ k + m. For u = v: c(p) = m − 1.

Argument. (b) ⇒ (a): X has vertex set C ⊔ {x_c : c ∈ ℤ_M}; G[C] as given; N(x_c) ∩ C = p^c ν
(well defined by p^M ν = ν); x_c ~ x_{c′} iff ā(c′ − c) = 1, where ā is the symmetric extension of
a to ℤ_M ∖ 0 by ā(δ) = ā(−δ) = a(δ) for 1 ≤ δ ≤ k (consistent by the second half of (b)) and
ā = 0 elsewhere. θ = p on C and x_c ↦ x_{c+1}. G is induced on C ∪ {x₀, …, x_k}.
(a) ⇒ (b) and the minimality: θ′ maps V ∖ u onto V ∖ v, so θ′u is v or lies outside V, and the
orbit of u leaves V until it returns at v; invariance of adjacency under θ′ along that orbit is
(b) with M the orbit length. The bound: any multiple M ≥ 2k + 1 of m satisfies (b). ∎

Theorem S of r12-t3 is the case c(p) = 1. Kocay's remark that a pseudosimilar pair needs
|C| ≥ 6 fits Lemma REF in §8: with C = ∅ the pair is similar.

### 4.2 Theorem A: an anchored hypomorph is a sibling of G in a cyclic completion

Let u ≠ v and g ∈ Aut(F). Let M_u, M_v be the sizes of the ⟨g⟩-orbits of the sets N_u, N_v.

**Theorem A (paper).** Define X_g on S ⊔ {u_c : c ∈ ℤ_{M_u}} ⊔ {v_c : c ∈ ℤ_{M_v}}:
X_g[S] = F; N(u_c) ∩ S = g^c N_u; N(v_c) ∩ S = g^c N_v; u_c ~ v_{c′} iff α; no edges among the
u_c, none among the v_c. Then
1. θ := g on S, u_c ↦ u_{c+1}, v_c ↦ v_{c+1} is an automorphism of X_g;
2. X_g[S ∪ {u₀, v₀}] = G, and X_g[S ∪ {u_c, v_{c′}}] ≅ G_{g^{c′−c}} for all integers c, c′
   (indices read modulo M_u, M_v; the right side does not depend on the representatives up to
   isomorphism, because g^{M_v} ∈ A_v);
3. X_g has n + M_u + M_v − 2 vertices; any graph X′ ⊇ G (induced) with an automorphism extending
   g has at least n + max(M_u, M_v) − 2 vertices, and at least n + M_u + M_v − 2 unless
   N_v ∈ ⟨g⟩N_u.

So every graph K anchored to G at (u, v) with e(K) = e(G) (Lemma A), in particular every
twisted phantom of Theorem R and every hypomorph that a pair census anchors, satisfies:
**G = X_g − (all u_c, v_c except u₀, v₀) and K ≅ X_g − (all except u₋₁, v₀).** If M_u = M_v = 2
(for example g an involution moving both traces): X_g has n + 2 vertices, θ exchanges u₀ ↔ u₁ and v₀ ↔ v₁,
G = X_g − u₁ − v₁ and K ≅ X_g − u₀ − v₁.

Argument. 1: s ~ u_c ⟺ s ∈ g^c N_u ⟺ gs ∈ g^{c+1} N_u; the rule between the two orbits is
constant. 2: apply θ^{−c′}. 3: an automorphism θ′ extending g preserves S, hence permutes the
rest; the images of u have the M_u distinct traces g^c N_u on S. ∎

What Theorem A gives and what it does not. It is a bounded symmetric perturbation exactly when
the twist has small order on the two traces. But the group is ⟨θ⟩, cyclic of order |g|, and its
orbits on S are those of g: **not** a group that is transitive on a large set. Different pairs
give different X_g; nothing relates them (the gap already named in r13-t2 §6).

### 4.3 Proposition V: without a bound the structure theorem is vacuous

By Hrushovski's theorem (§2), **every** finite graph G is an induced subgraph of a finite graph
X in which every partial isomorphism of G, of every level, is the restriction of an automorphism
of X. Kocay's Theorem 3.3 is the level-1 case with an infinite Y. So "G = X − D and Aut(X)
induces all card isomorphisms, all isomorphisms between two-deleted subgraphs and all their
automorphisms" holds for every graph, reconstructible or not, in the residual or not. A structure
theorem for the residual has content only through a bound on |D| or on the shape of D. The
reformulation it yields ("in such an X, two n-sets whose (n − 1)-subsets are orbit-equivalent in
pairs lie in one orbit") is the conjecture itself.

## 5. The bounded structure theorem fails for (H\*): one family

### 5.1 The family

Let T_m (m ≥ 7) be the path 1 – 2 – … – m with one extra leaf ℓ attached to vertex 3, and
G_m := T_m[K̄₃]: every vertex p of T_m is replaced by an independent triple (the **fibre** of p),
and two vertices in different fibres are adjacent iff their fibres are adjacent in T_m.
n = 3(m + 1).

Facts about B := T_m (paper).
- B is asymmetric: 3 is the only vertex of degree 3 and its three branches are paths with 1, 2
  and m − 3 ≥ 4 vertices.
- B is twin-free (no two vertices with the same open neighbourhood): two such vertices with a
  common neighbourhood of size ≥ 2 give a 4-cycle; with size 1 they are two leaves at one
  vertex, and the leaves 1, ℓ, m hang at 2, 3, m − 1.
- B has vertices of degrees 1, 2, 3.

Consequence (paper): in G_m, and in G_m minus any one or two vertices, two vertices are false
twins iff they lie in one fibre (their neighbourhoods are unions of fibres or of fibres minus
deleted points, and they agree iff the two base vertices have the same neighbourhood in B). So
every isomorphism between such graphs maps twin classes to twin classes, induces an automorphism
of B that respects class sizes, hence the identity on B: **it preserves every fibre setwise.**

### 5.2 What the family has and what it lacks (paper, all m ≥ 7)

1. **(H\*) holds at every pair, with at least three copies.** For u, v in different fibres:
   G − u − v ≅ G − u′ − v for the two twins u′ of u. For u, u′ in one fibre with third twin u″:
   G − u − u′ ≅ G − u − u″ ≅ G − u′ − u″. Also |Aut(G − u − v)| ≥ 4·6^{m−1}.
2. **Every copy is explained.** By the fibre-preservation fact a copy of G − u − v deletes
   points in the same fibres, so it is an Aut(G)-image. There is no pseudosimilar pair of pairs
   and no pseudosimilar pair of vertices.
3. **Aut(G) = S₃^{m+1}**, of order 6^{n/3}; every orbit is a fibre, of size 3.
4. **No one-vertex completion has a large orbit.** Let f : G − x → G − x′ be an R-swap (Theorem S):
   f(N(x)) = R ∖ x′ and (x ∈ R ⟺ x′ ∈ R). x and x′ lie in one fibre, N(x) = N(x′) is a union of
   fibres, f preserves it, so R ∖ x′ = N(x′). Hence R = N(x′) (r is a fourth twin; the orbit of r
   in X_R is the fibre plus r, size 4) or R = N(x′) ∪ {x′} with x = x′ (orbit {r, x′}, size 2;
   a second target y′ ≠ x′ would need N(y′) = N(x′) ∪ {x′} ∖ {y′}, impossible because N(y′) is a
   union of fibres and contains x′ without its twins). **The largest orbit of r over all R is 4
   of n + 1.**
5. **No phantom at any pair** (so G_m ∈ PC ⊆ PC⁺ at every pair). Let K be a weak phantom at
   (u, v). All copies are explained (item 2), so Lemma P applies: a re-paired K needs an even number q
   of copies, deg u = deg v and (G − u, v) ≉ (G − v, u). For u, v in different fibres q = 9; for
   u, u′ in one fibre (G − u, u′) ≈ (G − u′, u) through the transposition. So K is not re-paired:
   it is anchored, K ≅ G_g by Lemma A with g ∈ Aut(G − x − y), and g
   preserves every fibre, hence N(x) ∖ y. So G_g = G and K ≅ G: no phantom.

So: pervasive repetition of two-deleted subgraphs, with a group of exponential order, is
compatible with bounded orbits, no completion beyond a twin, and **membership in PC at every
pair**. The statement "(H\*) ⇒ G is a bounded perturbation of a graph whose group is transitive
on a large set" is defeated by G_m for the perturbations I can control on paper (zero vertices:
item 3; one vertex: item 4). I did not treat perturbations by a bounded number c ≥ 2 of vertices
or edges; I expect the same and did not argue it.

Honest scope. G_m is not in the ambient class (its diameter is m − 1). The statement it defeats
does not mention the ambient class. The same argument works for B[K̄₃] with any asymmetric
twin-free B that has two vertices of different degrees; a base B of diameter 2 with a complement
of diameter 2 puts the graph into the ambient class. I did not construct such a B on paper.

Weaker structure the family still has: a non-trivial module at every vertex. All its symmetry
is modular. That is the structure a correct theorem must split off first (§9).

### 5.3 Proposition CR: the step "per-vertex R_u ⇒ common R"

**Proposition CR (paper).**
1. In G_m every vertex u is the target of an R_u-swap with R_u = N(u) (take x = a twin u′ of u
   and f = the identity on V ∖ {u, u′} extended by u ↦ u′). The sets R_u take m + 1 different
   values, and no set R has swaps into more than 3 vertices. So the passage from "for every u
   some R_u" to "one R" **cannot be argued without a further hypothesis**.
2. One swap into u determines R ∖ u (r12-t3 §6.3). So a common R exists iff the sets
   f_u(N(x_u)) agree, which by Theorem S is the conclusion itself: the step has no slack.
3. The step **is** automatic when R is readable from the cards by an isomorphism-invariant rule
   and every card isomorphism must respect the rule. §6 is the case where this happens; §7 is the
   case where R is readable and the card isomorphisms still do not respect it.

## 6. Where the structure theorem is true: canonical completions

### 6.1 The principle

If Γ is d-regular, then N_Γ(y) is the set of vertices of degree d − 1 of Γ − y. So every
isomorphism Γ − y → Γ′ − y′ between cards of d-regular graphs extends by y ↦ y′ (the classical
argument for regular graphs). Call a perturbation G of a regular Γ **canonical at y** if
G − y = Γ − y. That happens iff every changed pair contains y. Two vertices with this property
exist iff the perturbation is a single pair: G = Γ − e or G = Γ + e.

### 6.2 Theorem K₁ (Kocay 1984b, Lemma 1.5 and p. 318; paper argument written out)

**Theorem K₁⁻.** Let G have two non-adjacent vertices u, v of degree k − 1 and all other degrees
k. Put Γ = G + uv (k-regular). Let H be hypomorphic to G. Then there are w ∈ N_Γ(u) and
θ ∈ Aut(Γ) with **H ≅ Γ − uw and θ(v) = w**. Consequently G ≅ H iff some automorphism of Γ maps
the edge uv onto the edge uw.

Argument. Let σ, f be the hypomorphism, a = σu, b = σv. By T0 the vertices of degree k − 1 of H
are a and b. G − u has exactly k vertices of degree k − 1 (N_G(u) and v) and none of smaller
degree. If a ~ b in H, then H − a has only k − 2 vertices of degree k − 1; so a ≁ b, and
Γ_H := H + ab is k-regular. G − u = Γ − u and H − a = Γ_H − a, so f_u extends to an isomorphism
E : Γ → Γ_H with E(u) = a. In the same way f_v extends to E′ : Γ → Γ_H with E′(v) = b. Put
w := E⁻¹(b) ∈ N_Γ(u) and θ := E⁻¹E′ ∈ Aut(Γ). Then E⁻¹ maps H = Γ_H − ab onto Γ − uw, and
θ(v) = w. The last sentence: an isomorphism Γ − uv → Γ − uw maps the two vertices of degree
k − 1 to the two vertices of degree k − 1, hence is an automorphism of Γ. ∎

**Theorem K₁⁺** (same argument). Let G have two adjacent vertices u, v of degree k + 1 and all
other degrees k, Γ = G − uv. Then H ≅ Γ + uw for a vertex w ∉ N_Γ[u] and some θ ∈ Aut(Γ) has
θ(v) = w. (G − u has no vertex of degree k + 1, so a ~ b in H.)

So for a regular graph ± one edge the structure theorem holds in the strongest form: **the mate
is the same regular graph perturbed at an edge through the same vertex, and the far ends are
similar in Γ.** A non-reconstructible G of this shape needs: Γ has an automorphism moving v to
another neighbour w of u, no automorphism of Γ maps the edge uv onto uw, and Γ − uv, Γ − uw are
hypomorphic. This is "pseudosimilar edges at a vertex with similar far ends".

The reconstruction part is open: Kocay settles k = 3 and announces part of k = 4. Later
literature not searched beyond one query; **UNVERIFIED** whether k ≥ 5 is known.

### 6.3 The class KS± (optional child; carving)

**KS⁻**: G as in K₁⁻, and for every θ ∈ Aut(Γ) with θ(v) ∈ N_G(u) some φ ∈ Aut(Γ) maps {u, v} onto
{u, θv}. **KS⁺**: G as in K₁⁺, and for every θ ∈ Aut(Γ) with θv ≠ u, θv ∉ N_G(u) the same.
Paper argument: Theorem K₁ gives w and θ; if w = v then H ≅ G; otherwise the clause gives φ and
G ≅ H. Contents (paper): Γ asymmetric (clause vacuous); for KS⁻, Γ edge-transitive; Γ with Aut(Γ)_u
transitive on N_Γ(u) ∩ Aut(Γ)·v. Kocay's Theorems 2.3 and 3.1 are instances of the last step in
which φ is a power of the one automorphism θ.

Honest reading. It is a structural class (it quantifies over automorphisms of one graph, not over
graphs K or sets D). It uses only T0 and regular extension, so its tournament analogue holds as
well (regular tournament with one arc reversed, score sequence in place of degrees, given that
the score sequence is deck data): it is a carving class. I do not know whether it contains a
graph of the residual, nor whether it is contained in PC⁺ or BS₄. Not a recommendation.

### 6.4 The frontier of the principle

- Perturbation = a star at z with |N Δ N_Γ(z)| ≥ 2 (an edge moved, or two edges added or
  removed at z): only the card of z is a card of Γ. One gets H ≅ Γ with z re-attached, which is
  1-anchoring and nothing more. For y ≠ z the card G − y is a re-attachment of Γ − y and its
  regular completion is not readable.
- Perturbation = a deleted vertex: §7.
- Other invariant rules than "degree d − 1": any class of graphs whose degree values pairwise
  differ by at least 2 has extending card isomorphisms (Kocay p. 318 for two degrees). I did not
  work out the ± edge version for it.

## 7. Regular minus a vertex: canonical R, non-canonical roles

Let G have degree sequence ((d − 1)^d, d^{n−d}), R its set of vertices of degree d − 1 and
X = G + r with N(r) = R (d-regular). A hypomorph H has the same shape: H = Y − r′, R_H, Y
d-regular. R is canonical. Theorem S with this R: r lies in an Aut(X)-orbit of size m iff G has
R-swaps into m − 1 vertices, and now no quantifier over R is left.

### 7.1 Theorem V (paper)

For x ∈ V put R_x = R ∖ x, N_x = N(x), D₂ = R_x ∩ N_x, D₁ = R_x Δ N_x. In G − x the vertices of
D₂ have degree d − 2, those of D₁ degree d − 1, the others d. Let f : G − x → H − y be a card
isomorphism (y = σx). It maps D₁, D₂ to the corresponding sets of H. Call f **straight** if
f(R_x) = R_H ∖ y, **swapped** if f(N_x) = R_H ∖ y, **mixed** otherwise.

**Theorem V.**
1. If some card isomorphism is straight, then G ≅ H.
2. If some card isomorphism is swapped at x, then Y ≅ X and H ≅ X − x. So G ≅ H iff G has an
   R-swap into x.
3. Hence a non-reconstructible G of this shape has a mate of one of two kinds:
   **(A)** H ≅ X − s with s ∉ Aut(X)·r (two hypomorphic, non-isomorphic cards of the same regular
   graph); **(B)** Y ≇ X, and then every card isomorphism of every hypomorphism is mixed.
4. If X is vertex-transitive, (A) is impossible: G = X − r is reconstructible iff no d-regular
   Y ≇ X has a card hypomorphic to G.
5. In case (B), in the labelling f, Y is X with a set T ⊆ D₁ switched between r and x:
   |T ∩ R_x| = |T ∩ N_x| ≥ 1, T ≠ D₁, the vertices of T ∩ R_x move from r to x and those of
   T ∩ N_x from x to r.

Argument. 1: f ∪ {r ↦ r′} is an isomorphism X − x → Y − y; X, Y are regular, so it extends by
x ↦ y, and its restriction to X − r is an isomorphism G → H. 2: f ∪ {x ↦ r′} is an isomorphism
G = X − r → Y − y; it extends by r ↦ y to Ψ : X → Y with Ψ(x) = r′, so H = Y − r′ ≅ X − x; an
isomorphism X − x → X − r extends to an automorphism, and Theorem S translates. 3: if Y ≅ X
through Φ then H ≅ X − Φ⁻¹(r′). 4: all cards of X are isomorphic. 5: both X and Y complete
G − x to a d-regular graph by two vertices with the same adjacency bit ([x ∈ R] = [y ∈ R_H] by
T0); both must be adjacent to D₂, and they split D₁ into two halves. ∎

### 7.2 Two cases with an argument

**Proposition NT (near-twin; paper).** If some x has |R_x Δ N_x| ≤ 2 and G has an R-swap into x,
then G is reconstructible. (|D₁| ≤ 2 leaves only straight and swapped; Theorem V.1–2.) Example:
powers of cycles minus a vertex, x a neighbour of r along the cycle. Carving; needs only T0.

**Proposition TG (tight regular action; paper, conditional).** Let Γ = Aut(X) be regular on
V ∪ {r}, and assume **tightness up to level 3**: every isomorphism between subgraphs X − T,
X − T′ with |T| = |T′| ≤ 3 is induced by Γ. If 0 < d < n, then G = X − r is reconstructible.
Argument. Identify V ∪ {r} with the group, r = 1. The pointed class of (x, y) is
{(x, y), (x⁻¹, x⁻¹y)}, and a two-deleted type F carries three pointed types t₁, t₂, t₃ with two
ordered pairs each, which G pairs along a triangle (r12-t3 §5.3; r11-t3 §5). H has the same
slice. A pairing of H over F is a multigraph with degree 2 at each t_i. If it has a non-loop
edge, that edge is an edge of the triangle, so H is anchored at a pair with Aut(F) = 1, and
H ≅ G. Otherwise every pair {a, b} of H over F is a loop: (H − a, b) ≈ (H − b, a), so
deg_H a = deg_H b. If this happens at every F, H is regular, against T0 and 0 < d < n. ∎
Graph-specific input: the loop step is not (for tournaments a loop forces scores that differ by
exactly one at every pair, impossible on three vertices by parity). The anchor step is: Lemma A
needs the bit [a ~ b], which T0 gives for graphs and which no card shows for tournaments
(r12-t3 §8). Tightness is the main hypothesis, and I cannot discharge it for a family.

### 7.3 Where it stops

Case (B) of Theorem V with X vertex-transitive: a second d-regular graph Y on n + 1 vertices with
one card hypomorphic to all cards of X. I found no argument against it. What I checked on paper:
the triangle counts through each vertex agree (Y is "triangle-regular" like X, by card-tied
neighbour degrees), and the edge counts inside R and R_H agree. Counting induced subgraphs
through r′ in Y needs degree-marked Kelly counts in H, which are not deck data by any argument I
know. This is the bidegreed problem in its smallest form.

### 7.4 Remark (on top of UNVERIFIED literature)

If the characteristic polynomial is reconstructible (Tutte 1979) then, with the formula for the
walk generating function from the characteristic polynomials of a graph and its complement,
φ(Y) = φ(H)·(λ − (d − λ)² W_H(λ) − 2(d − λ)n − λn + 2e(H)) is deck data, so **X and Y are
cospectral** in case (B). That is a necessary condition, not a kill.

## 8. Tournaments, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

| Piece | Tournaments | Graph-specific ingredient |
|---|---|---|
| Theorem C, Theorem A | hold with a circulant tournament rule on the orbit (ā(δ) + ā(−δ) = 1) | none |
| Proposition V | whether all partial isomorphisms of a finite tournament extend inside a finite tournament is, as far as I remember, an open problem (Herwig–Lascar; **UNVERIFIED**) | none |
| Lemma P | the two degree equations become score equations with a ±1; conclusion changes | T0 |
| Theorem K₁, KS±, Proposition NT | analogue holds given that the score sequence is deck data (Harary–Palmer for n ≥ 5, **UNVERIFIED**) | none beyond the degree sequence |
| Proposition TG | loop step holds (parity); anchor step fails (the arc between the two anchor points is in no card) | T0 for the bit |
| Lemma REF below | **fails** | edges are unordered |

**Stockmeyer's pairs in this language** (construction as in the repository script, UNVERIFIED
against the paper; the two facts below are verified on paper for all k). A_k is the tournament
on {1, …, 2^k} with i → j iff odd(j − i) ≡ 1 (mod 4), an interval of a shift-invariant tournament
on ℤ. B_k = A_k + z with z beating the odd vertices, C_k = A_k + z with z beating the even ones.
- The shift s : i ↦ i + 1 is a partial automorphism A_k − 2^k → A_k − 1 (arcs depend on j − i).
  Its open orbit is everything; the closed part is empty.
- s ∪ {z ↦ z} is an isomorphism B_k − 2^k → C_k − 1 (j odd ⟺ j + 1 even).
So B_k and C_k are **the same tournament with one vertex re-attached, and the two attachment sets
are exchanged by a card isomorphism of the base**. A structure theorem of the requested kind
holds for them with a perturbation of one vertex. This confirms the expectation in the task: the
structure part cannot be where graphs differ.

**Lemma REF (reflection; paper).** Let p : G − u → G − v be a card isomorphism of a graph with
empty closed part (C = ∅ in §4.1). Then ρ : x_i ↦ x_{k−i} is an automorphism of G with ρ(u) = v:
u and v are similar. More generally, in every completion of Theorem C the map x_c ↦ x_{−c} is an
automorphism of the subgraph induced on the orbit.
Argument. [x_i ~ x_j] = a(|i − j|), and ρ preserves |i − j|. ∎
For a tournament, [x_i → x_j] depends on the signed difference and ρ reverses every arc.

Reading. In a graph the open orbit of a card isomorphism is always dihedral; pseudosimilarity has
to live in the closed part C, on which a reflection (an involution ι ∈ Aut(G[C]) with ιpι = p⁻¹
and ιν = ν) need not exist. This is consistent with Kocay's remark that pseudosimilar pairs need |C| ≥ 6
(I did not derive the number 6). In
Stockmeyer's tournaments the closed part is empty and the whole mechanism sits in the open
orbit. **The graph analogue of the pair (B_k, C_k) is a pair of isomorphic graphs**: for
X = the graph on {1, …, m} with i ~ j iff |i − j| ∈ S, m even, the reflection i ↦ m + 1 − i is an
automorphism of X exchanging odd and even vertices, so X + z(odd) ≅ X + z(even).
This is a statement about one mechanism, not about all counterexamples.

3-uniform hypergraphs: Theorem C needs invariance on triples of the orbit and the reflection
argument has no analogue for the mixed triples; Kocay's 1987 family is not analysed here.
Infinite graphs: Kocay's Y is infinite by construction and the shift on ℤ is a total
automorphism; no finite count is available.

## 9. What this means for the step

1. **The requested structure theorem is not available.** Unbounded: true for all graphs
   (Proposition V). Bounded, from (H\*): false (§5). Bounded, from the unexplained partial
   isomorphisms of Corollary P: undecided. I have no argument and no verified family.
   A lexicographic product B[A] with a fixed seed A that is pervasively pseudosimilar at levels 1
   and 2 would be a fibrewise perturbation by n/|A| vertices of a graph with bounded orbits; I
   could not verify a seed on paper, so this is a direction, not a counterexample.
2. **Where it is available it does not finish.** Canonical completions exist only for regular
   ± edge, and there the reconstruction part is Kocay's open problem. For regular minus a vertex
   the mate may live in a second regular graph (Theorem V (B)).
3. **What a next structural attempt should assume.** (i) G prime for the modular decomposition:
   then the family of §5 and every fibrewise example disappear, and by the Brignall–Georgiou–
   Waters abstract a large decomposable family is reconstructible anyway (exact statement
   UNVERIFIED; r13-t3 Lemma J handles X[A] inside W3). A reduction "it suffices to reconstruct
   prime graphs" would be a real structural child; I did not find it stated and did not attempt
   it. (ii) Work in the closed part C of card isomorphisms (Lemma REF): the open orbit is
   dihedral for graphs, so any graph-specific leverage beyond T0 has to come from an involution
   on C. (iii) Use Theorem A only as a normal form: for an involutive twist the question is
   whether X_g − u₁ − v₁ and X_g − u₀ − v₁ can be hypomorphic and non-isomorphic, with the
   transfer coefficients Δ_j(g) of r13-t2 as the known necessary conditions.
4. **Residual bookkeeping.** Nothing here produces a member of the residual or a child that is
   known to contain one. KS± and NT are honest classes with all-n paper arguments and unknown
   intersection with the residual.

## 11. Where this could break

1. **Lemma P uses Lemma A and the slice only through types.** The claim "the pointed types over F
   are t₁, t₂" needs all copies in one Aut(G)-orbit of unordered pairs; with several orbits the
   lemma says nothing.
2. **§5 item 5** relies on Lemma A in the form "anchored with e(K) = e(G) ⇒ K ≅ G_g for some
   g ∈ Aut(G − x − y)" and on the twin-class argument for G minus two vertices. The twin-class
   argument needs fibres of size 3 (a fibre of size 1 after two deletions is still a twin class).
3. **Theorem C, minimality**: I argue that every extension contains a cyclic one on the orbit of
   u; completions that are not generated by one automorphism (Kocay's group case) are covered,
   but "completion number of a set of partial isomorphisms" is not treated.
4. **Theorem A item 3** is a lower bound for extensions of the one automorphism g, not for
   graphs in which G and G_g are both induced.
5. **Theorem K₁** uses k ≥ 1 and n ≥ 3 only through T0; the degenerate case k = 1 (u, v isolated)
   is consistent. In K₁⁺ the vertex w satisfies w ≠ u, w ∉ N_Γ(u), which is how the clause of KS⁺
   is written; a builder should re-derive the side conditions.
6. **Theorem V item 2** uses that an isomorphism X − x → X − r of a regular graph extends; if
   d = 0 or d = n the low set is degenerate. The ambient class excludes both.
7. **Proposition TG** copies the type calculus of r12-t3 §5.3, which assumes tightness; I did not
   re-derive it beyond the three-type count.
8. **Literature gaps.** Godsil–Kocay 1982, Hrushovski 1992, Kimble–Schwenk–Stockmeyer 1981,
   Lauri 1997 and Stockmeyer 1977 were not read. Kocay 1984b was read to p. 324 only. I do not
   know the present status of "regular minus an edge" for k ≥ 5, nor of EPPA for tournaments.
9. **Scope of the negative answer.** §5 defeats the structure theorem for the hypothesis (H\*)
   and for perturbations by at most one vertex. The version with unexplained partial
   isomorphisms on prime graphs is open; a positive theorem there is not excluded by anything in
   this sketch.
