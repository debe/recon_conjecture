## 1. Setting

G, H₀ graphs on n ≥ 3 vertices, σ a hypomorphism with card isomorphisms f_x. As in r13-t1 §3.1
pull H₀ back along σ: H on V with H − x ≅ G − x **for the same x**. Call (G, H) an **aligned
pair**. Everything is stated for aligned pairs.

- I_x := the isomorphisms G − x → H − x, each extended by x ↦ x to a permutation of V.
  I := ⋃ I_x. Γ_x := Aut(G − x), likewise inside Sym(V)_x. I_x is a right Γ_x-torsor.
- D := G △ H. Checked T0 gives deg_H = deg_G at every vertex (A0 of r13-t1).
- Π := {π ∈ Sym(V) : G − πx ≅ G − x for all x}, the product of the symmetric groups on the
  removal-similarity classes.
- St(x) := the pairs containing x.
- t_G{x, z} := the isomorphism type of G − x − z. τ_G(x, z) := the type of the pointed card
  (G − x; z). "x **pins** z" if some θ ∈ I_x fixes z, i.e. τ_H(x, z) = τ_G(x, z); otherwise
  "x **moves** z". U := {(x, z) : x moves z}. D₂ := {{x, z} : t_H{x, z} ≠ t_G{x, z}}.

**Remark 1.1 (isomorphic versus equal; generic).** H ≅ G iff H = πG for some π ∈ Π.
(If φG = H then H − x = φ(G − φ⁻¹x), so G − φ⁻¹x ≅ G − x for all x.) So reconstructibility of G
is "every aligned H lies in Π·G", and D = ∅ is the special case π ∈ Aut(G).

## 2. The holonomy

**Definition.** Hol(G, H) := ⟨θ′⁻¹θ : θ ∈ I_x, θ′ ∈ I_y, x, y ∈ V⟩ ≤ Sym(V). It contains every Γ_x.

**Lemma 2.1 (generators; generic).** Let θ ∈ I_x, θ′ ∈ I_y, x ≠ y, ρ = θ′⁻¹θ, y₁ = θ⁻¹y,
x₁ = θ′⁻¹x. Then ρ restricts to an isomorphism G − {x, y₁} → G − {x₁, y}, with ρ(x) = x₁,
ρ(y₁) = y. Equivalently ρG △ G ⊆ St(x₁) ∪ St(y).
Argument. Both θ and θ′ restrict to isomorphisms onto H − x − y. ∎

So the holonomy consists of **partial automorphisms of G of corank 2 that happen to be total
permutations**. Bookkeeping that I found useful: sc(π) := the least number of stars covering
πG △ G is a length function on Sym(V) (sc(πρ) ≤ sc(π) + sc(ρ), sc = 0 iff π ∈ Aut G, the
extended card isomorphisms of G are exactly sc ≤ 1), and Lemma 2.1 says generators have sc ≤ 2.

**Lemma 2.2 (coherent family; generic).** G_x := θ_x G (θ_x ∈ I_x) satisfies G_x △ H ⊆ St(x) and
G_x △ G_y ⊆ St(x) ∪ St(y). Conversely a family (G_x) of labelled copies of G with the second
property defines a unique H with the first (n ≥ 3), whose cards are cards of G.
So an aligned hypomorph is a point at star-distance ≤ 1 from the isomorphism class of G in each
of the n star directions.

**Proposition 2.3 (cocycle form; generic).** ρ_xy := θ_y⁻¹θ_x satisfies ρ_yz ρ_xy = ρ_xz. Changing
the choices is the gauge action ρ_xy ↦ γ_y⁻¹ρ_xy γ_x, γ_x ∈ Γ_x. D = ∅ iff ρ is gauge-trivial
(ρ_xy = γ_y⁻¹γ_x). In Sym(V) the cocycle is always the coboundary of (θ_x).
Reading. There is no cohomology group here: the "class" lives in the pointed set
∏ Sym(V)_x-families modulo ∏ Γ_x, cut out by the n(n − 1)/2 conditions of Lemma 2.1. This is
the precise sense in which the card groupoid has holonomy, and it is why orbit counting does
not apply to it directly.

**Lemma 2.4 (orbits; generic).** Every θ ∈ I maps every Hol-orbit onto itself. Hence Hol and
⟨I⟩ have the same orbits.
Argument. Let P be a Hol-orbit. For θ ∈ I_x, θ′ ∈ I_y: θ′⁻¹θ P = P, so P* := θP does not depend
on θ ∈ I. Every x has some θ ∈ I_x with θx = x, so x ∈ P ⟺ x ∈ P*. ∎

Consequence: the orbit colouring is preserved by all card isomorphisms, so (G, H) is also an
aligned pair of **vertex-coloured** graphs for it, with the same I. No maximality is needed.

## 3. What D ≠ ∅ forces

### 3.1 Three nested difference sets

**Lemma N (nesting).** D ⊆ D₂ ⊆ U ∩ Uᵀ. In words: if the adjacency of xz differs, then
G − x − z ≇ H − x − z; and if those differ, then no card isomorphism at x fixes z and none at z
fixes x.
Argument. D₂ ⊆ U ∩ Uᵀ is generic (a θ ∈ I_x fixing z restricts to G − x − z ≅ H − x − z).
D ⊆ D₂ is T0: e(K − x − z) = e(K) − deg x − deg z + [x ~ z] and e, deg agree. ∎
For D ⊆ U ∩ Uᵀ this is Lemma Sw of r13-t1. The middle term is new and is where §3.4 works.

**Lemma BAL (three balance laws).**
1. (generic) For every x the rows τ_H(x, ·) and τ_G(x, ·) are equal as multisets (they differ by
   the permutation θ_x). This is L1⁺ in place.
2. (generic) For every x, every k ≥ 2 and every type F: the number of k-sets K ∋ x with
   G − K ≅ F equals the number with H − K ≅ F (K ↦ {x} ∪ θ_x(K ∖ x)). For k = 2: the graphs
   P_F(G) := {{x, z} : G − x − z ≅ F} and P_F(H) have **the same degree at every vertex**.
3. (T0) For every v, every Hol-orbit Q and every k:
   #{y ∈ Q : vy ∈ D, v ~ y, deg y = k} = #{y ∈ Q : vy ∈ D, v ≁ y, deg y = k}; in particular
   |N_G(v) ∩ Q| = |N_H(v) ∩ Q|. (Lemma B of r13-t1 inside Q: θ_v permutes Q ∖ v and preserves card
   degrees; then the telescoping of the checked `level_bal`.)
Item 3 answers question 10.7(b) of r13-t1 in the form available: D is balanced towards every
partition that all card isomorphisms preserve, and the Hol-orbits are the finest such.

**Lemma RUN (degree runs; T0).** For θ ∈ I_x and y ≠ x:
deg θy − deg y = [x ~_H θy] − [x ~_G y] ∈ {−1, 0, 1}. Hence the degrees on a Hol-orbit form an
interval of integers, and Hol-orbits refine the **degree-run partition** of G (two vertices in
one class iff their degrees are joined by a chain of degrees of G with steps ≤ 1). That
partition is a property of G alone.

### 3.2 Normal form

**Theorem F (frozen part and moving core).** Let (G, H) be aligned, F := Fix(Hol) (the vertices
fixed by every card isomorphism), C := V ∖ F. Then
1. (T0) G and H agree on every pair that meets F. So D lies inside C, and c(v) := N(v) ∩ F is
   the same in G and H.
2. (generic) (G − F, c) and (H − F, c) are an aligned pair of coloured graphs, and their
   colour-preserving card isomorphisms are exactly the restrictions of the elements of I. Their
   holonomy is Hol restricted to C; it has no fixed point; every orbit has ≥ 2 points.
3. (generic) Every colour-preserving isomorphism G − F → H − F extends by the identity to an
   isomorphism G → H.
4. (T0) If D ≠ ∅ then |C| ≥ 4.
Argument. 1: for z ∈ F and v ≠ z every θ ∈ I_v fixes z, so vz ∉ D by Lemma N. 2: restrictions
preserve traces because they fix F pointwise; conversely a colour-preserving isomorphism
ψ : G − F − x → H − F − x extended by id_F preserves the pairs (z, v) (colour) and the pairs
inside F (item 1). 3: the same computation. 4: by Lemma B every vertex of D has a D-neighbour
adjacent in G and one non-adjacent in G; three vertices cannot carry that. ∎

### 3.3 Anchor descent (the only place where non-isomorphism is seen)

**Theorem A (anchor descent).** Let ∅ ≠ Z ⊊ V be such that every x ∉ Z has some θ_x ∈ I_x fixing
Z pointwise. Put c_K(v) := N_K(v) ∩ Z for K = G, H.
(a) (generic) (G − Z, c_G) and (H − Z, c_H) are hypomorphic coloured graphs (θ_x restricts).
(b) (generic) G[Z] = H[Z], and every colour-preserving isomorphism between them extends by id_Z
    to an isomorphism G → H.
(c) (T0) c_H = c_G and D lies inside V ∖ Z; if |V ∖ Z| ≤ 3 then H = G.
For Z = {z}: the hypothesis is "column z of U is empty" (every x pins z), and the conclusion is
that (G − z, N(z)) and (H − z, N(z)) are hypomorphic 2-coloured graphs. This is the remark after
Lemma DI of r13-t2 without the degree-isolation hypothesis.

Let cRC(m) be: hypomorphic vertex-coloured graphs on m vertices are colour-isomorphic.

**Corollary A.** If cRC(m) holds for all 3 ≤ m < n, then an aligned pair on n vertices (coloured
or not) with G ≇ H has no anchor set: F = ∅, every vertex is moved by some card, and no Z as in
Theorem A exists. After passing to the orbit colouring (Lemma 2.4) Hol is transitive on every
colour class and no class is a singleton.

This is the "minimal counterexample" statement of the angle. **It is about the coloured
conjecture.** The uncoloured induction hypothesis gives only: if G − K and H − K (1 ≤ |K| ≤ n − 3)
are hypomorphic in place then they are isomorphic; I found no use for that. Weinstein (Pacific
J. Math. 57 (1975) 307–314; abstract seen, text not read) treats coloured reconstruction and
states that a coloured graph with every colour on at most three vertices is reconstructible; that
would close Theorem A whenever every trace class of V ∖ Z has at most three vertices
(**UNVERIFIED**).

**Lemma PIN (a G-only criterion for pinning; T0).** If θ ∈ I_x moves z to z′, then z′ ∉ {x, z} and
there are x₁ ≠ z′ and β ∈ {0, 1} with

    G − z′ − x₁ ≅ G − x − z,   deg z′ = deg z − [x ~ z] + β,   deg x₁ − [x₁ ~ z′] = deg x − β.

Argument. x₁ := θ′⁻¹x for any θ′ ∈ I_{z′}, β := [x ~_H z′]; both θ and θ′ restrict onto H − x − z′;
card degrees are preserved. ∎
PIN(x, z) := no such (z′, x₁, β) exists. Then x pins z in every aligned hypomorph, with every
θ ∈ I_x. It is the half-window form of "window-unique" (r13-t1 §2.1). It fails at every vertex
that has a twin; a twin-quotient version is routine and not written.

### 3.4 The level-2 shadow: alternating type-quadrangles

**Lemma `thr_unique` (finite; compiles in Scratch).** Let R, B be graphs on a finite set with
the same degree at every vertex. If R has no alternating 4-cycle (a, b, c, d with ab, cd ∈ R,
b ≠ c, d ≠ a, bc ∉ R, da ∉ R), then R = B.
Argument (no induction). Let S be the set of vertices meeting R △ B and u ∈ S of maximal
R-degree. u has w with uw ∈ B ∖ R; w has w′ with ww′ ∈ R ∖ B; w′ ∈ S, so deg_R w′ ≤ deg_R u. If
every R-neighbour of u were w′ or an R-neighbour of w′, then (w ∈ N_R(w′) ∖ N_R(u)) we would get
deg_R u < deg_R w′. So some a ∈ N_R(u) is neither w′ nor adjacent to w′, and (u, a, w′, w) is an
alternating 4-cycle. ∎ (This is the classical uniqueness of threshold realisations; Hammer–
Simeone, Chvátal–Hammer; attribution from memory, UNVERIFIED; the argument is self-contained.)

**Theorem Q.** If D ≠ ∅ then some copy graph P_F(G) contains an alternating 4-cycle: there are
distinct a, b, c, d with G − a − b ≅ G − c − d and G − b − c ≇ G − a − b ≇ G − d − a.
Argument. Take xz ∈ D and F = t_G{x, z}. By Lemma N, xz ∉ P_F(H), so P_F(H) ≠ P_F(G); by
Lemma BAL.2 they have the same degrees; apply `thr_unique`. ∎
Only the last step ("a different type at a pair of D") is T0; for tournaments the same argument
gives the statement for D₂ in place of D.

### 3.5 Formal hypomorphs: the holonomy as a combinatorial object on G alone

π_x := θ_x⁻¹ is a family of permutations with π_x(x) = x and, for all distinct x, z,

    (L2)   G − x − π_x z ≅ G − z − π_z x                      (both are H − x − z),
    (L3)   (G − x − π_x y; π_x z) ≈ (G − y − π_y x; π_y z)      (both are (H − x − y; z)),

and so on at every level k ≤ n − 2; at level n − 2 the condition says that π_x⁻¹ is an
isomorphism from G − x onto one common graph, i.e. the family is a real aligned hypomorph.
Call a family satisfying the conditions up to level k a **formal hypomorph of level k** and call
it **bit-trivial** if deg_{G−x}(π_x z) = deg_{G−x}(z) for all x ≠ z. A real hypomorph also satisfies
the window clause (W): deg_{G−x}(π_x z) ∈ {deg z − 1, deg z} (it is deg z − [x ~_H z]).

**Proposition FH (T0).** The family of a real aligned hypomorph is bit-trivial iff H = G.
(deg_{G−x}(π_x z) = deg_{H−x}(z), and card degrees decide the bit.)

So for every k: *G has only bit-trivial formal hypomorphs of level k ⇒ every hypomorphism from G
is an isomorphism.* Call this class TR_k. TR₂ ⊆ TR₃ ⊆ … ⊆ TR_{n−2}, and TR_{n−2} is exactly the
face "every hypomorphism from G is an isomorphism". A formal hypomorph of level 2 is a
**symmetric re-pairing of the pointed deck in place**: it is the object of which the slices and
phantoms of r13-t2 are the one-type, counted shadows. With (W) it defines a formal graph
(b(x, z) := deg z − deg_{G−x}(π_x z) is symmetric by (L2) and the edge count, and has the degrees of
G), and its bit-changing pairs are a degree-balanced subset of Sw(G) in the sense of r13-t1
(witnesses y₁ = π_x z, w₁ = π_z x).

## 4. The structure statements, collected

### 4.1 Unconditional (all n ≥ 3)

Let (G, H) be aligned with D ≠ ∅ (in particular if G ≇ H). Then:
- (S1) G has an alternating type-quadrangle (Theorem Q).
- (S2) G has a formal hypomorph of every level that is not bit-trivial (§3.5).
- (S3) The moving core has ≥ 4 vertices, carries all of D, every holonomy orbit in it has ≥ 2
  points, orbits lie inside degree runs, and D is balanced towards every (orbit, degree) class
  (Theorem F, Lemmas BAL, RUN).
- (S4) D ⊆ D₂ ⊆ U ∩ Uᵀ (Lemma N).

### 4.2 Conditional on cRC below n

- (S5) F = ∅: every column of the pointed-type matrix changes somewhere; no anchor set
  (Corollary A). G-only shadow: every vertex z has a card x with ¬PIN(x, z), i.e. the port
  G − x − z has a degree-window copy at a pair (z′, x₁) with z′ ∉ {x, z}.

### 4.3 What this is not

It is not "a set of k vertices on which the holonomy is transitive". Transitivity on colour
classes is free (pass to the orbit colouring) and carries no information by itself; what one
would need is a lower bound on orbit sizes or a group acting by automorphisms of something.
The first open case is small: **if every Hol-orbit has at most 2 points, is G ≅ H?** I expect
yes (the card isomorphisms are then commuting involutions and the gluing obstruction is a
Z/2-cocycle on the orbit pairs, which the cards inside an orbit appear to kill), but I worked
through one configuration only (three orbits, matching-type adjacency between them) and do not
claim it.

### 5.1 THR and TR₂ (paper)

THR ⊆ TR₂: for a formal hypomorph π of level 2, B_F := {{x, z} : t{x, π_x z} = F} is well defined
by (L2), has the degrees of P_F(G) (π_x is a bijection), hence equals P_F(G) by `thr_unique`; so
t{x, π_x z} = t{x, z}, in particular the edge counts agree, which is bit-triviality.
TR₂ ⇒ the conclusion: Proposition FH.
With the window clause (W) added to the hypothesis on π the class grows to TR₂ʷ, and
NS ⊆ TR₂ʷ (r13-t1's class; §3.5). The compiled body `TR2` omits (W); adding it is one more
hypothesis line. THR is not comparable with NS, PC⁺ or BS₄ by any argument I have: it uses
**all two-deleted types at once and in place**, where PC⁺ uses one slice and counts.

Limits, stated plainly.
- THR lives on the face TW (isomorphic cards only at twins), like NS.
- THR fails as soon as G has two twin pairs {a, a′}, {c, c′} in different twin classes
  (G − a − c, G − a − c′, G − a′ − c, G − a′ − c′ are isomorphic; (a, c, c′, a′) alternates unless
  G − a − a′ or G − c − c′ has that type too). A refinement that only forbids alternating
  quadrangles whose non-pair da has an edge count within 1 of that of F is still sufficient
  (the 4-cycle produced by `thr_unique` has uw ∈ P_F(H) ∖ P_F(G), and Lemma N bounds the change of
  edge count by 1). Not elaborated.
- Whether THR contains a graph of this step's class is unknown.

### 5.2 RA (paper)

Let θ ∈ I_x. Every z ∈ Z is fixed by θ and by every θ_v, v ∉ Z (Lemma PIN). So vz ∉ D for v ∉ Z
(Lemma N): traces on Z agree in G and H. θ maps v ∉ Z ∪ {x} to some v′ ∉ Z ∪ {x} with
N_H(v′) ∩ Z = N_G(v) ∩ Z, hence v′ = v. So θ = id, H − x = G − x, and the degrees give the row of x:
H = G. ∎
Z = V ∖ {x} is the "star-anchored" case. RA is Theorem A with a trivially reconstructible
coloured quotient.

### 5.3 GP (paper)

Let L, U be the vertices of degree < k, > k. By Lemma RUN every card isomorphism preserves L and
U; by Lemma BAL.3 (for unions of orbits) d_L(v) := |N(v) ∩ L| is the same in G and H. Take
x ∈ U (the other case is symmetric) and θ ∈ I_x. Then d_L(θy) = d_L(y) exactly and
deg θy = deg y + [x ~_H θy] − [x ~_G y]. Inside each class (side, d_L) the telescoping of
`level_bal` gives: x has as many neighbours of each degree in G as in H. If θ sent a
G-neighbour y to an H-non-neighbour, the class of y would contain a G-neighbour of degree j + 1
and (same counts) a G-non-neighbour of degree j, against the hypothesis; symmetrically for
non-neighbours. So θ ∪ (x ↦ x) is an isomorphism G → H. ∎
Without the gap this is the classical "neighbourhood determined by card degrees", which is
inside the checked `level_half_pure`. With a gap I have no containment. GP only helps when the
degree set of G is not an interval.

## 6. Tournaments, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

Source read in this session: P. K. Stockmeyer, *A Census of Non-Reconstructable Digraphs, II:
A Family of Tournaments*, arXiv:2110.06158 (scan of a typescript; pp. 1–7, 9–13 read). It gives
for each p = 2ⁿ ≥ 4 tournaments G_p, G_p* on {1, …, p} and bijections σ_{p,k} of {1, …, p} ∖ {k}
with (Theorem 1) σ_{p,k} an isomorphism G_p − k → G_p* − k, so the pair is **aligned by the
identity**. From its Definition 3, Lemma 2(a), Lemma 3(b) and the paragraph before Theorem 2:

- σ_{p,k} fixes the antipode k̄ = k ± p/2, and σ_{p,k} = σ_{p,k̄} off {k, k̄}: **{k, k̄} is a
  mutually pinned pair with trivial holonomy ρ_{k k̄} = id**;
- extended by k ↦ k, σ_{p,k} maps G_p onto G_p* **except for the single arc between k and k̄**,
  which is reversed. So in the language of §2, θ_k G △ H is one pair, for every k;
- for i ≠ k in the same half as k, σ_{p,k}(i) lies in the other half: no vertex is fixed by all
  card isomorphisms, F = ∅.

| Statement | In that family | Ingredient that fails for tournaments |
|---|---|---|
| Lemma 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, Proposition 2.3 | hold | none |
| D₂ ⊆ U ∩ Uᵀ, Lemma BAL.1–2, `thr_unique` | hold | none |
| **D ⊆ D₂** (Lemma N) | **fails**: the antipodal pairs are in D, are mutually pinned, and σ_{p,k} maps G_p − k − k̄ onto G_p* − k − k̄ | T0 and the bit law: in a graph a flipped pair xz changes e(G − x − z) against degrees that the cards of x and z fix. A reversed arc changes no count that any card sees |
| Theorem F.1, Theorem A (c), Lemma RUN, Lemma PIN, BAL.3 | not available | T0 |
| Theorem A (a), (b), Corollary A | hold; not contradicted, since F = ∅ | none |
| Theorem Q | holds for D₂; says nothing about D | Lemma N |
| THR, TR₂, RA, GP as classes | the conclusion "H = G" is lost at the last line | Lemma N / Proposition FH |

So the theory forbids nothing that Stockmeyer's pairs do, and the one step that separates
graphs from tournaments is the first inclusion of Lemma N: **in a graph, a pair whose two ends
pin each other is never in D.** In r12-t3's language these pairs are 2-anchored with trivial
twist and the unseen bit, and θ_k G differs from H in that one arc and nowhere else. (For the
older family B_n, C_n of J. Graph Theory 1 (1977) I did not read the isomorphisms; no claim.)

3-uniform hypergraphs (Kocay 1987, as in CLAUDE.md): type-alignment at level 2 gives equal
codegrees, not equal edges; the analogue of Lemma N needs level 3. Infinite graphs: T0 and the
maximal-degree step of `thr_unique` are lost.

## 7. A second test object: large common decks

Source read in this session (pp. 1–6): S. Ivanov, *Nonisomorphic Graphs Can Share an Arbitrarily
Large Fraction of Their Vertex-Deleted Cards*, arXiv:2608.11930 (12 Aug 2026; a preprint; I
followed Lemma 2.1, Theorems 2.2 and 3.3 on the page and did not verify them independently).
Construction: r port classes P_i of false twins with consecutive sizes t + 1, …, t + r, pair
vertices q_ij joined to P_i ∪ P_j, and a clique of selector vertices that admits exactly the
even permutations of the ports. H is G with the selector attached through an odd relabelling.
Placing both graphs on a common vertex set (my own reading of the construction, on paper: give
H the port classes of G with roles 1 and 2 exchanged), the two graphs differ only in
selector–pair-vertex adjacencies, so D has bounded support, and the cards of every x ∈ P_j,
j ≥ 2, are shared in place.

In the terms of §2: for x ∈ P_j the card isomorphism swaps the two port classes whose sizes the
deletion of x has made equal; the holonomy acts on the port classes as the alternating group;
an isomorphism G → H would have to induce an odd size-preserving permutation of the classes and
there is none. **The gluing obstruction is a sign.** Three consequences for this fleet:

1. An argument that concludes G ≅ H from the cards of a (1 − ε)-fraction of the vertices is
   wrong, for every ε > 0. Theorem A uses only the cards outside Z, so its hypothesis must fail
   there, and it does (everything moves).
2. A large or highly transitive holonomy is not by itself contradictory.
3. In those pairs the unshared cards are those of one port class and of the selector, and the
   support of D is inside the selector. With a full deck Lemma N and Lemma BAL.3 apply at the
   vertices of D. So the constraints that a partial deck lacks are exactly the ones at V(D):
   **each vertex of D must be moved by the cards of its D-neighbours and balanced at its own
   card.** Any decisive argument has to sit there.

## 8. The exact obstruction

1. **In place versus up to Π.** Every unconditional statement of §§3–5 is about D ≠ ∅. By
   Remark 1.1 a graph with two removal-similar non-twin vertices has aligned hypomorphs with
   D ≠ ∅ that are isomorphic to it, and all of (S1)–(S4) hold for them. So nothing here can
   close a graph outside TW (Proposition TW of r13-t1 is the same ceiling). To pass it one must
   use the freedom H ↦ πH, π ∈ Π: an **extremal alignment**. The first-order condition for
   minimal |D| under a transposition (a b) ∈ Π is that at most half of the pairs {a, x}, {b, x},
   x ∈ N_H(a) △ N_H(b), lie in D. I found no way to use it.
2. **Descent needs colours.** Theorem A turns an anchor set into a smaller *coloured*
   hypomorphic pair. The tree has neither coloured statements nor induction across n. The
   coloured graph (G − z, N(z)) is the rooted graph (G; z), so "2-coloured at order n − 1" is
   "rooted at order n": the uncoloured hypothesis below n does not help.
3. **Level 2 is exhausted, level 3 is the conjecture's content.** At level 2 the deck gives
   equal row multisets of pointed types in place, and a symmetry condition; the formal
   hypomorphs of level 2 are all of that information, and TR₂ is its ceiling. On the symmetric
   face TR₂ is void. What a real hypomorph has in addition is that each π_x⁻¹ is an isomorphism of
   cards, i.e. conjugates the pointed-type matrix of G − x to that of H − x (L3 and above). I have
   no argument that uses (L3) globally; W3 uses it at one triple.
4. **No group.** Hol is a group of permutations of V, but its elements are only corank-2
   partial automorphisms, and products lose more points. The gauge classes form a pointed set.
   r12-t3 (no common R) and this round's `t4.md` (completions void or false) say the same from
   the completion side.

What would change the picture: (i) a proof that mutual pinning is forced somewhere *with
trivial holonomy* (then Lemma N closes, as it does not for tournaments); (ii) the 2-point-orbit
question of §4.3 and its extension to orbits of bounded size; (iii) a level-3 analogue of
`thr_unique` (uniqueness of a symmetric 3-tensor from conjugacy classes of its slices) on a
structured class; (iv) the coloured framework, after which Corollary A is a real reduction.
Kocay, *Hypomorphisms, orbits, and reconstruction*, JCTB 44 (1988) 187–200, studies exactly the
isomorphisms G − u → H − φ(u) (zbMATH summary through MaRDI; **paper not accessible to me**). It
should be read before any of (i)–(iv) is attempted; overlap with §§2–3 is possible and unknown.

## 10. Where this could break

1. **Nothing here closes the step,** and none of the four classes is known to contain a graph
   of the residual. They are argued to be reconstructible by mechanisms that use all cards in
   place; that is all.
2. **Lemma N's first inclusion is the load-bearing line** of THR, TR₂, RA and Theorem F. It is
   Lemma Sw of r13-t1 (inside the checked BS₄ proof) plus the bit law; I did not re-derive the
   checked statement, only the paper form.
3. **`thr_unique` compiles, the rest of THR does not exist in Lean.** In particular the degree
   equality of the copy relations needs the orientation of `extE` and σ matched once.
4. **Lemma PIN quantifies over one θ′ ∈ I_{z′}.** I checked the degree bookkeeping twice on paper
   (β = [x ~_H z′] enters both equations with opposite signs); it has not been compiled.
5. **GP uses Lemma BAL.3 for a union of orbits** (the two sides of the gap). The argument is the
   telescoping of `level_bal` on a θ-invariant set; written out only in outline.
6. **Theorem A (a), (b) are generic, so they hold for tournaments,** where coloured
   reconstruction fails; Corollary A is then vacuous. That is consistent, not a test.
7. **The reading of Stockmeyer's typescript** is from pages 1–7 and 9–13 of a scan; the statement
   "σ_{p,k} extended by k ↦ k is an isomorphism except at the arc k k̄" is the displayed formula
   on its page 12 for k = 1 together with Lemma 3(b) for general k. I did not read the proof of
   non-isomorphism (Theorem 2).
8. **Ivanov's preprint is from August 2026 and unrefereed as far as I know.** Only the
   mechanism is used here, and only as a consistency test.
9. **§4.3 (orbits of size ≤ 2) is a guess,** marked as such.
10. **The quadrangle of Theorem Q may already follow from the frozen residual** (outside PC⁺
    every two-deleted type has a phantom, which for a one-copy type is a twist; I did not
    decide whether that yields two disjoint copies somewhere). If it does, ¬THR adds nothing.
11. **Attribution.** For Theorem F, Theorem A, Lemma PIN, Theorem Q and the formal hypomorphs I
    found no source, and I could not read the one paper that is most likely to contain parts of
    §2–3 (Kocay 1988). Lemma BAL.1 is L1⁺ of round 11; BAL.3 is Lemma B of r13-t1 refined;
    D ⊆ U ∩ Uᵀ is Lemma Sw of r13-t1.
