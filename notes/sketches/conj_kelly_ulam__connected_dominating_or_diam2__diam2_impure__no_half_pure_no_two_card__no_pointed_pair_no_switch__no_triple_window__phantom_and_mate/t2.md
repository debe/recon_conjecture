## 2. Notation

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3, A = A_G (symmetric, 0/1, zero diagonal), 1 the all-ones vector.
For a vertex v: E = e_v e_vᵀ, A₀ = (I − E)A(I − E) (row and column v set to zero: the card
G − v plus an isolated vertex), b = 1_{N(v)}.

- c_k(v) = (A^k)_{vv} (closed walks at v), w_k(v) = (A^k 1)_v (walks from v), w_k the vector;
- τ_k = tr A^k, W_k = 1ᵀA^k1; for the card τ_k^{(v)} = tr A₀^k and W_k^{(v)} = 1ᵀA₀^k1 for k ≥ 1
  (these are the card's own trace and total walk count);
- W = [w₀, …, w_{n−1}] the walk matrix, r = rank W, M = span{w_k} the main space (the
  A-cyclic space of 1), N = M^⊥ the non-main space; both are A-invariant; P_M, P_N projections;
- G is controllable if r = n; "corank" = n − r = dim N.
- A hypomorphism is σ with card isomorphisms f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx. The **aligned pull-back** is
  H′ on V with H′.Adj x y ⇔ H.Adj σx σy; then G − x ≅ H′ − x for all x, and A′ = A_{H′}, Δ = A − A′.

## 3. Theorem H: everything spectral is tied along σ, by induction on the length

### 3.1 One identity for a principal submatrix

Work with formal power series in t. N(t) = (I − tA)⁻¹ = Σ t^k A^k, N₀(t) = (I − tA₀)⁻¹,
C(t) = N_{vv} = Σ c_k(v) t^k.

**(α)  C·(N − N₀ + E) = N E N.**

Paper argument. E N E = C·E. Put Z = N − N E N / C; then E Z = E N − (E N E) N / C = 0 and Z E = 0.
Since (I − E)(I − tA)(I − E) + E = I − tA₀,

    (I − tA₀)(Z + E) = (I − E)(I − tA) Z + E = (I − E)(I − E N / C) + E = (I − E) + E = I,

so Z + E = N₀. ∎ (It is the statement "the inverse of a principal submatrix is the Schur
complement of the inverse"; no symmetry of A, no determinant. C has constant term 1, and the
identity is stated multiplied through by C, so nothing is divided in Lean.)

### 3.2 The three single-graph identities

**(I)** (trace of (α); any square matrix). For k ≥ 1:

    k·c_k(v) = Σ_{i=0}^{k−1} c_i(v)·(τ_{k−i} − τ^{(v)}_{k−i}).

Argument: tr(N E N) = (N²)_{vv} and the coefficient of t^k in N² is (k + 1)A^k, so the right side
of (α) has trace Σ (k + 1)c_k t^k; tr(N − N₀ + E) = 1 + Σ_{k≥1}(τ_k − τ_k^{(v)}) t^k. Compare
coefficients and cancel c_k. ∎ (Equivalent to C = φ(G − v)/φ(G) with Newton's identities; not
used in that form.)

**(II)** (1ᵀ(α)1; **uses Aᵀ = A**). With d_0 := 1 and d_j := W_j − W_j^{(v)} for j ≥ 1, for k ≥ 0:

    Σ_{i=0}^{k} w_i(v)·w_{k−i}(v) = Σ_{i=0}^{k} c_i(v)·d_{k−i}.

Argument: 1ᵀN E N 1 = (1ᵀN e_v)(e_vᵀN 1) = (Σ w_k(v) t^k)² by symmetry; 1ᵀ(N − N₀ + E)1 =
1 + Σ_{k≥1}(W_k − W_k^{(v)}) t^k. ∎ In words: walks that visit v = (walks from v)² / (closed walks
at v). This is Hagos's equation (2), which he attributes to Godsil–McKay 1981.

**(III)** Σ_v c_k(v) = τ_k; Σ_v w_k(v) = W_k; and (**symmetry again**) W_{i+j} = Σ_v w_i(v) w_j(v),
used only as W_{k+2} = Σ_v w_1(v) w_{k+1}(v).

### 3.3 Theorem H

**Theorem H (aligned Hagos).** Let n ≥ 3, G and H graphs on n vertices and σ a bijection such
that for every v and k the cards G − v and H − σv have the same tr A^k and the same 1ᵀA^k1
(true when the cards are isomorphic). Then for every k ≥ 0:

    τ_k(G) = τ_k(H),   W_k(G) = W_k(H),   c_k^G(v) = c_k^H(σv),   w_k^G(v) = w_k^H(σv)   (all v).

Paper argument. Strong induction on k; k = 0 is trivial. Let k ≥ 1 and write δX = X(G) − X(H)
(vertex quantities compared along σ). Every term of (I), (II) with both indices below k is
tied by the induction hypothesis or by the cards, so subtracting the identities for H from
those for G leaves

    (a)  k·δc_k(v) = δτ_k            for every v      (from (I): only the i = 0 term survives),
    (b)  2·δw_k(v) = δW_k + δc_k(v)  for every v      (from (II): i = 0 and i = k survive).

Sum over v with (III): (a′) k·δτ_k = n·δτ_k and (b′) 2·δW_k = n·δW_k + δτ_k.
- k = 1: (a′) gives (n − 1)δτ_1 = 0, so δτ_1 = 0; (b′) gives (n − 2)δW_1 = 0, so δW_1 = 0
  **because n ≠ 2**.
- k ≥ 2: W_k = Σ_v w_1(v) w_{k−1}(v) is tied by the induction hypothesis, so δW_k = 0; then (b′)
  gives δτ_k = 0. (At k = n equation (a′) is empty; this is the order at which Tutte's theorem
  was thought to be needed. The split of W_n supplies the missing equation.)
Then (a) gives δc_k = 0 and (b) gives δw_k = 0. ∎

`lean/Recon/Scratch/<this step>/t2_hagos_induction.lean`: for sequences c, w, τ, W, c′, w′, τ′, W′
and common card data τc, Wc satisfying (I), (II), (III) in peeled form (predicate `Good`), and

## 4. Consequences (paper, all n ≥ 3)

### 4.1 Item (1): Q is a permutation

Theorem H for a hypomorphism σ gives W_H = P_σ W_G (P_σ the permutation matrix). If W_G is
invertible, then for every k, A_H P_σ w_k = A_H w_k^H = w_{k+1}^H = P_σ A w_k, so A_H P_σ = P_σ A:
**σ is an isomorphism**. In the language of the angle: G and H are generalised cospectral (τ_k
and W_k agree), both controllable, and the unique regular orthogonal Q = W_G W_H⁻¹ (Wang–Xu) is
P_σᵀ. Symmetry of A is used in (II) and (III) and nowhere else.

**S5core (corank ≤ 1).** In aligned form Δ w_k = w_{k+1} − w′_{k+1} = 0, so every row z_x of Δ is
orthogonal to M. If dim N ≤ 1 and Δ_{xy} ≠ 0, then z_x, z_y are dependent, a z_x + b z_y = 0; the
coordinate x gives b Δ_{yx} = 0 and the coordinate y gives a Δ_{xy} = 0; contradiction. So Δ = 0.
(`t3.md` S5; Liu–Siemons arXiv:1911.00062 per the novelty note.) The controllable case
**compiles in Scratch**: `iso_of_walks_tied` in `t2_spectral_statements.lean` (walk sequences
tied along σ and no nonzero integer vector orthogonal to all of them ⇒ G ≅ H via σ).

The same one-liner without a deck: **if K is any graph with W_K = P W_G for a permutation P and
rank W_G ≥ n − 1, then K ≅ G via P.** So a graph in the Godsil–McKay class has no "spectral
phantom" of any kind (§6.3).

### 4.2 Hong's class and the one-card form

From the resolvent identity N = N₀ + tN(A − A₀)N₀ one gets, with g(t) = Σ_m (bᵀA₀^m 1) t^m
(walks in the card that start in N(v)) and F(t) = t²·Σ_m (bᵀA₀^m b) t^m (first returns),

    (T1)  Σ w_k(v) t^k = C·(1 + t·g),        (T2)  C·(1 − F) = 1.

So Theorem H ties g and F along σ. Transport by f_v: with S := f_v⁻¹(N_H(σv)) and c = 1_S,

    1ᵀA₀^m (b − c) = 0   and   bᵀA₀^m b = cᵀA₀^m c   for all m.          (S5′)

(`t3.md` S5′, there derived from Tutte for G and Ḡ.) If the card G − v is controllable, the
first family gives b = c and f_v extends to an isomorphism (checked helper `iso_of_nbhd`). This
is Hong's theorem (Y. Hong, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 32 (1982); read only as quoted in
Wang–Wen–Guo, arXiv:2408.02488, whose abstract I read: they extend it to an almost controllable
card with a non-trivial automorphism; page numbers of Hong UNVERIFIED).

### 4.3 Removal-similar vertices (item (2), first half)

**Lemma RS.** If G − u ≅ G − v, then w_k(u) = w_k(v) and c_k(u) = c_k(v) for all k; equivalently
e_u − e_v ∈ N and u, v are cospectral.
Argument: the transposition (u v) is a hypomorphism from G to G; Theorem H. (Directly: C_u = C_v
by (I) and then (II) gives (Σ w_k(u)t^k)² = (Σ w_k(v)t^k)².) ∎
By Godsil–Smith (arXiv:1709.07975, Thm 3.1, **from memory, UNVERIFIED**) cospectrality is
equivalent to: the A-modules of e_u − e_v and e_u + e_v are orthogonal. With RS the first module
lies in N.

**Proposition PS.** If G − u ≅ G − v and u, v are not twins, then e_u − e_v and
d := 1_{N(u)∖v} − 1_{N(v)∖u} are independent vectors of N; so corank W ≥ 2.
Argument: A(e_u − e_v) = d + [u ~ v](e_v − e_u) ∈ N by invariance; disjoint supports. ∎
(This is S5core for the pull-back along (u v): Δ = (e_u − e_v)dᵀ + d(e_u − e_v)ᵀ.)

**Corollary (rank bound).** rank W(G) ≤ t(G), the number of isomorphism types of cards (even:
the number of classes of cards under generalised cospectrality). In particular a controllable
graph has n pairwise non-isomorphic cards; a graph of corank 1 has at most one pair of
isomorphic cards, and that pair is a twin pair; **a graph with two card types has at most two
main eigenvalues**, i.e. is regular or two-walk-linear. The last sentence turns the librarian's
computed observation F1 ("the two-card-type members have walk rank 2", `options_algebraic.md`)
and the normal form of Zinchenko's Lemma 2.4 into a consequence of Theorem H. It sharpens
"number of main eigenvalues ≤ number of automorphism orbits" (Cvetković–Fowler, via Rowlinson
2007 Prop. 1.5, as quoted in `options_algebraic.md`; not re-read). No source found for the
card-type form; not searched.

## 5. The dichotomy (item (2), second half)

**Theorem D (paper).** Let H′ be an aligned hypomorph of G (or any graph on V with the walk
sequences and closed-walk sequences of G at every vertex), Δ = A − A′.
1. M(H′) = M(G) =: M, and A = A′ on M. (W′ = W and A w_k = w_{k+1} = A′ w_k.)
2. Δ is symmetric with entries in {0, ±1}, zero diagonal, and **Δ = P_N Δ P_N**: every row of Δ
   lies in N. Hence rank Δ ≤ dim N; Δ_{xy} ≠ 0 only if P_N e_x ≠ 0 ≠ P_N e_y; and Δ ≠ 0 forces
   dim N ≥ 2 (S5core).
3. B := A|_N and B′ := A′|_N are isospectral (tr B^k = τ_k − tr A^k|_M), and every vertex has the
   same spectral measure in both: ⟨n_x, B^k n_x⟩ = ⟨n_x, B′^k n_x⟩ for n_x := P_N e_x and all k
   (closed walks, minus the common main part).
4. Q-form: the regular orthogonal matrices with QᵀAQ = A′ are exactly I_M ⊕ Q_N with Q_N
   orthogonal on N and Q_NᵀBQ_N = B′. (Q fixes every w_k because Q1 = 1.) So **all the freedom
   of Q sits in O(N)**; N = 0 gives Q = I, which is §4.1.
5. Every row z_x of Δ is "walk-neutral": Σ_y z_x(y) w_k(y) = 0 for all k, i.e. the neighbours of x
   lost and gained carry the same number of walks of each length. (k = 0, 1 are the degree
   balance and the two-walk key of the checked children; all k is new.)

**Card form (paper).** For every v, with M_c, N_c the main and non-main spaces of the card and
b, c as in (S5′): c = P_{M_c} b + U·P_{N_c} b for an orthogonal U on N_c commuting with the
card's adjacency operator there (equal spectral measures of the two non-main components).
Automorphisms of the card are such U. So **the re-attachment ambiguity at a card is an
orthogonal twist of the non-main component of N(v), inside ∏_θ O(mult_θ(N_c))**; with simple
non-main eigenvalues it is a choice of signs. Hong's class is N_c = 0. Corank 1 of the card:
N_c = ⟨ξ⟩ and the only alternative is the reflection c = b − 2⟨b, ξ⟩ξ, which is 0/1 only if
ξ is proportional to 1_X − 1_Y with |X| = |Y|, X ⊆ N(v), Y ∩ N(v) = ∅; for |X| = 1 the two
vertices are twins of the card and the alternative is automorphic (this is the shape of the
Wang–Wen–Guo extension; their theorem not re-derived here).

**Reading as a dichotomy.** Either corank W ≤ 1 and the hypomorphism itself is an isomorphism
(Godsil–McKay; generic: almost every graph is controllable, O'Rourke–Touri, arXiv:1511.05080,
SIAM J. Control Optim. 54 (2016) 3347–3378, abstract level, journal data secondary), or
dim N ≥ 2 and every hypomorph is G changed by a symmetric ternary matrix supported on the
vertices that are not main (e_x ∉ M), isospectral on N, with equal vertex measures. Corank 2 is
the balanced block switch of `t3.md` S6 (Zhu–Yue, arXiv:2609.36895, per the novelty note).

## 6. What this does to the residual

### 6.1 Class OC′ (child B)

A **spectral re-attachment at v** is a graph K on V with K − v = G − v (same labelled card) and
c_k^K(v) = c_k^G(v), w_k^K(v) = w_k^G(v) for all k. **OC′**: some vertex v such that every
spectral re-attachment at v is isomorphic to G.

**Theorem OC (paper).** Every graph in OC′ is reconstructible.
Argument: let H be hypomorphic via σ, (f_x). Let φ be f_v extended by v ↦ σv and K the pull-back
of H along φ. Then K − v = G − v, K ≅ H, and the sequences of K at v are those of H at σv, which
are those of G at v by Theorem H. So K ≅ G. ∎

Inclusions (paper):
- *Hong's class ⊆ OC′*: by (T1), a spectral re-attachment has 1ᵀA₀^m(b − c) = 0; a controllable
  card gives c = b, K = G.
- *US ⊆ OC′* (the checked `unique_switch`): by (α) summed, a spectral re-attachment K has
  Σ W_k(K)t^k = Σ W_k(G)t^k, hence the same main polynomial; so K is two-walk (a, b)-linear with
  the a, b of G, and Theorem U of round 11 applies to K (at most one alternative at a
  nonsingular vertex, and the non-extending card isomorphism realises it by a copy of G).
- OC′ and child A are not comparable by an argument I have (a controllable graph may have a
  generalised cospectral mate that shares a card).
- The librarian's "decided vertex" test F2 (`options_algebraic.md`, a truncated form of (S5′)
  restricted to automorphic re-attachments) is a sub-class of OC′; its recorded evidence was
  for the older residuals and is not re-run or relied on here.

OC′ quantifies over the 2^{n−1} re-attachments of one vertex, not over graphs with a census
property; it is the one-card analogue of PC⁺ and the ceiling of S5′-type arguments.

### 6.2 The structured remainder

**Theorem R′ (paper).** Let G be in this step's class and outside A and B. Then:
1. dim N ≥ 2 (corank W ≥ 2); G has a non-zero vector orthogonal to 1 in an A-invariant subspace
   orthogonal to all walk vectors, and at least two independent ones.
2. For **every** vertex v there is K_v ≇ G with K_v − v = G − v that is cospectral with G, has
   the walk generating function of G (so its complement is cospectral with Ḡ: a generalised
   cospectral mate), in which v has the closed-walk and walk sequences it has in G. In particular no card
   is controllable, and 1_{N(v)} has a non-zero component in the non-main space of G − v which a
   non-automorphic orthogonal twist moves to another 0/1 vector.
3. (Inherited, round 13.) A phantom at every pair and a labelled 4-mate.
For a non-reconstructible G all of these are one graph: K_v ≅ H for every v, and the 4-mate is

### 6.3 Why the frozen phantoms force nothing, and the optional semantic children

A phantom of round 13 has the keys (deg, w₂) = (w₁, w₂) and one slice. Nothing in a slice ties
w_k for k ≥ 3, and round-13 `t2.md` §7 records phantoms that are not cospectral with G. So
"phantom at every pair ⇒ rank W ≤ n − 2" has no argument, and I do not expect one.
With Theorem H the two round-13 children can be strengthened verbatim:
- **PC^W**: `slice_determined` with keys (w_k(x))_k and (c_k(x))_k for all k instead of (deg, w₂).
  Argument: the checked PC⁺ argument plus Theorem H. PC^W ⊇ PC⁺ ∪ {corank ≤ 1} (§4.1), and

  transfer coefficients Δ_j(g) vanish (round-13 TL.3), not only Δ₀, Δ₁.
- **BS₄^W**: `aligned_switch_kelly4` with the extra hypothesis that G △ D has the walk and

Both are semantic carving classes; not proposed now.

## 7. Where the spectral method stops (exact failure point)

1. **Inside N the deck is spectrally silent beyond Theorem D.3.** Theorem H exhausts what (α)
   gives: per vertex one closed-walk series and one walk series. Off-diagonal resolvent
   entries (N_{xy})² = C_x C_y − (two-deleted data) (Jacobi) involve G − x − y, which is where the
   pair censuses of rounds 9–13 live; I found no way to tie them along σ.
2. **One explicit family where walk and closed-walk keys see nothing (paper).** Let Γ, Γ′ be
   strongly regular with the same parameters (k, l, m), G = Γ − v₀, K = Γ′ − v₀′, and τ any
   bijection carrying N(v₀) to N(v₀′). Then w_k^K(τx) = w_k^G(x) and c_k^K(τx) = c_k^G(x) for all k, x.
   Verification: (i) both graphs are two-walk (a, b)-linear with a = k + l − m and b depending
   only on the parameters (round-11 `t1.md` Prop. F), so M = span{1, deg} and w_k(x) is a
   function of deg x, i.e. of [x ~ v₀]; (ii) A² = kI + lA + m(J − I − A) makes (xI − A_Γ)⁻¹ =
   αI + βA_Γ + γJ with α, β, γ rational functions of x depending only on the parameters; by
   Jacobi's complementary-minor formula φ(Γ − S) = φ(Γ)·det((xI − A_Γ)⁻¹[S, S]), so φ(Γ − v₀) and
   φ(Γ − v₀ − x) depend only on the parameters and on [x ~ v₀]; C_x = φ(G − x)/φ(G) (reversed
   polynomials) then gives (ii). For non-isomorphic Γ, Γ′ (Shrikhande and the 4 × 4 rook graph,
   parameters (16, 6, 2, 2)) G ≇ K, because a d-regular graph is recovered from any one of its
   cards (join a new vertex to the vertices of degree d − 1), so Γ − v₀ ≅ Γ′ − v₀′ would give Γ ≅ Γ′. So no class defined by walk and closed-walk keys alone can contain this
   face; card information is necessary, and on this face the only spectral tool with a card in
   it is the REL kernel argument of US (now a special case of OC′).
3. **Low rank is where the residual's shape points.** By the rank bound, few card types force
   low walk rank; the impure, nearly regular graphs of the earlier residuals have many
   isomorphic cards. I have **no** argument that the frozen clauses (¬PURE and the rest) force
   corank ≥ 2; child A may be disjoint from the residual. It is still a structural all-n class
   and it converts the residual's description from "phantoms" to "dim N ≥ 2".
4. **Nothing here uses diameter 2**, so nothing here can be the argument that closes the
   branch by itself.

## 10. Where this could break

1. **Theorem H is much shorter than the fleet's estimate for Tutte's theorem**, which is a
   reason to distrust it. Against an error: (α) is a five-line ring computation; (I), (II) were
   checked by hand on K₃ and P₃ (§3.4); the induction compiles in Scratch from (I)–(III); the
   statement is Hagos's Theorem 3.1 (read), whose ingredients (2), (3) are (II) and (I); the
   n = 2 anchor fails at exactly one place. Not done: (α), (I), (II) in Lean. A red team should
   re-derive (α) and the peeled forms of (I), (II) in `Good` (index shifts are the likely place
   for a slip: `Good` uses c₀ = w₀ = d₀ = 1 and sums over `Finset.range k` of the middle terms).
2. **The hypothesis of Theorem H in Lean** is "cards isomorphic"; the bridge to equal tr A₀^k and
   1ᵀA₀^k1 goes through the extension φ, which fixes nothing about N(v). That is intended.
3. **Child A may not meet the residual at all** (§7.3). It is in the literature; its value here
   is Theorem H and the clause "dim N ≥ 2" in the remainder.
4. **Child B is a ceiling class** over re-attachments of one vertex; "US ⊆ OC′" and "Hong ⊆ OC′"
   are paper arguments, the first through "equal walk generating function ⇒ same (a, b)", which
   uses that the main polynomial is the minimal recurrence of (W_k) (standard; Rowlinson 2007
   as quoted in `options_algebraic.md`; not re-read).
5. **Theorem D.4** (all Q are I_M ⊕ Q_N) is linear algebra over ℝ and is description only; no
   proposed Lean statement depends on it or on any real-number fact.
6. **§7.2** uses Jacobi's complementary-minor formula and the quadratic equation of a strongly
   regular graph from memory (Godsil, *Algebraic Combinatorics*, ch. 4; Brouwer–Haemers,
   *Spectra of Graphs*, ch. 9; UNVERIFIED). It is an obstruction remark, not part of any child.
7. **The step stays open.** Theorem R′ describes a hypothetical member; it classifies none.
8. **Attributions.** Godsil–McKay 1981 and Hong 1982 were not read; Wang–Xu (generalised
   spectral characterisation, uniqueness of Q for controllable graphs) is cited from the task
   text and from memory (W. Wang, C.-X. Xu, European J. Combin. 27 (2006) 826–840; UNVERIFIED);
   Johnson–Newman 1980 only through the Wang–Wen–Guo abstract.
