## 1. Notation

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3, A its adjacency matrix, deg = degree in G. H hypomorphic to G via σ with
card isomorphisms f_x : G − x → H − σx.

- **σ-aligned form.** G* := H pulled back along σ, a graph on V with G* − x ≅ G − x for every
  x; A* its matrix; Δ := A − A* (symmetric, entries 0, ±1, zero diagonal). G ≅ H if Δ = 0.
- **Card-aligned form at x.** C := G − x, N := N(x), d := |N|. Pull H back along f_x extended
  by x ↦ σx: a graph on V equal to G off x in which x has neighbourhood N′ := f_x⁻¹(N_H(σx)).
  δ_x := 1_{N′} − 1_N ∈ {0, ±1}^{V∖x}. If δ_x = 0 for one x then f_x extends and G ≅ H
  (checked helper `iso_of_nbhd`).
- A function g assigning a number to (graph, vertex) is **tied** if g_H(σv) = g_G(v) for all v,
  for every hypomorphism; equivalently g_{G*} = g_G as vectors. A card function ℓ(C, w) is
  **N-tied** if Σ_{w∈N′} ℓ(C, w) = Σ_{w∈N} ℓ(C, w), i.e. ⟨δ_x, ℓ(C, ·)⟩ = 0.
- w_k := A^k 1 (walks of length k from each vertex), 𝒲(G) := span{w_k}, 𝒟(G) := span of the
  indicators of the degree classes, **𝒰(G) := 𝒲(G) + 𝒟(G)**. The same for a card: 𝒲(C),
  𝒟(C), 𝒰(C), with degrees and walks taken in C.
- Θ(Q, M) for a graph Q and M ⊆ V(Q): the number of homomorphisms Q → C that send M into N.
  1-WL class counts of N are the statistics with Q a rooted tree and M = {root}.

Caveat on "per card" statements. If G − x ≅ G − y by ρ with x, y not similar, then σ := (x y)
with f_x = ρ is a hypomorphism of G to itself. So a tied g takes equal values on
removal-similar vertices. Trees have pseudosimilar vertices (Kirkpatrick, Klawe, Corneil,
*On pseudosimilarity in trees*, JCTB 34 (1983) 323–339; abstract and the 1981 UBC report
TR-81-03 seen, text not read), and on a forest the stable colouring is the orbit partition
(standard; UNVERIFIED citation). Hence **the stable colour of a vertex in G is not a tied
function**, and no lemma of the form "the full 1-WL colour of x is determined by the card
G − x" can hold. What can hold is tied *sums over neighbours* (class counts), §2.4.

## 2. Question 1: what of the WL / coherent data does the deck tie?

### 2.1 Literature position

- Kelly ⇒ hypomorphic n-vertex graphs have equal hom(F, ·) for all F on at most n − 1 vertices
  (`hom_counts_and_logic.md`, Prop. R; van Dobben de Bruyn et al., arXiv:2610.07927, Lemma 3.5
  [text, per that note]).
- k-WL equivalence ⇔ equal hom(F, ·) for **all** F of treewidth ≤ k, of every size
  (Dvořák, J. Graph Theory 64 (2010) 330–342; Dell–Grohe–Rattan, ICALP 2018,
  arXiv:1802.08876 [abstract]). The two families are incomparable: the deck has every
  treewidth but bounded size, WL has every size but bounded treewidth.
- I found **no source** stating that hypomorphic graphs are 1-WL or 2-WL equivalent, and no
  size bound of the form "tree patterns on fewer than n vertices suffice for n-vertex
  targets". Both statements follow from RC and are open as far as I can tell (UNVERIFIED
  negative; the librarian's note lists the same gap). Cotta–Morris–Ribeiro,
  *Reconstruction for Powerful Graph Representations* (NeurIPS 2021) relate decks to GNN
  expressiveness; content UNVERIFIED (search summary only).
- The missing layer in hom language: for a tree T on at least n vertices, hom(T, G) contains
  the vertex-surjective homomorphisms, e.g. for |T| = n the number of spanning trees of G
  isomorphic to T. Only the total number of spanning trees and counts by block type are in
  the literature (Tutte 1979, Kocay 1981; UNVERIFIED here).

### 2.2 Theorem T: the tied tower (paper)

**T0 (checked: `kelly_degree_sequence`, `deg_T0`).** deg is tied.

**T1 (round-2 colour).** For every b, m_b(v) := #{w ~ v : deg w = b} is tied. So the colour
(deg v, {{deg w : w ~ v}}) is tied; in matrix form **A·1_{deg=b} is tied for every b**.
Argument: with c_G(j), c_C(j) the numbers of vertices of degree j in G and in C = G − v,
c_C(j) = c_G(j) − [deg v = j] − m_j(v) + m_{j+1}(v) and m_0(v) = 0; solve upwards in j. c_G is
recognizable, c_C is a card invariant, deg v is tied. (Classical; the checked `w2_tied` is the
first moment.) Card form: **every indicator of a card-degree class is N-tied** (L1).

**T2 (walks; sibling t2, Theorem H, after Hagos, Electron. J. Combin. 7 (2000) R12).** w_k is
tied for every k ≥ 0, and so is c_k(v) := (A^k)_{vv}. Card form (t2 (T1), (T2)):
1ᵀA_C^k 1_N and 1_Nᵀ A_C^k 1_N are tied for all k, i.e. **every walk vector A_C^k 1 of the card
is N-tied**, and the spectral measure of 1_N with respect to A_C is tied.
A self-contained Kelly-only argument for the range k ≤ n − 3 (no Theorem H; written out
because the hand checks below use it):
- tr A^j is recognizable for j ≤ n − 1 and W_ℓ := 1ᵀA^ℓ1 for ℓ ≤ n − 2: such walks visit at most
  n − 1 vertices, and a hom count with image size < n is a sum over induced subgraphs of that
  size of an isomorphism invariant (checked `kelly_induced_count`).
- With f_i := 1_NᵀA_C^{i−2}1_N (first returns to x, f_1 = 0), c_0 = 1, c_m = Σ_{i≤m} f_i c_{m−i}:
  tr A^j − tr A_C^j = Σ_{i=1}^{j} i·f_i·c_{j−i} (rotate a closed walk through x to start at x;
  the base point lies in the first excursion). Leading term j·f_j, so f_j and c_j(x) are tied
  for j ≤ n − 1 by induction. Hand checks: K₂ (f_2 = 1: both sides 2 for even j); K₃ at j = 3
  (6 = 3·2) and j = 4 (18 − 2 = 2·2·2 + 4·2).
- With ũ_0 = 1, ũ_p = 1ᵀA_C^{p−1}1_N: W_ℓ(G) − W_ℓ(C) = Σ_{p+j+q=ℓ} ũ_p c_j ũ_q (cut a walk at its
  first and last visit of x; this uses A = Aᵀ: both tails are the same sequence). Leading term
  2ũ_ℓ, so ũ_ℓ is tied for ℓ ≤ n − 2.

**T3 (Schur closure, global).** Tied functions are closed under pointwise products, so
Σ_v Π_i g_i(v) is recognizable for tied g_i. With T1, T2: hom(F, G) is recognizable for every F
obtained by gluing paths, closed walks and stars at **one** vertex, of any size: all spiders
S(i, j, k, …), "kites" (cycle plus tails at one vertex), and with T1 the degree-labelled
versions. These go beyond the Kelly range (more than n vertices). I found no source; it is
immediate from Theorem H.

**T4 (degree-equitable graphs).** If the degree partition of G is equitable (each vertex of
degree a has exactly c_{ab} neighbours of degree b), then G* has the same property with the
same c, by T1. So G and H have a common equitable partition with equal parameters: they are
fractionally isomorphic, i.e. 1-WL equivalent (Ramana–Scheinerman–Ullman 1994; Tinhofer 1986;
citations UNVERIFIED, the statement is standard). This is the one case where
"hypomorphic ⇒ 1-WL equivalent" has an argument: colour refinement stops at round 1 and T1
reaches round 2.

### 2.3 The tied-closure principle (paper)

Let 𝒯_A be the set of vertex functions g with g **and** Ag tied. For g ∈ 𝒯_A:
Δg = Ag − A*g = 0. Since Δ is symmetric, **Δ = PΔP**, P the orthogonal projection onto
(span 𝒯_A)^⊥. By T1 and T2, span 𝒯_A ⊇ 𝒰(G) = 𝒲(G) + 𝒟(G).

- This sharpens t2's Theorem D (Δ lives on the non-main space 𝒲^⊥) to Δ living on
  **(𝒲 + 𝒟)^⊥**: vectors orthogonal to all walk vectors that also sum to zero on every degree
  class. Row form of Δ·1_{deg=b} = 0: every vertex gains and loses equally many neighbours in
  each degree class. That is the "degree-balanced" clause of the checked BS₄; Δw_k = 0 is t2's
  "walk-neutral". The principle says they are the same phenomenon and combine.
- Each new instance of **rooted Kelly, RK(g): "Ag is tied"** for a tied g removes one more
  direction. In WL terms RK(g) is one refinement step: it turns a tied colouring into the tied
  multiset of neighbour colours.

### 2.4 Where the tower stops

**Proposition X (the hereditary matrix method is exhausted at the walk level; paper).**
First-return and first-hit decompositions need a matrix M(G) whose principal submatrix on
V ∖ x is M(G − x). Deleting all other vertices in turn shows that the entry at (u, v) then
depends only on G[{u, v}], so the symmetric hereditary matrices are αI + βA + γ(J − I − A). Every word in
A_C and J applied to 1_N reduces to the numbers u_k = 1ᵀA_C^k1_N, f-numbers 1_NᵀA_C^k1_N and
card constants, because J = 11ᵀ. In particular complement walks add nothing:
span{Ā_C^k 1} = 𝒲(C). The degree matrix is not hereditary (the minor at x of tI − A − sD
contains diag(1_N)), so Laplacian-type polynomials give no card-computable minor. ∎

**The master system.** For any F: hom(F, G) − hom(F, C) = Σ over non-empty independent
S ⊆ V(F) of Π over components K of F − S of Θ(K, N_F(S) ∩ K). Unknowns are the Θ(Q, M) with Q
connected, i.e. rooted connected graphs (F, v) with v not a cut vertex; equations are unrooted
connected F. An F contributes one equation and one leading unknown per orbit of its non-cut
vertices. The system is triangular exactly for families with **one** such orbit that are
closed under the decomposition: cycles (vertex-transitive), paths (two ends in one orbit,
inner cuts give products of shorter paths), stars (degree powers). That is T1 and T2 and
nothing else. Branching at a vertex of the card is a Schur product and produces at least two
leaf orbits.

**First open statistics (graphs).**
- Order 4, pattern paw (n ≥ 5): through x the paw has x pendant, A_paw := Σ_{w∈N} t_C(w)
  (t = triangles through a vertex), x at a degree-2 vertex, B := Σ_{w∈N} deg_N(w)(deg_C(w) − 1),
  and x at the degree-3 vertex, t(x)(d − 2), known. One equation: A_paw + B is N-tied. Neither
  is known separately. In G-language: **RK(paw): is Σ_{w~v} t(w) tied?** (t is tied, t(v) =
  t(G) − t(G − v).) The degree argument of T1 fails because a neighbour w of v loses
  codeg(v, w) triangles in the card, not a constant.
- Order 5, pattern spider S(1,1,2) (n ≥ 6): X₁ := Σ_{w∈N} deg_C(w)·w₂^C(w) (x at a short leaf)
  and X₂ := Σ_{w∈N} Σ_{c~w} deg_C(c)² (x at the long leaf). One equation, obtained from the
  master system by listing all independent S: 2X₁ + X₂ + 2B′ is N-tied, where
  B′ = Σ_{w∈N} deg_C(w) deg_N(w) = B + 2e(N). X₁ is the first round-3 colour-refinement
  statistic (a neighbour count by the key (deg, w₂)). I re-derived the same equation from the
  recognizable degree-pair edge distribution (generating-function form); it gives nothing
  more, as it must (same pattern count).
- What symmetry gives for free: ⟨g, At⟩ = ⟨Ag, t⟩ is recognizable for g ∈ 𝒰(G). So the
  projection of the unknown vector At onto 𝒰(G) is tied. RK(paw) is only about the component
  of At in 𝒰(G)^⊥, the same space where Δ lives.

RC for n ≤ 13 implies RK(paw) there; that is no evidence about a proof. I have no argument
for RK(paw) for all n and no reason to think it false. It is the smallest statement whose
proof would move the tower one rung, and by §2.5 any proof must use symmetry of A.

### 2.5 One explicit pair: tournaments (hand verification)

Stockmeyer's smallest pair (J. Graph Theory 1 (1977) 19–25; the definition from memory, the
verification below is complete in itself). A₄ on {1,2,3,4}: i → j iff the odd part of j − i is
≡ 1 mod 4: arcs 1→2, 2→3, 3→4, 1→3, 2→4, 4→1. B := A₄ plus 0 with 0→1, 0→3, 2→0, 4→0.
C := A₄ plus 0 with 0→2, 0→4, 1→0, 3→0.

- Hypomorphic by 0 ↦ 0, k ↦ 5 − k. B − 0 = C − 0 = A₄. B − 1 and C − 4: a source (2, resp. 1)
  dominating a 3-cycle (0→3→4→0, resp. 0→2→3→0). B − 4 and C − 1: a 3-cycle (0→1→2→0, resp.
  0→2→3→0) dominating a sink. B − 2 → C − 3 by 4↦1, 0↦0, 1↦2, 3↦4: arcs 1→3, 3→4, 4→1, 0→1,
  0→3, 4→0 go to 2→4, 4→1, 1→2, 0→2, 0→4, 1→0, all arcs of C. B − 3 → C − 2 by 2↦1, 4↦3, 0↦0,
  1↦4: arcs 1→2, 2→4, 4→1, 0→1, 2→0, 4→0 go to 4→1, 1→3, 3→4, 0→4, 1→0, 3→0, all arcs of C.
- Not isomorphic. Scores in B: (0,1,2,3,4) ↦ (2,2,3,1,2); in C: (2,3,2,2,1). The unique
  score-1 vertex beats a score-2 vertex in B (3→4) and the score-3 vertex in C (4→1).
- The common card A₄ has scores (2,2,1,1) on (1,2,3,4); out-neighbour scores {2,1}, {1,1}, {1},
  {2}: **colour refinement is discrete after two rounds**, and A₄ is rigid.
- The two out-neighbourhoods of 0 are N = {1,3}, N′ = {2,4}: equal counts in the round-1
  (score) classes, different round-2 classes.
- Out-walk vectors of A₄: 1, s = (2,2,1,1), As = (3,2,1,2), A²s = (3,3,2,3). Sums over N and
  N′: 2, 2; 3, 3; 4, 4; **5, 6**. The four vectors are independent (A₄ is "controllable").
  1_N − 1_{N′} = (1,−1,1,−1) is orthogonal to the first three: N′ is the reflection of N in
  the hyperplane they span.
- Total walks: lengths 1, 2, 3 agree (10, 18, 33; Kelly, at most 4 vertices); **length
  4 = n − 1: 61 in B, 63 in C** (iterate the out-lists from the score vectors (2,2,3,1,2) and
  (2,3,2,2,1): (3,4,5,2,4), (6,7,9,4,7), (11,13,17,7,13) and (3,6,3,3,3), (6,9,6,6,6),
  (12,18,12,12,9)).
- Along σ nothing is tied: s_B(1) = 2, s_C(σ1) = s_C(4) = 1. The score of a deleted vertex is
  not a card function for tournaments (arcs(T) − arcs(T − v) = n − 1 always).

Lessons. (i) "Some card has a discrete stable colouring" does not give reconstructibility in
a signature where reconstruction fails, so a graph argument for it must use symmetry.
(ii) For tournaments T0 already fails; in card-aligned form the failure sits in the spanning
layer (walks of length n − 1), which for graphs Theorem H reaches through the split
W_{i+j} = Σ_v w_i(v) w_j(v). (iii) The reflection picture is exact here: one card, a
hyperplane of tied linear statistics, and the second neighbourhood is the mirror image.

## 3. Question 2: the classes that the tied tower gives

### 3.1 Theorem DWC (G-level; paper)

**DWC₀:** 𝒰(G) = ℝⁿ, i.e. the walk vectors and the degree-class indicators of G span.
**DWC₁:** dim 𝒰(G)^⊥ ≤ 1.

**Theorem DWC.** Every graph in DWC₁ is reconstructible.
Argument. By §2.3, Δg = 0 for g ∈ 𝒰(G) (T1 for indicators, T2 for walk vectors, both vectors
being the same for G and G* by T0, T2). If 𝒰 = ℝⁿ then Δ = 0. If 𝒰^⊥ = ℝb, |b| = 1, then
Δ = PΔP = λ·bbᵀ; the diagonal of Δ is zero, so λ·b_v² = 0 for all v, λ = 0. So G* = G and σ is
an isomorphism. ∎

- Contains Godsil–McKay's controllable graphs and t2's child `walk_corank_le_one` (𝒟 = 0
  added). The gain needs at least three distinct degrees: with two degrees a, b the indicators
  are (A1 − b1)/(a − b) and its complement, already in 𝒲. So **on the two-degree face of the
  residual DWC adds nothing to t2's child A**; for graphs with many degrees and small walk rank
  it adds up to (number of degrees − 2) dimensions.
- Symmetry is used three times: T0, Theorem H, and Δ = PΔP.

### 3.2 Theorem WR (card-level; paper)

**WR ("walk-rigid apex"):** some vertex x such that the only δ : V ∖ x → {−1, 0, 1} with
1. δ ≤ 0 on N(x), δ ≥ 0 off N(x),
2. Σ_{w : deg_C w = j} δ(w) = 0 for every j (balanced on every card-degree class),
3. Σ_w δ(w)·(A_C^k 1)(w) = 0 for every k (walk-balanced in the card C = G − x)

is δ = 0. Equivalently: no two disjoint sets S⁻ ⊆ N(x), S⁺ ⊆ V ∖ N[x], not both empty, with the
same card-degree histogram and, for every k, the same number of k-walks of G − x starting in
them. A sufficient linear condition: 1_{N(x)} ∈ 𝒰(G − x) (then ⟨δ, 1_N⟩ = 0 forces
|N′ ∩ N| = d = |N′|).

**Theorem WR.** Every graph in WR is reconstructible.
Argument. δ_x satisfies 1 by definition, 2 by L1 (T1, card form), 3 by T2 (card form). So
δ_x = 0, N′ = N, and f_x extends to an isomorphism. ∎

- Contains: "N(x) is a union of card-degree classes" (classical; here even without 3), Hong's
  class (a controllable card: 3 alone forces δ = 0; Y. Hong 1982 as quoted in Wang–Wen–Guo,
  arXiv:2408.02488, whose abstract I read; pages UNVERIFIED), and every x with
  𝒰(G − x) = ℝ^{V∖x}.
- Not contained in the checked PC⁺ by any argument I have: a PC⁺ phantom has the keys
  (deg, w₂) and one slice; it need not satisfy 3 beyond k = 1.
- Relation to t2's OC′ (re-attachments with the walk and closed-walk sequences of G at v):
  incomparable as stated. OC′ also uses the quadratic data and "≅ G"; WR also uses clause 2.
  **Adding "K has the degree multiset of G" to OC′'s re-attachments makes OC′ contain WR** at
  no cost (the pull-back of H has it, checked `kelly_degree_sequence`).
- WR fails at x whenever some automorphism of G − x moves N(x): then δ = 1_{gN} − 1_N
  satisfies 1–3. That is harmless for the conjecture (C + gN ≅ G) but shows the linear class
  is blind to symmetric cards; the remedy is the "up to Aut(C)" form, which is OC′.

### 3.3 The reflection picture (paper; explains Hong and the tournament pair)

All N′ with the tied linear data of N are the 0/1 points of the affine space 1_N + 𝒰(C)^⊥,
and they lie on a sphere around P_𝒰 1_N. If dim 𝒰(C)^⊥ = 1 there is at most one other
candidate, the mirror image N* (it exists iff 1_N − 2b ∈ {0,1}^V for the component b of 1_N in
𝒰^⊥). With only the Kelly range k ≤ n − 3 a controllable card is exactly in this situation
(n − 2 walk vectors in dimension n − 1): **Kelly alone leaves one bit, N or N\*, and the
spanning-layer count W_{n−1} decides it.** For graphs Theorem H supplies that count; for the
tournament pair of §2.5 the mirror image is the other tournament.

### 3.4 The 1-WL and 2-WL versions: conditional only

Let CC₁ be: for every stable colour class P of G − x, |N′ ∩ P| = |N ∩ P| (class counts are
N-tied). Let CC₂ be the same for the fibres of the coherent closure W(G − x).

- **If CC₁ holds, then** "N(x) is a union of stable classes of G − x for some x" implies
  reconstructible (same three lines as Theorem WR), and in particular "some card has a
  discrete stable colouring" does. Likewise CC₂ for 2-WL.
- CC₁ is the family RK along all rooted trees, restricted to patterns whose size does not
  reach the spanning layer; its first instances are A_paw (if one allows 2-WL patterns) and
  X₁, X₂ of §2.4. I have no argument. For tournaments the analogue is false (§2.5).
- I do **not** propose a class with hypothesis CC₁ or CC₂: its argument is missing.

### 3.5 Degree-equitable graphs: the layered switching set (paper)

Let G be degree-equitable with parameters c_{ab} (§2.2 T4), x of degree a₀, and K any graph
with K − x = G − x, deg_K x = a₀ and the round-2 colour histogram of G (the card-aligned
pull-back of H is one). Then K is degree-equitable with the same c. Write δ = 1_{N_K(x)} − 1_N,
ε_j := δ restricted to the card-degree class D_j, and C_a(j) := Σ_{b>j} c_{ab}.
Comparing, for each vertex w ≠ x and each b, the number of neighbours of degree b in K and in
G, and telescoping over b:

    (A_C ε_j)(w) = 0                                           for w outside supp δ,
    (A_C ε_j)(w) = C_{deg w + δ_w}(j) − C_{deg w}(j) − δ_w·[a₀ > j]   for w in supp δ,

for every j, together with Σ ε_j = 0. So each layer is a signed set with zero net adjacency
to every vertex outside the support and a prescribed integer net adjacency inside.
- Two degrees d, d + 1: only the layer j = d is non-zero and the equations read
  **A_C δ = γδ**, γ = c_{d+1,d+1} − c_{d,d+1} − [a₀ = d + 1]: δ is a {0, ±1}-eigenvector of the
  card supported on the middle degree class. This is the checked US (`unique_switch`) seen
  from the card: US asks that the integer γ is not an eigenvalue of G; the card form needs only
  that G − x has no such eigenvector with ⟨δ, 1_N⟩ ≠ 0.
- A single swap (|supp δ| = 2) forces the two vertices to be twins in G − x, so K ≅ G.
- This uses one card and round-2 keys only, so it is inside the PC⁺ ceiling in spirit (PC⁺
  with the full round-2 key instead of (deg, w₂)). It is a description of the phantoms of
  round 13 on the equitable face, not a new class for this step.

## 4. Question 3: the remainder, and what Babai's theory does not say

**Why "every card non-discrete" is the wrong remainder.** (i) No argument above or in the tree
turns a discrete card into an isomorphism (§3.4). (ii) The condition has no structure: a
graph with two disjoint pairs of twins has a twin pair in every card, the rest of the graph
being arbitrary. (iii) Split-or-Johnson (Babai, arXiv:1512.03547) and Sun–Wilmes
(arXiv:1510.02195; abstract read: primitive coherent configurations with more than
exp(Õ(n^{1/3})) automorphisms are classified) concern **primitive** configurations and
individualise polylogarithmically or n^{1/3} many points. The coherent closure of a card of a
non-regular graph is non-homogeneous; its fibres may be small, imprimitive or of rank 2; and a
card is a single individualisation. The theory gives no statement about how N(x) meets a
fibre. I could not extract any all-n consequence for this step, and I do not think one exists
at this level of generality.

**What the arguments do leave (paper).**

**Theorem R.** Let G be outside DWC₁ and outside WR. Then:
1. dim 𝒰(G)^⊥ ≥ 2: at least two independent vectors orthogonal to all walk vectors and
   summing to zero on every degree class. For any hypomorph, Δ = PΔP lives there.
2. For **every** vertex x there is a non-zero swap (S⁻ ⊆ N(x), S⁺ ⊆ V ∖ N[x]) with equal
   card-degree histograms and equal walk counts of every length in G − x. In particular every
   vertex has a neighbour w and a non-neighbour w′ with deg w = deg w′ + 1 (consecutive
   degrees around every vertex), and no card G − x has 𝒰(G − x) = ℝ^{V∖x}.
3. WL reading, per vertex x, with π the stable partition of G − x (note 𝒰(G − x) ⊆ ℝ^π):
   (b) N(x) is a union of stable classes but 1_N ∉ 𝒰: the "tree gap", closed by CC₁ if CC₁
   were available; or (c) x is **colour-impure**: some w ~ x and w′ ≁ x have the same stable
   colour in G − x. Case (c) splits into (c-sym), w′ = g(w) for an automorphism g of G − x
   (then Aut(G − x) ≠ Aut(G)_x), and (c-ps), a WL-equivalent non-similar pair.
4. (Inherited.) The phantoms and the labelled 4-mate of the frozen statement; t2's Theorem R′
   items for the non-main space.

This is a description, not a classification. Like t2 §7.3, I have **no** argument that the
frozen clauses of the residual force any of 1–3; DWC₁ and WR may be disjoint from the
residual. On the two-degree equitable face item 2 is the eigenvector statement of §3.5.

## 5. Lean

Statable without new definitions (bodies in the style of the frozen statements; none was
elaborated in this session):

- walks from v of length k: `∑ u : Fin n, ({p : G.Walk v u | p.length = k}.ncard : ℤ)`, or with
  `open Classical`, `∑ u, ((G.adjMatrix ℤ) ^ k) v u` (`adjMatrix_pow_apply_eq_card_walk`).
  Card walks: the same for `G.deleteIncidenceSet x` (edges at x removed, same vertex type;
  walks from w ≠ x never reach x).
- **DWC₀:** `∀ f : Fin n → ℤ, (∀ k, ∑ v, f v * walks G k v = 0) → (∀ j, ∑ v, Set.indicator
  {v | (G.neighborSet v).ncard = j} f v = 0) → f = 0`. DWC₁: any two such f are proportional
  over ℤ (`∃ a b, (a ≠ 0 ∨ b ≠ 0) ∧ ∀ v, a * f v = b * g v`).
- **WR:** `∃ x, ∀ δ : Fin n → ℤ, δ x = 0 → (∀ w, G.Adj x w → δ w = 0 ∨ δ w = -1) → (∀ w, ¬ G.Adj
  x w → δ w = 0 ∨ δ w = 1) → (∀ j, ∑ w, Set.indicator {w | ((G.deleteIncidenceSet x).neighborSet
  w).ncard = j} δ w = 0) → (∀ k, ∑ w, δ w * walks (G.deleteIncidenceSet x) k w = 0) → δ = 0`.
- **WL notions without iteration.** A colouring c : Fin n → ℕ is equitable on G − x if equal
  colours have equal numbers of neighbours (other than x) in every colour. The stable
  partition is the coarsest equitable one, and two vertices lie in one stable class iff
  **some** equitable colouring gives them the same colour. So "N(x) is a union of stable
  classes" is `∀ c, Equitable c → ∀ w w', c w = c w' → (G.Adj x w ↔ G.Adj x w')`, and
  colour-impurity is its negation. The 2-WL analogue quantifies over stable colourings of
  pairs. Babai's theorem is not needed anywhere.
