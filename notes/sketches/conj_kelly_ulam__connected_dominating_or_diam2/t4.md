## 1. Verdict

1. **New recognizable invariant (Lemma G, §3).** For every graph F on n − 2 vertices and every
   degree a, the number of *adjacent* ordered pairs (x, y) with deg x = a and G − x − y ≅ F is
   a deck invariant. One line of edge counting plus a telescoping sum. No source found.
2. **New reconstructible class (Lemma P, §4): a pure degree class.** If for some degree a any
   two pairs (x, y), (x′, y′) with deg x = deg x′ = a and G − x − y ≅ G − x′ − y′ are both
   adjacent or both non-adjacent, then G is reconstructible. It contains the regular graphs
   and every graph whose two-vertex-deleted subgraphs are pairwise non-isomorphic (the generic
   graph), and it needs no automorphism hypothesis. Exhaustive n = 8: 6523 of the 7180 graphs
   of the parent class (t1's (★): 3570). It covers all five n = 8 graphs that t2 found outside
   one2 and lemma C, and both n = 7 D2 graphs outside (★).
3. **Lemma C of t2, weakened twice (§5, §6).** The unique-copy hypothesis can be weakened to
   a degree-typed one or replaced by a degree gap, and it no longer needs the Kelly count. The
   rigidity hypothesis becomes a factorization Aut(F) = Stab(N_u)·Stab(N_v). In general an
   anchored pair forces every hypomorphic H to be a *twist* of G by an automorphism of
   G − u − v, and H depends only on a double coset.
4. **Residual (§8).** D2 (or dominating-edge) graphs with no pure degree class and no anchored
   factorizing pair. Purely combinatorial, no "∃ H" clause. Exhaustive n = 8: 31 of 7180 in
   the parent class (26 with a dominating edge, 5 in D2); 22 (3 in D2) when (★) and the
   anchors A2, A3 are used as well.
   Members found by a targeted hunt at n = 12 and n = 16.
   All members found are in t2's one2 class, so the union of the t2 and t4 classes still has
   **no known D2 graph outside it**. That is evidence about the lemmas, not about the conjecture.
5. **Graph-specific ingredient (§10).** The bit law e(G) − e(G − x − y) = deg x + deg y − [x ~ y]
   together with deg x = e(G) − e(G − x). It fails for tournaments (tested: the analogue of
   Lemma G is false on Stockmeyer's pairs at n = 5 and 9) and for 3-uniform hypergraphs (the
   bit becomes a codegree in [0, n − 2]).
6. **No route to the residual.** Every attempt in §9 stops there. The D2 members found all
   have the same shape: two consecutive degrees d, d + 1, half the vertices each, every
   degree class impure, small automorphism group.

## 2. Setting (all inputs checked or elementary)

A hypomorphism is σ : V → V(H) with card isomorphisms f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx. n ≥ 3 throughout.

- **T0 (degree tie).** e(G) = e(H) (`L_kelly_edge_count`) and deg_G x = deg_H σx for the
  *same* σ (`L_kelly_card_edges_add_degree` on both sides, as inside `L_kelly_degree_sequence`).
- **T1 (re-attachment form).** Put N′_u := f_u⁻¹(N_H(σu)) ⊆ V − u. Then |N′_u| = |N(u)|, and
  f_u extended by u ↦ σu is an isomorphism G ≅ H as soon as N′_u = N(u) for one u. (The
  extension is the construction of the checked helper `iso_of_card_iso_of_isolated`.)
- **T2 (level counts; t1 §3, t2 lemma B).** N′_u and N(u) meet every degree level of the card
  G − u in the same number of vertices.

Notation: for u ≠ v, S = V ∖ {u, v}, F = G[S], A = Aut(F), N_u = N(u) ∩ S, N_v = N(v) ∩ S,
A_u = setwise stabilizer of N_u in A, likewise A_v. D_a = set of vertices of degree a.

## 3. Lemma G: the two-deleted deck splits by adjacency

For F on n − 2 vertices and a degree a define, over ordered pairs (x, y), x ≠ y,

    π_a(F) = #{(x, y) : deg x = a, G − x − y ≅ F},   e_a(F) = #{... and x ~ y},   n_a(F) = π_a − e_a.

**L1.** π_a(F; G) = π_a(F; H) for hypomorphic G, H.
Paper argument: (x, y) ↦ (σx, f_x y) is a bijection of ordered pairs; f_x restricted to
V ∖ {x, y} is an isomorphism G − x − y ≅ H − σx − f_x y; deg is tied by T0. (Kelly's lemma is
not used.)

**L2 (Lemma G).** e_a(F; G) = e_a(F; H) for hypomorphic G, H, every F and a.
Paper argument. Put s = e(G) − e(F); it is the same for H by T0.
1. *Bit law* (single graph): e(G − x − y) = e(G) − deg x − deg y + [x ~ y]. So a pair of type F
   has deg x + deg y = s + [x ~ y].
2. The swap (x, y) ↦ (y, x) preserves type and adjacency, hence e_a = e_{s+1−a} and
   n_a = n_{s−a}.
3. π_a − π_{s−a} = (e_a + n_a) − (e_{s−a} + n_{s−a}) = e_a − e_{a+1}, since e_{s−a} = e_{a+1}.
4. Telescoping downward from a ≥ n (where e_a = 0): e_a = Σ_{j ≥ a} (π_j − π_{s−j}).
   The right side is a function of π and s, which agree for G and H by L1 and T0.

Tested as a single-graph identity (`t4_claim_pair_split.py`): NO WITNESS, exhaustive n = 3..7
plus 300 random graphs each on n = 8, 9, 10.

Consequences (paper): the multiset {G − x − y : xy ∈ E(G)} is recognizable, and so is the
multiset over non-edges. Summing over F gives the known "number of edges between degree
classes" (t2 §5 B). Related literature, none stating L2: Gupta–Mangal–Paliwal 2003 (pair
parameters pv, pav; via arXiv:2601.00620, primary UNVERIFIED) and Aravind–Monikandan
arXiv:2601.00620 (parameters for adjacent pairs obtained by recursion; search summary only).
**No source found for L2**; own derivation.

Lean body (elaborates), a `kelly`-program candidate:
```lean
∀ n : ℕ, 3 ≤ n → ∀ G H : SimpleGraph (Fin n), Recon.Hypomorphic G H → ∀ (k : ℕ) (F : SimpleGraph (Fin k)) (a : ℕ), {p : Fin n × Fin n | G.Adj p.1 p.2 ∧ (G.neighborSet p.1).ncard = a ∧ Nonempty (G.induce ({p.1, p.2}ᶜ : Set (Fin n)) ≃g F)}.ncard = {p : Fin n × Fin n | H.Adj p.1 p.2 ∧ (H.neighborSet p.1).ncard = a ∧ Nonempty (H.induce ({p.1, p.2}ᶜ : Set (Fin n)) ≃g F)}.ncard
```

## 4. Lemma P: a pure degree class

Call the degree a **pure** if for every F either all type-F pairs (x, y) with deg x = a are
adjacent or none is (e_a(F) ∈ {0, π_a(F)}). Equivalently: no two pairs (x, y), (x′, y′) with
deg x = deg x′ = a and G − x − y ≅ G − x′ − y′ have x ~ y and x′ ≁ y′. By the bit law such a
violation needs deg y = deg y′ + 1.

**L3 (Lemma P).** If some degree occurring in G is pure, G is reconstructible.
Paper argument. Let a be pure and u ∈ D_a. By L1 and L2, e_a(F; H) = e_a(F; G) ∈ {0, π_a(F; H)},
so a is pure in H as well, with the same alternative for each F. For y ≠ u let F = G − u − y;
then H − σu − f_u y ≅ F and deg_H σu = a, so [σu ~ f_u y] = [u ~ y]. Hence N′_u = N(u) and T1
gives G ≅ H.

Tested in strong single-card form (`t4_claim_pure.py`; no deck presupposed): for a pure
degree a and deg u = a, every one-vertex extension of G − u with the degree sequence of G and
the table π of G attaches the new vertex to exactly N(u). NO WITNESS, exhaustive n = 3..7 plus
150 random graphs each on n = 8, 9. Not vacuous: for n = 5..7 there are 3044 alternative
extensions with the right degree sequence at pure vertices, none isomorphic to G, and π
rejects all of them.

Corollaries (paper):
- **Regular graphs**: one degree class, and equal types force equal edge counts, so pure.
- **Pairwise non-isomorphic two-deleted subgraphs**: every class is pure. This is the generic
  graph (the fact that G(n, ½) has this property is standard; Müller 1976 and Bollobás 1990
  are the usual references, both UNVERIFIED here). No rigidity of Aut(F) is needed, unlike
  lemma C of t2.
- **U: a vertex u of unique degree.** Then G is reconstructible unless the card G − u has two
  removal-similar vertices y, y′ (G − u − y ≅ G − u − y′) with u ~ y, u ≁ y′.
- If the degree set of G contains no two consecutive integers, every class is pure.

Relation to (★) of t1: incomparable. (★) is a condition at one vertex using degree levels;
purity is a condition on a whole degree class using types, which refine levels.

Lean body (elaborates), proposed child `pure_degree_class`:
```lean
Recon.ClassReconstructible (fun n G => ∃ u : Fin n, ∀ x y x' y' : Fin n, (G.neighborSet x).ncard = (G.neighborSet u).ncard → (G.neighborSet x').ncard = (G.neighborSet u).ncard → x ≠ y → x' ≠ y' → Nonempty (G.induce ({x, y}ᶜ : Set (Fin n)) ≃g G.induce ({x', y'}ᶜ : Set (Fin n))) → (G.Adj x y ↔ G.Adj x' y'))
```
Formalizable now: yes (L1, L2 as helpers, then 40 lines). Estimated 500–750 lines in total.

## 5. Anchored pairs (lemma C of t2, first weakening)

Call (u, v) **anchored** for a hypomorphism if f_u(v) = σv and f_v(u) = σu. Three sufficient
conditions, each a paper argument valid for every hypomorphism:

- **A1 (t2 lemma C (i)).** G − u − v has a unique induced copy in G.
  Argument: by L1, Σ_a π_a(F) = 2 in H as well, so H has exactly one unordered pair of type
  F; {σu, f_u v} and {σv, f_v u} are such pairs. (t2 used the checked `kelly_induced_count`;
  L1 avoids it and the transport of F to `Fin k`.)
- **A2 (degree-typed uniqueness).** With a = deg u and c = deg u − [u ~ v]: every ordered pair
  (x, y) of type F with deg x = a or deg x − [x ~ y] = c is (u, v) or (v, u).
  Argument: the set 𝒜 of such pairs is a union of classes e_a, n_a, e_{c+1}, n_c, so
  |𝒜_H| = |𝒜_G| by L2. In H both (σu, f_u v) (first degree a) and (f_v u, σv) (first vertex
  has degree c in H − σv) lie in 𝒜_H. If |𝒜| = 1 they coincide. If |𝒜| = 2 then deg u = deg v,
  𝒜_H = {(σu, f_u v), (f_u v, σu)}, and (f_v u, σv) = (f_u v, σu) would give σv = σu.
- **A3 (degree gap).** Every x ∉ {u, v} has |deg x − deg u| ≥ 2 and |deg x − deg v| ≥ 2.
  Argument: w := f_u(v) has deg_H w = deg_G v − [u ~ v] + [σu ~ w], within 1 of deg_G v, and
  σ⁻¹w ≠ u, so σ⁻¹w = v by T0. Symmetric for f_v(u). Uses T0 only: no Kelly count and no
  transport of F to `Fin k`.

A1 implies A2 (the only ordered pairs of type F are (u, v) and (v, u)); A2 is strictly weaker
(n = 5..7: 7052 ordered A2 pairs against 6764 A1 pairs). A3 is independent of both and the
cheapest to formalize, but degree gaps are rare for small n (§7).

## 6. Gluing by double cosets (second weakening; the group-theoretic part)

**L5 (twist lemma).** Let (u, v) be anchored. Then g := (f_v|_S)⁻¹ ∘ (f_u|_S) ∈ A = Aut(F) and

    H ≅ G_g := the graph on V with G_g[S] = F,  N(u) ∩ S = N_u,  N(v) ∩ S = g(N_v),  [u ~ v] as in G.

Paper argument: f_u(S) = f_v(S) = V(H) ∖ {σu, σv}. N_H(σv) ∩ f_u(S) = f_u(N_v) because
σv = f_u(v); N_H(σu) ∩ f_v(S) = f_v(N_u) = f_u(g⁻¹N_u) because σu = f_v(u). Pull back along
f_u ∘ g⁻¹ on S. The pair {u, v}: degrees (T0).

**L6 (double cosets).** G_g ≅ G_{g′} whenever g′ ∈ A_u g A_v (apply an element of A_u to S).
Hence:
- G has at most |A_u \ A / A_v| reconstructions, and G_1 = G;
- **if A = A_u · A_v, G is reconstructible** (lemma C of t2 is the case A_u = A);
- by Burnside, |A_u \ A / A_v| = (1/|A_u|) Σ_{h ∈ A_u} #{X ∈ A·N_v : hX = X}, and
  A = A_u A_v iff |A| · |A_u ∩ A_v| = |A_u| · |A_v| (a mass formula with one class).

Explicit form used in Lean: for every g ∈ A there is h ∈ A with h(N_v) = N_v and gh(N_u) = N_u.
The map is y ↦ f_u(h y) on S, u ↦ σu, v ↦ σv.

**L7 (admissible twists; lemma B filters the cosets).** If H ≅ G_g is hypomorphic to G, T2
applied at v says that N_v ∖ gN_v and gN_v ∖ N_v have the same multiset of degrees *in the
card G − v* (g preserves F-degrees only). So reconstructibility already follows when every g
with this property, and the symmetric one at u, lies in A_u A_v.

Tested in strong two-card form (`t4_claim_glue.py`; no deck presupposed): for every anchored
pair of kind A1, A2, A3, every one-vertex extension H′ of G − u that has the degree sequence
of G, a second card ≅ G − v at a vertex of degree deg v, and (A1) exactly one copy of F or
(A2) the table π of G, is a twist (new neighbourhood ∈ A·N_u, same [u ~ v]), and is isomorphic
to G when A = A_u A_v. NO WITNESS, exhaustive n = 3..7 plus 150 random graphs each on n = 8, 9.
Not vacuous: for n = 5..7, 4122 / 3838 / 210 surviving H′ ≠ G for A1 / A2 / A3, of which
344 / 0 / 12 are not isomorphic to G, all at pairs where the factorization fails.

## 8. The residual, precisely

## 9. Where every attempt stops

1. **"Every D2 graph has an anchored factorizing pair, or a pure class."** False as a
   single-graph statement from n = 8 (§8). The members are neither rigid (A1–A3 fail or the
   twist group is too big) nor symmetric enough to be regular. The task's proposed dichotomy
   "lemma C or large automorphism group" does not hold: |Aut| is 2, 6 or 8 in the examples.
   A vertex-transitive G is regular (pure); with exactly two vertex orbits nothing is known.
2. **Refining Lemma G beyond degree classes.** The telescoping needs a card-tied vertex
   invariant κ with κ(x) + κ(y) determined by the type and one bit. Degree is the only one I
   found: for triangle counts the correction is [x ~ y]·codeg(x, y), not a bit. A per-vertex
   version of Lemma G ("the number of neighbours y of u with G − u − y ≅ F is tied to the
   card") would make the residual tiny, but for it I have no argument, and it is close to the
   conjecture itself.
3. **Third card for the twists.** L5–L7 reduce an anchored graph to finitely many twists G_g.
   Deciding which twists are hypomorphic to G needs the cards at w ∈ S; T2 gives only L7.
   No further all-n filter found.
4. **Vertex Nash-Williams identity (own derivation, `t4_check_nw_identity.py`, 298 checks).**
   For a bijection p let D_p be the graph of pairs where G and p⁻¹H disagree. Counting
   (p, independent k-set of D_p) gives (n − k)!·Σ_F ind(F, G)·ind(F, H)·|Aut F|, so for
   hypomorphic G, H:
   Σ_p I(D_p(G, H); −1) − Σ_p I(D_p(G, G); −1) = (−1)ⁿ(|Iso(G, H)| − |Aut G|).
   Stops: the independence polynomial at −1 has no sign, so there is no analogue of "every K
   of the right parity is realized" (Nash-Williams, as stated in Maynard–Siemons 2005 Lemma
   2.1; `groups_and_harmonic.md` S2). The identity holds verbatim for tournaments.
5. **Lovász-type inclusion–exclusion (paper, untested).** |Iso(G, H)| − |Aut G| =
   Σ over connected spanning F′ ⊆ G of (−1)^{e(F′)}·(inj(F′, H̄) − inj(F′, Ḡ)), because
   disconnected spanning subgraph counts are recognizable (Tutte 1979 / Kocay 1981,
   UNVERIFIED primaries). Stops: in D2 both G and Ḡ have at least 2n − 5 edges (Erdős–Rényi
   bound for diameter 2 without a universal vertex, UNVERIFIED), so the surviving terms are
   not sparse, and Lovász's edge-count cut-off (Lovász 1972, via ACKR 1989 p. 154) never fires.
6. **Burnside / orbit–fibre counting.** The fibre identity (`groups_and_harmonic.md` E2) and
   the mass formula of §6 are consistent with any value of |Aut H|; whether |Aut G| is
   recognizable is unknown to me (same open gap as in that note).
7. **Complement symmetry.** PURE, GLUE, (★) and one2 are all complement-invariant, and D2 is
   closed under complement, so passing to Ḡ gives nothing new on this branch.

## 10. What breaks for tournaments, 3-uniform hypergraphs, infinite graphs

- **Lemma G and Lemma P (graph-specific).** They use T0 and the bit law.
  *Tournaments*: a card always has C(n − 1, 2) arcs, so scores are not tied to cards (t1 §8),
  and no count sees the direction of the arc xy. Tested (`t4_stockmeyer_check.py`, using the
  construction of `t1_stockmeyer_check.py`, UNVERIFIED against Stockmeyer 1977): for B_k, C_k
  with n = 5 and n = 9 the two-deleted decks and the score sequences agree, but the table
  {(score of x, type of T − x − y) : x → y} differs. So the tournament analogue of Lemma G is
  false, as it must be.
  *3-uniform hypergraphs* (paper): e(𝓗) − e(𝓗 − x − y) = deg x + deg y − codeg(x, y) with
  codeg ∈ [0, n − 2], and the datum to reconstruct is the link, not a bit. The swap relations
  of step 2 do not close.
  *Infinite graphs*: all counts and the telescoping are finite arithmetic.
- **A3** uses T0: fails for tournaments, survives for hypergraphs (paper remark: L5 then
  fails, because the pair {u, v} lies in up to n − 2 edges that no card of u or v shows).
- **A1, L5, L6** are counting plus group theory and hold verbatim for tournaments (t2 §7).
  Stockmeyer's pairs must therefore be non-trivial twists or unanchored. They are not a route.
- **Nýdl-type warning**: Lemma G reads the (n − 2)-deck inside full cards and the exact value
  of n enters through deg = e(G) − e(G − x); nothing here works from a ⌊qn⌋-deck.

## 12. Where this could break

1. **The residual is still the open core.** Children 3 and 4 are smaller and combinatorial,
   but nothing here attacks them. A round spent on them without a new idea closes nothing.
2. **Density of the residual for large n is unknown.** The self-complementary family shows it
   is non-empty at n = 12 and 16; I have no proof that it is non-empty for all n ≡ 0 (mod 4),
   and no count.
3. **`bit_law` in Lean** needs the edge count of a card of a card. The checked statement is
   for `Fin n`; the card lives on a subtype. Either transport or re-derive (60–100 lines).
4. **Types as `Nonempty (… ≃g …)` between two induced subgraphs of the same G** avoid `Fin k`
   in the child statement, but L2 as a standalone statement quantifies over F on `Fin k`;
   inside the proof of L3 one can instead fix F := G.induce {x, y}ᶜ and never mention `Fin k`.
5. **A2 is delicate** (the |𝒜| = 2 case); it is tested but not proposed as a child.
6. **Attribution.** Lemma G, Lemma P, A2, A3 and L5–L7 have no source; if a librarian finds
   them (Bondy–Hemminger 1977, Nash-Williams 1978, Lauri–Scapellato ch. 10 are the places to
   look), only the attribution changes.
7. **Statistics at n = 8 rely on the vertex-extension list** (12346 graphs, the known count).
