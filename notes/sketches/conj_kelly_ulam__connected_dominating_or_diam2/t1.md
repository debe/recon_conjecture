# t1: dominating-edge branch of `conj_kelly_ulam__connected_dominating_or_diam2`

Thinker t1, 2026-11-25. **A sketch, not a result.** This step is an open core of the
conjecture. Nothing here closes it. The deliverable is a reduction plan: one child that the
checked machinery can carry now, one sub-case that the literature covers, and an honest open
residual. Step record: `dead_ends` is empty, so no earlier attempt constrains this one.

Vocabulary: "checked" means a file in `lean/Recon/Checked/`. "The literature states" means a
published or preprint claim that this repo has not checked. "Paper argument" means mine.

Notation. `Dom(G)`: G has a dominating edge, i.e. `∃ u v, G.Adj u v ∧ ∀ x, x = u ∨ x = v ∨
G.Adj x u ∨ G.Adj x v`. Ḡ is the complement. The parent class is

    P = { G : G connected, Ḡ connected, Dom(G) or (diam G ≤ 2 and diam Ḡ ≤ 2) }.

My branch is D = { G : G connected, Ḡ connected, Dom(G) }.

## 1. Summary

1. D is exactly "G, Ḡ connected and diam Ḡ ≥ 3". It splits into two pieces, and P into three:
   - D3 = { Dom(G) and Dom(Ḡ) } = { diam G = diam Ḡ = 3 }, the class 𝒢₃ of Gupta–Mangal–Paliwal;
   - D2 = { Ḡ connected, Dom(G), diam G ≤ 2 };
   - Q = { both diameters ≤ 2 }.
   D is 97% of P at n = 8 (6962 of 7180 graphs), so this branch is most of the parent.
2. One sub-case is within reach of the checked machinery: graphs with a vertex whose
   neighbourhood is forced by degrees (condition (★) below; in the literature a
   "ds-completable card", Steane arXiv:1810.02602, attributed there to Mulla 1978). It contains
   every regular graph. It covers about half of D for n ≤ 8 and thins out as n grows.
3. One sub-case is covered by the literature: triangle-free graphs in D. They all lie in D3,
   and triangle-free graphs of 𝒢₃ are stated reconstructible by Clifton–Liu–Mahmoud–Shantanam
   (arXiv:2210.00338, Theorems 1.5 and 1.9) together with Balakumar–Monikandan (AJC 53, 2012).
   Formalizing that is hard.
4. The residual is open: D3 with a triangle in G and in Ḡ, D2, and Q, each without (★). I have
   no route for any of them.
5. The example in the task (an endpoint of the dominating edge of degree n − 1) is vacuous
   here: a universal vertex makes Ḡ disconnected, which the class excludes. The next case, an
   endpoint of degree n − 2, is a pendant vertex of Ḡ, and graphs with pendant vertices are not
   known reconstructible in general.

## 2. Structure of the class (paper arguments, each tested, see §9)

Write a dominating edge uv and A = N(u) ∖ N[v], B = N(v) ∖ N[u], C = N(u) ∩ N(v), so
V = {u, v} ⊔ A ⊔ B ⊔ C.

- **F1.** uv dominates G iff u, v are non-adjacent in Ḡ with no common Ḡ-neighbour, i.e.
  d_Ḡ(u, v) ≥ 3. Hence D = { G, Ḡ connected, some pair at Ḡ-distance ≥ 3 }. One direction is
  the checked helper `Recon.Checked.R_conj_kelly_ulam_r1.dominating_compl`. (Test T1.)
- **F2.** Dom(G) forces G connected with diam G ≤ 3. So Dom(G) ∧ Dom(Ḡ) iff G, Ḡ connected and
  diam G = diam Ḡ = 3. This describes 𝒢₃ without any distance function, which suits Lean.
  (Test T2.)
- **F3.** P = D3 ⊔ D2 ⊔ Q, disjoint. D2 ∪ Q = { Ḡ connected, diam G ≤ 2 }. (Test T3.)
- **F4.** In D3 take a dominating edge uv of G and a dominating edge xy of Ḡ. Then
  x – u – v – y (or y – u – v – x) is an induced P₄ of G, x and y have no common neighbour, and
  the same four vertices carry the P₄ u – y – x – v of Ḡ. Every other vertex z is adjacent to
  u or v and not to both x and y: 9 of the 16 possible traces on the P₄. (Test T6.)
- **F5.** A cut vertex of a graph with dominating edge uv is u or v, because uv still dominates
  G − w for w ∉ {u, v}. So graphs in D have at most two cut vertices, and all cards except
  possibly G − u and G − v have a dominating edge. (Test T8.)
- **F6.** If G ∈ D is triangle-free then C = ∅, N(u) and N(v) are independent, G is bipartite
  with sides N(v) ∋ u and N(u) ∋ v, G is not complete bipartite (Ḡ is connected), so diam G = 3
  and G ∈ D3. No triangle-free graph lies in D2. (Test T5; the stats give 0 for n ≤ 8.)

## 3. The degree-level lemma (formalizable now)

**Condition (★) at v:** no neighbour x of v and non-neighbour y ≠ v of v satisfy
deg x = deg y + 1.

**Claim K1.** A graph on n ≥ 3 vertices with (★) at some vertex is reconstructible.

Source. Steane, arXiv:1810.02602, §2: a card G − v is "ds-completable" when "for each degree d,
vertices of degree d in the card are either all neighbours or all non-neighbours of v", and
his Theorem 3 (attributed to Mulla 1978) gives the criterion "no non-neighbour of v (excluding
v itself) has a degree one less than a neighbour of v". That is (★). I read it through an
ar5iv extraction, not line by line; the Mulla primary is UNVERIFIED. The same paper reports
that the class holds about half of all graphs on ≤ 9 vertices and 37% on 10, which matches my
counts. The argument below is self-contained and does not depend on that source.

Paper argument. Let σ be a hypomorphism G → H and e : G − v ≅ H − σv.

1. deg_H(σw) = deg_G(w) for every w. This is the argument inside the checked
   `kelly_degree_sequence`: deg = |E| − |E(card)|, using `kelly_edge_count` and
   `kelly_card_edges_add_degree`. So the degree multisets agree, and they still agree after
   removing v on one side and σv on the other.
2. For w ≠ v let c(w) be its degree in the card G − v, N = N_G(v), and
   S = { w ≠ v : H.Adj (σv) (e w) }. Then deg_G(w) = c(w) + 1_N(w) and
   deg_H(e w) = c(w) + 1_S(w), because e preserves card degrees.
3. **Level counts.** For every threshold t,
   #{w ≠ v : c(w) + 1_N(w) ≥ t} = #{w ≠ v : c(w) + 1_S(w) ≥ t} by steps 1–2, and each side is
   #{c ≥ t} + #{c = t − 1, w ∈ N resp. S}. Cancel. So for every level L_k = { w ≠ v : c(w) = k }:

       |N ∩ L_k| = |S ∩ L_k|.

   No induction is needed. This level-count statement is the reusable brick; it is the
   quantitative form of "the degrees of the neighbours of v are reconstructible"
   (Lauri–Scapellato 2003, quoted as Lemma 1.1 in arXiv:2210.00338).
4. (★) says each level is inside N or disjoint from N. In the first case |S ∩ L_k| = |L_k|, so
   L_k ⊆ S; in the second S ∩ L_k = ∅. Hence S = N.
5. An isomorphism e of the cards with e(N_G(v)) = N_H(σv) extends by v ↦ σv to G ≅ H. This
   generalises the checked helper `iso_of_card_iso_of_isolated` in `L_discon_isolated_vertex`
   (the same map; only the adjacency case at v changes).

Tests: T4 confirms the level counts and S = N under (★) for every graph on ≤ 7 vertices, every
vertex and every candidate S, plus random graphs on 8–10 vertices.

Residual means: no (★) at any vertex, and for D3 also a triangle in G and in Ḡ. At n = 8 the
residual is 3572 of 7180 graphs, and the share grows with n: in a random graph every level
holds both neighbours and non-neighbours of every vertex, so (★) fails. The children
`both_dominating` / `triangles_both`, `dominating_diam2` and `both_diam2` are therefore the
conjecture's open core, restated. No argument in this sketch touches them.

What the residual of my branch looks like:
- `triangles_both`: the P₄ frame of F4 with a triangle and an independent triple. Self-
  complementary as a class. Every card except those at the four frame vertices keeps both
  dominating edges (F5).
- `dominating_diam2`: diam G = 2, Dom(G), diam Ḡ ≥ 3. Its complement class is "connected,
  diameter ≥ 3, complement of diameter 2", which contains the complements of pendant-vertex
  graphs: if deg_G(u) = n − 2 then u is a pendant vertex of Ḡ. The level lemma then says the
  one non-neighbour b of u sits in a known level of the card G − u, and (★) at u holds iff
  that level has no other vertex. Otherwise this is the classical open case of pendant vertices.

## 7. Supporting facts for a later attack (not children)

- **S1. The number of dominating edges is a Kelly count.** For an edge uv let miss(uv) be the
  number of vertices adjacent to neither end. Counting pairs (edge, j-set of missed vertices)
  gives M_j := Σ_uv C(miss(uv), j) = Σ over (j + 2)-subsets W of the number of K₂-components of
  G[W]. For connected G on n ≥ 3 vertices miss ≤ n − 3, so

      #dominating edges = Σ_{j=0}^{n−3} (−1)^j M_j,

  and every M_j with j ≤ n − 3 is determined by induced counts on fewer than n vertices, which
  the checked `conj_kelly_ulam__disconnected__kelly_induced_count` supplies. With the checked
  `connected_recognizable` this would give `Recon.Recognizable (fun n G => DOM(G))` without
  the vertex-pair parameters of the literature. Lean: medium (`Int.alternating_sum_range_choose`
  and a double count). `ClassReconstructible` does not need it, but any argument for the
  residual will want H in the same class. (Test T9.)
- **S2. One card is not enough for the triangle-free case.** Let G be triangle-free with
  dominating edge uv, X = N(v), Y = N(u), Y₀ the degree-1 vertices of Y. Paper argument from
  the level counts and the independence of S (the triangle count is a Kelly count): a
  candidate neighbourhood S in the card G − u is Y, or it is (X − u) ∪ Y₀ with
  |Y ∖ Y₀| = |X| − 1 (the "balanced swap": the new vertex is attached to the other side of the
  bipartite core). (Test T7, exhaustive n ≤ 7 and 6000 random bipartite graphs with n ≤ 11.)
  In an exhaustive search the swap gave a graph isomorphic to G for every core with ≤ 4 + 4
  vertices and ≤ 2 pendant vertices, but not in general: G = `J?B}RBOW?N_`, H = `J?B}RBOWFo?` (graph6, n = 11) share the card at vertex 10
  and the degree sequence, both are triangle-free, and they are not isomorphic. They are not
  hypomorphic either, so a second card separates them. A formalization of `triangle_free` must
  therefore follow the multi-card case analysis of the literature; there is no one-card shortcut.

## 8. What breaks for tournaments, 3-uniform hypergraphs, infinite graphs

- **The reduction steps (level 1, level 2).** Case splits plus complement. They assert nothing
  about reconstructibility, so they cannot be wrong in any setting. They have no content for
  tournaments: there every pair is adjacent, the complement is the converse, and "distance ≥ 3
  in the complement" has no meaning. For 3-uniform hypergraphs the dominating-pair lemma has an
  analogue (a pair in no common edge and with no 2-step connection lies in every triple of the
  complement), so the reduction transfers and proves nothing there, as it should.
- **K1, the degree-level lemma.** It looks like pure counting, so it has to break somewhere.
  - *Tournaments:* step 1 breaks. For graphs the degree of the deleted vertex is read off its
    card, deg v = |E(G)| − |E(G − v)|. A tournament card always has C(n − 1, 2) arcs.
    Tested: for Stockmeyer's pair B₂, C₂ on 5 vertices the score analogue of (★) holds at two
    vertices of C₂, the tournaments are hypomorphic and not isomorphic, and the matched
    vertices have different scores (3 vs 2 and 1 vs 2). Script `t1_stockmeyer_check.py`. The
    construction is as I recall it from Stockmeyer, J. Graph Theory 1 (1977) 19–25 (UNVERIFIED
    against the paper; the script confirms hypomorphic and non-isomorphic directly).
  - *3-uniform hypergraphs:* step 1 survives, step 5 breaks. Re-attaching a vertex needs its
    link, a graph on the other n − 1 vertices, and vertex degrees give only the degree
    sequence of that link.
  - *Infinite graphs:* the edge count and the level counts are finite cardinal arithmetic.
- **`triangle_free`.** The cited proofs use the unique bipartition of a connected bipartite
  graph, cut sets and distances: undirected, binary, finite. No tournament analogue exists.
- **The open children.** Nothing is claimed. A future argument that does not use finiteness,
  undirected binary edges and near-full cards would also apply in a failing setting, and would
  therefore be wrong (`structural_reductions.md`, "What breaks in failing settings").

## 10. Where this could break

1. **K1 is thin.** It removes about half of the small cases and a vanishing share of large
   ones. Its value is a reusable checked brick (level counts, the extension lemma) and the
   regular graphs as a corollary, not a dent in the open core.
2. **`triangle_free` rests on a preprint and an unread 2012 paper.** If either has a gap, the
   child is merely open, never unsound: the gate would not accept it. Formalizing it needs a
   connectivity case analysis (κ = 1, 2, ≥ 3) without Mathlib support for vertex connectivity.
   Risk high.
3. **The open children carry `¬ STAR`.** That keeps the partition exact but makes them awkward
   to match against the literature, which never states that hypothesis. Dropping it is safe
   (the classes only grow), and then K1 is no longer needed by the implication and should be
   proposed as a root in the `regular` program instead.
4. **K1's statement and the checked degree statement.** `S_kelly_degree_sequence` exposes only
   `∃ σ`, not that the hypomorphism itself preserves degrees. The builder must re-derive that
   from `kelly_edge_count` and `kelly_card_edges_add_degree`; both are checked.
5. **No route for the residual.** If a round is spent on `triangles_both`,
   `dominating_diam2` or `both_diam2` without a new idea, it will close nothing.
