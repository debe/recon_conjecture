## 1. Notation and inputs

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3. A hypomorphism to H is σ with card isomorphisms f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx.
P_k(V) = ordered k-tuples of distinct vertices. For p = (x; y₁, …, y_{k−1}) ∈ P_k the
**k-pointed card** is (G − x; y₁, …, y_{k−1}); p ≈ q means the pointed cards are isomorphic
(points in order), also across two graphs.

- **T0 (checked inputs).** e(G) = e(H); deg x = e(G) − e(G − x). So K ~ G and K − a ≅ G − x give
  deg_K a = deg_G x.
- **L1^k (transfer).** Φ_k(x; y⃗) = (σx; f_x y⃗) is a bijection P_k(G) → P_k(H) with
  Φ_k(p) ≈ p. (Round-11 t3, L1⁺, verbatim for k points.) Hence the number of tuples of each
  k-pointed type, of each type of G − p, and of each pointed sub-deletion is the same in G and H.
- Round-11 results used as vocabulary: Theorems T, T⁺ with clauses (a⁺), (b⁺), (d), (i), (ii),
  (iii); Lemma A (twist), Lemma B (degree-preserving twists are trivial).

## 2. The anchoring chain

**Definition.** H is **k-anchored** to G at (p, q), p ∈ P_k(G), q ∈ P_k(H), if for every i there is
an isomorphism e_i : G − p_i → H − q_i with e_i(p_j) = q_j for all j ≠ i.

**Lemma AC (paper).**
1. (k+1)-anchored at (p, q) implies k-anchored at every pair of sub-tuples (drop a coordinate).
2. H ~ G implies 1-anchored at (x, σx) for every x.
3. For n ≥ 3: n-anchored ⟺ G ≅ H. (The e_i are restrictions of one bijection φ, and every pair
   of vertices avoids some p_i.) For n ≥ 4 already (n − 1)-anchored implies G ≅ H.

So for hypomorphic G, H there is an **anchoring depth** d(G, H) = max k, 1 ≤ d ≤ n, and the
conjecture says d = n for n ≥ 3. Theorems T and T⁺ are statements "d ≥ 2 at a named pair, and
then the twist is trivial". The checked `unique_copy_gluing` is the same with a unique-copy
anchor. t1's C₃ is "d ≥ 3 at a named triple, then the twist is trivial".
Computed (§8): every non-reconstructible digraph pair I could enumerate has d ≤ 2.

## 3. Twists of a k-anchored hypomorph: coset simplices

Let H be k-anchored at (p, q), k ≥ 2. S = V ∖ {p₁, …, p_k}, F = G[S], N_i = N(p_i) ∩ S,
A_i = stabilizer of N_i in Aut(F). Every e_i maps S onto V(H) ∖ q.
Put g_i := (e₁|S)⁻¹ ∘ (e_i|S) ∈ Aut(F), g₁ = 1.

**Lemma CS (coset simplex; paper).**
1. For x ∈ S and i ≠ j: [q_j ~ e_i x] = [p_j ~ x]. Hence N′_j := {x ∈ S : q_j ~ e₁ x} equals
   g_i N_j for **every** i ≠ j. Consequences: g_i⁻¹ g_{i′} ∈ A_j whenever j ∉ {i, i′}.
2. For k ≥ 3: [q_i ~ q_j] = [p_i ~ p_j] (seen in e_l, l ∉ {i, j}). For k = 2 the bit is not seen;
   for graphs it follows from T0 (round-11 Lemma A.1).
3. H ≅ G′, the graph on V that agrees with G except that p₁ is attached inside S to
   N′₁ = g₂N₁ (= g_iN₁ for all i ≥ 2). All other attachments are those of G.
4. Conversely every family (g_i) as in 1 gives a graph G′ that is k-anchored to G at (p, p).
5. Put X_j := g_i A_j (any i ≠ j; well defined by 1). Any k − 1 of the cosets X₁, …, X_k have a
   common element (g_i ∈ X_j for all j ≠ i). **If all k have a common element c, then G ≅ H**:
   c N_j = N′_j for all j, so c ∪ id_p is an isomorphism G → G′.

For k = 2: X₁ = g₂A₁, X₂ = A₂, no intersection is given, and "X₁ ∩ X₂ ≠ ∅ for all g₂" is
Aut(F) = A₂A₁, the factorization clause of the checked gluing class. For k = 3 the three cosets
meet pairwise, and the sufficient condition is the Helly property below. This is t1's Lemma G
and clause (OI), obtained independently; I keep only what differs: general k and the
"(k − 1)-wise ⇒ k-wise" form.

**Lemma H (three subgroups; paper, elementary).** For subgroups A₁, A₂, A₃ of a group, equivalent:
(H1) any three pairwise meeting cosets x₁A₁, x₂A₂, x₃A₃ have a common element;
(H2) A₃ ∩ A₁A₂ = (A₃ ∩ A₁)(A₃ ∩ A₂); (H2′) the same after any permutation of the indices.
Argument. (H2) ⇒ (H1): translate so that X₁ = A₁, X₂ = A₂; X₃ = xA₃ meets them in a₁ = xs,
a₂ = xs′. Then a₁⁻¹a₂ = s⁻¹s′ ∈ A₃ ∩ A₁A₂, so s⁻¹s′ = b₁b₂ with b_i ∈ A₃ ∩ A_i, and
c := a₁b₁ = a₂b₂⁻¹ lies in A₁ ∩ A₂ ∩ xA₃. (H1) ⇒ (H2): for t = a₁a₂ ∈ A₃ the cosets A₁, a₁A₂,
A₃ meet pairwise (in a₁, 1, t); a common element c gives t = c·(c⁻¹t) with c ∈ A₃ ∩ A₁,
c⁻¹t ∈ A₃ ∩ A₂. (H1) is symmetric, hence (H2′). ∎
Standard in coset geometries (Tits; Buekenhout–Cohen, *Diagram Geometry*, 2013: a web search did
not return the statement, so the attribution is UNVERIFIED; the argument above is self-contained).

Cases where (H2) is automatic: two of the A_i nested (Dedekind's modular law; Mathlib
`Subgroup.mul_inf_assoc`, `Subgroup.inf_mul_assoc`); Aut(F) cyclic (lcm and gcd distribute); in
particular Aut(F) trivial or of prime order. The smallest failure is three distinct subgroups of
order 2 in a Klein four-group. Tested: §10, `t3o_claim_triangle.py` (items 3, 4 as an "iff").

## 4. Three kinds of census at level k

Every census argument has the form: L1^k fixes the number of tuples of each type in H; a counting
or uniqueness hypothesis on G then forces a tuple q of H whose k pointed cards have the types
of the k pointed cards of one tuple p of G, i.e. a k-anchor. The hypotheses come in three kinds.

**(α) Pointed refinement (apex form, k = 3).** For distinct u, v, w:
- (a1) every (x; y, z) with (G − x − y; z) ≅ (G − u − v; w) satisfies (x; y, z) ≈ (u; v, w) or
  (x; y, z) ≈ (v; u, w);
- (a2) every (x; y, z) with (G − x − y; z) ≅ (G − u − w; v) satisfies (x; y, z) ≈ (u; w, v) or
  (x; y, z) ≈ (w; u, v);
- (b) for (u, v): clause (i) of T⁺ (deg u − deg v ∉ {−α, 1 − α}) or (u; v, w) ≈ (v; u, w); the
  same for (u, w).

*Claim A3 (paper).* Under (a1), (a2), (b) every H ~ G is 3-anchored at (u, v, w).
Argument. (a; b, c) := Φ₃(u; v, w) has e₁ : G − u → H − a, v ↦ b, w ↦ c. The H-triple (b; a, c) is
Φ₃ of some (x; y, z), and (G − x − y; z) ≅ (H − b − a; c) ≅ (G − u − v; w) (restrict e₁). By (a1)
its type is that of (u; v, w) or of (v; u, w). The first gives deg_H b = deg u (T0) and
deg_H b = deg v − α + [a ~ b] (from e₁), against (i), unless the two types coincide. So
(H − b; a, c) ≈ (G − v; u, w). The card of c is handled by (a2) and (i) for (u, w). ∎
With Lemma CS.5 and (H2) this is a class ("T3" in the scripts). Graph-specific through (i).

**(β) Unique copy.** K a k-set, k ≥ 2 (the twist lemma for it needs k ≥ 3 or T0), such that
- (U_k) G − K has a unique copy among the k-subsets;
- (b_k) the k graphs G − (K ∖ z), z ∈ K, are pairwise non-isomorphic.

*Claim U (paper).* Under (U_k), (b_k) every hypomorphism has f_x(z) = σz for all x ≠ z in K, so H
is k-anchored at (K, σK).
Argument. L1^k: H has exactly one k-set K_H with H − K_H ≅ G − K, and σx ∪ f_x(K ∖ x) = K_H for
x ∈ K; so σK = K_H and θ_x := σ⁻¹f_x permutes K ∖ x. Put E_G(z) = type of G − (K ∖ z),
E_H(c) = type of H − (K_H ∖ c). Restricting f_x: E_G(z) = E_H(σθ_x z) for z ≠ x. So E_G and E_H∘σ
have the same multiset on K ∖ x for every x; adding over x (k − 1 times the multiset on K) and
subtracting, E_H(σx) = E_G(x) for all x ∈ K. E_G is injective on K by (b_k), so θ_x = id. ∎
For k = 3 this is t1's Lemmas C and S with the stricter straightness clause (b₃); t1's (S) also
allows "some automorphism of the card G − x swaps the other two", which is what the known
members need (§7). Nothing here is graph-specific.

**(γ) Counting with the degree clause** (T, T⁺ of round 11; t1 §9.2 and sibling t2 for
triples). Not developed here; §5.1 says what it can gain from a third point.

## 5. Ties

### 5.1 Proposition M: a pointed refinement never breaks a tie

**Proposition M (paper).** If (a1) holds at (u; v, w) for some w, then every ordered pair (x, y)
with G − x − y ≅ G − u − v satisfies (x, y) ≈ (u, v) or (x, y) ≈ (v, u). So the pair census of T⁺
at (u, v) holds with empty remainder ℛ (resp. that of T with ℬ = ∅) whenever the apex triple
census holds.
Argument. Let φ : G − x − y → G − u − v and z := φ⁻¹(w). Then (G − x − y; z) ≅ (G − u − v; w), so
(a1) gives (x; y, z) ≈ (u; v, w) or ≈ (v; u, w); forget the last point. ∎

Counting form (paper, same idea): with 𝒜₃ the class of (u; v, w), o = |Aut(G − u)_v · w| and
O = |Aut(G − u − v) · w| ≥ o: |𝒜₃| = o·|𝒜| and every (x, y) ∈ ℛ contributes O triples to the
triple remainder, so |ℛ₃| < |𝒜₃| implies |ℛ| < |𝒜|.

Reading. **An unrelated copy of G − u − v carries an unrelated copy of every pointed
G − u − v.** A census of kind (α) at level 3 anchors a triple only where the pair census already
anchors both pairs through the apex. Its only gain over T⁺ is the twist clause (Lemma CS.5
instead of (iii)). Tested: `t3o_claim_monotone.py`.

This is the analogue of Proposition L one level up: Proposition L says a first-vertex key cannot
refine a degree class that is an orbit; Proposition M says a second point cannot separate two
pairs that the two-deleted subgraph does not separate. Neither needs a group.

### 5.2 Proposition L_k: unique-copy censuses on a vertex-transitive graph minus a vertex

**Proposition L_k (paper; all n, all 1 ≤ k ≤ n).** Let X be a graph on V ∪ {r}, Γ ≤ Aut(X)
transitive on V ∪ {r}, G = X − r, K ⊆ V with |K| = k, T = K ∪ {r}, Γ_T its setwise stabilizer.
1. The number of k-sets K′ ⊆ V with G − K′ ≅ G − K is at least (k + 1)·|Γ_r| / |Γ_T|.
2. If G − K has a unique copy, then Γ_r ≤ Γ_T, Γ_T is transitive on T, and T is a block of
   imprimitivity of Γ. In particular k + 1 divides n + 1.
3. In that case the graphs G − (K ∖ z), z ∈ K, are pairwise isomorphic: (b_k) fails, and t1's
   clause (S) can hold only through its second alternative.
4. If Γ is primitive on V ∪ {r} (for example n + 1 prime), no k-set with 1 ≤ k < n has a unique
   copy: the checked `unique_copy_gluing`, C₃ and every U_k are void on G.

Argument. 1: for γ ∈ Γ with r ∈ γT, K′ := γT ∖ {r} has G − K′ = X − γT ≅ X − T. The orbit of T has
|Γ|/|Γ_T| members; counting pairs (T′, t ∈ T′) and using transitivity, each vertex lies in
(k + 1)|Γ| / ((n + 1)|Γ_T|) = (k + 1)|Γ_r|/|Γ_T| of them. 2: uniqueness gives |Γ_T| = (k + 1)|Γ_r|;
the Γ_T-orbit of r lies in T and has size |Γ_T : Γ_T ∩ Γ_r| ≥ k + 1 = |T|, so equality holds
everywhere. The orbit of a subgroup containing the point stabilizer is a block (Mathlib:
`MulAction.IsBlock.of_orbit`, `MulAction.block_stabilizerOrderIso`), and a block's size divides
the degree. 3: γ ∈ Γ_T with γz = z′ maps T ∖ z to T ∖ z′, and both contain r. 4: a primitive group
has only trivial blocks, of size 1 and n + 1. ∎

Remarks.
- For Γ regular (X a GRR): G − K is a unique copy only if K ∪ {1} is a **subgroup** of order k + 1.
  This explains round-11 t3 §2 ("3 or 6 copies, never 1"): 13 is prime.
- Second alternative of (S) at a block T of size 4: for each x ∈ K the group induced on T must
  contain an element preserving {r, x} and swapping the other two points; true if it contains
  the Klein four-group, false for a cyclic group of order 4.
- Only "unique ⇒ block" has an argument. The converse needs that every isomorphism between
  k-deleted subgraphs comes from Γ ("tightness"), which I cannot argue in general.
- Computed (`t3o_blocks.py`, ten circulants on 12–16 vertices minus a vertex, k ≤ 3): the
  unique-copy k-sets are exactly the subgroups minus 0, for example {6}, {4, 8}, {3, 6, 9} in
  C₁₂(1, 2, 3) − 0, and there is none in C₁₃(1, 2, 4) − 0 (= `KbcrMZwA~c@r`) or C₁₃(1, 5) − 0.

### 5.3 Kind (α) on a graphical regular representation minus a vertex

Let Γ = Aut(X) be regular, G = X − 1, and assume tightness. Ordered k-tuples of G correspond to
Γ-orbits of ordered (k + 1)-tuples t of X (first entry in the role of r); the k-pointed type of t
is {t, t∘(1 2)} (exchange the roles "r" and "deleted vertex"; the card isomorphism is the left
translation that is not an automorphism of G). So:
- each k-pointed class has two members, lying in two different k-sets of G;
- a pointed two-deleted class (G − x − y; z⃗) holds **three** (k = 3: 2-pointed) types, one for each
  choice of the "r"-role among {r, x, y}. So (a1) fails at every triple: the apex census is tied.
  This is the 3-cycle of round-11 t3 §5, one level up, and it is forced by Proposition M.
- The pair census of T⁺ is not tied there (round 11: all members found are in T⁺).

Computed (`t3o_vt_apex.py`): in C₁₃(1, 2, 4) − 0 all 110 pointed two-deleted classes hold exactly
three 2-pointed types and no triple passes the apex census; in the D₆ member
``JhCkmT`kIw_`` 147 of 156 classes hold three; both have pairs with the T and the T⁺ census.

### 5.4 Is a tie at all levels a group condition?

Not in the full sense: by Lemma AC a hypomorph that is anchored at no level above d is a
counterexample, so "every census at every level ties, for every admissible notion of census" is
the negation of reconstructibility and cannot follow from a symmetry hypothesis that
reconstructible graphs satisfy. The group-theoretic statements available are the two above:
blocks for kind (β), the role exchange for kind (α). Kind (γ) is where the graph-specific
degree clause (i) enters, and it is the kind that does not tie on the known examples.

## 6. Structure: one-vertex completions and the card groupoid

### 6.1 Theorem S (paper)

The card isomorphisms of G form a groupoid (objects V, morphisms x → x′ the isomorphisms
G − x → G − x′). For R ⊆ V let X_R := G + r with N(r) = R. Call a card isomorphism
f : G − x → G − x′ an **R-swap** if f(R ∖ x) = N(x′), f(N(x)) = R ∖ x′ and (x ∈ R ⟺ x′ ∈ R); its
completion f^× is the permutation of V ∪ {r} that extends f by x ↦ r, r ↦ x′.

**Theorem S.** Aut(X_R) = {φ ∪ (r ↦ r) : φ ∈ Aut(G), φR = R} ⊔ {f^× : f an R-swap}. Hence the
orbit of r under Aut(X_R) is {r} ∪ {targets of R-swaps}, and **G is a vertex-deleted subgraph of
a vertex-transitive graph iff some R ⊆ V has an R-swap into every vertex.** If X_R is regular of
degree d, then R is the set of vertices of degree d − 1 of G, so R is determined by G.
Argument. γ ∈ Aut(X_R) with γr = r restricts to an automorphism of G preserving N(r). If
γr = x′ ≠ r and x := γ⁻¹r, then f := γ|(V ∖ x) is an isomorphism X − r − x → X − x′ − r, and
γ(N_X(r)) = N_X(x′), γ(N_X(x)) = N_X(r) are the three swap conditions (the bit x ∈ R is the edge
rx, mapped to the edge x′r). Conversely the swap conditions say that f^× preserves the pairs
through x and through r; the remaining pairs lie in G − x. ∎

So the answer to "when is the groupoid a group": the sub-family Aut(G)_R ∪ Swap(R) is closed
under the completed composition and is the group Aut(X_R); it acts on V ∪ {r}, not on G.
An R-swap into x′ twists x′ (in the sense of round-11 t2) unless r and x′ are twins in X_R.
A card automorphism ρ of G − x with ρ²N(x) = N(x) is an R-swap for R ∖ x = ρN(x): every vertex
twisted by an involutive card automorphism lies in a 2-point orbit {x, r} of its own completion.

### 6.2 Relation to pseudosimilarity

For X = X_R and γ = f^×, the vertices x = γ⁻¹r and x′ = γr of G = X − r are removal-similar; this is
the one-deleted-vertex case of the construction attributed to Herndon–Ellzey and Godsil–Kocay
(JCTB 32 (1982) 146–155). Secondary sources say that Godsil and Kocay obtain every pseudosimilar
pair from an automorphism of a larger graph with part of an orbit deleted, and that Kocay
(J. Austral. Math. Soc. A 37 (1984) 181–189, Theorem 3.3) extends X to a graph Y in which every
isomorphism between the vertex-deleted subgraphs of k mutually pseudosimilar vertices is induced
by an automorphism of Y. I read neither paper; statements as returned by a web search of the
Cambridge record and of arXiv:2609.00394, **UNVERIFIED**. In that language Theorem S is the
exact criterion for |V(Y) ∖ V(X)| = 1, and the members of this residual show that one added
vertex is not enough.

Shape of the three members: asymmetric, pairwise non-isomorphic cards, every card has an
automorphism group of order 2 or 4. Each vertex x is twisted by a card *automorphism* ρ_x, each
ρ_x has its own completion R_x, and the R_x do not agree. There is no group acting on V ∪ {r}
that explains all twists at once, and none is needed for the census: with pairwise
non-isomorphic cards σ is forced and the pair census anchors (§7).

For the tie of kind (β) the converse fails for a trivial reason: "no k-set has a unique copy"
holds, for example, in any graph in which every vertex has a twin and K is not a union of twin
classes. A unique-copy tie only says that every deleted subgraph repeats.

The step where a structure theorem fails, exactly: from "for every u there is a swap into u with
respect to some R_u" one cannot pass to a common R. An R-swap f : G − x → G − u forces
R ∖ u = f(N(x)), so one swap into u determines R up to the bit u ∈ R; a common R exists only if
the sets obtained from the swaps into the different vertices agree. In the members they do
not (at most 2, 2, 4 points of V ∪ {r} are served by one R).

## 9. Leads

### 9.1 For this step: the twist clause

Both residual twins have, as far as known, no census-tied member. Two ways to strengthen the
twist clause are on the table, both in sibling sketches; I only cross-checked them.
- **Three cards (t1, class C₃).** Generic; contains the three members through (S), second
  alternative. By Proposition L_k it is void on X − r unless a block of size 4 through r exists.
- **Two cards, T⁺ with clause (iii′)** instead of (iii): for every (x, y) in the pointed class 𝒜
  and every automorphism g of G − x − y whose twist of x passes the level-count degree filter
  (round-11 t3 §3.1), g factorizes as in the checked gluing clause. Paper argument: T⁺ steps
  1–2 anchor H at some (x′, y′) ∈ 𝒜; Lemma A.3 and T0 give that the twist passes the filter (the
  degree multiset on S is that of G); (iii′) gives the factorization; the endgame is that of the
  checked `unique_copy_gluing`. T⁺ ⊆ T⁺(iii′) by Lemma B (a degree-preserving twist that passes
  the filter has ε = 0). Computed (`t3o_pair_adm.py`): the class contains `Ffk}W` (4 ordered
  pairs), `Ff]mw` (6), `Gl]o^[` (15). Sibling t2 studies this family of clauses; I did not test the
  census form of this variant.

### 9.2 For the symmetric face (D2 twin)

Proposition L_k tells the reducer where not to look: on a primitive X − r no unique-copy class
at any level applies, and Proposition M says a pointed triple census cannot help either. What
remains there is kind (γ) with a better twist clause, or an argument that uses the group: by
Theorem S the hypomorph H would have to be compared with X itself, which is regular, hence
reconstructible by the classical argument (CLAUDE.md §3; `desk.py list` shows no checked step
for regular graphs). I did not find an argument that passes from "X is reconstructible" to
"X − r is".

### 9.3 The apex class T3 (kind (α)), for the record

Hypotheses (a1), (a2), (b) of §4 and the Helly clause of §3 at (u, v, w). Computed
(`t3o_stats.py`): 4, 20, 106, 860 graphs for n = 4, 5, 6, 7; the Helly clause never fails at a
census-anchored apex triple for n ≤ 7; 94 graphs at n = 7 are in T3 or in (β)-strict and outside
T ∪ T⁺. It contains **no** known member of this residual, so I do not propose it as a child.

## 12. Where this could break

1. **Proposition L_k is one-directional.** "Block ⇒ unique copy" is not claimed. Item 3 kills
   only the strict (b_k); whether t1's (S) holds at a block depends on the group induced on it.
2. **§5.3 assumes tightness** (all isomorphisms between deleted subgraphs come from Aut(X)). It is
   an explanation of the computed counts, not an argument for a family.
3. **Proposition M is stated for the uniqueness form and for one natural counting form.** A
   census that mixes triple-deleted types with pointed types (kind (β) without uniqueness;
   t1 §9.2, sibling t2) is not covered: Claim U shows that a triple can be anchored where the
   pair census is tied at (u, v). So "ties propagate upward" is false for kind (β).
4. **§5.5 is n ≤ 7 only.** Ties of the pair census may appear at larger n; I have no example and no
   argument either way.
5. **"Depth ≤ 2 for digraph counterexamples" is an observation on 22 pairs**, of which only
   Stockmeyer's n = 9 pair is not forced by a cyclic Aut(F). It says nothing about Stockmeyer's
   larger families, and I did not check that the n = 5 tournament pair found by enumeration is
   B₂, C₂ (the numbers of 2-anchorings agree: 12 ordered = 6 partial bijections).
6. **Transcriptions.** I did not re-derive the residual predicate from the frozen Lean body; the
   three members are taken from the task and from t1. T and T⁺ are the round-11 Python
   predicates. An earlier version of my pointed-class routine used two quantities that are not
   invariants of the pointed card (deg_G of a point, the bit to the deleted vertex); it was
   corrected and every number in this file comes from the corrected routine.
7. **Theorem S covers one added vertex.** The many-vertex version is Kocay's Theorem 3.3 as
   reported (UNVERIFIED); I did not work it out.
8. **Attribution.** I found no source for Propositions M, L_k, Theorem S or the anchoring chain.
   They are elementary; L_k is orbit counting, S is bookkeeping.
