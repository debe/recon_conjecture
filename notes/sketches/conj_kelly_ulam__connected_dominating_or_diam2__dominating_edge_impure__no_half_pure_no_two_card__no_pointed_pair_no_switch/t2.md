## 1. Notation and inputs

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3; a hypomorphism to H is σ with card isomorphisms f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx.
deg = degree in G unless a subscript says otherwise. For u ≠ v: S = V ∖ {u, v},
F = G[S] = G − u − v, α = [u ~ v], δ = deg v − α = deg_{G−u} v.

- **T0 (checked).** e(G) = e(H); deg_K x = e(K) − e(K − x). Hence for K hypomorphic to G and any
  isomorphism K − a ≅ G − x: deg_K a = deg_G x (checked helper `deg_T0`).
- **Doubly pointed card** (G − x; y, z), x, y, z distinct. (x; y, z) ≈ (x′; y′, z′) means: an
  isomorphism G − x → G − x′ with y ↦ y′, z ↦ z′ (also across two graphs).
  It implies (G − x, y) ≈ (G − x′, y′) (pointed cards of t3) and
  (G − x − y; z) ≈ (G − x′ − y′; z′) (the **pointed port**: restrict the isomorphism).
- **L1⁺⁺ (triple transfer).** Φ₃(x, y, z) = (σx, f_x y, f_x z) is a bijection from the ordered
  triples of distinct vertices of G onto those of H, and Φ₃(x, y, z) ≈ (x, y, z) through f_x.
  So every set of triples defined by the type of (x; y, z), by the pointed port type, and by
  deg x (T0: deg_H σx = deg_G x) has the same size in G and in H.
  Same argument as the checked `census_A` / `census_R` (pattern `pairEquiv`); no Kelly count.

## 2. Why the known members are outside T and T⁺ (computed, `t2_probe_known.py`)

All six members have |Aut G| = 1. The three named ones (n = 7, 8), looked at pair by pair,
have two-deleted subgraphs with up to 8 automorphisms.
- The census of T⁺ ((i) and (ii)) holds at several pairs in each of the three. What fails is
  (iii): some automorphism of G − x − y does not preserve G-degrees.
- In several of these cases the offending automorphisms are harmless: they fix N(x) ∖ {y}, so
  the twist they define is G itself. Example `Gl]o^[`, pair (5, 0): |Aut F| = 2, both automorphisms
  fix N(5), one of them moves degrees. T⁺ asks for degree preservation and so rejects a pair
  at which every twist is trivial.
- `Ff]mw`: at every pair passing a pair census some automorphism of F is a non-trivial twist
  that moves degrees; at (4, 2) the two such automorphisms move the vertex 0, and the
  stabiliser of 0 consists of trivial twists.

So the residual members are not "symmetric face" graphs (Proposition L does not apply: all
vertices have distinct cards). They are asymmetric graphs whose *cards and two-deleted
subgraphs* have automorphisms. The fix is in the kill clause, and the third point is the
natural way to shrink the group the kill clause is asked of.

## 3. Pointed anchors and pointed twists (paper)

Say H is **anchored at (u, v; w)** if there are distinct a, b, c in H and isomorphisms
f : G − u → H − a with f(v) = b, f(w) = c, and f″ : G − v → H − b with f″(u) = a, f″(w) = c.

**Lemma A₃.** Let H be hypomorphic to G and anchored at (u, v; w). Then
1. deg_H a = deg_G u, deg_H b = deg_G v (T0);
2. f(S) = f″(S) = V(H) ∖ {a, b}, and g := (f″|S)⁻¹ ∘ (f|S) is an automorphism of F with
   **g(w) = w** (f(w) = c = f″(w));
3. for x ∈ S: [a ~ f x] = [u ~ g x], and deg_H f(x) = deg x − [u ~ x] + [u ~ g x];
4. [a ~ b] = [u ~ v] (by 1 and 3: |N_H(a) ∩ f(S)| = |N(u) ∩ S|). So H is isomorphic to the
   **twist** G_g: the graph that agrees with G off u and has u adjacent to
   {x ∈ S : u ~ g x}, and to v iff u ~ v;
5. Σ_{x∈S} (deg_H f x)² = Σ_{x∈S} (deg x)² (T0 termwise over σ, minus the terms a, b and u, v).

1–3 and 5 are Lemma A of t3 (checked inside `endgame`); the only new line is g(w) = w.
Conversely every twist by g ∈ Aut(F)_w shares both doubly pointed cards with G (tested,
claim A of `t2_claims.py`), so Lemma A₃ loses nothing: the candidates for H at an anchor are
exactly the twists by the pointed stabiliser.

**Lemma K (kill ladder).** In Lemma A₃ put d_x = deg x and ε_x = [u ~ g x] − [u ~ x] on S.
By 3 and 5: 2 Σ_S d_x ε_x + Σ_S ε_x² = 0. If ε ≡ 0 then f ∪ {u ↦ a} is an isomorphism G → H
(checked tail of `endgame`). Each of the following hypotheses on g gives ε ≡ 0, and each
implies the next:
- (K-triv) g fixes N(u) ∩ S. Then ε ≡ 0 by definition; no degree argument.
- (K-dp) g preserves G-degrees on S. Lemma B of t3: Σ d_x [u ~ g x] = Σ d_{gx} [u ~ g x]
  = Σ d_y [u ~ y], so Σ d ε = 0, so Σ ε² = 0.
- (K-mw) "minimum weight": Σ_{x∈S, u~gx} d_x ≥ Σ_{x∈S, u~x} d_x. Then Σ d ε ≥ 0 and
  Σ ε² ≥ 0 sum to 0, so both vanish. (K-triv and K-dp are the equality cases.) Read over a
  group: N(u) ∩ S has the least degree-sum among its images.
- (K-sq) the twist is trivial or changes the sum of squared degrees. This is the weakest
  hypothesis that the identity 5 can use.

Beyond the identity: (K-seq) "G_g has another degree multiset or G_g ≅ G", and the ceiling
(K-iso) "G_g hypomorphic to G ⇒ G_g ≅ G". With (K-iso) the theorem below becomes a pure
reduction: *census ⇒ every reconstruction of G is a twist by a pointed stabiliser*. It is
statable in Lean (g as a permutation of `Fin n`, the twist as `SimpleGraph.fromRel`), and its
proof needs no degree sum at all. I do not propose it as the child: its hypothesis is not
checkable from G without a deck comparison. It is the right statement to keep in mind when
someone has a sharper kill.

Tested (`t2_claims.py` through `tools/search/small_graphs.py counterexample`, exhaustive
n = 3..7 plus 60 random graphs each on n = 8, 9; every pair, every g ∈ Aut(F)):
A (twists are anchored), B (degree formula of 3), C (K-triv or K-dp or K-mw, together with
equal sums of squares, force G_g = G), D (Lemma O below). **NO WITNESS, 1369 checks.**

## 4. The window census (paper)

Fix distinct u, v, w. For a graph K on V (K = G or H) define, always against the reference
cards of **G**:
- 𝒜_K = {(x, y, z) distinct : (K − x; y, z) ≈ (G − u; v, w)};
- 𝒞_K = {(x, y, z) distinct : (K − x; y, z) ≈ (G − y′; x′, z′) for some (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜_G}
  (types of the members of 𝒜 with the first two entries swapped);
- ℛ_K = {(x, y, z) distinct : (K − x − y; z) ≈ (G − u − v; w), deg_K x ∈ {δ, δ + 1},
  (x, y, z) ∉ 𝒞_K}.

**Lemma W.** Let H be hypomorphic to G and |ℛ_G| < |𝒜_G|. Then H is anchored at (x′, y′; z′)
for some (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜_G.
Paper argument.
1. By L1⁺⁺, Φ₃ maps 𝒜_G onto 𝒜_H and ℛ_G onto ℛ_H (membership depends on the type of
   (x; y, z), on its port, and on deg x, all transported). So |ℛ_H| < |𝒜_H|.
2. Let (a, b, c) ∈ 𝒜_H through e : (H − a; b, c) ≅ (G − u; v, w). Restricting e,
   (H − a − b; c) ≈ (G − u − v; w), and the port is symmetric in a, b. Also
   deg_H b = deg_{H−a} b + [a ~ b] = deg_{G−u} v + [a ~ b] ∈ {δ, δ + 1}.
   So (b, a, c) ∈ 𝒞_H ∪ ℛ_H.
3. The swap (a, b, c) ↦ (b, a, c) is injective. If it mapped 𝒜_H into ℛ_H we would get
   |𝒜_H| ≤ |ℛ_H|. So some (a, b, c) ∈ 𝒜_H has (b, a, c) ∈ 𝒞_H: there is (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜_G
   with (H − b; a, c) ≈ (G − y′; x′, z′), and (H − a; b, c) ≈ (G − u; v, w) ≈ (G − x′; y′, z′). ∎

Remarks.
- **The window is the full two-card compatibility.** Two doubly pointed cards (K₁; y, z) and
  (K₂; x, z) with isomorphic pointed ports can be the views (H − x; y, z), (H − y; x, z) of
  one graph H with e(H) = e(G) iff β := e(G) − e(port) − deg_{K₁} y − deg_{K₂} x ∈ {0, 1}
  (glue along the port; the only free bit is the pair xy). That is the bit law of t4 /
  `debe_codegree.md` §2, and it is the window. The third point adds no further constraint
  (deg_H z computed from either view agrees as soon as the ports agree).
- **It absorbs (i) and (a⁺).** If (i) of T⁺ holds, deg u ∉ {δ, δ + 1}, so 𝒜 ∩ ℛ = ∅
  automatically. If (a⁺) of T holds, 𝒜 ⊆ 𝒞. In general (u, v, w) ∈ ℛ iff deg u ∈ {δ, δ + 1}
  and 𝒜 ⊄ 𝒞, and then the census fails at this class, as it must (a reversed pair inside
  𝒜_H cannot be excluded).
- Pair form: the same with pointed cards (G − x, y), ports G − x − y, and the swap of t3.
  It contains the census of T⁺ (ℛ_window ⊆ ℛ_{T⁺} under (i)).

## 5. Theorem W3

**Theorem W3.** Let u, v, w be distinct vertices of G with
- (W) |ℛ_G| < |𝒜_G| (§4);
- (K) for every (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒜_G and every automorphism g of G − x − y with g(z) = z:
  g fixes N(x) ∖ {y} (i.e. x ~ g t ⇔ x ~ t for all t ≠ x, y), **or** g preserves G-degrees
  (deg g t = deg t for all t ≠ x, y).

Then G is reconstructible.

Paper argument. Let H be hypomorphic to G. By Lemma W, H is anchored at some
(x′, y′; z′) ∈ 𝒜_G. By Lemma A₃ the twist g is an automorphism of G − x′ − y′ fixing z′.
By (K) and Lemma K (K-triv or K-dp), ε ≡ 0 and G ≅ H. ∎

**Pair analogue W2** (same argument without the third point): pointed pair class 𝒜 of (u, v),
window census, and (K) for every (x, y) ∈ 𝒜 and every automorphism of G − x − y.

Relations (paper unless marked).
- T⁺ ⊆ W2: census by the remark in §4; (iii) is the second disjunct of (K).
- T ⊄ W2 formally: T asks its clause (d) at (u, v) only, W2 at every member of 𝒜.
- W2 vs W3: neither inclusion follows. If some vertex of S is fixed by Aut(F), W2 at (u, v)
  gives W3 at (u, v, that vertex) (then the port orbit and the stabiliser do not change).
  Computed: W2 ⊆ W3 for n ≤ 8, and W3 ∖ W2 = {`FaOH_`, `Ff]mw`} at n = 7.
- Wider variants with the same skeleton: (K-mw) instead of (K) (one inequality in the checked
  sum-of-squares step; the Lean definition with a subtype sum elaborates, §10); a third
  disjunct "g fixes N(y) ∖ {x}" (then f″ ∪ {y ↦ b} is the isomorphism; the endgame with the
  roles of the two cards exchanged). No graph computed needs either.

Sanity (paper): if all pointed ports (G − x − y; z) over unordered {x, y} are pairwise
non-isomorphic and have trivial pointed stabiliser (the generic graph: Müller 1976,
Bollobás 1990, both UNVERIFIED here), then for any u, v with deg u ∉ {δ, δ + 1} — e.g. two
non-adjacent vertices with deg u < deg v, or two adjacent ones with deg u > deg v — we get
𝒜 = {(u, v, w)}, 𝒞 = {(v, u, w)}, ℛ = ∅, (K) vacuous. So every such non-regular graph is in W3.

## 6. What the triple buys, what it costs

### 6.1 The census never gets easier (paper)

**Lemma O.** If the triple window census holds at (u, v, w), the pair window census holds at
(u, v). More precisely, with o₁ = |Aut(G − u)_v · w| ≤ o₂ = |Aut(F) · w|:
|𝒜₃| = o₁ |𝒜₂| and |ℛ₃| ≥ o₂ |ℛ₂|.
Argument. For (x, y) ∈ 𝒜₂ the third entries z with (x, y, z) ∈ 𝒜₃ form one orbit of the
pointed-card automorphisms, of size o₁. For (x, y) ∈ ℛ₂, each of the o₂ vertices z with
(G − x − y; z) ≈ (F; w) gives (x, y, z) ∈ ℛ₃ (if (x, y, z) were in 𝒞₃, forgetting z would put
(x, y) in 𝒞₂). Then o₂|ℛ₂| ≤ |ℛ₃| < |𝒜₃| = o₁|𝒜₂| ≤ o₂|𝒜₂|. ∎ (Tested: claim D.)

So the third point is paid for by the factor o₁/o₂ and repaid only in the kill clause:
Aut(G − x − y)_z instead of Aut(G − x − y). In group terms the triple pigeonhole is a
counting criterion for "the double coset of the twist under the two card stabilisers meets
the stabiliser of w" (t4's double cosets): the twist g is defined up to
g ↦ k″ g k with k ∈ Aut(G − u)_v|S, k″ ∈ Aut(G − v)_u|S, and pointing by w selects a
representative fixing w when the census allows it.

### 6.2 k points

The same statement holds with a tuple W of marked vertices of S instead of w: the twist
fixes W pointwise. For W a base of Aut(F) the kill clause is vacuous and the theorem is a
pure census ("rigid pointed port"). By the argument of Lemma O the census at W ∪ {w′}
implies the census at W. I do not propose k ≥ 2: the statement grows and no graph computed
needs it.

### 6.3 S₃ and three cards

The transposition of the two marked points is harmless (the type of (x; z, y) is a function of
the type of (x; y, z)). Swapping the deleted vertex with the *second* point instead of the
first is the same theorem with v, w renamed. So the S₃-images of a class give two censuses,
one per port, and Lemma W is "either of them".
Three-card variant (paper): let ℛ′, 𝒞′ be the sets of §4 for the swap (a, b, c) ↦ (c, b, a)
(port (G − u − w; v), window around deg w − [u ~ w]). If |ℛ| + |ℛ′| < |𝒜|, some
(a, b, c) ∈ 𝒜_H has both swaps in 𝒞_H, 𝒞′_H: H is a twist at (x′, y′; z′) by
g₁ ∈ Aut(G − x′ − y′)_{z′} *and* a twist at (x″, z″; y″) by g₂ ∈ Aut(G − x″ − z″)_{y″}, for two
members of 𝒜, and the two descriptions of N_H(a) agree through a pointed isomorphism
(G − x′; y′, z′) ≅ (G − x″; y″, z″). The kill clause may then be asked of the pairs (g₁, g₂)
whose twisted neighbourhoods agree. Stronger census, weaker kill. Not proposed (two members
of 𝒜 enter, the statement is long, nothing computed needs it).

### 6.4 Ceiling of the census

For a fixed pointed port π the swap is a perfect matching on the triples of H with port π,
between compatible types (window), with the type multiplicities of G (L1⁺⁺). H has *no*
anchor at any class iff its matching shares no pair of types with the matching of G, on every
port. Lemma W is the one-class pigeonhole for this; a Hall-type condition over several
classes at once would be weaker, at the price of a kill clause at every class involved.
Not developed.

## 8. Where W3 stops: the new residual

- **Paper: a regular graph whose doubly pointed cards are pairwise non-isomorphic is not in
  W3.** There 𝒜 = {(u, v, w)}, 𝒞 = {(v, u, w)}, and (u, v, w) itself lies in ℛ (all degrees
  are in every window). Computed: the Frucht graph `KhCKM?_EGK?L` has no class with (W).
  Regular graphs satisfy the predicate of the checked class `pure_degree_class` (paper:
  e(G − x − y) = e(G) − 2d + [x ~ y], so isomorphic two-deleted subgraphs have equal adjacency),
  so they are in neither residual.
- For a non-regular graph the census can fail at a class only through *other* triples: a
  second copy (x, y, z) of the pointed port with deg x in the window whose doubly pointed card
  is not the swap of a member of 𝒜. A graph outside W3 needs this, or a pointed-stabiliser
  twist that moves N(x) and moves degrees, at **every** class. I know no non-regular example
  and no argument that there is none; if there were such an argument it would close the
  parent, so I expect examples at larger n with heavy pseudosimilarity (many copies of every
  pointed port that are not related by card isomorphisms).
- The reducer's residual child is "this step's predicate ∧ ¬ W3-predicate". It has no known
  member. The same holds for the D2 twin with the same child.

## 9. What breaks for tournaments, 3-uniform hypergraphs, infinite graphs

| Step | Tournaments | Graph-specific ingredient |
|---|---|---|
| L1⁺⁺, ports, swap, pigeonhole | hold verbatim | none |
| window in ℛ (transport of deg x; deg_H b ∈ {δ, δ+1}) | the second part holds for scores; the transport fails | T0: deg x = e(G) − e(G − x); a tournament card always has C(n − 1, 2) arcs |
| Lemma A₃.2–3 (twist fixes w; arcs between a and f(S)) | hold verbatim | none |
| Lemma A₃.1, 4, 5 | fail | T0 |
| Lemma K: K-triv | gives f ∪ {u ↦ a} correct on every pair except {u, v} | the pair uv is fixed by deg_H a = deg u |
| Lemma K: K-dp, K-mw | fail with 5 | T0 and the symmetric bit |

So the tournament analogue (census without window, kill K-triv) ends with "H is G or G with
the arc uv reversed", and stops there. Whether Stockmeyer's pairs (J. Graph Theory 1 (1977);
UNVERIFIED) have a triple with the window-free census and trivial pointed twists was not
tested (t3's test of T on B_k, C_k found no qualifying pair). 3-uniform hypergraphs: the pair
{u, v} lies in up to n − 2 edges that neither card shows, so A₃.3 fails (t4 §10). Infinite
graphs: the census compares finite cardinalities.

## 11. Where this could break

1. **Nothing here closes the step.** W3 removes every known member; the new residual has no
   known member and no argument for emptiness (§8).
2. **A class that contains every graph on at most 8 vertices is suspicious until the gate has
   seen it.** What I did against a logic error: the census-form tests of §7 (H ranges over all
   graphs with the degree sequence of G, 29158 classes, and 476 non-isomorphic H pass when (K)
   is dropped, so the test is not vacuous); the single-graph claims; the independent
   transcription `t2_direct.py`. What I did not do: evaluate the Lean body itself on graphs.
3. **(K) quantifies over all members of 𝒜**, because Lemma W does not say at which member H
   is anchored (same as (iii) of T⁺).
4. **ℛ and 𝒞 are defined with existential isomorphisms inside a negation.** A fidelity
   reviewer should check the direction of every swap: 𝒞 contains (x, y, z) when
   (G − x; y, z) ≈ (G − y′; x′, z′) with (x′, y′, z′) ∈ 𝒜 — deleted vertex y′, first mark x′.
5. **The window uses T0 twice** (transport of deg x; deg_H a = deg u at the anchor), hence
   n ≥ 3 through `L_kelly_edge_count`.
6. **The membership list of this residual** (six graphs) rests on `t3_lib.py`'s encoding of
   the frozen body (for T, T⁺ on that encoding alone) and on "not two-walk-linear ⇒ not US";
   the three n = 6 graphs are new and deserve a cross-check before they are cited. The sibling
   `t1.md` reports 2 and 1 members at n = 7, 8 from its own exhaustive runs, which matches.
7. **Lemma O is an inequality, not an equivalence.** I did not characterise when the triple
   census holds given the pair census.
8. **Attribution.** I found no source for the window census, Lemma A₃ or Theorem W3, and I
   did not search the literature beyond the notes of this repository. They are two-card
   arguments in the tradition of Müller 1976 / Bollobás 1990 (UNVERIFIED) and direct
   extensions of t3's T⁺ (pointed census, Lemma A, Lemma B) and of t4's double cosets.
