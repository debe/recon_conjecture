## 3. Setting

n ≥ 3. G, H graphs on V, |V| = n; a hypomorphism is a bijection σ with card isomorphisms
f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx. For a card isomorphism f : G − x → H − a write F = f ∪ {x ↦ a} (the
checked `extE`): a bijection V → V(H) that preserves adjacency on all pairs avoiding x.

Fix distinct u, v, w. T = {u, v, w}, R = V ∖ T, K = G[R], A = Aut(K).
U, V′, W ⊆ R are the traces of N(u), N(v), N(w) on R, and A_U, A_V, A_W ≤ A their set
stabilizers. F_xy := G − x − y.

Say H is **fully anchored at (u, v, w)** if there are distinct a, b, c in H and card
isomorphisms f_u : G − u → H − a, f_v : G − v → H − b, f_w : G − w → H − c with
f_u(v) = b, f_u(w) = c, f_v(u) = a, f_v(w) = c, f_w(u) = a, f_w(v) = b.

## 4. The class C₃ and its paper argument

**Class C₃.** G is in C₃ if for some distinct u, v, w:
- **(U)** K = G − {u, v, w} has a unique copy: no other (n − 3)-set of vertices induces a graph
  isomorphic to K;
- **(S)** for each x ∈ T with {y, z} = T ∖ {x}: G − x − y ≇ G − x − z, **or** some automorphism
  of the card G − x maps y to z;
- **(OI)** if U′ ⊆ R is the image of U under an automorphism of K preserving W and also the
  image of U under an automorphism of K preserving V′, then U′ is the image of U under an
  automorphism of K preserving both V′ and W. In symbols A_W·U ∩ A_V·U = (A_V ∩ A_W)·U.

**Short form (M)** of the last clause: A_W ≤ A_U, i.e. every automorphism of K that preserves
adjacency to w preserves adjacency to u. (M) implies (OI) (take the identity; `M_le_OI` in the
Scratch file). The class with (M) is called C₃(M); the roles of u, v, w are free, so (M) asks
for one containment among the stabilizers of two of the three traces.

**Theorem G3 (paper argument below).** Every graph in C₃ is reconstructible.

### 4.1 Lemma C (the unique copy transfers; Kelly count for one graph)

Let G satisfy (U) and let H be hypomorphic to G via σ, (f_x). Then H has exactly one
(n − 3)-set TH inducing a copy of K; for **every** card isomorphism f : G − x → H − σx with
x ∈ T, F(R) = TH; hence, with P := V(H) ∖ TH, P = {σu, σv, σw} and F(T ∖ x) = P ∖ {σx}.

Paper argument. Kelly's lemma in induced form for the graph K on n − 3 < n vertices (checked:
`conj_kelly_ulam__disconnected__kelly_induced_count`) gives that H has as many (n − 3)-sets
inducing K as G, namely one. F preserves adjacency on R (R avoids x), so F(R) induces K and
F(R) = TH. Taking complements, F(T) = P, and F(x) = σx. Applied to x = u, v, w: σu, σv, σw ∈ P,
they are distinct, |P| = 3. ∎

This is the block `hKG`, `hK`, `huH`, `himg` of the checked `unique_copy_gluing` proof with
{u, v}ᶜ replaced by {u, v, w}ᶜ; the lemma is used for arbitrary card isomorphisms, not only the
given f_x (needed in 4.2).

### 4.2 Lemma S (straightness; where pseudosimilarity enters)

Assume (U), H hypomorphic to G, P as in Lemma C. Call a card isomorphism f : G − x → H − σx,
x ∈ T, {y, z} = T ∖ x, **straight** if F(y) = σy (then F(z) = σz), **crossed** if F(y) = σz.
Under (S) at x there is a straight card isomorphism G − x → H − σx.

Paper argument. Let f = f_x be crossed.
- *Second alternative of (S):* ρ ∈ Aut(G − x) with ρ(y) = z. Then f ∘ ρ is a card isomorphism
  G − x → H − σx with (f ∘ ρ)(y) = f(z) = σy: straight. (Lemma C applies to f ∘ ρ, so z ↦ σz.)
- *First alternative:* F_xy ≇ F_xz. Restricting f: F_xy ≅ H − σx − σz and F_xz ≅ H − σx − σy.
  By Lemma C, F_z maps {x, y} onto {σx, σy}; let p ∈ {x, y} with F_z(p) = σx. Restricting f_z:
  G − z − p ≅ H − σz − σx. So F_xy ≅ F_zp ∈ {F_zx, F_zy}; p = x would give F_xy ≅ F_xz, so
  F_xy ≅ F_yz. In the same way F_y maps {x, z} onto {σx, σz}; with F_y(q) = σx, q ∈ {x, z}:
  F_xz ≅ H − σx − σy ≅ F_yq ∈ {F_yx, F_yz}; q = x is excluded, so F_xz ≅ F_yz. Together
  F_xy ≅ F_xz, a contradiction. So f was not crossed. ∎

Remarks.
- The first alternative needs no assumption on the other two vertices (their isomorphisms may
  be crossed or not).
- (S) fails at x exactly when y and z are removal-similar in the card G − x
  ((G − x) − y ≅ (G − x) − z) but not similar in it: **y, z are pseudosimilar vertices of
  G − x** in the sense of Godsil–Kocay and Kimble–Schwenk–Stockmeyer (menu option 4). Computed:
  (S) holds at every vertex of every triple of every graph on n ≤ 7 vertices, of 60 random
  graphs on 8 vertices and of the three members. (Harary–Palmer 1966: the smallest graphs with
  pseudosimilar vertices have 8 vertices; from memory, UNVERIFIED; it would make (S) automatic
  for n ≤ 8.)
- A degree test also excludes crossing (deg y ≠ deg z and deg_{G−x} y ≠ deg_{G−x} z, through
  T0); it is graph-specific and not needed for any known member, so it is left out.

### 4.3 Lemma G (three-card gluing)

Let H be fully anchored at (u, v, w) by f_u, f_v, f_w (any graphs G, H on n vertices; no
hypomorphism needed). Put g₁ := (F_v⁻¹ ∘ F_u)|R and g₃ := (F_u⁻¹ ∘ F_w)|R. Then
1. g₁, g₃ ∈ A; g₁ ∈ A_W; g₃ ∈ A_V; g₁g₃ ∈ A_U;
2. for x ∈ R: [a ~ F_u x]_H = [u ~ g₁x]_G; and [a ~ b]_H = [u ~ v]_G, [a ~ c]_H = [u ~ w]_G;
3. if (OI) holds at (u; v, w), then G ≅ H.

Paper argument.
1. F_u, F_v, F_w map R onto V(H) ∖ {a, b, c}, so g₁, g₃ are bijections of R, and they preserve
   adjacency (compositions of card isomorphisms on sets avoiding the deleted vertices).
   For x ∈ R: [w ~ x] = [F_u w ~ F_u x] = [c ~ F_v(g₁x)] = [F_v w ~ F_v(g₁x)] = [w ~ g₁x].
   [v ~ x] = [F_w v ~ F_w x] = [b ~ F_u(g₃x)] = [F_u v ~ F_u(g₃x)] = [v ~ g₃x].
   g₁g₃ = (F_v⁻¹ ∘ F_w)|R and [u ~ x] = [F_w u ~ F_w x] = [a ~ F_v(g₁g₃x)] = [u ~ g₁g₃x].
2. [a ~ F_u x] = [F_v u ~ F_v(g₁x)] = [u ~ g₁x] (u, g₁x ≠ v). The pair {a, b} is shown by the
   third card: [a ~ b] = [F_w u ~ F_w v] = [u ~ v]; the pair {a, c} by the second:
   [a ~ c] = [F_v u ~ F_v w] = [u ~ w].
3. By 1, U′ := g₁⁻¹(U) = g₃(U) lies in A_W·U ∩ A_V·U. (OI) gives h ∈ A_V ∩ A_W with h(U) = U′,
   i.e. [u ~ g₁(hx)] = [u ~ x] for x ∈ R. Extend h by the identity on T to h̃; h̃ fixes v and w
   and preserves V′ and W, so it is an automorphism of G − u. Φ := F_u ∘ h̃ (with u ↦ a)
   preserves adjacency off u, and by 2: [a ~ Φx] = [u ~ g₁(hx)] = [u ~ x] for x ∈ R,
   [a ~ Φv] = [a ~ b] = [u ~ v], [a ~ Φw] = [u ~ w]. So Φ : G ≅ H. ∎

With (M): g₁ ∈ A_W ≤ A_U, so [a ~ F_u x] = [u ~ x] and F_u itself is the isomorphism; f_w is
used only for the pair {a, b}. This is the lemma `glue3` that compiles in Scratch (§8).

### 4.4 The algebra of (OI) (menu option 3)

The three pairwise transition maps g₁ ∈ A_W, g₂ := (F_w⁻¹ ∘ F_v)|R ∈ A_U, g₃ ∈ A_V satisfy the
cocycle condition g₃g₂g₁ = 1: H is G re-glued from its three cards along K. Changing the chart
f_x by an automorphism of G − x that fixes the other two anchors changes the cocycle by an
element of A_V ∩ A_W, A_U ∩ A_W, A_U ∩ A_V. (OI) is equivalent to
A_W A_U ∩ A_V A_U = (A_V ∩ A_W) A_U (products of subgroups as sets). It holds whenever
- A_W ≤ A_U or A_V ≤ A_U (then h = 1), or A_V ≤ A_W or A_W ≤ A_V (then h = g₃, resp. g₁⁻¹);
- in particular when K with one of the three traces as a colour class is asymmetric, or K
  itself is asymmetric (the generic graph: Bollobás' situation).
**(OI) is symmetric in u, v, w (paper argument, elementary).** For subgroups X, Y, Z of a
group, XY ∩ XZ = X(Y ∩ Z) holds iff any three pairwise intersecting cosets xX, yY, zZ have a
common element: translate so that the Y- and Z-coset meet in g and the X-coset is X; then
X ∩ gY ≠ ∅ iff g ∈ XY, X ∩ gZ ≠ ∅ iff g ∈ XZ, and a common element t = gm, m ∈ Y ∩ Z, t ∈ X
exists iff g ∈ X(Y ∩ Z). The coset condition does not single out a subgroup. So (OI) reads:
**three pairwise compatible partial isomorphisms have a common refinement**: a Helly property
of the coset geometry of (Aut(K); A_U, A_V, A_W), the condition under which every triangle of
pairwise incident cosets is a flag (Tits' coset geometries; Buekenhout–Cohen, *Diagram
Geometry*, 2013, ch. 1; from memory, UNVERIFIED, not used). Computed in agreement: in every
triple tested (OI) holds in all six roles or in none (§6), and `t1tri_coset_check.py` finds
no role-asymmetric case among 800 random triples of subgroups of S₄, S₅ (159 of them fail the
condition).

It can fail only when the three stabilizers are in general position; the smallest instance is
K = 4K₁ with V′ = {1, 2}, W = {1, 3}, U = {1, 4} (n = 7): U′ = {2, 3} is in both orbits and
not in the joint one, and re-attaching u to {2, 3} gives a fully anchored non-isomorphic H
(§6: the test finds such instances exactly where (OI) fails).
Two cards alone give the double-coset picture of t4/t3 (twists in Aut(G − u − v) modulo two
stabilizers); the third card intersects it with a second such picture and pins w.

### 4.5 Proof of Theorem G3

Let G ∈ C₃ at (u, v, w), H hypomorphic to G. Lemma C: P = {σu, σv, σw}. Lemma S at u, v, w:
straight card isomorphisms f′_u, f′_v, f′_w. Straightness says exactly that H is fully anchored
at (u, v, w) with (a, b, c) = (σu, σv, σw). Lemma G.3: G ≅ H. ∎

No step uses T0, degrees, or the edge count of G.

### 6.1 Reading of the runs

- The exhaustive n = 7 run is the real test of Lemma G: it has 252 triple-matched graphs H that
  are not isomorphic to G, so "matched ⇒ isomorphic" is false without the last clause, and
  every one of them sits at a triple where (OI) fails. With (OI) the count is zero.
- Lemma S is tested in a form stronger than needed (the other two isomorphisms are arbitrary,
  no unique copy is assumed): 79442 instances at n = 7.
- In every triple computed (n ≤ 7 exhaustive, 60 graphs at n = 8, the members) (OI) holds in
  all six roles or in none, as the symmetry argument of §4.4 says. (M) is not symmetric.
- Not tested: Lemma C. It is the checked Kelly count plus the `himg` step of the checked
  gluing proof; a test would need hypomorphic non-isomorphic pairs, which do not exist for
  n ≤ 13.

## 9. What is left, and where this could break

### 9.1 The residual after C₃ (three obstructions, no known member)

After C₃ a member of this step's class has, for every triple: K is not a unique copy, **or**
two of the three vertices are pseudosimilar in the card of the third, **or** (OI) fails in all
six roles. I know no member (n ≤ 8 is exhausted with the transcribed predicates). I have no
argument that the new residual is empty and I expect it is not: on the symmetric face no
triple-deleted subgraph is unique, and for larger n cards can have pseudosimilar pairs
(Kimble–Schwenk–Stockmeyer, Godsil–Kocay construct them).

### 9.2 Census instead of uniqueness (option 2; not worked out)

L1⁺ lifts to triples: Φ₃(x, y, z) = (σx, f_x y, f_x z) is a bijection of ordered triples of
distinct vertices that preserves the doubly-pointed card (G − x; y, z). If the pointed types of
(u; v, w), (v; w, u), (w; u, v) coincide and that type holds more than 2/3 of the ordered
triples whose deletion leaves K, then by pigeonhole on the cyclic classes some triple of H is
fully anchored (the analogue of T's clause (b⁺)). The asymmetric case needs a T⁺-style
exclusion of the bad cyclic patterns; I did not find a clean one. This is where the sibling
triple-census sketch of this round should connect: Lemma G is the endgame for any such anchor.

### 9.3 More than three cards

For a k-set T with a unique copy of G − T and straight card isomorphisms, the transition maps
g_xy satisfy the cocycle condition on every 3-subset of T. The trivial sufficient condition
becomes weaker with k (more pins), but K gets smaller and less likely to be unique. Not
needed for any known member.

### 9.4 Variants that widen the class (each costs Lean lines)

- Drop (S) at w and get [a ~ b] = [u ~ v] from T0 as in the checked gluing proof (`huv'`):
  graph-specific, about 50 lines.
- Add the degree test of 4.2 as a third alternative in (S).
- Replace (M) by "every g ∈ A_W preserves U **or** preserves all G-degrees" and finish with
  t3's Lemma B (the twist of the anchored pair (u, v) fixes w): graph-specific, needs the
  sum-of-squares identity from the checked `pointed_pair_symmetric` file.
None is needed for a known member.

### 9.5 Where the sketch could be wrong

1. **Lemma S, first alternative**, is the only non-routine case analysis. It is tested (5724
   instances at n = 6, 9406 on the n = 8 sample) in the relaxed form, where the other two
   isomorphisms are arbitrary.
2. **(OI) can fail only from n = 7 on.** The exhaustive n = 7 run is the main test (54 failing
   triples, 252 non-isomorphic matched H, all there); the n = 8 sample has 3 failing triples
   of 3360. Larger K with richer automorphism groups were not tested.
3. **"Meets the residual" depends on transcribed predicates** of seven earlier classes. A red
   team should re-check `Gl]o^[` against the frozen Lean body, clause by clause.
4. **The Lean bodies were not evaluated on graphs.** The Python predicates were; the two agree
   by reading, and by the remark on "swap" versus "maps y to z" in §8.
5. **Genericity.** Because nothing is graph-specific, a red team can attack Theorem G3 with
   tournaments, digraphs and hypergraphs: a non-reconstructible digraph with a triple
   satisfying (U), (S), (OI) would refute the paper argument. Bowler–Brown–Fenner-type pairs
   with many common cards and Stockmeyer's other families (n = 2^k + 2) are natural targets;
   I checked only B_k, C_k for k = 2, 3.
6. **Novelty.** I found no source for the class or for the stabilizer condition. The idea is
   Müller's and Bollobás' (unique asymmetric large subgraphs), localized to one triple and
   with asymmetry weakened; I have not read either paper.
