- **Literature placement.** Gupta, Mangal and Paliwal (2003), *Some work towards the proof of the
  reconstruction conjecture*, Discrete Math. 272, 291–296, reduce the conjecture to graphs with
  diam(G) = 2, or with diam(G) = diam(Ḡ) = 2. I did not read their full text. I know the statement
  from the secondary sources below.
  Aravind and Monikandan (2026), arXiv:2601.00620, *A Reduction of the Reconstruction Conjecture
  using Domination and Vertex Pair Parameters*, Theorem 7, show that it suffices to reconstruct
  the 2-connected graphs with γ(G) = 2 or with diam(G) = diam(Ḡ) = 2. Their §7 lists
  "all connected graphs with diam(G) = diam(Ḡ) = 2 are weakly reconstructible" as **open Problem 2**.
  This is a preprint and is not peer reviewed. I read it only through a summarizing fetch, so the
  exact statements are UNVERIFIED in this repository.
  Our step is that class with two reconstructible sub-classes taken out (t4 and t5 Lemma P, and
  the unique-copy gluing of t4 §6). No classical argument covers it. A closed step here would be
  a new theorem that comes close to the conjecture itself. I found no source that resolves it.
- **No structural decomposition applies.** Lemma S1 in §2 shows that every member is 2-connected,
  that its complement is 2-connected, and that 2 ≤ deg v ≤ n − 3. So the classical block routes do
  nothing here: Kelly 1957 (disconnected graphs and trees), Bondy 1969 (separable graphs without
  end vertices) and Yang 1988 (reduction to 2-connected graphs). The component, block and
  end-vertex routes are all vacuous on this class. I record this as a dead end below.
- **Kelly counting adds nothing new beyond Lemma G.** See §3. Every recognizable pair count
  that I know of is already implied by t4/t5 Lemma G and the bit law. These are the pair-type
  counts (F, deg x, deg y) and the Gupta-style pair parameters pv/pav/dv/dav (Aravind–Monikandan
  Theorems 1, 15 and 18, attributed there to Gupta et al.).

## 2. Structural lemmas (paper arguments, tested)

Let 𝒟 be the class G with G connected, Ḡ connected, and edist ≤ 2 in both G and Ḡ.

**S1 (2-connectivity and degree bounds).** If G ∈ 𝒟 and n ≥ 3, then:
(a) no vertex is universal in G or in Ḡ, so 1 ≤ deg v ≤ n − 2;
(b) G has no cut vertex, and neither does Ḡ;
(c) 2 ≤ deg v ≤ n − 3.

*Proof.*
(a) A universal vertex of G is isolated in Ḡ, which contradicts Ḡ connected (n ≥ 2). The same
holds with G and Ḡ swapped.
(b) Let c be a cut vertex of G, and let x and y lie in different components of G − c. Then
x ≁ y, and their only possible common neighbour is c. By `two_lt_edist_iff`, edist ≤ 2 forces
x ~ c. Every vertex other than c has a partner in another component, so c is universal, which
contradicts (a). The complement works the same way, because 𝒟 is closed under complement.
(c) If deg x = 1 with neighbour c, then every y ∉ {x, c} is non-adjacent to x and has c as its
only possible common neighbour with x. So c is universal, which contradicts (a). Apply the same
argument to Ḡ.

**S2 (complement closure).** Let u be a vertex. Then u has a pure degree class in G iff it
has one in Ḡ. Also, (u, v) is a unique-copy factorizing pair in G iff it is one in Ḡ. So the step's
class is closed under complement.

*Proof.*
- Degree classes are the same in G and Ḡ, since deg_Ḡ = n − 1 − deg_G.
- G − x − y ≅ G − x′ − y′ iff the complements of these graphs are isomorphic.
- Adjacency of x and y flips in both pairs, so the ↔ is preserved.
- For gluing: F̄ occurs in Ḡ exactly where F occurs in G, and Aut(F̄) = Aut(F). The complement
  of N_u in S has the same setwise stabilizer as N_u.

**Tested.** `t2_claim_structure.py` (this directory) checks (b), (c), purity invariance (all n)
and gluing invariance (n ≤ 7). It was run with
`uv run python tools/search/small_graphs.py counterexample --claim …/t2_claim_structure.py --n-min 3 --n-max 7 --random 200 --random-n 8 9`.
Result: **NO WITNESS after 1649 checks** (exhaustive n = 3..7, random n = 8, 9).

S1 and S2 are true but weak. They are useful only as hygiene for a builder: they tell you which
hypotheses are free.

## 3. Why Kelly-style pair counting stalls (the exact gap)

The setting is T0–T2 of t4 §2. σ is the hypomorphism, f_u : G − u ≅ H − σu, and the aim is
f_u(N_G(u)) = N_H(σu) for one u.

1. **Bit law** (t4 §3): for a pair (x, y) of type F = G − x − y,
   e(F) + deg x + deg y = e(G) + [x ~ y]. So the triple (F, deg x, deg y) already determines the
   bit. Lemma G (t5 cut identity) then makes the count of pairs with each (F, a, b) recognizable.
   Any finer recognizable pair count has to be an invariant of the pair *beyond* its two-deleted
   card and its degrees.
   - Gupta's pv/pav count pairs by number of common neighbours.
   - Aravind–Monikandan's dv/dav count pairs by the profile (common, x only, y only).

   For a given pair, all of these are functions of (F, deg x, deg y) together with the
   positions of x and y in the card G − x. On counts they therefore give nothing that Lemma G
   does not give. (This is a paper remark. I did not test it separately.)
2. **Where it stops.** Fix u with deg u = a. For w ≠ u, the H-pair (σu, f_u w) has type
   F_w = G − u − w and first degree a. Its bit is fixed by the bit law as soon as
   deg_H(f_u w) is known. But deg_H(f_u w) = deg_{G−u}(w) + [σu ~ f_u w], which is circular.
   ¬PURE says exactly that for every degree class some type is shared by an adjacent and a
   non-adjacent pair, so counting cannot pin the bit. ¬GLUE says that no two-card overlap is
   rigid enough to glue. The step's hypotheses are defined so that both tools fail.
3. **Induction on vertices.** The members have no pure class and no gluing pair. Nothing says
   that a card G − v of a member lies in a reconstructible class with a *recognizable* witness.
   And reconstructibility of the cards is no help anyway: the problem is re-attaching v, not
   identifying G − v. Induction on edges would need edge reconstruction (Harary 1964; Lovász
   1972, Müller 1977 bounds). The parent's sketches note that members are dense (both G and
   Ḡ have diameter 2), and Lovász's bound m > n(n−1)/4 fails for G or for Ḡ in a
   balanced member. The t4 members have m ≈ n(n−1)/4.

## 4. Candidate intermediate lemma (would make the step easy, but no argument)

**(Q) Degree transport for one class.** For some degree a and every u with deg u = a, and every
w ≠ u: deg_H(f_u w) = deg_G(w).

(Q) and the bit law give f_u(N(u)) = N_H(σu) at once. This is t4 §9 item 4 ("a per-vertex
degree-preserving card isomorphism"), and t4 already says there is no argument for it. In general
(Q) is as hard as the conjecture. The degree sequence of H − σu is recognized only as a
multiset, and (Q) asks for the f_u-pointwise version. For tournaments (Stockmeyer 1977) the
analogue fails, so any proof of (Q) must use the symmetry of the adjacency bit. I offer (Q) only
as the precise shape of the missing piece. It is **not** a proposed child: as a child it would just
restate the residual.

## 5. What breaks for tournaments, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

S1 and S2 are graph facts. S1 uses undirected distance, and S2 uses complements that are again
simple graphs. For tournaments, the complement of a tournament is its reverse, and the
"diameter ≤ 2" analogue (strong kings) is not obstructed by Stockmeyer's families. That matches
the expectation that this class can carry no tournament-valid proof.

## 6. Dead end to record

"Structural decomposition (components, blocks, end vertices; Kelly 1957, Bondy 1969, Yang 1988)
on diam2_impure: vacuous, because every member and its complement are 2-connected with
2 ≤ deg ≤ n − 3 (S1, tested n ≤ 7 exhaustive). Kelly pair counting stops at the bit-transport gap
(§3.2) that ¬PURE and ¬GLUE are defined to leave open."

## 7. Lean notes (if anyone formalizes S1 as a helper)

- `SimpleGraph.two_lt_edist_iff` and `SimpleGraph.edist_eq_two_iff` (Mathlib
  `Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Metric.lean`, lines 145 and 162) turn "edist ≤ 2" into "adjacent, or has a
  common neighbour".
- `SimpleGraph.compl_adj` (Basic.lean:248) gives the complement case.
- There is no ready 2-connectivity predicate. S1 is better stated directly as "no universal vertex,
  and 2 ≤ deg". Use `SimpleGraph.commonNeighbors` and `Set.ncard`/`Finset.card` on
  `neighborFinset`. These are finite facts that a builder can carry with `Fintype` on `Fin n`.
