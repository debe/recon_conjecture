## 1. The statement, unfolded

`ClassReconstructible C` unfolds to: for all n ≥ 3 and G : SimpleGraph (Fin n) with C n G, and
for all H with `Hypomorphic G H` (∃ σ : Fin n ≃ Fin n, ∀ x, Nonempty (G.induce {x}ᶜ ≃g
H.induce {σ x}ᶜ)), we have Nonempty (G ≃g H). Here C n G is:

1. `G.Connected ∧ Gᶜ.Connected`;
2. `∀ u v, G.edist u v ≤ 2 ∧ Gᶜ.edist u v ≤ 2` (D2);
3. `¬ PURE n G`, the negation of the body of the checked `pure_degree_class`;
4. `¬ GLUE n G`, the negation of the body of the checked `unique_copy_gluing`.

What a direct proof can use:
- Clause 1 is implied by clause 2 for n ≥ 2, since finite `edist` means reachable. Mathlib:
  `SimpleGraph.edist_ne_top_iff_reachable`, `SimpleGraph.connected_iff_exists_forall_reachable`
  (`Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Metric.lean`, `Connectivity/Connected.lean`). It adds nothing.
- Clause 2 gives, through `SimpleGraph.edist_eq_one_iff_adj` (Metric.lean:131) and
  `SimpleGraph.compl_adj` (Basic.lean:248), for every non-adjacent pair a common neighbour,
  and for every adjacent pair a common non-neighbour. These are local, purely positive facts.
  Through the checked `found_hypomorphic_compl` and `found_reconstructible_compl` the class is
  closed under complement, but so are PURE and GLUE (parent t4 §9.7). Complementing gives
  nothing.
- Clauses 3 and 4 are **negative**. A direct proof can use them only by contraposition, that
  is, by proving "D2, hypomorphic to some H with G ≇ H ⇒ PURE ∨ GLUE". That is a theorem about
  every possible counterexample in D2. It is not an unfolding.

**The unfolded statement has no term in it that produces an isomorphism G ≃g H.** In the
checked library every isomorphism comes from one of two places. Either it extends a card
isomorphism f_u by u ↦ σu, once N_H(σu) = f_u(N_G(u)) is forced (`pure_degree_class`,
`discon_isolated_vertex`, t1's (★)). Or it is a twisted gluing of two cards along an anchored
pair (`unique_copy_gluing`). Clauses 3 and 4 say exactly that the two existing ways of forcing
the neighbourhood fail in their checked form. A direct proof therefore needs a **third way to
force a neighbourhood**. Neither Mathlib nor the literature I could find supplies one (§4).

`t1_members.py` (next to this file; run with the repo's `.venv/bin/python`) evaluates the four
clauses through `screen_lib.py`, the clause-by-clause transcription of the Lean body used by
the reducer's falsity screen. It runs them on the members reported in parent t4 §8:

| `KbcrMZwA~c@r` | 12 | 5⁶6⁶ | yes | yes |

So the Lean hypotheses are satisfiable and no vacuity argument exists. Membership is a fact
about single graphs. None of them is a counterexample (they are reconstructible: n ≤ 13,
McKay). For a Lean proof this means the following. **At n = 8 the proof must, in effect,
reconstruct `GhEJ]o` and its two companions.** At n = 12 and 16 it must reconstruct the
self-complementary family with degrees n/2 − 1 and n/2 (parent t4 §8). That family has two
degree classes swapped by an antimorphism, and in each degree class half of the type-F pairs
are adjacent.

## 5. What a real route would need (open; no source)

- A per-vertex recognizable statistic that separates, inside a degree class, the vertices y
  in the card G − u that are adjacent to u from those that are not, beyond degree levels
  (parent t4 §9.2, t2 hypothesis H\*). Every candidate I tried is either circular (§2, last
  row) or reduces to T2.
- Or a structure theorem for D2 graphs with no pure class and no factorizing unique-copy pair
  (for example "two consecutive degrees, half each, …"). The members found suggest such a
  theorem but do not prove it, and none is in the literature I know. Aravind–Monikandan
  arXiv:2601.00620, Problem 2, asks whether connected graphs with diam G = diam Ḡ = 2 are even
  *weakly* reconstructible. This is per `notes/literature/structural_reductions.md` and parent
  t2 §2, HTML summary only, UNVERIFIED. So the literature treats the whole D2 branch as open.
- No reconstruction result for self-complementary graphs was found (parent t2 §2; UNVERIFIED
  either way).

## 6. What breaks for tournaments, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

Nothing new is claimed, so there is nothing new to break. The only graph-specific inputs the
peels use are T0 (degree tie) and the bit law, both of which fail for tournaments (parent t4
§10, tested on Stockmeyer pairs at n = 5, 9). The gluing (A1, L5, L6) holds verbatim for
tournaments, so it cannot be the whole route.
