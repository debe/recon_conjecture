## 3. Setting and three lemmas

n ≥ 3. G, H graphs on V, |V| = n. A hypomorphism is a bijection σ with card isomorphisms
f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx. A = A_G. For x: deg x, and w₂(x) := Σ_{y~x} deg y (walks of length 2 from
x). G is **two-walk (a, b)-linear** ("2wl(a, b)") if w₂(x) = a·deg x + b for all x, with
integers a, b. For u′ put λ := a − deg u′.

Literature: G has exactly two main eigenvalues iff it is 2-walk (a, b)-linear for unique
rationals a, b (Hagos, Linear Algebra Appl. 356 (2002) 103–111; read only through secondary
sources: arXiv:1608.08702, arXiv:1012.0963); Hou and Tian: a, b are integers (secondary, same
sources; primary UNVERIFIED). Neither fact is used in the argument: 2wl(a, b) with integers
a, b is a *hypothesis* of the class. For two consecutive degrees d, d + 1 it is the statement
"the degree partition is equitable" (w₂(x) = d·deg x + #{high neighbours of x}), i.e.
Zinchenko's constants b, e (arXiv:2611.13728, Lemma 2.4) without his card-type hypothesis.

**T0 (checked).** deg_H σx = deg_G x (`L_kelly_edge_count`, `card_add_deg`, as in the checked
`level_half_pure` proof, `hdeg`).

**W1 (w₂ is card-tied).** w₂^H(σx) = w₂^G(x).
Paper argument. Q(X) := Σ_y deg_X(y)², q(X, x) := Σ_{y≠x} (deg_{X−x} y)². Single-graph identity,
from deg_{X−x}(y) = deg y − [x ~ y]:

    q(X, x) = Q(X) − deg(x)² − 2·w₂(x) + deg(x).

Q(G) = Q(H) by T0 (sum over the bijection σ). q(G, x) = q(H, σx) because f_x is a bijection of
the card vertices that preserves card degrees. deg is tied by T0. Solve for w₂. ∎
Tested as a single-graph identity: part W1 of `t1us_claims.py`.

**REC (corollary).** If G is 2wl(a, b) and H is hypomorphic to G, then H is 2wl(a, b), same
a, b. (Every vertex of H is some σx.) This is the librarian's `twoMainRecognizable`
(`options_algebraic.md` O3) with a, b fixed.

**REL (switch relation).** Let G on V and K on W be 2wl(a, b) with the same a, b, let u ∈ V,
w ∈ W with deg_G u = deg_K w =: δ, and let e : G − u ≅ K − w. Define z : V → ℤ by z(u) = 0 and
z(x) = [u ~ x] − [w ~ e x] for x ≠ u. Then

    Σ_{y ~_G x} z(y) = (a − δ)·z(x)   for every x ≠ u,        Σ_{y ~_G u} z(y) = k := #{y ~ u : w ≁ e y},

and Σ_x z(x) = 0.
Paper argument. For x ≠ u: deg_K(e x) = deg_{K−w}(e x) + [w ~ e x] = deg_{G−u}(x) + [w ~ e x]
= deg_G x − z(x). Then, splitting the K-neighbours of e x into w and the rest (= e of the
G-neighbours of x other than u),

    w₂^K(e x) = [w ~ e x]·δ + Σ_{y ~ x, y ≠ u} (deg_G y − z(y)) = w₂^G(x) − δ·z(x) − Σ_{y ~ x} z(y)

(z(u) = 0 lets the last sum run over all neighbours). Both graphs are 2wl(a, b):
w₂^K(e x) = a·(deg_G x − z(x)) + b = w₂^G(x) − a·z(x). Subtract. At u: z(y) = 1 − [w ~ e y] on
N(u). Σ z = δ − δ. ∎
For two consecutive degrees this is Zinchenko's Lemma 3.1(c) (a − δ = θ − s there); the
derivation above needs neither two degrees nor one card type per degree class. No symmetry of
A is used.
Tested: part REL of `t1us_claims.py`.

## 4. Theorem U and the class US

**Theorem U (unique switch).** Let G be 2wl(a, b) and u′ a vertex such that λ = a − deg u′ is
not an eigenvalue of A_G (it suffices: no nonzero integer vector f with A f = λ f). Then:

1. there is at most one set S ≠ N(u′), |S| = deg u′, S ⊆ V − u′, such that G − u′ with u′
   re-attached to S is 2wl(a, b);
2. **(class US)** if moreover there are a vertex u, an isomorphism α : G − u ≅ G − u′ and a
   neighbour x₀ of u with α(x₀) ≁ u′, then G is reconstructible.

Paper argument for 2 (1 is the same computation). Let H be hypomorphic to G via σ, (f_x).
- By REC, H is 2wl(a, b). By T0, deg_H σu′ = deg u′. REL with (K, w, e) = (H, σu′, f_{u′}) gives
  z′ with z′(x) = [u′ ~ x] − [σu′ ~ f_{u′} x], k′ ≥ 0, Σ z′ = 0.
- G − u ≅ G − u′ gives e(G − u) = e(G − u′), so deg u = deg u′. REL with (K, w, e) = (G, u, α⁻¹)
  gives z_α with z_α(x) = [u′ ~ x] − [u ~ α⁻¹x] and k_α ≥ 0.
- f := k_α·z′ − k′·z_α satisfies Σ_{y~x} f(y) = λ f(x) for x ≠ u′ (REL twice) and for x = u′
  (left side k_α k′ − k′ k_α = 0, right side λ·0). By the hypothesis on λ, f = 0:
  **k_α z′ = k′ z_α**.
- If k′ = 0: z′ has no entry +1 and sums to 0, so z′ = 0, i.e. [σu′ ~ f_{u′} x] = [u′ ~ x] for
  all x ≠ u′. Re-attachment (checked helper `iso_of_nbhd`) gives G ≅ H.
- If k′ ≥ 1: at y₀ := α(x₀) we have z_α(y₀) = −1, so k_α z′(y₀) = −k′ < 0; as z′(y₀) ∈ {−1, 0, 1}
  this forces z′(y₀) = −1 and k_α = k′. Hence z′ = z_α: [σu′ ~ f_{u′} x] = [u ~ α⁻¹x] for all
  x ≠ u′. Then ψ := f_{u′} ∘ α : G − u ≅ H − σu′ satisfies [σu′ ~ ψ x″] = [u ~ x″] for all
  x″ ≠ u, and `iso_of_nbhd` gives G ≅ H. ∎

Reading: at a nonsingular vertex a 2wl graph has at most one "other" re-attachment, and a card
isomorphism that does not extend to an automorphism already produces one, isomorphic to G. So
there is no room for a non-isomorphic reconstruction. This is the role Zinchenko's Theorem 4.4
(|C_G(v)| = 2; about 1000 Lean lines of his Stages A–B) plays for the class N, obtained here
from a determinant condition instead of the normal form.

Tested in strong single-card form (no deck presupposed; `t1us_claims.py` parts U, US): for
every 2wl graph, every vertex and every re-attachment that keeps 2wl(a, b), the relation holds;
at nonsingular vertices there is at most one alternative; where a card isomorphism does not
extend, the alternative is isomorphic to G. NO WITNESS: exhaustive n = 3..7 (1249 graphs, 91
of them 2wl and not regular) and n = 8 (12346 graphs, 142 of them 2wl and not regular), and at
every vertex of the eleven named 2wl graphs with n ≤ 16 (exhaustive over the up to 6435
subsets per vertex; each vertex has exactly one alternative). Not vacuous: for n = 4..7 there
are 52 pairs (G, u′) with a nonsingular u′ and exactly one alternative, and 48 singular pairs
with two or more alternatives (so the spectral hypothesis in part 1 cannot be dropped).

## 5. US meets the remainder

**Proposition F (paper, all n).** Let Γ be a primitive strongly regular graph with parameters
(v, k, l, m) (0 < m < k) and v₀ a vertex not fixed by Aut(Γ). Then G := Γ − v₀ is in US.

Paper argument.
- *2wl.* Degrees k − 1 on N(v₀) and k elsewhere; a low vertex has k − 1 − l high neighbours, a
  high vertex k − m. So G is 2wl(a, b) with a = k + l − m, b = (k − 1 − l) − (l − m + 1)(k − 1),
  and λ ∈ {θ, θ − 1}, θ := l − m + 1 (low, high u′).
- *Nonsingular at every u′.* Let r > s be the restricted eigenvalues of Γ (r + s = l − m).
  Eigenvectors of Γ for r or s that vanish at v₀ restrict to eigenvectors of G (|V(G)| − 2
  dimensions in all); they are orthogonal to 1 and to the indicator of N(v₀); the span of
  these two is A_G-invariant and carries the roots of μ² − aμ − b. Evaluating:
  θ² − aθ − b = −(k − 1 − l) and (θ − 1)² − a(θ − 1) − b = m, both nonzero. θ = r iff s = −1,
  θ − 1 = s iff r = 0, and θ = s, θ − 1 = r are impossible (r ≥ 0 > s); s = −1 and r = 0
  characterize the imprimitive cases (disjoint cliques, complete multipartite; Brouwer and
  Haemers, *Spectra of Graphs*, 2012, ch. 9, from memory, UNVERIFIED). Here k − 1 − l ≠ 0 and m ≠ 0 by primitivity (l = k − 1 forces disjoint cliques).
  Computed check: no singular vertex in any tested graph Γ − v of §2.
- *A card isomorphism that does not extend.* Take τ ∈ Aut(Γ) with τv₀ ≠ v₀, u′ := τv₀,
  u := τ⁻¹v₀. τ restricts to α : G − u ≅ G − u′ and α(N_G(u)) = N_Γ(v₀) ∖ {u′}, while
  N_G(u′) = N_Γ(u′) ∖ {v₀}. These differ unless v₀, u′ are twins in Γ, and a primitive strongly
  regular graph has no twins (non-adjacent twins give m = k, adjacent twins l = k − 1). ∎

So US contains Γ − v for every vertex-transitive primitive strongly regular Γ. Whether such a
graph lies in the frozen remainder depends on ¬D, ¬GLUE, ¬N and both diameters ≤ 2. **I have no
all-n argument for that**; computed members: Shrikhande − v, co-Shrikhande − v (n = 15) and
L₃(ℤ₆) − v (n = 35). The rank-3 examples are in N (one card type per degree class), the less
symmetric ones tested (Chang, a non-group Latin square of order 5) are in D. Guess: L₃(ℤ_m) − v
for m ≥ 6 and other Cayley strongly regular graphs that are not rank 3 are in the remainder.

## 6. Companions (paper arguments; none is needed for US)

**Theorem S (class EV; Zinchenko's Lemma 3.3 outside the normal form).** If G is 2wl(a, b) and
A_G has an eigenvector f for the eigenvalue a − deg u′ with f(u′) ≠ 0, then G is
reconstructible (no alternative re-attachment at u′ at all).
Argument: with z′, k′ as in §4, k′·f(u′) = ⟨f, (A − λ) z′⟩ = ⟨(A − λ) f, z′⟩ = 0, so k′ = 0.
This step **uses the symmetry of A**. Tested (`t1us_claim_ev.py`): NO WITNESS, n = 3..7; the
hypothesis holds at 86 pairs (G, u′) in 20 graphs. EV is disjoint from US at the same vertex
and has no known remainder member (all named members are nonsingular everywhere).

**Explicit alternative.** Under the hypotheses of Theorem U part 1, every reconstruction of G is
isomorphic to G or to the one graph G^{(u′)} obtained from the unique admissible z (if there is
one). So a 2wl graph with one nonsingular vertex has at most two reconstructions, and it is
reconstructible iff G^{(u′)} does not exist, is isomorphic to G, or is not hypomorphic to G.
US is the case "isomorphic to G, witnessed by a card isomorphism".

**Proposition DJ (what the two-deleted spectral identity gives).** Let G be 2wl(a, b),
φ(G; λ) ≠ 0 for λ = a − deg u′, and let z ≠ 0 be an admissible switch at u′ with k lost
neighbours. Then φ(G − u′; λ) = 0 and, for every x ≠ u′,

    z(x)² = − k² · φ(G − u′ − x; λ) / φ(G; λ).

Hence the switch set is X := {x : φ(G − u′ − x; λ) ≠ 0}, |X| = 2k, |X ∩ N(u′)| = k,
φ(G − u′ − x; λ) is constant on X, and G^{(u′)} is G with the pairs {u′, x}, x ∈ X, toggled.
Argument: z = k (A − λ)⁻¹ ε_{u′} = −k · adj(λI − A) ε_{u′} / φ(G; λ); z(u′) = 0 gives
φ(G − u′; λ) = 0; Jacobi's identity for the symmetric matrix λI − A,
adj_{x u′}² = φ(G − x) φ(G − u′) − φ(G) φ(G − x − u′) (Godsil, *Algebraic Combinatorics*, 1993,
ch. 4, cited through `options_algebraic.md` O6; primary UNVERIFIED), finishes it. Computed
check: `t1us_probe_dj.py`, all ten named 2wl graphs with n ≤ 16, six vertices each.
So on this face the deck does force two-deleted data: X is read off from the characteristic
polynomials of the two-deleted subgraphs G − u′ − x at the single integer λ, and X is a union
of Aut(G − u′)-orbits. A class "X is not evenly split by N(u′), or the values are not
−φ(G; λ)/k²" is reconstructible by the same argument, but it needs Desnanot–Jacobi (Mathlib
gap, 200–400 lines by the librarian's estimate) and no known remainder member lies in it
(every vertex of every named member has a switch, of automorphism type). Not proposed.

**Walk rank r > 2 (remark, not worked out).** With main polynomial of degree r the relation
becomes p(A) z ∈ span{ε_{u′}, A ε_{u′}, …, A^{r−2} ε_{u′}} with p of degree r − 1, provided the
walk counts W_j(x), j ≤ r, are card-tied. W₃ is card-tied by an elementary count that needs
Kelly's lemma for triangles; general j needs Tutte-level input (t3's S4). The solution space
is no longer a line, so uniqueness needs extra constraints. I did not find a clean class.
This is the exact reason `KbcrMZwA~c@r` (walk rank 6) is out of reach here.

## 7. Where the method stops (the exact failure points)

After US, a 2wl graph in the remainder has, at every vertex u′: λ_{u′} ∈ spec(A_G), **or** every
card isomorphism G − u → G − u′ maps N(u) onto N(u′).
1. **Class E** (normal form, every card isomorphism extends): US is empty there by definition.
   In the nonsingular case Proposition DJ still names the only candidate G^{(u′)}, but nothing
   here shows G^{(u′)} ≅ G or that it is not hypomorphic. That is Zinchenko's §§5–9.
2. **Singular vertices**: two or more alternatives occur (48 pairs for n ≤ 7). With a nontrivial
   alternative present, Theorem S says every λ-eigenvector vanishes at u′; I found no way to
   turn that into uniqueness without the card-type hypothesis (Zinchenko's Cor 3.5(b)).
3. **Not two-walk-linear**: nothing. `KbcrMZwA~c@r` and the six n = 8 members of the
   dominating-edge twin are of this kind. For the twin, US is a valid child but I know **no**
   member of the twin's remainder in US.
4. I did not search for 2wl graphs other than strongly regular minus a vertex beyond n = 8
   (no `geng` on this machine; n = 8 was built by one-vertex extension).

## 8. Relation to the sibling child N (`two_card_narrow`)

A normal-form graph is 2wl(a, b) with integers a, b (equitable degree partition, or regular),
and the third clause of N is the third clause of US. So **N ∩ {a − deg u′ ∉ spec A_G} ⊆ US**.
Every N-member tested (the three self-complementary face members and eleven strongly regular
graphs minus a vertex, n ≤ 24) has no singular vertex. N itself is not contained in US: the
spectral hypothesis is extra, and Zinchenko obtains "θ is not an eigenvalue" only at his
Theorem 7.2, for a hypothetical counterexample. A reducer of N could use the children
"US" and "N with a − deg u′ ∈ spec A_G for the witnessing u′".

## 9. What breaks for tournaments, hypergraphs, infinite graphs

| Step | Graph-specific ingredient | Tournaments | 3-uniform hypergraphs |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0, W1, REC | deg x = e(G) − e(G − x) and deg_{G−x}(y) = deg y − [x ~ y] | e(T − v) is constant, so the score is not tied to the card (t4 §10, debe_zinchenko §5); W1 then ties only a mixture of s(x) and Σ_{y→x} s(y) | the card degree drops by a codegree; no relation of this shape |
| REL | same two facts; **no symmetry of A** | holds formally *if* scores were tied (debe_zinchenko §5, item 2) | no adjacency matrix |
| kernel step | linear algebra | holds | – |
| Theorem S, Prop. DJ | symmetry of A | fail | – |

So Theorem U is graph-specific only through the bit law and T0, like Lemma G and Theorem D.
It is not a "one card plus a recognizable multiset" class in the sense of debe_codegree's
Theorem E: the hypothesis has no "for every G′" clause; uniqueness comes from a determinant.
Infinite graphs: every sum is finite and the kernel step is finite-dimensional.

## 12. Where this could break

1. **It does not close the step.** The remainder minus US still contains `KbcrMZwA~c@r` and
   everything of §7. US is one more carving, with a spectral certificate instead of a purity
   certificate.
2. **"Meets the remainder" rests on three computed graphs** (n = 15, 15, 35) and on my
   transcription of the five clauses in `t1us_lib.py` (pure, glue from t4's library; D and N
   re-transcribed here). A red team should re-check Shrikhande − v against the Lean clauses.
3. **REL at u ≠ u′.** The second use of REL needs deg u = deg u′; it follows from the card
   isomorphism (equal edge counts) and must not be forgotten in Lean.
4. **Integers.** The class asks for integer a, b and an integer kernel. Hou–Tian's
   integrality (secondary source) says nothing is lost; if it were false the class would only
   be smaller, the argument is unaffected.
5. **Regular G.** 2wl(a, b) then holds for many (a, b); the argument does not care (REC
   transfers whichever pair was chosen). Regular graphs are pure anyway.
6. **Proposition F** uses standard facts on strongly regular graphs from memory (marked). It
   is not part of the child; the child's argument is §§3–4 only.
7. **Novelty.** REL is Zinchenko's Lemma 3.1(c) with his card-type hypothesis removed (his own
   remark R4 in debe_zinchenko.md notes that §3 needs only the constants). I found no source
   for Theorem U or for the two-deleted description of the switch set; I searched only for
   the two-main-eigenvalue terminology.
8. **Lean spelling.** `Set.indicator` under `∑ y : Fin n` elaborates and `prop_of_kernel`
   compiles in Scratch, but W1 and REL were not drafted.
