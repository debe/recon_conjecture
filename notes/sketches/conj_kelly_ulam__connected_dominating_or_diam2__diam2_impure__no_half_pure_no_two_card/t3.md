## 1. Notation

G on V, |V| = n ≥ 3. A hypomorphism to H is σ with card isomorphisms f_x : G − x ≅ H − σx.
For u ≠ v: S = V ∖ {u, v}, F = G[S]. deg = degree in G unless a subscript says otherwise.

- **T0 (checked inputs).** e(G) = e(H) (`L_kelly_edge_count`), and for any graph K and vertex
  x, deg_K x = e(K) − e(K − x) (`L_kelly_card_edges_add_degree`). Hence: *if K ~ G and
  K − a ≅ G − x then deg_K a = deg_G x*. In particular deg_H σx = deg_G x.
- **Pointed card.** (G − x, y) for x ≠ y: the card with one distinguished vertex. Write
  (x, y) ≈ (x′, y′) if there is an isomorphism G − x → G − x′ mapping y to y′ (also across
  two graphs). (x, y) ≈ (x′, y′) implies G − x − y ≅ G − x′ − y′ (restrict;
  checked `restrictIso`).
- **Pointed transfer (L1⁺).** Φ(x, y) = (σx, f_x y) is a bijection from the ordered pairs of
  distinct vertices of G onto those of H, and Φ(x, y) ≈ (x, y). So for every pointed type ω,
  G and H have equally many ordered pairs of type ω, and equally many ordered pairs with a
  given type of G − x − y. (Same argument as the checked `pi_eq`; no Kelly count.)
  `debe_codegree.md` §3 observed that the rooted card is the only card-tied pair statistic;
  this sketch uses exactly that statistic.

## 2. The remainder: vertex-transitive graphs minus a vertex

Computed with `t3_lib.py` (clause-by-clause encoding of the frozen body).

- `KbcrMZwA~c@r` (n = 12, |Aut| = 2, six vertex orbits) is in the remainder and is isomorphic
  to C₁₃(1, 2, 4) − 0 (``KzlXWkJ`YDsJ`` in `t3_hunt_vt.py`; equal canonical forms).
- Its 66 two-deleted subgraphs fall into 14 types, of sizes 6 (|Aut F| = 1) and 3
  (|Aut F| = 2). **No two-deleted subgraph has a unique copy.** So the checked gluing class,
  and any class anchored by a unique copy, cannot contain it.
- Every card G − v has |Aut(G − v)| = 2 while Aut(G)_v = 1: **every card has an automorphism
  ρ_v that does not extend**, and ρ_v(N(v)) = L ∖ {v}, L the set of vertices of minimum degree.
  This is the situation of `debe_zinchenko.md` B6 turned around: B6 says that in a
  counterexample in normal form every card isomorphism extends; here none of the ρ_v does,
  and G is not in normal form (six card types), so the narrow class N does not apply.

Explanation (paper, standard): let X be vertex-transitive and G = X − r.
- An automorphism t of X with t(r) = v restricts to an isomorphism
  G − t⁻¹(r) = X − r − t⁻¹(r) → X − v − r = G − v. So the card types of G are fused by the
  whole group Aut(X), not only by Aut(G) ⊇ Aut(X)_r.
- The type of G − u − v is the Aut(X)-orbit type of the triple {r, u, v}. An orbit of triples
  of size |Aut(X)| / |stabilizer| contains r in 3·(orbit size)/(n + 1) triples. With a
  small stabilizer this is 3 or 6, never 1: the "3 or 6 copies" above.
- If t ∈ Aut(X) swaps r and v, then t restricted to G − v is an automorphism of the card that
  maps N(v) to N_X(r) ∖ {v} = L ∖ {v}: it does not extend unless r and v are twins in X.
- If Aut(X) is regular (X a graphical regular representation), Aut(G) is trivial and the
  card-equivalent pairs v, v⁻¹ are **pseudosimilar** in the sense of Godsil–Kocay
  (JCTB 32 (1982), secondary, UNVERIFIED): removal-similar with no automorphism between
  them. The two D₆ members ``JhCkmT`kIw_``, `JUwkeXelqv?` are of this kind (|Aut G| = 1,
  |Aut X| = 12, computed).

Why the earlier classes miss this family (paper): degrees are d, d + 1; purity fails by
Proposition L whenever the degree classes behave like orbits; the alternative re-attachment
ρ_v(N(v)) = L ∖ v has the level counts of N(v), so T2-type filters at one card see nothing.
A second card is needed, and the unique-copy anchor is unavailable.

Kocay, *Partial automorphisms and the reconstruction conjecture*, J. Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A
(1984; pages UNVERIFIED, abstract seen through a web search only), treats graphs with two
vertices of degree k − 1 and the rest of degree k, and states that one vertex of degree k − 1
is easy. A regular graph minus a vertex has d + 1 vertices of the smaller degree. I found no
source for "a vertex-transitive graph minus a vertex is reconstructible".

## 3. The twist at an anchored pair (paper; steps shared by T and T⁺)

Say H is **anchored at (u, v)** if there are a ≠ b in H and isomorphisms
f : G − u → H − a with f(v) = b and f″ : G − v → H − b with f″(u) = a.

**Lemma A (twist).** Let H ~ G be anchored at (u, v). Then:
1. deg_H a = deg_G u, deg_H b = deg_G v (T0).
2. f(S) = f″(S) = V(H) ∖ {a, b} and g := (f″|S)⁻¹ ∘ (f|S) is an automorphism of F.
3. For x ∈ S: [a ~ f(x)] = [u ~ g(x)] (f″ is an isomorphism G − v → H − b with u ↦ a), and
   deg_H f(x) = deg_G x − [u ~ x] + [u ~ g x] (card degrees agree under f).
4. Σ_{x ∈ S} (deg_H f(x))² = Σ_{x ∈ S} (deg_G x)².
   Argument: Σ_w deg_H(w)² = Σ_z deg_H(σz)² = Σ_z deg_G(z)² by T0 termwise; remove the terms
   a, b on the left and u, v on the right, equal by 1; f|S is a bijection onto V(H) ∖ {a, b}.

**Lemma B (degree-preserving twists are trivial).** In Lemma A, if deg_G(g x) = deg_G(x) for
all x ∈ S, then [u ~ g x] = [u ~ x] for all x ∈ S, and G ≅ H.
Paper argument. Put d_x = deg_G x, ε_x = [u ~ g x] − [u ~ x] ∈ {−1, 0, 1}. By 3 and 4,
Σ_S (d_x + ε_x)² = Σ_S d_x², so 2 Σ_S d_x ε_x + Σ_S ε_x² = 0. Reindexing by y = g x and using
d_{g x} = d_x: Σ_S d_x [u ~ g x] = Σ_S d_{g x} [u ~ g x] = Σ_S d_y [u ~ y]. So Σ_S d_x ε_x = 0,
hence Σ_S ε_x² = 0 and ε ≡ 0. Then the map f on V ∖ {u}, u ↦ a preserves adjacency on V ∖ u
(f), on pairs {u, x}, x ∈ S (ε = 0 and 3), and on {u, v} (deg_H a = deg_G u and the
neighbourhoods inside S correspond). This is the hypothesis of the checked `iso_of_nbhd`. ∎

Remarks.
- Lemma B is the degree filter L7 of t4 in closed form: t4 kept it as a diagnostic because the
  general statement "every admissible twist factorizes" was awkward. For degree-preserving g
  the filter decides everything, with no factorization and no double coset.
- An automorphism g of F preserves G-degrees iff it preserves x ↦ [u ~ x] + [v ~ x], i.e. the
  partition of S into common neighbours, private neighbours and common non-neighbours of
  u, v. It may still exchange N(u) ∖ N(v) with N(v) ∖ N(u); Lemma B says a hypomorphic H
  cannot use that.
- Wider variants (same argument, more plumbing): "g preserves degrees **or** g factorizes as
  in the checked gluing clause" (`T_star` in the Scratch file), and "every g passing the
  level-count filter of §3.1 factorizes". Tested: for n ≤ 7 the first variant gives the same
  class as the plain one (542 graphs at n = 7), so I do not recommend it.

### 3.1 Level-count form (not needed for T, T⁺)

For any g ∈ Aut(F): the degree sequence of the twist equals that of G iff
for all k, #{x ∈ S : u ~ x, u ≁ g x, deg x = k + 1} = #{x ∈ S : u ≁ x, u ~ g x, deg x = k}.
Tested as claim C1 of `t3_claim_twist.py`.

## 4. Theorem T (symmetric pointed pair)

**Theorem T.** Let u ≠ v and F = G − u − v. Put
𝒜 = {(x, y) : x ≠ y, (x, y) ≈ (u, v)}, ℬ = {(x, y) : x ≠ y, G − x − y ≅ F, (x, y) ∉ 𝒜}. Assume
- (a⁺) (v, u) ≈ (u, v): some isomorphism κ : G − u → G − v has κ(v) = u;
- (b⁺) |ℬ| < |𝒜|;
- (d) every automorphism of F preserves G-degrees.

Then G is reconstructible.

Paper argument. Let H ~ G with σ, f_x.
1. Define 𝒜_H, ℬ_H in H with the same reference pointed card (G − u, v) and the same F.
   By L1⁺, Φ maps 𝒜 onto 𝒜_H and ℬ onto ℬ_H. So |ℬ_H| < |𝒜_H|.
2. If (a, b) ∈ 𝒜_H then H − b − a ≅ F. If no (a, b) ∈ 𝒜_H had (b, a) ∈ 𝒜_H, the swap would
   map 𝒜_H injectively into ℬ_H, contradicting 1. So some (a, b) has
   f : (G − u, v) ≅ (H − a, b) and f₀ : (G − u, v) ≅ (H − b, a). Then f″ := f₀ ∘ κ⁻¹ is an
   isomorphism G − v → H − b with f″(u) = f₀(v) = a. H is anchored at (u, v).
3. Lemma A, then Lemma B with (d). ∎

(a⁺) holds when an automorphism of G swaps u and v, but it is weaker: κ is only a card
isomorphism. (b⁺) holds when G − u − v has a unique copy and (a⁺) holds; it also holds in
the 3-copy situation of §2: for `KbcrMZwA~c@r` and (u, v) = (0, 2), the type has the ordered
pairs (0, 2), (2, 0), (0, 10), (2, 8) in 𝒜 (the last two through ρ₀, ρ₂) and (10, 0), (8, 2)
in ℬ: 4 > 2.

## 5. Theorem T⁺ (degree-asymmetric pointed pair)

For G = X − r with Aut(X) regular there is no pair with (a⁺) in general. The census then has
the shape of a 3-cycle of pointed classes (the three pairs through r of a triple orbit), and
counting alone does not force an anchored pair: the bad case is a pair (a, b) of H with both
(a, b) and (b, a) of the type of (u, v). Degrees exclude the bad case for suitable (u, v).

**Theorem T⁺.** Let u ≠ v, α = [u ~ v], F = G − u − v. Put
- 𝒜 = {(x, y) : x ≠ y, (x, y) ≈ (u, v)};
- 𝒞 = {(x, y) : x ≠ y, (x, y) ≈ (y′, x′) for some (x′, y′) ∈ 𝒜} (the pointed types of the
  reversed members of 𝒜);
- ℛ = {(x, y) : x ≠ y, G − x − y ≅ F, (x, y) ∉ 𝒜 ∪ 𝒞}.

Assume
- (i) deg u − deg v ∉ {−α, 1 − α} (u ~ v: deg u ≠ deg v − 1, deg v; u ≁ v: deg u ≠ deg v,
  deg v + 1);
- (ii) |ℛ| < |𝒜|;
- (iii) for every (x, y) ∈ 𝒜, every automorphism of G − x − y preserves G-degrees.

Then G is reconstructible.

Paper argument. Let H ~ G. Define 𝒜_H, 𝒞_H, ℛ_H in H by the same reference pointed cards of
G. By L1⁺ their sizes are those of 𝒜, 𝒞, ℛ.
1. *No reversed pair inside 𝒜_H.* Let (a, b) ∈ 𝒜_H and β = [a ~ b]. T0: deg_H a = deg_G u.
   The card isomorphism gives deg_{H−a} b = deg_{G−u} v = deg v − α, so deg_H b = deg v − α + β.
   If also (b, a) ∈ 𝒜_H then deg_H b = deg_G u, so deg u − deg v = β − α ∈ {−α, 1 − α},
   against (i).
2. The swap maps 𝒜_H injectively into the ordered pairs of type F outside 𝒜_H. If it missed
   𝒞_H it would map into ℛ_H, against (ii). So some (a, b) ∈ 𝒜_H has (b, a) ∈ 𝒞_H: there is
   (x′, y′) ∈ 𝒜 with (H − b, a) ≈ (G − y′, x′), and (H − a, b) ≈ (G − u, v) ≈ (G − x′, y′).
   H is anchored at (x′, y′).
3. Lemma A at (x′, y′), then Lemma B with (iii). ∎

The two theorems are siblings: a common form is "((v, u) ∈ 𝒜 or (i)) and |ℛ| < |𝒜| and
(iii)". I keep them apart because T needs the degree clause at one pair only and has the
shorter statement.

Sanity (paper): if all two-deleted subgraphs of G are pairwise non-isomorphic and asymmetric
(the generic graph; Müller 1976, Bollobás 1990, both UNVERIFIED here), then 𝒜 = {(u, v)},
𝒞 = {(v, u)}, ℛ = ∅, (iii) is vacuous, and any pair with (i) puts G in T⁺.

## 6. Why this passes Proposition L

Proposition L (`debe_codegree.md` §9): on a graph whose degree classes are automorphism
orbits, every *first-vertex* key is constant on degree classes, so purity refinements
collapse. T and T⁺ do not refine a first-vertex key. They use (1) the pointed type of a pair,
(2) the symmetry of the census under the swap, (3) one global degree identity (Lemma A.4).
`GhEJ]o` and ``K[N_ZpBVe`q{`` (two vertex orbits, outside LHP by Proposition L) are in T
(8 and 6 pairs).

Relation to the **twist obstruction** of sketch t2 in this directory (written in parallel;
read only in its §2): at a vertex u whose card receives an isomorphism that does not extend,
no hypothesis on G forces N′_u = N(u) for the *given* f_u, because G is hypomorphic to itself
with a twisted f_u. T and T⁺ are not of that kind. They never use the given f_u at u: the
census produces a pair (a, b) of H together with *new* pointed isomorphisms f, f″, and
Lemma B is a statement about those. On H = G with f = a non-extending card automorphism ρ
fixing v and f″ = id, Lemma B would say "ρ|S preserves G-degrees ⇒ ρ preserves N(u)"; its
hypothesis fails there, since ρ moves N(u) and hence changes degrees. Clause (d) / (iii) is
what excludes that case. In `KbcrMZwA~c@r` every vertex is twisted (computed, §2), and the
class T still contains it at the six pairs {u, τu}, τ the non-trivial automorphism.

## 8. What breaks for tournaments, 3-uniform hypergraphs, infinite graphs

| Step | Tournaments | Graph-specific ingredient |

So for a Stockmeyer pair with a pair (u, v) satisfying the census hypotheses, the other
tournament would be a non-trivial twist by a score-preserving g, and the place where the
argument stops is A.1/A.4. Tested (`t3_tournament_check.py`, construction of

the test is vacuous.
3-uniform hypergraphs: the pair {u, v} lies in up to n − 2 edges that neither card shows, so
Lemma A.3 fails (t4 §10). Infinite graphs: the census and the sums are finite.

## 10. What is left, and where this could break

1. **Nothing here closes the step.** After T and T⁺ the D2 remainder has no member that I
   know, but the hunts covered only Cayley-type families up to n = 14 and one random family.
   I have no argument that the new residual is empty, and I expect it is not: a
   vertex-transitive X whose triple orbits through r fuse more pairs (larger stabilizers
   that are not rank 3), or graphs that are not cards of regular graphs at all.
2. **No argument that every vertex-transitive graph minus a vertex is in T ∪ T⁺.** The
   hypotheses (b⁺), (ii), (d), (iii) are properties of the triple orbits and of Aut(F);
   they hold in every case computed, and I have no all-n argument for a family. The clean
   statement "X Cayley on an abelian group with Aut(X) = translations ⋊ ⟨−1⟩ ⇒ X − 0 ∈ T at
   (u, −u)" would need Aut(X − {0, u, −u}) = ⟨−1⟩ and exactly three copies of the type,
   neither of which follows from the hypothesis.
3. **Clause (iii) of T⁺ quantifies over all pairs of 𝒜**, because the argument does not
   control at which member of 𝒜 the graph H is anchored. If a red team finds that (iii) at
   (u, v) alone is not enough, that is expected; the census test without (iii) has 256
   non-isomorphic H at n = 7.
4. **The census conditions are strict inequalities between `ncard`s of sets defined with
   existential isomorphisms.** Statements of this kind are easy to get subtly wrong; the
   bodies above elaborate, and their Python transcriptions were tested, but the Lean bodies
   themselves were not evaluated on graphs.
5. **Lemma A.4 uses the whole degree sequence, hence n ≥ 3 through `L_kelly_edge_count`.**
6. **The twin step's numbers** use my encoding of its predicate; cross-check before citing.
7. **Attribution.** I found no source for T, T⁺ or Lemma B. They are two-card arguments in
   the tradition of Müller 1976 / Bollobás 1990 (UNVERIFIED) and of t2 lemma C, t4 L5–L7;
   the pointed census and the sum-of-squares step are my own derivations. Kocay 1984
   ("partial automorphisms") is the closest literature I saw by title and abstract; not read.
8. **Other parts of the angle that gave nothing.**
   - Double cosets Aut(G − u) \ Iso / Aut(G): for a non-extending card automorphism ρ_v the
     two re-attachments N(v) and ρ_v N(v) give isomorphic graphs, so one card never decides;
     this is why the argument needs the second card.
   - Individualisation–refinement: not used; canonical forms served only in the scripts.
   - A Godsil–Kocay lift (is every impure pair cut out of an automorphism of a larger
     graph?): true by construction for the members of §2, where the larger graph is X; I have
     no argument that remainder members are always of this form, and no use for it beyond
     the hunt.
