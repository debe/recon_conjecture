## 2. Field sweep: what each identity gives on the remainder

Setting (t4 §2): a hypomorphism σ with card isomorphisms f_x. N′_u := f_u⁻¹(N_H(σu)), and
G′ := G with u re-attached to N′_u is isomorphic to H and agrees with G off u.
**Triv(u)** := {α(N(u′)) : α an isomorphism G − u′ → G − u}. Then G ≅ H ⇔ N′_u ∈ Triv(u)
(paper: an isomorphism φ : G → H gives α = f_u⁻¹ ∘ φ restricted to G − φ⁻¹σu; conversely
α ∪ {u′ ↦ u} is an isomorphism G → G′). Call u **twisted** if |Triv(u)| ≥ 2, i.e. some card
isomorphism α : G − u′ → G − u has α(N(u′)) ≠ N(u).

**Twist obstruction (paper).** If u is twisted by α : G − u′ → G − u, there is a hypomorphism
from G to H := G itself with N′_u ≠ N(u):
- u′ = u: σ = id, f_x = id for x ≠ u, f_u = α. Then N′_u = α⁻¹(N(u)) ≠ N(u).
- u′ ≠ u: σ = the transposition (u u′), f_u = α⁻¹ : G − u → G − u′, f_{u′} = α, f_x = id
  otherwise. Then N′_u = α(N(u′)) ≠ N(u).

So no hypothesis on G can force N′_u = N(u) for every hypomorphism at a twisted u. Every class
whose argument ends with "N′_u = N(u), then T1" (Lemma P, Theorem D, Corollary A1, Theorem B,
t4's class T) has its hypothesis false at every twisted vertex; in particular a level-wise
half-pure vertex is untwisted, and a graph in which every vertex is twisted lies outside all of
them.

## 3. The class N⁺

### 3.1 Definition

G on n ≥ 3 vertices is in **N⁺** if for some d:
1. every degree is d or d + 1;
2. **equitable**: vertices of equal degree have equally many neighbours of degree d + 1;
3. **(H3)** every vertex v of degree d is twisted, or every vertex v of degree d + 1 is twisted
   (twisted: some u and some isomorphism α : G − u → G − v with a neighbour x of u such that
   α(x) is not adjacent to v).

Conditions 1 and 2 say "Σ_{y~x} deg y is an affine function of deg x" for two consecutive
degrees, i.e. at most two main eigenvalues (Hagos 2002, Linear Algebra Appl.; from memory,
UNVERIFIED; not used in the argument). Regular graphs satisfy 1–3 trivially (choose d so that
the quantified class is empty); the argument covers them.

**Theorem N⁺ (paper argument, §4).** Every graph in N⁺ is reconstructible.

### 3.2 N ⊆ N⁺ (paper)

Let G be in N: degrees d, d + 1; equal degree ⇒ isomorphic cards; α : G − u → G − u′ with
α(N(u)) ≠ N(u′). Equitable: the number of vertices of card-degree d + 1 in G − x is
q − χ(x) − h(x) (§4, A1), a card invariant, and deg x = e(G) − e(G − x). (H3) for the degree
class of u′: for v with deg v = deg u′ take γ : G − v → G − u′. If γ(N(v)) ≠ N(u′), then γ⁻¹ is
a non-extending isomorphism into v. Otherwise γ⁻¹ ∘ α : G − u → G − v maps N(u) to
γ⁻¹(α(N(u))) ≠ γ⁻¹(N(u′)) = N(v).

So a checked N⁺ gives N in about 80 lines, and the builder of `two_card_narrow` does not need
A6 (transport of C_G(u) along a permutation) at all.

## 4. Paper argument for Theorem N⁺

Suppose G ∈ N⁺, H hypomorphic to G, H ≇ G. By the checked `found_reconstructible_compl` we may
assume (H3) for the low class (the complement swaps the classes and keeps 1–3; §4.5).

Notation. L, U = vertices of degree d, d + 1 ("low", "high"); χ = indicator of U; q = |U|;
h(x) = number of high neighbours; h = b on L and e on U; θ = e − b. 𝒦 = labelled graphs on V
hypomorphic to G (so G, H ∈ 𝒦, and 𝒦 is closed under isomorphic copies on V: checked
`found_hypomorphic_iso_invariant`, `_symm`, `_trans`). The same letters with index K for K ∈ 𝒦.
K′ is an **extension** of K at u if K′ ∈ 𝒦 and K′, K agree on all pairs avoiding u;
C_K(u) = set of extensions. For K₁, K₂ agreeing off u: z(x) = [x ~₁ u] − [x ~₂ u] (z(u) = 0),
k = #{z = 1}. (A_K f)(x) = Σ_{y ~_K x} f(y).

### 4.1 Stage A: valid for every K ∈ 𝒦 (uses 1, 2; not (H3))

- **A0 (T0, checked pattern).** deg_K ρx = deg_G x along the hypomorphism ρ. So K has degrees in
  {d, d + 1} and q high vertices.
- **A1 (the counting identity).** h_K(y) = b + θχ_K(y) for all K ∈ 𝒦, y.
  In K − y a vertex w has card-degree deg_K w − [w ~ y] (checked `deg_in_card`), so the number of
  vertices of card-degree d + 1 is q − χ_K(y) − h_K(y) (no vertex has degree d + 2). Cards
  K − ρx ≅ G − x have equal counts, and χ_K(ρx) = χ_G(x).
- **Lemma Z (eigen-relation).** K₁, K₂ ∈ 𝒦 agree off u, s = χ_{K₁}(u). Then
  (a) deg_{K₁} u = deg_{K₂} u (edge counts, checked), Σ z = 0, and deg_{K₁} x − deg_{K₂} x = z(x)
  for x ≠ u; (b) z(x) = 1 ⇒ x high in K₁, low in K₂; z(x) = −1 ⇒ low in K₁, high in K₂; hence
  χ_{K₁} − χ_{K₂} = z; (c) (A_{K₁} z)(x) = (θ − s) z(x) for x ≠ u and (A_{K₁} z)(u) = k;
  (d) k = 0 ⇒ K₁ = K₂.
  (c): θz(x) = h_{K₁}(x) − h_{K₂}(x) = Σ_y [x ~₁ y](χ₁ − χ₂)(y) + Σ_y ([x ~₁ y] − [x ~₂ y])χ₂(y)
  = (A_{K₁}z)(x) + z(x)·s, since rows x of K₁, K₂ differ only in column u, by z(x).
- **A3 (extensions exist).** For K ∈ 𝒦, u, X ∈ 𝒦: K − u ≅ X − τu by some g; pull X back along
  g ∪ {u ↦ τu}. The result agrees with K off u and is isomorphic to X. With X = G and X = H:
  C_K(u) has a member ≠ K, for every K ∈ 𝒦 and every u.
- **Lemma V (vanishing).** K ∈ 𝒦, s = χ_K(u), f : V → ℤ with A_K f = (θ − s) f. Then f(u) = 0.
  Take K′ ≠ K in C_K(u): k·f(u) = ⟨f, (A_K − (θ − s))z⟩ = ⟨(A_K − (θ − s))f, z⟩ = 0, k ≥ 1.
  **Uses A_K symmetric.**
- **A5 (rigidity).** K₁, K₂ ∈ C_K(u) ∖ {K} have k₁ = k₂, and z₁ = z₂ on {x : χ_K(x) = χ_K(u)}.
  y = k₂z₁ − k₁z₂ satisfies A_K y = (θ − s)y, so y = 0 on that class (Lemma V at each of its
  vertices). The class contains x₀ with z₁(x₀) ≠ 0 (s = 0: a gained vertex, low in K; s = 1: a
  lost vertex, high in K; both exist as k₁ ≥ 1). k₂z₁(x₀) = k₁z₂(x₀) with z₂(x₀) ∈ {−1, 0, 1}
  forces z₂(x₀) = z₁(x₀) and k₁ = k₂.

So for each v there is κ(v) ≥ 1 with k = κ(v) for every K ∈ C_G(v) ∖ {G}; for v low the gained
set R₀(v) ⊆ L ∖ N[v] is the same for all K, the lost set S(K) ⊆ U ∩ N(v) may depend on K; for
w high the lost set R₁(w) ⊆ U ∩ N(w) is the same for all K. And, with z_K := z[G, K; v]:

    (∗)   (A_G − (θ − χ(v))) z_K = κ(v) ε_v.

**κ may depend on v.** This is the difference to N, where A6 makes κ constant on degree classes.

### 4.2 Stage B without A6

- **B1.** K at v, K′ at v′ ≠ v. Pair (∗) for K with z_K′ and (∗) for K′ with z_K; A_G is symmetric.
  (b) deg v = deg v′: κ(v) z_K′(v) = κ(v′) z_K(v′). Hence for high w, w′:
  w ∈ R₁(w′) ⇔ w′ ∈ R₁(w). (And κ(w) = κ(w′) then; not needed.)
  (a) v low, w high (K′ at w): ⟨z_K, z_K′⟩ = κ(w) z_K(w) − κ(v) z_K′(v).
- **B2.** If A_G f = θf and f = 0 on L, then f(x) = f(w) for all high w and x ∈ R₁(w).
  Pair (∗) at w with f: Σ_{x ∈ R₁(w)} f(x) = κ(w) f(w) (the gained part lies in L). Then
  Σ_{w ∈ U} Σ_{x ∈ R₁(w)} (f(x) − f(w))² = Σ_x κ(x)f(x)² − 2Σ_w κ(w)f(w)² + Σ_w κ(w)f(w)² = 0,
  the first term by the symmetry B1(b) and |R₁(x)| = κ(x).
- **B3.** v low, K₁ ≠ K₂ in C_G(v) ∖ {G} ⇒ S(K₁) ∩ S(K₂) = ∅ (R3 of `debe_zinchenko.md`):
  A5 with base K₁ and its extensions G, K₂: −z_{K₁} and z_{K₂} − z_{K₁} agree on L_{K₁}, and a
  vertex of S(K₁) is low in K₁.
- **B4 (two steps).** Let v be low with K₁ ≠ K₂ in C_G(v) ∖ {G}. f := z_{K₁} − z_{K₂} =
  1_{S(K₁)} − 1_{S(K₂)} satisfies A_G f = θf (same κ(v) by A5) and f = 0 on L.
  *Step 1.* For w ∈ S(K₁): f = 1 on R₁(w) by B2, so R₁(w) ⊆ S(K₁) ∖ {w} and κ(w) ≤ κ(v) − 1.
  *Step 2.* Take K′ ∈ C_G(w) ∖ {G} (A3), Γ := its gained set (|Γ| = κ(w) ≥ 1, Γ ⊆ L ∖ N(w)),
  and v′ ∈ Γ. **If** C_G(v′) has two members M₁ ≠ M₂ other than G: z_{Mᵢ}(w) = 0 because
  S(Mᵢ) ⊆ N(v′) and w ≁ v′. f′ := z_{M₁} − z_{M₂} has f′(w) = 0, so f′ = 0 on R₁(w) by B2, so
  R₁(w) ∩ S(M₁) = ∅. B1(a): ⟨z_{M₁}, z_K′⟩ = κ(w)·0 + κ(v′) = κ(v′). Termwise: high terms
  vanish, low terms are ≤ 1 and are nonzero only on Γ ∖ {v′}. So κ(v′) ≤ κ(w) − 1.
- **B5 (descent; the new step).** Under (H3) every low vertex v has three members in C_G(v):
  G, the copy X_v of G obtained by re-attaching v to α(N(u)) ≠ N(v), and a copy of H (A3;
  X_v ≅ G ≇ H, so they differ). Choose a low v with κ(v) minimal. Steps 1 and 2 give a low v′
  (also with three members) and κ(v′) ≤ κ(w) − 1 ≤ κ(v) − 2. Contradiction.

Low vertices exist: an extension K′ ≠ G at any vertex has k ≥ 1, hence a gained vertex, which
is low (Lemma Z (b)). This also disposes of regular G. ∎

In N the paper gets κ(v′) = κ(v) from A6 and stops after one round; here nothing relates κ(v′)
to κ(v), and the descent needs the hypothesis at *every* low vertex because Γ depends on the
unknown H.

### 4.3 What the theorem says in a real graph

With 𝒦 replaced by the graphs with the same parameters (d, q, b, e) ("relaxed extensions"), the
argument shows: if every vertex of G and every low vertex of every relaxed extension of G at a
low vertex has a relaxed extension, then some low vertex of G has at most one. Shrikhande − v,
its complement, 4×4 rook − v, Paley(13) − v, Paley(17) − v, Petersen − v, `GhEJ]o` and
``K[N_ZpBVe`q{`` all have **exactly one** relaxed extension at every vertex, and it is the
twisted copy of G: there is no room for an H (`t2_model_check.py`). That is the statement
tested in §6; it is not vacuous on these graphs.

### 4.5 Complement

Ḡ has degrees n − 2 − d and n − 1 − d (old high = new low), is equitable (the number of Ḡ-high
Ḡ-neighbours of x is p − [x low] − (deg x − h(x))), has the same card isomorphisms, and
α(N_Ḡ(u)) ≠ N_Ḡ(v) ⇔ α(N_G(u)) ≠ N_G(v). So "every high vertex twisted" for G is "every low
vertex twisted" for Ḡ.

## 5. N⁺ meets the remainder

The Shrikhande graph: Cayley graph of ℤ₄ × ℤ₄ with connection set ±(1,0), ±(0,1), ±(1,1)
(S. S. Shrikhande 1959, Ann. Math. Statist. 30; from memory, UNVERIFIED; every property used
below was computed from this definition). G := Shrikhande − v, `NhfJgcXBGMoHoXWKcBg`:

| Clause of the frozen step | Value (computed) |
|---|---|
| G, Ḡ connected, both diameters ≤ 2 | yes |
| no pure degree class | yes (no pure degree) |
| no unique-copy factorizing pair | yes (no two-deleted subgraph has a unique copy) |
| no level-wise half-pure vertex | yes (no pair is forced at all) |
| not in N | yes: degrees 5⁶6⁹, three card types (class sizes 6, 6, 3) |
| in N⁺ | yes: (d, b, e) = (5, 3, 4), every low and every high vertex twisted |

Why (paper, for the reducer's understanding, not needed): x ↦ u + v − x is an automorphism of an
abelian Cayley graph swapping u and v, so it restricts to an automorphism of G − u that moves
N(u); an SRG minus a vertex is equitable; and Aut(Shrikhande)_v has two orbits on the
non-neighbours of v. The same recipe (abelian Cayley SRG of rank ≥ 4, minus a vertex) should
give members for larger n; the cyclic Latin-square graph on ℤ₅² minus a vertex is in N (two
card types), so I have **no second family** and no member with n < 15.

Hunt (`t2_probe_hunt.py`, about 1100 D2 graphs: random self-complementary n = 8..13,
near-regular and bidegree n = 9..12): no remainder member at all. The remainder is thin; its
known members are `KbcrMZwA~c@r` (not in N⁺) and the two n = 15 graphs (in N⁺).

## 8. Where this could break

1. **Steps 1–2 of B4 and the descent are untested** in any non-degenerate instance (§6). A
   red team should re-derive B1(a) with signs, the claim z_{Mᵢ}(w) = 0, and the termwise bound.
   (B1(a) and B1(b) themselves are tested: 84 and 15626 instances.)
2. **I did not read Zinchenko's paper.** Stage A and B1–B4 are taken from the repo's audit
   `debe_zinchenko.md` and re-derived here line by line; the paper (arXiv:2611.13728v1,
   unrefereed) is cited through that audit only. That audit says of the equitable case with
   several card types: "A6 fails, and with it B4. No argument beyond A5." §4.2 is my answer:
   B1(b) and B2 never used a constant κ, and B5 replaces the equality.
3. **(H3) is strong.** It asks for a twist at every vertex of a degree class, because the
   vertex v′ of Step 2 is chosen by the unknown H. I found no way to restrict it.
4. **The class is thin.** Known members outside N: two graphs on 15 vertices. Equitable
   two-degree graphs are rare (128 of the 10249 graphs tested in §6), and those with a twist
   everywhere rarer. N⁺ does not shrink the remainder in any range we can enumerate.
5. **`KbcrMZwA~c@r` stays out.** Two degrees, not equitable (h takes the values 2, 3, 4 in each
   class; walk-matrix rank 6). Lemma Z needs h to be a function of the degree: in general
   h_{K₁}(x) − h_{K₂}(x) is a difference of card invariants of two different cards and I cannot
   express it by z. With r main eigenvalues the same computation gives Q_u(A)z ∈
   span{ε_u, Aε_u, …} with deg Q_u = r − 1, and Lemma V degrades to one linear condition on
   (f(u), (Af)(u), …); the sign argument of A5 is lost (as `debe_zinchenko.md` §8 found for
   three degrees).
6. **More than two degrees.** Lemma Z holds verbatim whenever Σ_{y~x} deg y = λ·deg x + μ, with
   eigenvalue λ − deg u (own derivation, untested). A5 then needs a flipped vertex of degree
   deg u, which level balance does not provide, and Stage B uses "lost = high, gained = low".
7. **Twist obstruction as a guide, not a theorem about the remainder.** The obstruction itself
   is a paper argument. "Every graph left by Theorem D has every vertex twisted" is an
   observation on twelve graphs.
8. **The twin lease.** Nothing here was computed for the dominating-edge twin beyond the table
   in §2.
